Literature without authorship: Variation and style in AI-generated
fiction

Generative AI has shown that Large Language Models can generate literary
fiction (Danesi, 2024). Fiction, however, is culturally and historically
tied to the notion of authorship. Literary fiction presupposes
originality, and originality presupposes variation: each work of
literature is expected to be unique. If style depends on different
sources (writers) making different choices, and AI texts all come from
the same source (the same LLM), then AI cannot *have* style in the
authorial sense. The emergence of AI as a producer of fictional texts
therefore raises the question of the consequences for literary fiction
when texts are generated without an authorial subject, and therefore
without the main cultural mechanism that underwrites originality through
variation. Understanding AI-generated fiction enables us to see what
remains of literature when authorship is removed (Danesi, 2024). If
meaning arises not from authorial intention (Barthes, 1977), then a text
generated without an author is not an illegitimate text. The key issue
is therefore not whether AI can generate texts that pass as literary
texts, but whether fiction generated without authorship preserves the
variability that underlies originality in literary production. To
address this issue, a corpus of 1000 human-authored short stories was
collected for 30 authors. For each human text, an AI text was generated
by GPT. The corpus was analyzed using Lexical Multidimensional Analysis
(Berber Sardinha & Fitzsimmons-Doolan, 2025), designed to identify sets
of correlated lexical items that when interpreted reveal stylistic
dimensions. The analysis was conducted by POS-tagging and lemmatizing
the corpus, and entering the lemma counts in a factor analysis, which
returned factors of correlated lexical items. This enabled us to measure
the variation among human-authored texts and AI-generated texts. The
dimensions and the comparisons between human and AI texts will be
discussed in the presentation.
