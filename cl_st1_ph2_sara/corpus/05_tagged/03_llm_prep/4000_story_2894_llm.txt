All night the wind had walked behind the Pequod like
a barefoot executioner, making no noise save in the high
rigging, where the slender cords complained as if invisible fingers
tried their strength upon them. The sea, black and moderately
heaving, showed here and there a wan combing of foam;
but no moon looked down to silver it, and the
stars, withdrawn into their immense blue chambers, seemed to abandon
that ship to the single red eye which burned beneath
Ahab's brow.

He stood upon the quarter-deck, one palm pressed against the
mizzen shrouds, his ivory leg socketed in its auger-hole. No
man spoke to him. Even the helmsman turned the spokes
softly, lest the click of wood on wood should disturb
that inward council wherein the old captain disputed with gods,
devils, charts, winds, and the one white shape that outranked
them all.

Suddenly Ahab lifted his head.

"Ha!"

It was not a shout, but the sharp intake of
one who, passing a sealed tomb, hears knocking within.

Again he breathed. His nostrils widened; his whole scarred face
leaned eastward. Then, with a motion almost animal, he crossed
to the weather rail and bent over the obscure water.

"D'ye smell him?" he demanded.

The watch stared.

"Captain?"

"The musky reek—the warm, rank breath of him! There's a
living furnace somewhere under that black cloth. Call me not
mad when my blood points truer than your compasses. Mr.
Starbuck!"

The chief mate came swiftly from the cabin companionway, buttoning
his coat against the salt wind.

"Sir?"

"Bring her two points to leeward. Brace round! Let the
topgallant-sails draw. He has crossed us."

Starbuck glanced at the binnacle, then at the indistinguishable horizon.
"No light, sir; no spout; no cry from aloft."

"No light? I have his whiteness here!" Ahab struck his
breast. "No spout? My ears hear it beneath the world.
There—look!"

Along the starboard quarter, a broad, oily disturbance stretched through
the waves, faintly luminous with broken sea-fire. It was no
common current. The wake ran onward in a straight, majestic
line, as though some submerged king, refusing the vulgar air,
traveled beneath the ocean with a road unrolling behind him.

Starbuck's grave face contracted.

"A whale's wake."

"The whale's," said Ahab. "Hard up! Let her follow the
writing of his tail."

The braces were manned; blocks rattled; the yards swung; and
the Pequod, obedient in every timber, turned her black beak
into that phosphorescent avenue. Stunsails were set until she seemed
to spread more wings than any natural bird could own.
The wind entered them all; the masts bowed; the coppered
hull hurried onward, cleaving the nocturnal floor where Moby Dick
had passed unseen.

Now there is a scent proper to the sperm whale,
differing alike from the sour breath of the right whale
and the fishy exhalations of lesser tribes. At close quarters
it mingles oil, salt, and a certain medicinal musk, such
as might steam from the pharmacy of Neptune. Old whalemen,
whose noses have been disciplined by years of blubber-rooms, try-works,
and crowded cruising grounds, may sometimes detect this odor before
the eye discovers its source. Yet that Ahab should have
found it upon so vast a night, amid tarred rigging,
wet canvas, smoke, men, and the universal respiration of the
sea—this seemed less an act of sense than of sorcery.

But obsession is a strange dog-vane. Though every wind lie
dead, it still points toward its private tempest.

Throughout the dark hours the Pequod followed that vanishing trace.
Sometimes the wake broadened and gleamed, then faded among common
ripples; sometimes a far concussion trembled under the hull, as
if an enormous shoulder had brushed the foundations of the
deep. Ahab paced little. He stood fixed, and thereby seemed
to move more terribly than all moving things. The ship
sped; clouds traveled; constellations wheeled; but he, the nailed center
of that rushing circle, changed only in the gathering fierceness
of his gaze.

Near dawn the east grew gray—not bright, but less dark,
like iron breathed upon. Then came a seam of saffron;
then a widening tiger-yellow stain; and at last daybreak, throwing
back the ocean's curtain, revealed a world smoothed almost to
quietude.

"Man the mastheads!" cried Ahab. "Every eye aloft! If ye
see a gull, report the feather; if ye see foam,
tell whether wind or fin hath fathered it. Look sharp!
He is before us!"

Men climbed. Feet pattered in ratlines. Queequeg, Tashtego, and Daggoo
mounted with the easy upward flowing of smoke, each gaining
his appointed height. Below them, the crew strained across the
pale waters. Breakfast went untouched. Pipes died between teeth. Every
man's vision seemed drawn out of him and spread, net-like,
over the sea.

The sun raised its full round forehead.

"There she blows!"

The cry descended from the mainmast as lightning descends a
rod.

"Where away?" thundered Ahab.

"Three points on the lee bow! There again! A white
bush—high and straight!"

We saw it then: first a vapor, hanging remote and
solitary against the morning; next, beneath it, the brief rolling
disclosure of a back so white that the surrounding sea
appeared suddenly blue beyond all former blueness. It rose like
a snow-hill lifted from beneath the tropics; it sank; and
the long polished ridge of its departure shone as marble
shines beneath running water.

"D'ye mark the crooked jaw?" Ahab called upward.

"Aye! The same!"

"The hump?"

"Like a white tower, sir!"

"Moby Dick!" cried Ahab; and the name passed through the
Pequod not as a word passes, but as flame travels
a powder-train.

Then all repose was shattered.

"Stand by the boats! Lower away! Mr. Starbuck, the ship
is thine. Keep close, but run us not down. Stubb!
Flask! Give way the instant your keels kiss water. My
boat first—mine first, I say!"

The tackles shrieked through their blocks. Boats dropped from the
davits, struck the sea, and rode for one elastic moment
like brown leaves upon glass. Men tumbled into them; oars
were thrust out; harpoons glimmered in their crotches. Fedallah took
the bow of Ahab's boat, his turban dark, his eyes
having that secret midnight glitter which belongs to cats and
prophets.

"Lower!"

Down went the captain, neither climbing nor descending so much
as falling under command of his own will. His ivory
leg struck the thwart; he seized the steering oar.

"Give way!"

Three boats leaped forward. Their double-banked oars dipped together, and
at each stroke cast off round bracelets of brightness. Behind
them, the Pequod shortened sail and followed like a mother
hawk restrained from overtaking her young.

Far ahead, Moby Dick went leisurely, showing now the corrugated
brow, now the immense wrinkled back, now nothing but the
regular silver blossoming of his breath. Around him fluttered a
congregation of white sea-fowl. Some hovered over the vapor of
his spout; others settled in the tranquil furrow behind him,
plucking at small creatures raised by his passage from the
lower waters. He appeared not pursued, but attended—as though those
winged acolytes bore witness to a mild oceanic divinity proceeding
toward its altar.

"Oh, whale!" murmured an old sailor in Stubb's boat, though
he pulled till the veins rose purple on his arms.
"Thou look'st peaceful as Sunday."

"Aye," said Stubb, "and Sunday hath churchyards. Pull, boys! We'll
ring his bell for him!"

The boats flew onward; yet the whale's unhurried movement mocked
them. There is in certain vast bodies a majesty which
makes speed invisible. A frigate under crowded canvas seems to
labor; an eagle need but alter one feather; the planet
itself races while grave men call the ground steadfast. So
with Moby Dick. His flukes rose and fell with the
drowsy measure of a sleeper's hand, yet every stroke carried
him beyond the furious reach of many oars.

"Pull!" cried Ahab. "Break backs and mend them afterward! He
steals the world from under us!"

The white whale sounded.

His head dipped. His long back rolled upward, acre following
acre; then the small of it narrowed toward the tail.
Up came the flukes—broad, scarred, dripping, their ragged edges cutting
the sky. For a suspended instant they hung there like
the two leaves of some enormous gate through which the
dead pass downward. Then they vanished, and the sea closed
softly over them.

"Peak your oars!" ordered Ahab.

The blades stood erect. All three boats stopped and listened.

For when a whale goes down near pursuing craft, eyesight
resigns its kingship to hearing. Every splash may be the
upward rush; every pressure beneath the keel, the first touch
of a rising brow. The crews leaned over the gunwales,
peering into the green crystalline dimness. Below, shafts of morning
descended and failed. A few bubbles climbed, expanding as they
rose; beneath them there was nothing—then less than nothing; for
the thought of a concealed immensity makes vacancy itself monstrous.

"How long?" whispered Flask.

"He sounded shallow," said Starbuck's boat-steerer.

Stubb spat overboard. "He'll come where he pleases, and charge
no pilotage."

Ahab's face moved from side to side. His hand gripped
the steering oar so violently that the tendons stood like
cords along his wrist.

"Back!" he shouted. "Back all!"

Even as he spoke, the sea directly beneath his boat
changed color. The deep green whitened, not with foam, but
with a vast ascending pallor. It enlarged; it took shape.
A forehead emerged from the abyss, straight under the keel.

"Starboard! Pull, ye dead men!"

The crew obeyed with one convulsive stroke. Ahab wrenched the
steering oar, and the boat slewed half around. Thus its
center escaped the full battering ascent; but Moby Dick rose
beside it, his enormous head lifting water in smooth, sliding
cataracts. One small black eye appeared—not enraged, not hurried, but
fixed upon them with a terrible composure.

For a heartbeat whale and man regarded one another.

Then the jaw opened.

The long lower mandible, white and scratched as an ancient
ivory portcullis, passed beneath the boat. The upper jaw descended
over it. Ahab sprang backward, but the whale closed his
mouth upon the cedar hull midway between bow and stern.

There came a dry, appalling crack.

The boat bent like a wand, split, and burst in
two. Oars flew. Men were tossed outward among planks, tubs,
lines, paddles, and the glittering heads of lances. Ahab himself,
flung from the fractured stern, struck the water with his
scarred head foremost and disappeared.

"Mates! Captain overboard!" shouted Flask.

But no rescue could yet approach. Moby Dick released the
wreck and turned among it. His broad forehead pushed the
floating fragments into a revolving ring. Swimming men were drawn
with them, helpless satellites around that pale, deliberate planet. Each
time the whale circled, his flank showed beneath the surface,
so close that barnacle-scars and old iron marks could be
seen upon it. The water mounded over his shoulders. His
tail, sweeping slowly, possessed power enough in its least careless
movement to pulp a man against the sky.

Ahab broke the surface, coughing, his gray hair plastered upon
his brow.

"Swim clear!" cried Stubb. "All hands, swim clear!"

"Clear?" Ahab struck out toward the whale. "I am clear
only near him!"

Fedallah seized the captain's coat and dragged him backward as
the immense head came around again. Moby Dick's jaw opened
slightly; splintered cedar dropped from between his teeth. He seemed
to examine those scattered men—not ravenously, for the sperm whale
is no common devourer of sailors, but with that unfathomable
attention by which Nature sometimes appears to consider whether she
shall permit an insect one more minute of sunlight.

The surviving boats backed water, unable to pierce the circle.
Their crews shouted, brandished lances, and beat the sea with
oars. The whale turned inward. His brow bore down upon
the swimmers.

Then the Pequod came.

Starbuck had seen the ruin through his glass. Casting caution
behind him, he ordered the yards braced and drove the
ship toward the white whale, though boats and men lay
strewn before her. The vessel leaned under her canvas. Her
bow-wave rose; her black hull grew rapidly above the swimmers,
a moving cliff furred with weed and copper-green stains.

"Mind the wreckage!" Starbuck cried to the helmsman. "Port—steady—steady!"

The Pequod's cutwater entered the circle. Moby Dick swung away
from the advancing hull. For one magnificent instant he lay
broadside, his whiteness shining against the ship's tar-black flank, the
two antagonistic immensities divided only by a strip of churning
blue. Then he sank, passed under the bows, and emerged
beyond the starboard quarter. A volley of spray sprang from
his flukes. He moved off.

"Pick them up! Every soul!" said Starbuck.

Stubb's and Flask's boats darted among the fragments. Hands reached
down; drowning men clutched gunwales; Queequeg hauled one sailor bodily
across two thwarts as though landing a codfish. Fedallah entered
without aid and crouched dripping in the bow. Last came
Ahab, held beneath the arms by Stubb and a harpooneer.

His face was bloodless. His ivory leg hung strangely; his
ribs seemed crushed inward; and for a moment even those
who feared him felt that his animating fire had been
quenched. He lay along the bottom boards while seawater ran
from his clothes, collecting redly where his brow was cut.

"Easy, old man," said Stubb, with unusual gentleness. "The whale
hath hammered thee broadside."

Ahab's eyelids opened.

"Which way?"

"Sir?"

"Which way went he?"

Stubb stared, then laughed despite himself. "There's the captain alive
inside the corpse! To leeward, sir—fast."

"Then pull."

"Pull? Our men are spent; your boat's kindling; and your
leg—"

"My leg? I have one rooted deeper than ivory. Pull!"

When they gained the ship, tackles hoisted the boats and
injured men together. Ahab refused the sling prepared for him.
With both hands upon the side ladder, and one sailor
supporting his shattered artificial limb, he ascended, pausing at each
ratline while pain shook through him. Yet the visible weakness
of his body only made more fearful the unbroken command
in his eye. It was as though a temple, cracked
by earthquake, still contained its undiminished god.

Starbuck met him at the rail.

"Captain, go below. The carpenter must attend your leg; the
surgeon, your side. Let the whale go. Heaven itself hath
spoken in that broken boat."

"Heaven?" Ahab dragged himself aboard. "Then Heaven speaks cedar and
whale-bone, and must find a better tongue. Carpenter!"

The carpenter approached.

"Patch this splinter. Make me stand."

"The lower socket's stove, sir."

"Then bind it. Lash me to the mast if need
be. But make me upright."

Starbuck pointed toward the sea, where the whale's spout had
become a small white plume far ahead. "He outruns the
boats. The men cannot pull another mile."

"Then the ship shall pull for them. Set all sail!"

"Ahab—"

"Set all sail!"

His voice struck the deck like dropped iron. Men sprang
to braces. Topsails, topgallant-sails, royals, and stunsails opened one above
another until the Pequod carried a white mountain of canvas.
The wind freshened as if bribed by madness. The hull
surged forward.

The three boats, hoisted but not yet secured, swung at
the davits. Where Ahab's boat had hung there remained only
two severed tackle-ends and a few dripping splinters. The wreckage
astern dwindled: planks, an oar, a smashed line-tub, all nodding
together upon the waves like mourners too poor to follow
the funeral.

A young seaman gazed after them and made the sign
against evil.

Ahab saw.

"What's that hand doing?"

"Nothing, sir."

"Nothing hath no fingers. Speak!"

"The boat, sir—it broke clean in the whale's mouth. Some
would call it an omen."

"Would they?" Ahab turned so that all the deck might
hear him. The carpenter had strapped a temporary splint along
his ivory leg, and he stood leaning upon a lance.
"Then read it rightly. The boat was between Ahab and
Moby Dick; therefore the boat was broken. Put a world
between us, and the world shall split likewise. Omens? Cowards
make signboards of accidents, then fright themselves by reading their
own paint. That wreck points forward!"

He seized the gold doubloon still nailed to the mast—the
bright coin he had promised to the first man who
sighted the white whale. With the iron end of his
lance he pried at its rim until it fell into
his palm.

"Tashtego sighted him," said Starbuck quietly.

"Aye; and I scented him before sight was born this
morning. The gold returns to the hand whose purpose minted
it."

He held the coin aloft. Sunlight flashed from it.

"But hear me, men! This ounce was for the eye
that found him. When Moby Dick rolls dead—dead, d'ye hear?—then
ten times its weight shall be divided among the boats'
crews. Gold for your wives; gold for your rum; gold
to plate your coffins if ye spend it ill. Only
keep your hearts screwed to mine!"

A murmur passed among the sailors. Some shouted. Some looked
down. Queequeg smiled his dark, unreadable smile; Stubb slapped the
rail and called for a pipe; Flask calculated shares beneath
his breath. But Starbuck stood apart, his lips tightened by
a sorrow too severe for anger.

"Men," he said, "no gold can purchase back the breath
once beaten from you."

Ahab wheeled upon him.

"Breath? The whale breathes! Shall he monopolize the air? Look
there—he spends it in silver every minute, while ye hoard
your one poor puff like misers. Oh, Starbuck, thou hast
a soul built like a safe harbor: good anchorage, sound
bottom, church bells inland. But I was launched where no
coast stands. Speak not to me of shelter."

"I speak of duty."

"And I of destiny."

"Destiny may be but sin wearing a crown."

"Then let kings judge it!"

All that day the chase continued. Sometimes Moby Dick disappeared
for nearly an hour, and the Pequod sailed through vacancy,
guided only by Ahab's instinct and the reports from aloft.
Then, leagues ahead, the spout would rise again, calm and
perpendicular, and every sail trembled with renewed pursuit. The whale
maintained his lead without apparent effort. He neither fled in
panic nor turned to fight, but traveled with the mighty
mildness of repose in swiftness, making the ocean his moving
throne.

Toward afternoon the sea became wondrously soft. Long, low undulations
passed beneath the ship, their surfaces blue-green and lustrous as
folded silk from an eastern bazaar. Flying fish broke out
ahead and skittered away like handfuls of silver coin. Clouds
gathered around the horizon in pale architectural masses—domes, stairs, ruined
pillars, and vast gates fit for the entrance of judgment.

Through this painted quiet moved whale and ship: the one
a white mystery, the other a dark insistence.

From time to time Ahab raised his quadrant, not because
any instrument could measure the distance between his soul and
its object, but because even madness loves tools. He questioned
the mastheads, the helmsman, the wind. He watched the dog-vane
and the compass. Every practical act was performed with exactitude;
yet beneath each ran the same consuming current. His monomania
did not make him careless. Rather, it enslaved all caution,
seamanship, number, muscle, and skill, compelling sober arts to serve
an unsober end.

Late in the day Moby Dick's wake grew clearer. The
Pequod gained.

"Another knot!" cried Ahab.

"We'll strain the foremast, sir," said Starbuck.

"Let it strain. Wood hath no widow."

The yards bent; the shrouds hummed. Water rushed white along
the lee rail. The whale's spout now rose larger, reddened
by the lowering sun. For several minutes it seemed they
might approach within boat-range before nightfall.

But Ahab looked west. The sun had descended into a
bank of purple vapor, and already the east put on
its blue-black hood.

"Take in the royals," he said suddenly. "Haul down the
stunsails."

The crew hesitated, surprised by this moderation.

"D'ye hear? Reduce sail!"

Starbuck approached him. "You will not overtake him?"

"Not in darkness. He hath eyes where mine are blind.
Run upon him tonight, and he may turn under our
keel before a boat is cleared. No. Let him think
himself unwatched. At dawn we strike."

Sail was shortened. The Pequod's furious rush subsided to a
long, controlled stride. Ahead, the white whale continued toward the
darkening rim, his diminishing spout touched for an instant with
fire from the sun, like smoke rising from a sacrifice.

Night assembled over the waters.

Ahab ordered the course held by the last bearing of
the whale. Extra lookouts went aloft with night-glasses, though glasses
are feeble lanterns where darkness has no wall. The men
were sent by watches to food and rest. Wet clothes
steamed near the galley. Bruised arms stiffened; palms split by
oars were dressed with grease and cloth. The carpenter worked
beneath a swinging lamp upon the captain's damaged leg, fitting
iron bands to the cracked ivory.

Yet Ahab would not go below.

They made him a low support beside the mizzenmast, where
he could lean without seeming seated. There he remained, wrapped
in a dark coat, one hand on the rail, his
face turned toward the invisible wake. The doubloon lay in
his pocket; at times his fingers closed around it, and
the buried gold seemed to give off a faint imperial
heat.

Near midnight Starbuck came to him.

"The men sleep, such as can," he said. "You should
do likewise."

"Sleep is for those whose tomorrows differ from their yesterdays."

"Your body is sorely hurt."

"My body is a rented inn. Let the walls crack;
the guest departs when his business ends."

"And if the business be damnation?"

Ahab smiled—not merrily, nor wholly bitterly, but with a strange
exhausted grandeur.

"Then damnation keeps an appointment."

Starbuck's eyes filled with the darkness of unspoken prayer. He
turned away.

The ship moved on under reduced canvas. The waves, soft-toed
now, came alongside and laid black palms against the hull.
Above, the constellations resumed their cold procession. Astern, the sea
closed over every sign of the broken boat; ahead, somewhere
beyond mortal vision, Moby Dick traveled beneath the same stars,
bearing in his flesh old irons, scars, legends, terrors, and
the new mark of splintered cedar.

So passed the night: the whale unseen, the ship pursuing,
and Ahab wakeful between them.

At intervals he leaned forward, fancying he smelled again that
musky breath across the miles. Perhaps he did. Perhaps the
whole round ocean had become to him one enormous whale-scented
chamber, and every wind merely the respiration of his foe.
Who may draw the boundary where sharpened sense ends and
madness begins? The compass itself is mad for north, the
tide for the moon, flame for the upper air. Call
these obedient; call Ahab lost. Yet all are drawn.

Shortly before dawn, the east showed a thin death-glimmer.

Ahab straightened. Pain crossed his face and vanished into purpose.

"Call all hands," he said.

The bell struck. Sleepers rose. Feet gathered on the deck.
Men climbed again toward the mastheads while the first pale
light unfolded over the sea.

Ahab laid both hands upon the rail and stared into
the awakening distance.

"Now," he whispered, whether to crew, whale, Fate, or morning
none could tell. Then louder, with his voice restored to
its full iron:

"Stand by! At sunrise—boats!"
