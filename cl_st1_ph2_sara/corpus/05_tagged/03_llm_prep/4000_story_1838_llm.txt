## THE ORCHID AND THE ALIBI
### A Farce in One Act

**PERSONS**

**ASTON TYRRELL**, a novelist of cultivated reputation.
**SIR JASPER**, his intelligence, polished by use and blunted by
amusement.
**CAIN**, his appetite, without education but not without eloquence.
**ASTON'S DUMMY**, dressed faultlessly and capable of six opinions.

**TOPSY GARRICK**, a young painter who also writes verses.
**BELLE**, her sensual curiosity, adventurous and smiling.
**HENRIKA**, her innocence, perpetually expecting thunder.
**TOPSY'S DUMMY**, charming, correct, and almost entirely hollow.

**THE FLOWERS**, who have never read Freud, but understand him.

---

### THE CONSERVATORY AT LADY MUMBLEDOWN'S WINTER BALL

Beyond the glass, December lies embalmed in moonlight. The park
is a black-and-white engraving: iron trees scratch a pewter sky;
snow upholsters the statues; a fountain, arrested in mid-compliment, holds
out crystal fingers from which nothing falls. Here and there
a carriage lamp creeps along the drive like a yellow
beetle condemned to punctuality.

Within the conservatory, however, Nature has been allowed champagne.

The atmosphere is green, gold, crimson, humid, palpitating, and faintly
indecent. Great leaves overlap like the ears of prehistoric elephants.
Orchids hang from branches in postures which would be prohibited
in sculpture. A serpentine creeper, purple as a bishop's private
intention, coils round a marble Diana and inserts a tendril
beneath her lifted arm. The ponds are lacquered black; lilies
float upon them with the complacency of ladies who know
that their shoulders are admirable. From the ballroom comes the
muffled convulsion of a waltz—violins sighing, cornets protesting, drums enforcing
morality.

Among the vegetation are several botanical celebrities. There is the
**Alocusia Cantatrix**, whose scarlet throat sings when stroked; the **Twangum
Tree**, covered with little bladder-like fruits which explode at compliments;
the **Kadapoo**, a pale, pendulous plant reputed to exhale chloroform
when emotionally disturbed; and the **Mendelia Duplex**, whose blossoms reproduce,
with scientific accuracy, the worst characteristics of both parent plants.

At the back, two doors lead to the ballroom. Beside
each stands a gilt chair.

Enter **ASTON**, carrying his **DUMMY** under one arm. The Dummy
resembles him as a successful portrait resembles its purchaser: the
brow is nobler, the waist smaller, the expression better connected.

Behind Aston come **SIR JASPER** and **CAIN**.

Sir Jasper is thin, silver-haired, elegant, and appears to have
been born leaning against a mantelpiece. Cain is broad, red-handed,
impatient, and dressed in a dinner coat that seems continually
on the point of splitting from honest disagreement with civilisation.

ASTON places his Dummy upright.

**ASTON'S DUMMY.** What a very good band.

**ASTON.** Not yet.

**DUMMY.** I beg your pardon.

**SIR JASPER.** It has been saying that all evening. At
dinner it praised the soup during the fish.

**CAIN.** Why bring it?

**ASTON.** Because society is alarmed by sincerity.

**CAIN.** Leave society. Find a woman.

**SIR JASPER.** He has found several. It is keeping them
which has proved literary.

**ASTON.** I came here for air.

**SIR JASPER.** You came because Miss Garrick came.

**CAIN.** Same thing.

**ASTON.** Miss Garrick interests me professionally.

**SIR JASPER.** Naturally. She is twenty-two.

**CAIN.** Yellow hair.

**ASTON.** Her work—

**CAIN.** Yellow hair.

**ASTON.** —shows a certain freshness.

**SIR JASPER.** Yellow hair.

**ASTON.** You are both intolerable.

**SIR JASPER.** We are both you.

The right-hand door opens. Enter **TOPSY**, carrying her **DUMMY** by
the waist. Belle and Henrika follow. Topsy wears a white
gown with silver embroidery. Belle wears the same gown as
it imagines itself at midnight. Henrika wears it as it
was described to Topsy's aunt.

Topsy sets her Dummy upon the second chair.

**TOPSY'S DUMMY.** What a very good band.

**ASTON'S DUMMY.** And a perfect floor.

**TOPSY'S DUMMY.** Are you going to the Necropole on Thursday?

**ASTON'S DUMMY.** I believe so.

The Dummies smile. Their smiles remain after the occasion has
passed.

**TOPSY.** Oh! Mr Tyrrell.

**ASTON.** Miss Garrick.

**BELLE.** He followed us.

**HENRIKA.** Let us go back.

**TOPSY.** I did not know you had left the ballroom.

**ASTON.** I did not know it myself until the air
improved.

**SIR JASPER.** Opening number seven: atmospheric coincidence.

**CAIN.** Stand nearer.

Aston moves half a pace nearer.

**HENRIKA.** He moved!

**BELLE.** So did she.

Topsy has also moved, though in the opposite direction; owing
to the geometry of flirtation, this brings them closer.

**ASTON.** It is intolerably crowded.

**TOPSY.** Lady Mumbledown says she invited only intimate enemies.

**ASTON.** There must be eight hundred.

**TOPSY.** She is very affectionate.

They laugh.

The **Twangum Tree** inflates three blue fruits and pops them
softly.

**TOPSY.** What was that?

**ASTON.** Approval.

**SIR JASPER.** Do not be modest. It may have been
indigestion.

**TOPSY.** Your latest novel has made everyone terribly unhappy.

**ASTON.** Then it has fulfilled its social purpose.

**TOPSY.** I cried over the duchess.

**SIR JASPER.** He did not.

**ASTON.** I am touched.

**CAIN.** Touch her.

**ASTON.** The duchess was intended less as a woman than
as a symbol of—

**SIR JASPER.** A woman who did not answer his letters.

**TOPSY.** I thought she was Desire pursued by Responsibility.

**ASTON.** Exactly.

**SIR JASPER.** Aston was Responsibility. Desire escaped at Boulogne.

**BELLE.** Ask him about the poems.

**HENRIKA.** Don't! They are in the blue notebook.

**BELLE.** Which she brought.

**HENRIKA.** By accident.

**BELLE.** In her glove.

Topsy thrusts her left glove behind her.

**ASTON.** You write yourself, I believe?

**TOPSY.** Paint.

**BELLE.** And write.

**TOPSY.** Only paint.

**HENRIKA.** Tell the truth.

**TOPSY.** Sometimes I write things which are not exactly writing.

**ASTON.** That is how most books begin.

**TOPSY.** Little verses.

**ASTON.** Ah.

His "Ah" is deep, encouraging, experienced, and has been used
upon manuscripts, soufflés, and moral difficulties.

**SIR JASPER.** Opening number twelve: the great man stoops to
the budding talent.

**ASTON.** May I see them?

**TOPSY.** Certainly not.

**BELLE.** Yes.

**HENRIKA.** No!

**TOPSY.** They are absurd.

**ASTON.** Absurdity is merely truth in its evening clothes.

**SIR JASPER.** That sentence was rejected from his last novel
for excessive meaning.

**TOPSY.** They are very modern.

**ASTON.** Then they need not scan.

**TOPSY.** They do not.

**ASTON.** Or rhyme.

**TOPSY.** They cannot.

**ASTON.** Or communicate.

**TOPSY.** They refuse.

**ASTON.** My dear Miss Garrick, you are already celebrated.

Topsy laughs. Belle slips the blue notebook from the glove
and places it in Aston's hand.

**TOPSY.** Belle!

**ASTON.** Thank you.

**TOPSY.** I did not give it to you.

**ASTON.** Then I am doubly grateful.

**HENRIKA.** Take it back.

**BELLE.** Let him keep it overnight.

**HENRIKA.** Why overnight?

**SIR JASPER.** Because morning has such excellent legal advisers.

Aston opens the book.

**TOPSY.** Not here!

He shuts it.

**ASTON.** I shall read it reverently.

**CAIN.** In bed.

**ASTON.** With professional care.

**SIR JASPER.** In bed.

**ASTON.** And send you a frank opinion.

**SIR JASPER.** He will say: "There is something here." There
is always something there. Generally a meeting.

**TOPSY.** I do want honesty.

**ASTON.** Honesty is the one luxury an established artist can
afford.

**SIR JASPER.** Since he no longer practises it.

The ballroom music grows louder, then dissolves into a languorous
measure. The conservatory lights turn amber. Several flowers unclose moistly.

**BELLE.** How warm it is.

**HENRIKA.** Too warm.

**CAIN.** Good.

**TOPSY.** What extraordinary plants.

**ASTON.** Lady Mumbledown's late husband collected them.

**SIR JASPER.** He died of botany in Sumatra.

**ASTON.** This, for example, is the Alocusia Cantatrix.

He indicates a fleshy flower, vermilion at the lip and
gold within. Its stamens tremble like the eyelashes of a
contralto awaiting applause.

**TOPSY.** Does it really sing?

**ASTON.** If sympathetically approached.

**TOPSY.** How does one approach a flower sympathetically?

**CAIN.** Show her.

**ASTON.** One touches the lower petal.

He strokes it with one finger. The flower emits a
low, voluptuous note: "O-o-oh."

Topsy starts. Belle claps. Henrika covers her ears.

**TOPSY.** How dreadful!

**BELLE.** Again.

**ASTON.** Humboldt considered its song a form of self-defence.

**SIR JASPER.** Humboldt was frequently abroad.

Topsy extends her hand, hesitates, and touches the petal. The
flower ascends an octave and sings a phrase of the
waltz.

**TOPSY.** It knows the tune!

**ASTON.** Plants have no taste, but excellent memories.

Nearby, a spotted blossom opens an eye.

**TOPSY.** That one is looking at us.

**ASTON.** The Oculata Moralis. It watches everything and understands nothing.
Botanists believe it related to the chaperon.

The blossom blinks severely.

**HENRIKA.** I dislike it.

**BELLE.** It dislikes us. That is much more stimulating.

They proceed down a path between enormous leaves. The companions
follow: Belle beside Topsy, Cain beside Aston, Henrika and Sir
Jasper bringing up the rear like Conscience and the Review
Press.

**TOPSY.** Did you always mean to write novels?

**ASTON.** No. At twenty I intended to reform humanity.

**TOPSY.** What happened?

**ASTON.** I met it.

**SIR JASPER.** At twenty-five he intended to understand women.

**ASTON.** Jasper—

**SIR JASPER.** At thirty he reduced his ambitions and described
them.

**TOPSY.** It must be wonderful to understand people.

**ASTON.** One need only notice what they conceal.

**TOPSY.** And what do I conceal?

**HENRIKA.** Nothing.

**BELLE.** Everything.

**CAIN.** Ask closer.

Aston looks at Topsy. She meets his gaze, then looks
toward the frozen garden.

**ASTON.** A good deal of courage.

**TOPSY.** Conceal courage?

**ASTON.** The world forgives a pretty woman almost anything except
fearlessness. So she learns to blush while she conquers it.

Belle smiles triumphantly. Henrika blushes for Topsy.

**SIR JASPER.** Very neat. He once used it on a
suffragist in a gondola.

**TOPSY.** You say such finished things.

**ASTON.** They are unfinished until believed.

**TOPSY.** I am not sure I believe them.

**ASTON.** Then I shall have to improve them.

The **Twangum Tree** bursts six fruits in a row: pop,
pop, pop, pop, pop, pop.

**SIR JASPER.** The house applauds.

**TOPSY.** And what do you conceal?

**ASTON.** My age.

**TOPSY.** Besides that.

**ASTON.** My opinions.

**TOPSY.** Besides those.

**CAIN.** Me.

Aston glances toward Cain.

**ASTON.** An hereditary barbarian.

**TOPSY.** How interesting.

**SIR JASPER.** She thinks he means an ancestor.

**CAIN.** Tell her I am alive.

**ASTON.** We all possess a certain primitive remnant. Darwin gives
him a tail; Freud gives him a sofa; society gives
him opera gloves and hopes for the best.

**TOPSY.** And does the best happen?

**ASTON.** Almost never. That is why there are novels.

They reach the **Mendelia Duplex**. Its paired blossoms are grotesquely
dissimilar: one white, smooth, and virginal; the other striped, hairy,
and equipped with minute teeth. As they watch, the white
blossom bites the striped one.

**TOPSY.** Oh!

**ASTON.** Mendel would have been delighted.

**SIR JASPER.** Mendel was a monk.

**BELLE.** The white one did it.

**HENRIKA.** Perhaps it was frightened.

**CAIN.** Teeth are teeth.

A gust of cold air enters through an imperfect pane.
A row of orchids shrieks in tiny soprano voices and
folds itself up. Aston removes his coat and places it
over Topsy's shoulders.

**TOPSY.** I am not cold.

**ASTON.** The orchids are.

**TOPSY.** Then give it to them.

**ASTON.** They have compromised themselves already.

She keeps the coat.

**BELLE.** It smells of him.

**HENRIKA.** It smells of tobacco.

**BELLE.** Precisely.

The music becomes softer. The leaves move though there is
no wind. A moon-coloured flower droops above Topsy and releases
a thread of perfume.

**TOPSY.** What is that scent?

**ASTON.** The Kadapoo.

**SIR JASPER.** Stand back.

**ASTON.** A harmless narcotic.

**SIR JASPER.** So is flattery, in professional hands.

**TOPSY.** Narcotic?

**ASTON.** Only when the plant is emotionally disturbed.

**TOPSY.** How does one disturb a plant emotionally?

At once Cain pinches one of its stems. The Kadapoo
quivers. A pearl of clear liquid swells at its throat
and bursts into vapour.

**CAIN.** Like that.

**ASTON.** Cain!

**TOPSY.** What did you say?

**ASTON.** "Careful."

**TOPSY.** I feel rather—

She sways.

Henrika screams. Belle catches Topsy's train but not Topsy. Aston
supports her at the waist.

**ASTON.** You are faint.

**TOPSY.** No. Only the room has become unreliable.

**SIR JASPER.** It has taken lessons from the company.

**HENRIKA.** Take her back!

**BELLE.** Not yet.

**ASTON.** Breathe slowly.

**TOPSY.** I am breathing slowly.

**CAIN.** Slower.

**ASTON.** Look at me.

She does. The waltz enters through the glass in broken
pulses. The Alocusia hums accompaniment. A great crimson blossom expands
behind them like an anatomical diagram of a heart.

**TOPSY.** Your face is moving.

**ASTON.** I regret to say it has done so for
years.

**TOPSY.** You have kind eyes.

Sir Jasper looks appalled.

**SIR JASPER.** Intoxication is no excuse for inaccuracy.

**ASTON.** Sit here.

He guides her to a marble bench. Belle arranges the
coat round her shoulders and draws one shining lock of
hair forward. Henrika attempts to push it back. The lock
travels between them like a disputed frontier.

**TOPSY.** I must look idiotic.

**ASTON.** You look—

**SIR JASPER.** Take care.

**CAIN.** Say beautiful.

**ASTON.** —very young.

**TOPSY.** That is worse.

**ASTON.** Very beautiful.

**SIR JASPER.** There. The familiar catastrophe.

**TOPSY.** That is better.

She closes her eyes.

**HENRIKA.** Topsy, open them.

**BELLE.** Keep them closed.

**TOPSY.** I can hear everybody thinking.

**ASTON.** What do they say?

**TOPSY.** The flowers say we are absurd.

A circle of orchids inclines toward them.

**ASTON.** Flowers are notorious realists.

**TOPSY.** Belle says—

She stops.

**ASTON.** Who is Belle?

**TOPSY.** Nobody.

Belle kisses Topsy's ear.

**TOPSY.** She says you will kiss me.

A pause. Even the ballroom band appears to lean upon
the dominant seventh and listen.

**HENRIKA.** No!

**CAIN.** Yes.

**SIR JASPER.** We have now reached the portion omitted from
the memoirs.

**ASTON.** And what do you say?

**TOPSY.** I say Belle is vulgar.

**BELLE.** Coward.

**ASTON.** Then Belle is mistaken.

He bends and kisses Topsy's forehead with grave tenderness. It
is a beautiful action, admirably lit, and would have satisfied
any audience not paying attention.

Topsy opens her eyes.

**TOPSY.** Oh.

**BELLE.** That was not what I meant.

**CAIN.** Nor me.

**ASTON.** Forgive me.

**TOPSY.** For that?

**ASTON.** For everything it may have meant.

**SIR JASPER.** An ingenious insurance policy.

Topsy rises, still unsteady.

**TOPSY.** You make even apologies sound like prefaces.

**ASTON.** It is a professional disease.

**TOPSY.** And is tenderness another?

**ASTON.** No.

For one instant Aston is quiet, unguarded, and almost a
human being. Sir Jasper turns away with distaste. Henrika watches
hopefully. Belle becomes still.

Cain does not.

He steps behind Aston, thrusting close as a shadow at
sunset. His hands close over Aston's arms; his face appears
beside Aston's face, coarser, hungrier, stripped of syntax.

**CAIN.** Enough words.

**ASTON.** Go away.

**CAIN.** She stayed.

**ASTON.** She is faint.

**CAIN.** She spoke of kissing.

**ASTON.** She trusted me.

**CAIN.** Same door. Different label.

**SIR JASPER.** Aston, this scene is deteriorating.

**CAIN.** Nyum, nyum. Perfume. Hair. Mouth.

He drives Aston forward.

Aston catches Topsy by the shoulders and kisses her mouth
violently.

For a second Belle flares scarlet with startled triumph—then recoils.
Henrika gives a thin, tearing cry. Topsy struggles. The Oculata
Moralis opens all its eyes. The toothed Mendelia snaps. The
Alocusia emits a hideous high note. Outside, the black trees
remain perfectly decorous.

Topsy tears herself free. A bright line of blood marks
her lower lip.

Silence.

Cain licks his own lip and laughs.

**CAIN.** There.

Aston stares at Topsy, then at his hands, as if
both have been translated into a language he has always
pretended not to know.

**HENRIKA.** Don't touch us! Don't touch us!

She clings to Topsy. Belle stands behind them, pale and
sickened.

**TOPSY.** You hurt me.

**ASTON.** I—

**CAIN.** We.

**ASTON.** No.

He turns on Cain.

**ASTON.** Get out.

**CAIN.** You called.

**ASTON.** Get out!

**CAIN.** You fed me compliments, perfume, dark paths, fainting. Now
the plate comes, you blame the knife.

Aston strikes him. Cain staggers into the Twangum Tree. Its
fruits explode like pistol shots. Cain laughs once more, but
less confidently.

**ASTON.** Out!

Cain retreats among the leaves. As he goes, the plants
bend toward him, recognising a relative. At last he disappears
behind the Kadapoo, though his breathing remains audible.

Sir Jasper examines his fingernails.

**SIR JASPER.** Violence against one's instincts is generally postponed until
after their success.

**ASTON.** Be silent.

**SIR JASPER.** Certainly. Intelligence is always dismissed when testimony becomes
inconvenient.

He withdraws a few paces.

Aston approaches Topsy, but Henrika thrusts out both arms.

**HENRIKA.** No nearer.

Aston stops.

**ASTON.** Miss Garrick—

**TOPSY.** Do not.

She wipes the blood from her lip with Aston's white
handkerchief. The small stain spreads into a rose, vulgar and
undeniable.

**ASTON.** There is no excuse.

**TOPSY.** No.

**ASTON.** I thought—

**SIR JASPER.** You did not.

**ASTON.** I allowed—

**TOPSY.** Do not make grammar of it.

He bows his head.

**ASTON.** Forgive me.

**TOPSY.** You said that before.

**ASTON.** Before, it was vanity. Now—

**TOPSY.** Now it is discomfort.

**ASTON.** Shame.

**TOPSY.** That is discomfort with a mirror.

Belle looks at Topsy with admiration. Henrika continues trembling.

**ASTON.** I would give anything—

**TOPSY.** You have given me a criticism already.

She holds out her hand.

**TOPSY.** My notebook.

Aston produces it. For a moment he clutches it, not
from desire to keep the poems but from the human
tendency to retain some object when one has forfeited the
person. Then he returns it.

**ASTON.** I shall never forgive myself.

**TOPSY.** That sounds exhausting. You will manage by breakfast.

**ASTON.** You are cruel.

**TOPSY.** I am being concise.

The ballroom strikes up a polka of invincible stupidity. Through
the doors comes laughter, bright and mechanical.

**TOPSY.** This did not happen.

**ASTON.** But—

**TOPSY.** It did not happen. You followed me here. We
discussed flowers. I felt faint. You fetched me water.

**ASTON.** I did not.

**TOPSY.** Then you may begin by fetching it now.

Aston pours water from a silver jug beside the pond.
His hand shakes. He offers the glass without approaching. Belle
takes it and gives it to Topsy.

**TOPSY.** Thank you.

**ASTON.** Must it be forgotten?

**TOPSY.** It will not be forgotten. It will be denied.
That is entirely different.

**SIR JASPER.** Admirable. She has learnt society in one lesson.

**HENRIKA.** I want to go home.

**BELLE.** We must go back first.

**HENRIKA.** Back there?

**BELLE.** Especially there.

Topsy removes Aston's coat and lays it on the bench.
Belle repairs her hair. Henrika wipes her lip. The stain
remains faintly visible.

**TOPSY.** Is it noticeable?

**BELLE.** Only if anyone looks.

**SIR JASPER.** Nobody at a ball looks. They merely observe.

Topsy crosses to her Dummy. Aston crosses to his. They
stand behind the puppets and insert their hands beneath the
satin backs. The Dummies wake at once, smiling with the
horrible freshness of things without memory.

Cain crawls from behind the Kadapoo and takes his place
at Aston's shoulder. A red fruit is stuck in his
hair. Aston does not look at him.

Sir Jasper resumes his place. Belle stands behind Topsy, suddenly
older. Henrika follows with both hands over her heart.

**TOPSY'S DUMMY.** What a very good band.

**ASTON'S DUMMY.** And a perfect floor.

**TOPSY'S DUMMY.** Are you going to the Necropole on Thursday?

**ASTON'S DUMMY.** I believe so.

**TOPSY'S DUMMY.** Lady Mumbledown's conservatory is quite delightful.

**ASTON'S DUMMY.** Quite.

**TOPSY'S DUMMY.** Such curious flowers.

**ASTON'S DUMMY.** Purely æsthetic.

**SIR JASPER.** Naturally.

**CAIN.** Nyum.

Aston jerks his Dummy's head toward Cain.

**ASTON'S DUMMY.** I beg your pardon?

**TOPSY'S DUMMY.** I said it is very warm.

**ASTON'S DUMMY.** Oppressively.

They move toward the ballroom doors, the Dummies first, their
owners half-hidden behind them. From the ballroom a dozen white-gloved
hands beckon. The frozen park gleams through the glass like
a clean conscience rented for the evening.

At the threshold, Topsy pauses. For a moment her real
face appears beside the Dummy's painted one. Aston sees it.
He takes one involuntary step toward her.

Henrika flinches.

Aston stops.

Topsy disappears behind her Dummy.

**TOPSY'S DUMMY.** A perfect floor.

**ASTON'S DUMMY.** A very good band.

They pass into the ballroom. Belle and Henrika follow. Sir
Jasper goes next, pausing to bow to the plants.

Cain lingers. He plucks a white flower, sniffs it, finds
its scent insufficiently candid, and eats it. Then he follows
Aston.

The doors close.

For several seconds the conservatory remains motionless. Then the Oculata
Moralis blinks. The Mendelia's white blossom bites its striped companion
again. The Alocusia Cantatrix clears its throat and imitates Topsy's
little cry with botanical precision.

One orchid giggles.

Another answers.

Soon the whole conservatory is chuckling: leaves shaking, pods rattling,
throats gurgling, petals opening and shutting like painted mouths. The
Twangum Tree swells with laughter until its fruits burst in
a vulgar fusillade. The Kadapoo exhales a final narcotic sigh.

From behind the marble Diana comes a prolonged, succulent hiccough.

The ballroom polka continues magnificently.
