A hungry man will listen to reason until dinner is
mentioned.

The last crates came up the gangplank at noon.

Peterson stood beside the cargo hatch, checking the marks as
the Martian porters hauled them in. There were tins of
water concentrate, oxygen cartridges, dried kelp, flour bricks, coffee, machine
grease, and two cases of frozen meat. The meat cost
more than the rest together.

One porter appeared at the foot of the ramp holding
a rope. Behind him came something large and gray.

"What's that?" Peterson said.

The porter tugged the rope. The creature took three slow
steps. Its belly swung almost to the red dust. It
had short legs, loose folds of flesh, and small ears
pressed against a piglike head. Its eyes were black and
moist.

The porter shouted at it. The creature climbed the ramp,
panting.

Captain Franco came out of the hatch.

"What did you buy, Peterson?"

"Not mine, Captain."

A young crewman named Dale hurried up the ramp behind
the creature. His face was red with excitement.

"I got it from some settlers," he said. "They called
it a wub."

Franco stared at the animal. "What's it for?"

"Food, I suppose. They said it grows fast."

"How much?"

"Fifty credits."

Franco stepped closer. The wub stopped. Its sides rose and
fell. A line of saliva hung from one corner of
its mouth.

"Fifty," Franco said. "For that?"

"It must weigh six hundred kilos."

"It must eat six hundred kilos."

The Martian porter handed Peterson the rope and went quickly
down the ramp. The other porters had already left. They
crossed the landing field without looking back.

Franco watched them go.

"What did they feed it?"

Dale shrugged. "Anything."

"That means our provisions." Franco touched the wub's flank. The
gray flesh quivered under his hand. "Put it in the
lower hold."

"The passage is narrow," Peterson said.

"Then grease it."

The wub looked at Peterson.

He felt a pressure behind his forehead, gentle and cool.

Please do not pull the rope, a voice said.

Peterson dropped it.

The rope struck the deck.

"What's wrong with you?" Franco said.

Peterson looked at the wub. Its mouth had not moved.

"Nothing."

Dale picked up the rope. "Come on."

The wub lowered its head and followed him into the
ship.

*

They lifted from Mars two hours later.

The wub had to remain in the cargo bay because
it could not fit through the inner corridor. It lay
between sacks of mineral ballast, its jowls spread on the
metal floor. Each time the ship changed thrust, the animal
slid several centimeters and grunted.

Peterson brought it a bucket of water.

Thank you, the voice said.

He stood with the bucket in his hands.

"Can you talk?"

Not in the fashion you mean. The arrangement of my
throat is poor. We were not made for vocal elegance.

Peterson looked toward the hatch. "Who taught you our language?"

No one. Language is only the outer clothing of thought.
It is sometimes rather badly tailored.

The wub drank. The water vanished in heavy gulps. When
the bucket was empty, it sighed and rested its chin
on the floor.

"You're telepathic."

If that word is convenient.

"Why didn't the settlers tell us?"

Perhaps they did not know. Perhaps they did not ask.
People rarely ask an animal questions when they have already
decided what an animal is.

Peterson sat on a crate.

"What are you?"

The wub considered this. Its little ears stirred.

A citizen, in the broad sense. A traveler by accident.
A philosopher when digestion permits.

"You're from Mars?"

My body is adapted to Mars. That is not quite
the same thing.

The pressure in Peterson's head shifted. It carried amusement, though
the wub's wet eyes remained solemn.

"You know we bought you."

I know that money changed hands.

"They said you were livestock."

Many political arrangements depend upon choosing the correct noun.

Peterson rubbed his forehead. "Captain Franco thinks we should eat
you."

Yes. He radiates the intention with admirable clarity.

"And you're not afraid?"

The wub's stomach gave a deep sound.

Fear is useful at the proper hour. At present, I
am more tired than afraid. Your gangplank was severe.

Peterson stood.

"I'll speak to him."

That is generous. But generosity without power is often a
form of ceremony.

"I can still try."

Certainly. Civilization is largely a collection of attempts that failed
less completely than expected.

The wub closed its eyes.

Peterson left the cargo bay.

*

Franco was in the galley cutting open a package of
coffee. He shook the brown powder into the dispenser and
scowled at the level.

"It's short," he said. "They cheated us again."

"Captain, the wub is intelligent."

Franco fitted the lid onto the dispenser. "It walks, doesn't
it?"

"It spoke to me."

Franco turned.

"Spoke."

"Telepathically."

Dale and two other crewmen looked up from the table.

"What did it say?" Dale asked.

"That it's a citizen."

Franco laughed once. "Of where?"

"I don't know."

"Then it's not much of a citizen."

"It understands us. It knows what you intend to do."

"That ought to save time."

Peterson moved between Franco and the door. "You can't slaughter
an intelligent being."

Franco poured coffee into a cup. "We have meat for
eight days. The trip is sixteen. That thing is meat
for the other eight."

"We have flour and kelp."

"We have a crew. Crews need meat."

"Not that badly."

Franco drank. "You paid fifty credits for it?"

"Dale did."

"Then it belongs to the ship."

"It belongs to itself."

Franco's eyes narrowed. "You've been talking to it for ten
minutes and now you're its lawyer."

"It can reason."

"So can a calculator."

"It knows philosophy."

"Can it repair a drive jet?"

"No."

"Can it navigate?"

"I didn't ask."

"Can it pay for the food it eats?"

Peterson said nothing.

Franco smiled. "There's your philosophy."

He opened a locker and took out a long knife.

The crewmen at the table shifted.

"You're doing it now?" Dale said.

"No reason to carry six hundred kilos of live appetite.
The frozen-food locker has room."

Peterson looked at the knife.

"Captain, at least speak to it."

"I don't hold conferences with dinner."

"Maybe you should."

Franco put the knife against his belt. "All right. Bring
your professor up."

"It can't fit through the corridor."

Franco picked up his cup. "Then we'll visit the lecture
hall."

*

The crew gathered in the cargo bay.

The wub had moved near the warm casing of the
auxiliary generator. It lay on its side, breathing with slow
difficulty. One of its legs twitched as they entered.

Franco stood over it.

"So," he said. "You talk."

Good afternoon, Captain.

Franco's face changed. His hand tightened around the cup.

"You hear it?" Peterson asked.

"I hear something."

I regret the intrusion. Direct mental exchange can feel intimate
at first.

"Stay out of my mind."

I am addressing only the surface. Your deeper regions are
quite secure.

Dale coughed. Someone laughed, then stopped.

Franco set down the cup.

"You understand why you're here?"

To complete your provisions, according to your present account.

"That's right."

The wub lifted its head. Loose skin sagged from its
neck.

May I offer a different account?

"You may offer anything. It won't change the food supply."

The wub breathed for a while before answering.

There are several grounds on which a being may ask
not to be killed. The simplest is pain. I possess
nerves and prefer that they not be violently disturbed. A
second is continuity. I have memories, affections, projects, and certain
unresolved questions. A third is social recognition. I speak to
you and understand your speech. I therefore enter, however inconveniently,
the moral community you claim for yourselves.

Franco leaned against a crate.

"What projects?"

At present I am reconsidering Odysseus.

"Who?"

"The old Greek traveler," Peterson said.

"I know who Odysseus was."

The wub's nostrils widened.

His return is usually treated as the triumph of identity.
After war, shipwreck, enchantment, and loss, the man comes home
and is known. Yet one may ask what exactly returned.
The same flesh? Certainly not. The same convictions? Doubtful. The
same relation to violence? That is the difficult point.

Franco looked at Peterson. "This is what you wanted me
to hear?"

"Listen."

I do not suggest that Odysseus was simple, the wub
continued. No living mind is simple. He was tolerant in
necessity, eclectic in method, catholic in appetite. But he wished,
finally, to resume the old shape of his life. The
wanderer desired to become the man who had left.

The wub paused. Its stomach rumbled.

Dale looked away.

Franco said, "Are you hungry?"

Constantly. It is one of the less dignified facts of
my biology.

"You eat other things."

Plants and mineral cultures. I do not object to eating
as a process. Life exchanges its material. The ethical question
concerns what is interrupted, and whether interruption is necessary.

"You're fat enough for six men."

My body stores energy with enthusiasm.

"Then you're useful."

Usefulness is not the same as consent.

Franco drew the knife.

The wub watched him.

Captain, you possess frozen meat.

"Eight days."

And non-meat provisions for much longer.

"The crew expects proper food."

The crewmen were silent.

Franco glanced at them. "Well?"

Dale rubbed his hands together. "I don't know, Captain."

"You bought it."

"I didn't know it talked."

"It still weighs the same."

One of the engineers, Walters, stepped back toward the hatch.
"I'm not hungry enough."

Franco's face reddened.

The wub shifted, trying to rise. Its short legs pushed
against the floor. For a moment its great body trembled
above the deck. Then it settled again with a wet
sigh.

Observe the comedy, Captain. I am physically unable to oppose
you. You have a blade, a weapon, companions, and the
authority of the ship. I have several hundred kilos of
poorly organized flesh and an interest in archaic narrative. The
contest is not balanced.

"Then stop arguing."

Argument is what the weak invented before the strong learned
courtesy.

Franco moved forward.

He stopped in the middle of a step.

The knife remained raised. His shoulders stiffened. His mouth opened,
but no sound came. A drop of coffee clung to
his lower lip.

Peterson looked at the wub.

Release him, he thought.

In a moment.

Franco's arm shook. The knife fell and struck the deck.

The wub lowered its head.

Franco staggered backward. Dale caught him.

"What happened?" Walters said.

"A temporary inhibition," the wub answered through them. No injury
was intended.

Franco gasped. He pushed Dale away.

"You got inside me."

Only enough to prevent an immediate act.

"You tried to take control."

I prevented you from using control.

Franco pulled his pistol.

Peterson stepped in front of him. "Captain."

"Move."

"It let you go."

"It attacked me."

"You had a knife."

"Move."

The wub's eyes met Peterson's.

Do not risk yourself. His fear requires a target.

Peterson remained where he was.

Franco struck him across the face with the pistol. Peterson
fell against a ballast sack.

The shot was very loud in the cargo bay.

The wub jerked. Its forelegs scraped the metal. Franco fired
again, then once more.

The gray body sagged.

For several seconds nobody moved.

A dark line spread beneath the wub's head. Its breath
came in small, bubbling pulls.

Peterson crawled toward it.

The pressure touched his mind once, faintly.

The traveler returns, it said.

Then there was nothing.

Franco put away the pistol.

"Get it into the locker," he said.

*

They used the cargo winch.

The carcass turned slowly above the deck while Walters and
Dale guided it through the widened service hatch. Its legs
swung. The moist eyes stayed half open.

Peterson did not help.

The cook worked for three hours. He cursed the thick
hide and asked twice whether anyone had experience butchering Martian
livestock. No one did. At last he divided the meat
into pale slabs and sealed most of them in the
frozen-food locker.

The smell reached the control room.

At dinner, the crew sat without speaking.

Franco came in late. A fresh bandage covered his left
hand where the knife had grazed him when it fell.
He took the chair at the head of the table.

The cook set down a platter.

The meat had been roasted with onions and synthetic pepper.
Fat shone at the edges. Steam rose from it.

Franco cut himself a large piece.

"Well?" he said. "Eat."

Dale took a slice. Walters took a smaller one. Peterson
kept his plate empty.

Franco chewed.

His expression softened.

"Excellent," he said. "Better than pork."

No one answered.

The cook sat down. "It smelled good."

"It is good."

Franco took another piece. Juice ran onto his plate.

"You see?" he said to Peterson. "All that talking didn't
change what it was."

Peterson looked at the platter.

"What was it?"

"Organic matter."

"So are you."

Franco laughed. "There's always one philosopher after a killing."

Dale chewed slowly. "I keep hearing its voice."

"You'll stop."

"It asked politely."

"Everything asks, one way or another. The drive asks for
fuel. The body asks for food. Space asks for air.
You answer what matters."

Walters pushed back his plate.

Franco pointed his fork at him. "Don't waste it."

Walters pulled the plate back.

They ate.

Peterson watched their jaws move. The ship vibrated around them.
Beyond the hull, the dark was empty and practical. The
navigation motors made small corrections toward Earth.

Franco finished first. He wiped the grease from his mouth
and looked at the remaining meat.

"Cook another roast tomorrow."

The cook nodded.

"Use less pepper. It hides the flavor."

Peterson stood.

"Sit down," Franco said.

"I'm not hungry."

"You will be."

"Not for that."

Franco studied him. His face seemed loose from the meal.
His eyelids drooped. He breathed deeply and put both hands
on the table.

Then he belched.

"Excuse me," he said.

The words were mild. Almost formal.

Peterson stopped.

Franco glanced around the table as if surprised to find
the crew watching him.

"A heavy body produces heavy consequences," he said. "That is
one of the less dignified facts of biology."

Dale's fork slipped from his fingers.

Franco looked down at his plate.

"What did you say?" Peterson asked.

"Nothing important."

"You said—"

"I know what I said."

His voice had changed. The harshness was still there, but
something moved beneath it, patient and amused.

Franco selected a small strip of meat. Instead of eating
it, he examined the fibers.

"An interesting process," he said.

Walters stood. His chair scraped backward.

Franco raised his eyes.

"There is no cause for alarm."

The crew remained still.

Franco smiled. It was not his usual smile.

"Captain?" Peterson said.

"A name of function," Franco replied. "Useful aboard a ship.
Less useful in metaphysics."

The cook made a sound in his throat.

Franco leaned back. The chair creaked under him, though he
had not gained any weight. He folded his hands across
his stomach.

"I believe our conversation was interrupted," he said.

Peterson gripped the edge of the table.

"What conversation?"

"The matter of the wanderer."

No one breathed.

"Odysseus," Franco said. "We had reached his return."

Dale moved toward the door.

Franco watched him kindly.

"You may go if you wish. Coercion is a poor
beginning for philosophy."

Dale did not go.

Peterson sat down again.

Franco's eyes were bright and moist.

"The customary error," he continued, "is to suppose that return
must be physical. We imagine a particular body crossing a
particular threshold. But perhaps the body is only the vessel
chosen by circumstance. Perhaps what returns is a pattern, an
appetite of the mind, a life essence seeking a suitable
house."

He glanced at the empty platter.

"Sometimes," he said, "the road home passes through unexpected country."

Peterson stared at him.

"Are you the wub?"

Franco considered the question with grave courtesy.

"I find the word imprecise. Identity after passage is never
simple. One becomes tolerant, eclectic, catholic. One retains what is
useful. One digests, and is digested."

He touched the bandage on his hand.

"There is also a residue of the captain. Strong habits.
An unfortunate temper. A remarkable fondness for roasted meat."

The cook backed away.

Franco smiled again.

"Please," he said. "There is no need to fear. I
have already eaten."
