# THE LAST RIDER

I have ridden since the first rose of fire unfolded
in the hollow of nothingness.

Before the mountains had lifted their granite foreheads from the
boiling seas; before the moon, pale widow of the heavens,
wandered among the unburied stars; before man had shaped from
bronze the image of his pride, or taught the trembling
reed to mourn—I was abroad. God breathed, and worlds awakened;
God commanded, and I followed. Creation went before me scattering
suns, creatures, forests, nations; I came behind, silent and insatiable,
extinguishing the stars, whitening the forests, emptying the cities, and
strewing eternity with bones.

Beneath me strides my horse, my only companion.

His hide is the pallor of ashes beneath snow; his
mane burns without warmth; his ribs are bars of iron
enclosing no heart. Flames breathe from his nostrils, yet the
dew of graves glitters forever upon his flanks. He hath
drunk from every river, and every river hath afterward run
red. He hath eaten kings from their golden shields and
infants from their mothers' breasts. The marrow of conquerors, the
tears of virgins, the flesh of prophets, slaves, poets, judges,
harlots, and saints—these have been his provender. Still is he
lean. Still do his brazen hoofs demand the road.

Onward, pale brother! Through the bridal chamber and the prison;
over the marble stair and the dung-hill; through temples loud
with thanksgiving and caverns wherein the hunted curse their gods.
Tread down the sceptre with the shepherd's crook, the laurel
with the weed, the book of wisdom with the fool's
painted toy. We have no choice. We obey.

I have loosed arrows into the breasts of armies till
their blood made mirrors for the sun. I have breathed
upon navies, and the sea hath swallowed their trumpets, their
banners, their oaths, and all the bronze thunder of their
guns. I have entered cities by the mouth of pestilence;
I have sat invisible at feasts and touched, one by
one, the laughing guests; I have passed through palaces while
music shook the porphyry floors, and at dawn the musicians
played to corpses.

Empires have risen beneath my gaze like foam upon an
ocean. I saw their towers climb, their laws harden, their
armies multiply; I saw emeralds blaze upon their altars and
diamonds tremble in the crowns of their masters. Then came
I. The towers cracked; the laws became forgotten scratches; the
armies sank into mud; the emeralds adorned the skulls of
thieves, and the diamonds rolled among the dust. Where are
they now—the cities that believed their walls eternal? Where are
the hands that carved, the lips that commanded, the eyes
before whose anger provinces shook? Nothing. A little powder blown
between two silences.

Yet I remain.

O immortality, thou crueler grave! Men kneel to heaven and
beg not to be mine; they clutch their wounds, their
lovers, their chains, and implore another hour of agony. Fools!
They know not what treasure I bring beneath my shroud.
Sleep, cool and shoreless; repose without dream; release from appetite,
memory, hope, remorse. I give to the beggar what the
emperor cannot purchase. I open one door, and pain forgets
the name of him who suffered.

But who shall open that door for me?

When, O God, shall the last heart cease beneath my
hand? When shall the final sun, exhausted by its own
splendour, fall black into the abyss? When shall oceans dry
in their basins, mountains crumble to dust, angels fold their
singed wings, and the great machinery of the constellations shudder,
halt, and break? Then, perhaps, my horse shall stumble. Then
his iron knees shall strike the cold foundation of the
void. Then shall I lie beside him, wrap the universe
about us like a gigantic winding-sheet, and sink—down, down—into beloved
nothingness.

How beautiful must oblivion be!

I have seen winter scatter great white tears upon the
tombs, and envied the dead beneath them. I have heard
storms rock ruined kingdoms with their enormous lullabies, and desired
to sleep among the stones. I have watched the drowned
descend through green darkness, their hair rising softly like weeds,
their faces losing fear; and I, who cast them there,
could not follow. The worm hath a feast and afterward
rest. The star burns and afterward is blind. Man sins,
suffers, loves, hates, and afterward is free. Death alone cannot
die.

Onward, then! Faster, my pale companion! There are worlds beyond
these worlds, and wombs still labouring beneath alien moons. There
are unborn tyrants dreaming in the blood of unborn mothers;
there are cities not yet founded that already belong to
ruin. We must reap them all. Thy hoofs shall thresh
the planets; my scythe shall harvest the ages. Not one
seed of life must remain hidden beneath the ashes—not one
warm atom, not one whisper, not one desire. All! All!
Until annihilation, merciful and absolute, descends upon executioner and victim
alike.

[An immeasurable plain. The ground is composed of broken crowns,
anchors, swords, tablets, toys, and human bones. Above it hang
clouds like vast winding-sheets. DEATH sits upon his pale horse.
A red fissure opens in the earth, and SATAN ascends
through smoke.]

SATAN: Thou complainest loudly for one so fortunate.

DEATH: Who speaks?

SATAN: He whom heaven abhors, hell obeys, and earth embraces
while denying his name.

DEATH: Satan.

SATAN: Death.

[They regard one another. Far off, a star falls soundlessly.]

DEATH: Hast thou come to mock my weariness?

SATAN: To envy it. Thou hast an end appointed, though
it be hidden behind the funeral of creation. When thy
scythe hath mown the last breathing field, thou too shalt
perish. But I—where shall I find conclusion? If this universe
dissolve, I must wander to another. If God create anew
beyond the ashes, there must I crawl, carrying my ancient
fire into the fresh-built morning.

DEATH: Thou givest evil willingly.

SATAN: And dost thou kill unwillingly?

DEATH: I obey necessity.

SATAN: As do I. Call it rebellion, temptation, chaos—names are
toys with which eternity consoles its slaves. I labour because
the impulse of my nature drives me. I enter the
child as envy; the youth as lust; the ruler as
ambition; the priest as pride. I whisper to the poor
man that bread is worth murder, and to the rich
man that gold is worth a thousand famished children. I
teach lovers suspicion, brothers hatred, patriots slaughter. With a breath
I turn a boundary-stone into an altar demanding millions of
hearts.

DEATH: And thou gloriest therein.

SATAN: I have gloried, as the tortured sing to drown
their chains. Dost thou think delight inhabits me? I have
watched ambition climb over the bodies of friends, reach the
throne, and discover terror seated there. I have poured wine
into desire until desire became madness, disgust, and thirst again.
I have placed beauty in one hand and jealousy in
the other; the hands met, and beauty was strangled. Age
after age, the same crimes wear new garments, the same
dagger receives a different jewel, the same lie is engraved
upon another flag.

DEATH: Yet men love thee.

SATAN: They love my gifts and curse the giver. In
heaven my name is thundered with contempt. In hell my
servants obey because they tremble. On earth men perform my
will and attribute it to virtue. Where, then, is my
kingdom? Where my companion? I stand beside every bed of
unlawful pleasure, every council of treachery, every scaffold, marketplace, battlefield,
and secret chamber; yet no creature beholds me and says,
"Thou art weary. Rest."

DEATH: Rest! Speak not that word to me.

SATAN: Why not? It is thy inheritance.

DEATH: An inheritance delayed beyond the endurance of stone. I
have slain generations innumerable, yet life foams up behind my
horse's hoofs. I empty a city; two cities rise. I
drown a navy; ten keels divide the waves. I take
the father, and in the same hour his son engenders
another victim. Creation mocks me with fertility.

SATAN: Nevertheless thou appro achest thy reward.

DEATH: As the traveller approaches a horizon.

SATAN: Better the horizon than the wheel. I move, yet
return forever. I inflame the conqueror; thou coolest him. I
corrupt the maiden; thou preservest her from further corruption. I
kindle war; thou stillest it beneath one impartial frost. All
passions I begin, and all passions thou endest.

DEATH: Wouldst thou exchange thy fire for my cold?

SATAN: Instantly!

DEATH: Thy multitude for my solitude?

SATAN: Gladly!

DEATH: Thy liberty to desire for my compulsion to destroy?

SATAN: There is no liberty in desire. It is a
chain that pretends to be wings.

[A noise arises beyond the plain: cymbals without music, wheels
without chariots, applause from fleshless hands.]

DEATH: What pageant comes?

SATAN: One of thy old harvests, dreaming itself alive.

[The tombs split. From them issue SKELETONS clothed in purple
rags, rusted armour, bridal veils, priests' vestments, and chains. Some
carry extinguished torches; others lift cups filled with dust. Skeleton
horses rear beneath shredded plumes. At the procession's head rides
NERO in a chariot plated with tarnished gold. Roses grow
from his empty eye-sockets and fall as ashes.]

NERO: Wider the gates! Let Rome behold her sun!

[The SKELETONS strike their bones together in applause.]

NERO: Hark! The seven hills thunder my name. Caesar! Caesar!
The marble itself cries Caesar! Throw perfumes beneath the wheels.
Let cinnamon burn in bronze lions; let fountains leap with
wine; let the streets be roofed with silk stolen from
the morning! Where are my singers? Where the Nubian dancers
whose limbs flow like black flame? Where the boys crowned
with violets, the women clothed in nets of pearls, the
dwarfs, the wrestlers, the tame leopards, the slaves bearing dishes
of peacocks' tongues?

SATAN: Here is appetite crowned.

DEATH: Here is dust speaking to dust.

NERO: Faster and faster! My horses must outrun sound. The
people wait, piled from pavement to roof, from roof to
temple, from temple to heaven. Their mouths are roses opening
for my glory. Caesar passes! Shower him with gold! Break
emeralds beneath his wheels! Let a thousand doves, dyed crimson,
eclipse the sun!

[The skeleton horses gallop, though the chariot remains before DEATH.]

NERO: To the palace! I am weary of triumph and
hunger for pleasure. Fill the baths with heated milk. Scatter
roses knee-deep through the banquet chamber. Bring me music so
sweet that the soul aches to escape the body and
kiss the strings. Tonight no lamp shall be of clay,
no cup of anything but crystal, no garment unembroidered with
stars.

SATAN: Dost thou remember nothing?

NERO: I remember all that pleased me. Memory was invented
for emperors; remorse for slaves.

SATAN: And the blood?

NERO: Blood is but another purple.

DEATH: And the cries?

NERO: Music badly governed.

DEATH: And the dead?

NERO: They should be grateful. I gave their small endings
magnitude.

NERO: [Rising in his chariot.] Bring in the Christians! Smear
them with pitch and set them among the gardens. Living
torches burn more beautifully than dead ones. Release the beasts!
I would see faith argue with lions. If their God
loves them, let Him descend. If He descends, I shall
appoint Him governor of some remote province.

SATAN: Thou wert an apt pupil.

NERO: Pupil? I surpassed thee. Thou temptest in darkness; I
sinned beneath ten thousand lamps. Thou whisperest; I proclaimed. Thou
gavest men dreams of cruelty; I built those dreams in
marble and made the world applaud.

SATAN: True—and therefore thou art mine.

DEATH: No. He is mine, as all are mine.

NERO: I belong to Rome!

[A lurid glow rises. Behind him appears a phantom city:
domes, tenements, temples, theatres, and palaces crowded beneath a red
night.]

NERO: Behold! How dull the roofs, how tedious the crooked
streets, how unworthy of me this ancient confusion. Rome must
be remade—not mended, but purified.

SATAN: By what baptism?

NERO: Fire.

[He raises a torch. The phantom city ignites.]

NERO: Ah! At last, a spectacle equal to my soul!
See the flames climb from window to window, licking the
beams, embracing the towers, crowning the temples! Hear tiles explode,
bronze melt, statues crack in the heat! The streets run
with citizens; from above they seem black ants fleeing a
golden flood. Higher, fire! Devour their cupboards, contracts, marriage beds,
household gods! Leave me a level field upon which to
write NERO in avenues of porphyry.

Faster and faster! Let the conflagration race the wind. Faster
and faster! Let night become noon, and noon blush before
it. Bring me my lyre. I shall sing the fall
of Troy while Rome furnishes the flames. What is history
but fuel for art? What is suffering but the drum
beneath a sovereign's song?

[A blood-covered MESSENGER crawls from among the SKELETONS.]

MESSENGER: Caesar—

NERO: Do not interrupt eternity.

MESSENGER: The legions revolt.

NERO: Then purchase them.

MESSENGER: They have named another emperor.

NERO: Then kill him.

MESSENGER: The Senate condemns thee.

NERO: The Senate? Those perfumed corpses whom I permitted to
breathe?

MESSENGER: Thy guards have fled. Thy servants plunder the palace.
The people tear down thy images. The roads are watched.
The gates are shut.

[The applause ceases. The phantom Rome goes dark. NERO's chariot
cracks, revealing worm-eaten wood beneath the gold.]

NERO: No. Call back the music.

MESSENGER: There is no music.

NERO: The women, then. Acte! Sporus! Phaon! Bring wine. Bring
roses. Bring anyone who hath kissed my hand.

MESSENGER: None comes.

NERO: I am Caesar.

DEATH: Thou wast.

NERO: [Seeing DEATH fully.] What colourless slave art thou?

DEATH: Thy last.

NERO: I did not summon thee.

DEATH: All summon me by being born.

NERO: Stand aside. There remain provinces I have not governed,
pleasures I have not tasted, cruelties I have only imagined.
I have designed a palace whose halls shall measure a
day's journey. I have composed half an ode. I have
not yet learned whether fear or love makes the sweeter
music in a woman's voice. Give me one year.

DEATH: No.

NERO: One month. Rome shall pay thee ten thousand lives
for mine.

DEATH: They are already mine.

NERO: A day! Until sunset! There must be law even
among monsters. I am young. My blood is warm.

DEATH: Thy blood was warm. Touch thy face.

[NERO raises a hand. His cheek falls inward. Roses pour
from his mouth, changing to worms upon the ground.]

NERO: Satan, help me! Was I not thy masterpiece?

SATAN: I can open doors into sin, not doors out
of death.

NERO: Then thou art a fraud, a beggar in a
crown of smoke! Give me back my feast, my city,
my beautiful crimes!

SATAN: Face at last the one power thou couldst neither
flatter nor burn.

NERO: Death, have pity.

DEATH: I have none.

NERO: Have mercy.

DEATH: I am mercy.

NERO: I fear the dark.

DEATH: It hath no eyes with which to behold thy
shame.

NERO: I fear silence.

DEATH: It will cure thee of applause.

NERO: I fear nothingness!

DEATH: And I desire it.

[DEATH dismounts. His height increases until his shroud mingles with
the clouds. NERO shrinks within his ruined chariot.]

NERO: Let me keep one memory—one kiss, one song, one
hour beneath the roses.

DEATH: Memory is a lamp, and thou goest where no
flame burns.

NERO: My name, then! Rome will speak it.

DEATH: For a while.

NERO: The world?

DEATH: For less than eternity.

NERO: Am I to lose all?

DEATH: All.

NERO: Instantly?

DEATH: Instantly.

[NERO attempts to flee. His purple robes fall empty; within
them crouches a small, trembling skeleton. DEATH spreads a vast
shroud.]

SATAN: Courage, Caesar. Thou didst demand that thousands die bravely
for thy amusement. Perform now for an audience of two.

NERO: O gods! O Rome! O vanished light—

[DEATH wraps him in the shroud. The skeleton procession collapses
in a single wave, becoming a field of bones. The
pale horse bends its knees. DEATH lifts the bundled emperor
and lays him across the saddle.]

DEATH: Another dream silenced; another splendour reduced to weight. How
light the tyrant is when emptied of command.

SATAN: And art thou nearer rest?

DEATH: By one.

SATAN: One among infinities.

DEATH: Yet one.

[DEATH mounts. The clouds divide, revealing innumerable worlds burning in
the abyss.]

SATAN: Whither now?

DEATH: Everywhere.

SATAN: And afterward?

DEATH: Everywhere again.

SATAN: Then we are brothers.

DEATH: No. Brothers may die together.

[The pale horse begins to move. Beneath each hoof, a
kingdom rises, flourishes, and falls. SATAN walks beside him for
a little while.]

SATAN: Shall I accompany thee?

DEATH: Until desire separates us.

SATAN: That will be soon.

DEATH: All separations are soon to those condemned to forever.

[They pass into the darkness. Behind them the red fissure
closes; before them stars tremble like candles beneath an approaching
breath.]

DEATH: Onward, pale brother. Kings await the shroud; lovers await
silence; unborn cities await their ruins. Onward through blood, foam,
roses, iron, and dust. Onward until the seas are empty
bowls and the heavens a cold tomb; until Satan hath
no heart to corrupt, and I no heart to still;
until God's last syllable fades from the abyss and immortality
itself lies slain.

Then—O then!—may thy hoof cease. May my hand release the
scythe. May we fall beside the extinguished stars, not conqueror
and steed, not terror and servant, but two weary shadows
forgiven by nothingness.

Onward.

Faster.

Faster.

To the end.
