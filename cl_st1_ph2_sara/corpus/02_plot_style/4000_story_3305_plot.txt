Lost on a stormy moor at midnight, highwayman Gabriel Foot seeks shelter in a large, isolated house whose front door has been left open. Inside, he avoids what appears to be a sleeping mastiff and encounters a composed clergyman who seems unsurprised by the intrusion. After learning that the clergyman recently dismissed his butler, Parkinson, Gabriel jokingly offers to abandon burglary and take the vacant post. Impressed by Gabriel’s ability to distinguish Marsala from Madeira even after several glasses of brandy, the man hires him and gives him a room for the night.

Unable to sleep, Gabriel hears someone quietly leave the house at dawn. Investigating, he discovers that the supposedly stuffed mastiff is real and has had its throat cut. He rushes upstairs and finds an elderly man murdered in bed—the true owner of the house. Gabriel realizes that his host was actually Parkinson, the former butler, who had killed his master, assumed his identity and appearance, and escaped after tricking Gabriel into taking his place as the new butler. Fearing he will be blamed for the murders, Gabriel flees across the moors.
