A beloved grandmother celebrates her seventy-third birthday after recovering from a serious illness. To avoid tiring her, her family spreads a series of affectionate surprises throughout the day. Her lively grandsons wake her in costume, friends and neighbors visit with gifts, and people she has helped repay her kindness with humble offerings.

That evening, the family leads her in procession to a decorated study, where the boys appear as elaborately costumed Indian chiefs beside a table of presents. Grandma receives practical, humorous, and sentimental gifts, each accompanied by a poem. Among them is a mousetrap presented in the name of the family’s cat, whose failed attempts at catching mice and determined return after being given away have become a family story. A lock of Grandma’s mother’s hair moves her especially deeply.

The boys then perform a comic war dance, mock battle, and hunting expedition. More guests arrive, the birthday cake is shared, and everyone drinks a toast to Grandma before dancing and singing around her chair. At day’s end, the family gathers around her bed for good-night kisses, and she declares it the happiest and brightest birthday of her life.
