Mrs. Bell tells her wealthy friend Mrs. Ellis that Anna May, a poor seamstress once employed by Ellis, is dying. Although Mrs. Ellis fears death and initially refuses to visit someone on her deathbed, Mrs. Bell persuades her to come.

At Anna’s modest home, they find the young woman physically weak but spiritually peaceful. Anna describes death not as a frightening end, but as an angel leading her to God and the eternal home promised in heaven. When Mrs. Ellis asks how anyone can be prepared for death, Anna explains that because God is love, people must become like him by loving others and seeking their good rather than living selfishly.

Anna then dies quietly and without fear. Deeply moved, Mrs. Ellis regrets having thought so lightly of the humble seamstress and resolves to become wiser and better. Mrs. Bell reminds her that Anna’s peaceful death is attainable by anyone who follows her lesson: to imitate God through selfless love.
