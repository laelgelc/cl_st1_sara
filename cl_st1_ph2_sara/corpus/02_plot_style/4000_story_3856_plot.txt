An ethnologist investigating the folklore of a remote mountain people complains that their traditional legends have been displaced by stories of a British lieutenant who is invulnerable, can leap over elephants, and flies on black wings. The lieutenant explains that the legend arose during a military expedition.

Sent with only nine soldiers into a supposedly friendly village, he and his men are attacked and forced to retreat. When enemies move to cut them off in a narrow gorge, the party climbs a steep hillside along what appears to be a path, only to find themselves trapped on a high ledge ending at a sheer cliff. Though the position is defensible, they have no food or water. During two days of intense thirst, one soldier disappears while confronting the enemy, a wounded Sepoy falls from the cliff, and their mule also plunges over the edge after being shot for food.

Realizing rescue may come too late, the lieutenant fashions a crude parachute from the canvas tent and its ropes. After briefly losing his nerve, he leaps from the ledge at night and descends toward the river. He lands on and kills one of three tribesmen who are decapitating the fallen Sepoy; the other two flee in terror at the sight of a man descending from the sky. The lieutenant drinks from the river, reaches the main camp, and sends a rescue force back for his men. Most survive, though two more Sepoys have jumped from the ledge. The witnesses transform his parachute descent into the legend of a winged flying man.
