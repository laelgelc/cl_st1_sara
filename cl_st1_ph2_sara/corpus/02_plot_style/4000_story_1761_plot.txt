During a late-night discussion about presentiments, the skeptical and ironic Minver recalls an unsettling episode from his childhood. His uncle Felix, a melancholy merchant prone to forebodings, leaves his Midwestern home on a business trip to New York despite an overpowering sense that something dreadful will happen and that he will never return alive.

Throughout the journey, Felix remains crushed by an undefined feeling of doom. After surviving a steamboat fire and helping rescue a woman, he declares that he will never see his wife again. The foreboding then becomes fixed on his own expected death, and he completes his business and returns home in resigned certainty that disaster awaits him.

Felix nevertheless reaches home safely. As he approaches his house, however, his presentiment suddenly changes. Seeing neighbors gathered at the gate, he immediately knows that his wife is dead. She had died unexpectedly the previous night while preparing with their daughter for his return. The listeners debate the psychological significance of the incident, then lapse back into their usual bantering relationship.
