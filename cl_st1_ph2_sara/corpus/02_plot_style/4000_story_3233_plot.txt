Secret Service chief Captain Walker recounts his search for stolen government plates capable of producing counterfeit Liberty Bonds. Suspecting the master criminal Mulehaus but unable to locate him, Walker retreats to Atlantic City, where he jokingly offers a disreputable, drunken chair attendant twenty dollars to find a former German soldier who had also been a sailor and a tanner.

The hobo soon claims to have found a wealthy, banker-like man fitting the description. Using shrewd observations about the man’s gait, hands, behavior, and interest in a secluded shed near the inlet, he concludes that stolen goods have been hidden there for transfer to a passing ship. Although Walker suspects a scheme for money, he follows him. At the shed, the hobo reads footprints to prove that someone carried a heavy object inside, then pours water over the packed-earth floor. Air bubbles reveal disturbed soil, beneath which they find the stolen plates concealed in a false-bottomed trunk.

Walker honors his promise and gives the hobo the five-thousand-dollar reward. Later, however, he receives a letter from Mulehaus revealing that the hobo was Mulehaus himself. After learning that Walker had agents aboard every outgoing ship, Mulehaus realized he could not escape with the plates, so he disguised himself, led Walker to them, and collected the reward to cover his expenses.
