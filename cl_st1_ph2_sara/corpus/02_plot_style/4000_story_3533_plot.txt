Ivan Aksionov, a young merchant, ignores his wife’s ominous dream and sets out for a fair. After sharing an inn with another merchant, he leaves before dawn. The merchant is later found murdered, and a bloodstained knife planted in Aksionov’s luggage leads to his wrongful conviction. Even his wife doubts him. He is flogged and sent to Siberia, where twenty-six years of imprisonment transform him into a devout, meek old man respected by prisoners and authorities alike.

A new convict, Makar Semyonich, arrives and inadvertently reveals that he is the real murderer. Aksionov is consumed by rage but later discovers Makar digging an escape tunnel. When questioned by the governor, Aksionov refuses to betray him. Overcome by this mercy, Makar confesses privately to the murder and begs forgiveness, promising to admit his guilt publicly. Aksionov forgives him, finding spiritual peace and losing his desire for freedom. Makar confesses, but Aksionov dies before the order for his release arrives.
