Destitute and aimless, Martin Stoner wanders toward the sea until he seeks shelter at an isolated farm. An elderly servant mistakes him for Tom Prike, the farm’s long-absent heir, and welcomes him home despite the unseen aunt’s refusal to forgive or meet him. Exhausted and hungry, Stoner accepts the mistake, then decides to remain when he is given food, lodging, clothes, a horse, and a place in the farm’s life.

Stoner discovers that he closely resembles Tom and that the entire countryside hates Tom for an unexplained past offense. Although uneasy about exposure, he assumes Tom’s identity and finds temporary security, companionship, and work at the otherwise grim farm.

Near Christmas, the servant warns him that Michael Ley has returned and intends to shoot him. Given money and instructions to hide in a coastal village until it is safe, Stoner leaves secretly. Relieved to abandon the false identity and hopeful of making a fresh start, he walks away from the farm—only to be confronted in a dark lane by the armed, hate-filled Michael, who has mistaken him for Tom and traps him before he can escape.
