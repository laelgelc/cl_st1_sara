In a near-future authoritarian America, a grandfather replies to his grandson Robbie, whose friend—or possible lover—J. has been detained for refusing to provide information about G., an undocumented person, and M., who knew G.’s status. The grandfather explains that courts, police, and government institutions now punish anyone deemed disloyal, and warns Robbie that intervening could endanger both J. and their entire family.

He recalls how democracy eroded gradually through corrupt elections, pardons, attacks on judges, intimidation, and normalized abuses while ordinary citizens—including himself and his wife—continued their daily lives, assuming someone else would restore order. Though ashamed of his generation’s passivity, he argues that resistance now may be futile and urges Robbie to survive, preserve his sanity, and wait for the country to recover.

Yet his certainty falters. Remembering J. and realizing how deeply Robbie may love her, he mentions money set aside for an emergency and asks what Robbie intends to do. The letter ends with the grandfather torn between fearful caution, regret over his own inaction, and a faint renewed desire to resist.
