Mademoiselle Gautier, a beautiful, talented, strong-willed Parisian actress, leads a celebrated but dissipated life and falls deeply in love with fellow actor Quinault Dufresne. When he rejects the prospect of marriage, the heartbreak leaves her profoundly melancholy and remorseful about her past.

On her thirty-first birthday, Gautier unexpectedly attends mass and is overwhelmed by conscience. For six months she divides her life between daily worship and nightly pleasures, but eventually resolves to abandon society. After making a general confession, bidding farewell to friends and patrons, settling her affairs, and reconciling with an enemy, she secretly leaves Paris.

Gautier tests religious life in several communities. At Pondevaux she discovers peace in prayer and seclusion; at Anticaille she submits to increasingly demanding acts of penitence under Father Deveaux, including self-flagellation. Though initially angry and resistant, she comes to embrace such discipline and concludes that she must sacrifice her liberty completely. Inspired by accounts of famous penitents, she chooses the severe Carmelite order rather than a more lenient convent.

After openly disclosing her former profession and immoral life, Gautier is admitted to the Carmelite convent at Lyons. She eagerly accepts menial labor and, after three months’ probation, takes her vows with unwavering composure. She spends the rest of her life in humility, work, charity, and seclusion, retaining her warmth and liveliness even after becoming blind. Though sincerely transformed, she preserves one harmless remnant of vanity: remembering her celebrated beauty, she obtains permission to receive visitors without a veil.
