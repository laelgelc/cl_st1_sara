A literary critic evaluates Gogol’s writing, praising his sharp observation, vivid characterization, realism, and command of satire while criticizing his excessive irony, loose plotting, melodrama, and fixation on grotesque or corrupt figures. He surveys Gogol’s historical, supernatural, psychological, and domestic fiction, finding the supernatural tales effective but the historical and madness-centered works uneven. He concludes by praising a simple story about a devoted elderly couple: after the wife dies, her seemingly carefree husband declines and soon follows her, revealing the depth of his love.
