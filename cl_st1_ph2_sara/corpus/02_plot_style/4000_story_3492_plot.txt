Fourteen-year-old Phatik, the unruly leader of a village gang, quarrels with his younger brother, Mākhan, after a prank involving a log. When Mākhan blames him and their widowed mother takes the younger boy’s side, Phatik feels misunderstood and unwanted. His visiting uncle, Bishamber, offers to take him to Calcutta and educate him, and Phatik eagerly leaves, giving his prized possessions to Mākhan.

In Calcutta, however, Phatik becomes an unwelcome burden to his aunt. He struggles at school, is beaten by his teacher, mocked by his cousins, and increasingly longs for his village and mother. After losing a schoolbook and being harshly scolded, he develops a fever and runs away, hoping to return home. The police find him drenched and gravely ill and bring him back to his uncle’s house.

Delirious, Phatik repeatedly asks whether the holidays have come, associating them with permission to go home. Bishamber sends for his mother, who arrives as the boy is dying. Hearing her call him “my darling,” Phatik turns toward her and says that the holidays have finally come, implying that death has released him and allowed him to return home.
