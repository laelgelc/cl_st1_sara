John Adderley Syson, now married and living away from his childhood home, returns to the countryside and farm where he once courted Hilda Millership. On his way, he meets Arthur Pilbeam, a young gamekeeper who is now Hilda’s lover and resents Syson’s continued letters and gifts to her.

At the farm, Syson and Hilda revisit their shared past, but he gradually realizes that he had idealized her and never understood her true nature. Hilda explains that she has found freedom and fulfillment in her relationship with Arthur, whom she loves on her own terms. She also accuses Syson of trying to shape her into someone she was not, while he counters that she encouraged the ambitions that drew him away from her.

After an uneasy meeting among all three, Syson leaves wounded by the recognition that his imagined spiritual bond with Hilda never truly existed. Secretly observing Hilda comforting and embracing Arthur, he hears her affirm that Syson has passed entirely out of her life, though she resists marrying Arthur immediately because she values the beauty and independence of their present relationship. Accepting the final separation between himself and Hilda, Syson returns to town.
