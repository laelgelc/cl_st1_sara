At Westfield Hall, Mrs. Anstruther decides to replace a gloomy clearing near the churchyard with a rose garden. Despite the gardener’s misgivings, she orders the remains of an old summer-house removed, including a deeply embedded wooden post.

A former resident, Miss Wilkins, recalls that the site terrified her and her brother as children. Her brother once dreamed there of being tried, condemned, and taken to execution, while she heard a voice within the post whispering, “You pull, I’ll push.” After their father investigated, he demolished the summer-house, and an old servant cryptically said that someone was safely confined there as long as no one released him.

Once the Anstruthers uproot the post, the gardener falls ill, strange voices and owl-like cries sound around the estate, and Mr. Anstruther dreams the same trial and execution in greater detail. The following evening, Mrs. Anstruther encounters a large, sweating, corpse-like face peering from the shrubbery and suffers a collapse.

An archaeological inquiry later reveals that a notorious, disgraced Lord Chief Justice had retired to Westfield and supposedly died there in remorse. After his death, the district was haunted until local clergymen ritually “laid” his spirit beneath a stake in a field west of the churchyard—the very stake removed for the rose garden. The Anstruthers leave, while the rector hopes the disturbances will eventually subside and reflects on an old warning added to the parish register: do not disturb what is at rest.
