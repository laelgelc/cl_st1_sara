A parish priest defends indigenous Irish poetry against claims that it is merely comic or crude, offering examples of sincere verse and recalling Michael Finley, a gifted oral poet who refused to let his work be written down. Finley’s distrust of preservation eventually causes his reputation and most of his poetry to disappear after his death, though the narrator secretly secures one complete ballad.

The ballad recounts how the formidable Phaudhrig Crohoore loves Kathleen O’Brien, whose family instead arranges her marriage to his rival, Michael O’Hanlon. Phaudhrig interrupts the wedding and asks Kathleen whether she is marrying freely. Her silent grief reveals that she still loves him. When O’Hanlon challenges him, Phaudhrig kills him with a single blow, carries Kathleen away on horseback, and escapes before the wedding party can react. Years later, Phaudhrig joins the Irish uprising of 1798 and dies fighting bravely.
