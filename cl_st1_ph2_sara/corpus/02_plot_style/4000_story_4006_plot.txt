On a hot July day in Kew Gardens, a succession of visitors passes an oval flower bed while a snail slowly navigates the earth beneath the plants. Simon, walking with his wife Eleanor and their children, recalls proposing to another woman fifteen years earlier; Eleanor responds with a cherished memory of an unexpected childhood kiss. Next, a patient young man guides an elderly, mentally disturbed companion who speaks of spirits, distant lands, and lost women. Two working-class women follow, gossiping until one becomes transfixed by the flowers. Finally, a shy young couple awkwardly express their emerging intimacy before going in search of tea.

As the snail chooses a path beneath a dead leaf, the human figures drift away into the heat and greenery. Individual conversations dissolve into the garden’s larger mixture of voices, traffic, movement, and flashing color, emphasizing the fleeting inner lives unfolding amid the continuous life of nature and the city.
