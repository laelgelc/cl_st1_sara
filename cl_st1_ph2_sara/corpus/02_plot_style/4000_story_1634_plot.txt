A small-town newspaper editor is visited by a ragged old man calling himself Michob Ader, who claims to be the immortal Wandering Jew. He says he was cursed to roam forever after striking Christ and refusing to let him rest on the way to the Crucifixion. The man recounts bizarre, often comic versions of historical events, yet his anguish over his supposed offense appears genuine.

The next morning, the editor discovers that the visitor is actually Mike O’Bader, a local shoemaker known for periodic drunken episodes in which he tells this story. Unsatisfied with whiskey as the sole explanation, the editor investigates Mike’s past. He learns that decades earlier, Mike’s estranged daughter returned after running away with a circus. When townspeople drove her out, she sought refuge at her father’s door, but he struck her and refused her entry. She was later found drowned.

The editor realizes that Mike’s delusion reenacts his guilt: like the legendary shoemaker who denied Christ rest, Mike denied shelter to his own suffering child and has been psychologically condemned to wander through remorse ever since.
