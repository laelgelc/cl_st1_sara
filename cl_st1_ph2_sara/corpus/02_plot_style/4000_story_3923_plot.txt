Cowboys Tom and Alfred are escorting a hostile band of Mexican vaqueros across the Arizona desert when one of the men, José, murders their cook, Black Sam. Tom shoots José, and he and Alfred flee on horseback toward a narrow ravine leading through an otherwise impassable cliff. Pursued by the vaqueros, they lose their injured horse and barricade themselves behind its body, killing several attackers but becoming trapped under armed watch.

Realizing that reinforcements will soon arrive and attack from above, they decide one man must hold off the Mexicans while the other climbs out and runs fifteen miles for help. Tom secretly rigs a card draw so that he will remain behind, allowing the smaller, less athletic Alfred to escape while Tom drives back the attackers with gunfire.

Alfred barely reaches a nearby ranch, but the rescue party arrives too late. Tom has been killed, mutilated, and robbed, though his sacrifice enables Alfred to survive. Near Tom’s body lies a clay slab he carved while waiting, bearing words about a love greater than the love of a woman—words that had haunted him because of an old heartbreak and that now poignantly reflect his selfless devotion to his friend.
