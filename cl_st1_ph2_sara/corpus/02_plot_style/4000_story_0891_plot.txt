Mrs. Pottle seeks a thousand-dollar donation for a day nursery from wealthy, notoriously miserly Felix Winterbottom, but he refuses because the socially prominent Gulick family is involved. Determined to raise the money herself, she organizes a grand pageant celebrating Granville’s two-hundred-year history. The domineering Gulicks exploit the production to glorify their ancestors, forcing Mrs. Pottle to alter history, assign them the leading roles, and add costly scenes.

Preparations become increasingly chaotic, while her husband Ambrose—reluctantly serving as treasurer and the Spirit of History—discovers records that contradict the Gulicks’ self-important family legends. At the packed performance, forgotten lines, awkward acting, an uncooperative horse, and hired “Indians” turn the dignified pageant into comic disorder. Provoked beyond endurance, Ambrose abandons the flattering official account and reveals the less distinguished truth about the Gulick ancestry. Their public humiliation delights Winterbottom, whose resulting generosity secures the money needed for the nursery.
