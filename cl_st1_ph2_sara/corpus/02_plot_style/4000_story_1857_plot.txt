Near Boston, the miserly Tom Walker and his equally greedy, violent wife live in bitter poverty, constantly trying to cheat each other. While crossing a haunted swamp, Tom encounters the devil, disguised as a black woodsman. The devil reveals that he controls pirate treasure buried nearby and offers it to Tom in exchange for his soul and its use in wicked enterprises.

Tom initially refuses, largely because his wife urges him to accept. She attempts to bargain with the devil herself, taking the household valuables as an offering, but disappears after a violent struggle. Tom, more relieved than grieved by her death, eventually seeks out the devil and agrees to become a usurer, exploiting desperate borrowers for immense profit.

Tom grows rich by charging ruinous interest, seizing property, and bankrupting his clients. As old age approaches, however, he fears damnation and tries to escape his bargain through ostentatious religious devotion, carrying Bibles while continuing his predatory business. During a storm, as he prepares to foreclose on a desperate debtor, Tom angrily invokes the devil. The devil immediately arrives on a black horse and carries him into the swamp.

After Tom vanishes, his bonds and mortgages turn to ashes, his money becomes worthless debris, his horses become skeletons, and his mansion burns down. His restless spirit is thereafter said to haunt the swamp, serving as a warning against greed, hypocrisy, and wealth gained through exploitation.
