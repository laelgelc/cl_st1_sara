A destitute young wanderer trudges through a rainy city, mocked for his ragged appearance and searching for food and shelter. After obtaining free soup at a saloon, he meets a drunken, sinister-looking homeless man and agrees to provide the few extra cents needed for them both to stay in a cheap lodging house.

Inside, the youth is overwhelmed by the crowded room’s filth, stench, cold, and tomb-like atmosphere. Unable to sleep, he watches the sprawled bodies and hears one lodger’s anguished cries, which he interprets as the collective protest of an impoverished and forgotten class.

In the morning, he and his companion leave, and the youth lends him more money for a meager breakfast. They share stories and briefly enjoy the warmth of food and companionship before settling on benches in City Hall Park. Watching well-dressed people hurry past without noticing them, the youth becomes painfully aware of his exclusion from comfort, dignity, and opportunity. Confronted by the towering, indifferent city, he accepts that he is an outcast and begins to internalize the guilt and shame society assigns to him.
