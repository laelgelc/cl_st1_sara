In an isolated Arctic mining camp, Big George, a formidable whaler who becomes violent when drunk, provokes a newly arrived prospector known as Captain. Their confrontation erupts into a brutal fight, but it is interrupted by news that Captain’s childhood friend Barton and another man, Kid Sullivan, have been swept out to sea on drifting ice during a blizzard.

When the other townsmen consider a rescue impossible, Captain volunteers to go alone. Recognizing George’s courage despite their quarrel, he asks him to help, and George agrees. Accompanied by George’s Indigenous wife, they travel by dog sled to the coast, obtain a three-person skin canoe, and venture into the storm-tossed, ice-choked sea while she waits ashore.

Captain and George eventually find Barton and Sullivan on a disintegrating floe, with Sullivan nearly frozen to death. Because the canoe can hold only three people, George insists on remaining behind while Captain takes the two stranded men ashore. After reaching land, Captain sends Barton and Sullivan toward shelter, then returns for George with George’s wife, who helps navigate through the darkness and storm. They find him and bring everyone safely to an Inuit village.

After recovering, Captain praises George’s bravery and his wife’s devotion, and apologizes for their fight. George reciprocates, acknowledging Captain’s courage. Their former hostility becomes mutual respect and friendship.
