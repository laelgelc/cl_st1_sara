In a peaceful little kingdom, young King Hubert learns of a prophecy: during his fourteenth year, the animal whose song sounds sweetest to him will save his life, and honoring that animal’s kind will preserve his dynasty and kingdom. Pressured to choose in advance, Hubert listens to many birds. A poor orphan girl brings her beloved donkey, whose braying she considers beautiful, but the court mocks her and drives her away. That evening Hubert chooses the nightingale, which is proclaimed sacred throughout the land.

Later, while hunting, Hubert becomes lost, falls down a rocky slope, breaks his leg, and lies helpless beside his dead horse. A nightingale, thrush, and linnet sing nearby, but none brings rescue. Near death, Hubert hears the distant braying of the orphan girl’s donkey and realizes that its call is the sweetest sound because it promises real aid. The donkey finds him, kneels so he can mount, and carries him to the girl’s cottage, where she tends him and alerts a search party.

After recovering, Hubert declares donkeys sacred, replaces the kingdom’s nightingale monuments with images of asses, appoints the rescuing donkey chief minister, and promises to marry the orphan girl when she is old enough. The legend humorously explains the kingdom’s donkey statues, its tradition of appointing asses to government, and its custom of beginning important works and proclamations with a bray.
