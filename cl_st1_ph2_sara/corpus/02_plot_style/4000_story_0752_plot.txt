After being rejected by a visiting belle, wealthy Montana rancher Jack Winfield resolves to marry. His foreman and friend, Dick Cutter, recalls the nine unmarried Bailey sisters of Tusculum, New York. Jack and several neighboring bachelors finance Dick’s trip east to persuade the family to relocate to Montana, where their father could find surveying work and the daughters might find husbands.

Dick discovers that Mr. Bailey has been dead for nine years and that the impoverished sisters support themselves through teaching and needlework. Their eldest sister, Euphrosyne, nevertheless accepts the proposal, hoping Montana will offer her younger sisters better prospects. Dick escorts all nine west, but the identical traveling outfits he selected make strangers mistake them for a female theatrical or sporting troupe. Humiliated by the gossip and impropriety of nine unmarried women traveling with one man, Euphrosyne decides they cannot continue.

Dick solves the problem by abruptly proposing to Euphrosyne, whom he genuinely wants to marry. They wed within an hour, giving the group a respectable married chaperone, and proceed to Montana. Within a year, the sisters have prospered and married the local bachelors: Jack marries Aurora, Clytie is the last to become engaged, and Dick and Euphrosyne become successful ranch owners.
