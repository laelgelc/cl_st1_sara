Pelayo, a young nobleman raised in the Pyrenees to be a hardy, virtuous warrior, encounters a servant fleeing from Gascon bandits who have ambushed a wealthy Bordeaux merchant and his family. Pelayo confronts the robbers nearly single-handedly, kills their leader, drives off the survivors, and rescues the captives. He refuses the merchant’s gold, protects the family through the mountains, and arranges shelter for them with a hermit.

During the night, the hermit prophesies Spain’s imminent conquest and tells Pelayo that he is destined to help redeem his country after preparing himself through pilgrimage and chivalric service. Pelayo accepts this higher calling.

The merchant’s daughter falls deeply in love with her rescuer, though the innocent and duty-bound Pelayo does not return her romantic feelings. At their parting, she gives him a ring, which he promises to keep in remembrance of her. Taking his acceptance as a spiritual bond, she remains unmarried for life and writes a book of meditations on love, chivalry, temptation, and faith, forever cherishing the memory of him.
