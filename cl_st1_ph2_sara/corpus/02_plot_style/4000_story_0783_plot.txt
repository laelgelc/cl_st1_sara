Bud Kent kills Tully’s brother and is condemned to an improvised lynching by his fellow lumbermen. He fights them off, escapes in a canoe, and paddles toward a remote island surrounded by fog, a voyage considered almost certainly fatal.

After reaching the island, Kent nearly dies from hunger and exhaustion but is rescued by a young Indigenous woman who lives there. Though they cannot understand each other’s language, they fall in love. She teaches him how to make fire, hunt, and fish, and they build a peaceful life together. Eventually they have a son, Chaské.

Fatherhood revives Kent’s memories of the killing and fills him with fear that his past will destroy his family. The child seems supernatural, casting a white shadow and glowing at night. When the boy is about a year old, the woman and child dissolve into the island’s encroaching mist. As Kent desperately searches for them, a torch appears through the fog, followed by the terrifying face of Tully, who stands beside him holding a coil of rope, apparently having finally come to exact revenge.
