A traveler arrives in Interlaken and admires Switzerland’s scenery, republican history, and the towering Jungfrau. He reflects on William Tell and the Swiss tradition of resisting tyranny, then humorously recounts the legend of St. Fridolin, who supposedly won a land dispute by bringing the dead owner’s skeleton into court as a witness.

Studying the Jungfrau over several afternoons, the traveler notices that shifting shadows form a gigantic human profile and other shapes across its snowy face. He treats the mountain as an enormous natural clock, its changing shadows marking the hours from four to six.

The narrative concludes with an anecdote about the King of Greece traveling incognito. A German-American brewer mistakes him for an underpaid foreman with a large family, lectures him about his poor career choices, and generously offers to bring him to Rochester, employ him in his brewery, and “make a man” of him.
