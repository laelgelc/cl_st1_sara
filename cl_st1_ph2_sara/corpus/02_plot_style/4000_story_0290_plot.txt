In the week before his wedding, a young man is overwhelmed by sudden success: he receives a government appointment, wins recognition as a poet, and becomes the center of public admiration. Excited, sleepless, and inflated by praise, he finds himself unexpectedly obsessed with the ordinary wife of a neighborhood tobacconist, even though he remains convinced that he truly loves his fiancée.

The day before the wedding, he impulsively asks the tobacconist’s wife to come to his apartment, and she silently agrees. That same day, a tender letter from his fiancée briefly strengthens his resolve to reject the other woman. Yet when the woman arrives, he embraces her and spends an intimate hour with her. Afterward, he visits his fiancée and feels newly humbled and prepared to enter marriage as an equal rather than as her superior. He believes the other woman’s decisiveness and earthy courage have brought him down from the self-important “perch” created by public praise.

He marries the next day and never sees the tobacconist’s wife again. His marriage is genuinely happy, but the memory of that encounter occasionally returns with intense physical force. Each time, however, it ultimately fades as he turns back toward the wife with whom he has chosen to share his life.
