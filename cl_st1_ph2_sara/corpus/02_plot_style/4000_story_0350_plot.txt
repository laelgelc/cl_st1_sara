Andrew Lyon and his wife sympathize with Mrs. Arnold, a sickly widow struggling to support three children and owing ten dollars in rent. Unable to spare the full amount himself, Lyon decides to collect one dollar each from several supposedly charitable acquaintances.

Instead of responding warmly, most contributors give grudgingly, suspiciously, or coldly, while one refuses outright. Their attitudes leave the sensitive Lyon embarrassed and humiliated, but concern for the widow drives him to persevere. After collecting only five dollars, he supplies the remaining five from his own limited funds, enabling Mrs. Arnold to pay her rent.

The experience convinces Lyon never again to solicit charitable aid. It reveals that many people who profess benevolence resist giving even when a recipient is unquestionably deserving, and that those seeking help for others should be treated with respect rather than made to feel ashamed.
