Jim Basset, a young cowboy suspected of altering cattle brands, is ostracized by his community and excluded from a Christmas dance. He is especially wounded because Hetty Rhodes, the woman he loves, plans to attend with the man whose jealous accusations first ruined Jim’s reputation. Before the dance, Jim encounters Hetty in town and impulsively buys her an expensive blue-bordered silk handkerchief. She refuses it as a gift, but he keeps it and defiantly announces that he will attend the dance despite the ban.

Jim arrives armed but agrees to surrender his gun in exchange for one dance with any woman he chooses. With the women’s consent—prompted by Hetty—he enters and chooses her. Their dance becomes a prolonged, increasingly tense challenge to Hetty’s escort and the assembled men. Ignoring demands that Jim’s allotted dance has ended, Jim and Hetty continue until he sweeps her out of the room, mounts his waiting horse with her, and rides away. Before dawn, they have traveled sixteen miles together to be married, with Hetty wearing the blue-bordered handkerchief as her only wedding gift.
