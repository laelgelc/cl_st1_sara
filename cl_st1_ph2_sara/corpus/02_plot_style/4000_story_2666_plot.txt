On his last day in England before returning to his modest fruit farm in Rhodesia, insecure and financially struggling Reginald resolves to propose to Anne, a wealthy, beautiful young woman he deeply loves. After escaping his possessive widowed mother, he visits Anne and confesses his feelings.

Anne rejects him, explaining that although she likes him more than anyone else and can tell him anything, she cannot marry a man who constantly makes her laugh. Comparing them to her pet doves—where the female runs laughing ahead while the male follows, bowing—she insists such a relationship would be wrong in real life. Heartbroken, Reginald tries to leave, but Anne cannot bear his unhappiness and repeatedly calls him back. Finally, she addresses him as “Mr. Dove,” implicitly accepting the very relationship she had rejected, and he returns to her.
