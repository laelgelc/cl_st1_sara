Gabriel Foot is acquitted of murdering a jeweller, but his barrister’s knowing glance reveals that the lawyer believes him guilty. Shunned by townspeople despite the verdict, Gabriel wanders onto the moors and enters an isolated cottage seeking food and shelter. There he unexpectedly finds his barrister, whom he resolves to kill for knowing too much.

The lawyer calmly reconstructs the true crime: the jeweller’s postillion, Roger Tallis, murdered his employer, after which Gabriel murdered Tallis and secretly buried him. To save his life, the lawyer offers Gabriel property worth ten thousand pounds if he will spare him. He then leads Gabriel to Tallis’s grave near some abandoned lime kilns and makes him dig up the remains. Among the bones they discover the missing diamond, which Tallis had swallowed.

The lawyer explains that he had already deduced the diamond’s location and had been tempted to dig it up himself. Gabriel’s arrival and their bargain prevented him from becoming a thief. Satisfied with the diamond, Gabriel honors his promise, and the lawyer departs, relieved that he remains merely a lawyer rather than a criminal.
