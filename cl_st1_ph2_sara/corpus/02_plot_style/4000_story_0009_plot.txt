After graduating from college in poor health, wealthy young New Yorker Alexander Wells travels to his family’s land grant near San Antonio, Texas, to recuperate. Captivated by frontier life, he settles into a remote cabin and spends several years hunting with Tiburcio, a skilled Mexican woodsman who becomes his close friend. Though his health quickly improves, Wells repeatedly deceives or evades his parents to prolong his stay.

Eventually, his mother comes to Texas and persuades him to return home. Wells leaves his belongings to Tiburcio and goes back to New York, but he fails at the business ventures his family arranges and rejects a proposed marriage. Unable to adapt to conventional Eastern society, he remains consumed by memories of Texas. After he turns thirty, his parents finally consent to his return, and he eagerly departs to establish a cattle ranch, convinced that the freedom of the West is worth every social sacrifice.
