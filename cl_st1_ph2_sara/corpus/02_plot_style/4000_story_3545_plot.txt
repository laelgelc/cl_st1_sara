In the era of Russian serfdom, Michael Simeonovitch, a former peasant elevated to superintendent, brutally exploits the serfs under his control. He forces them to work beyond their legal obligations, abuses and flogs them, profits from their labor, and even orders them to plough during the Easter holiday.

Driven to desperation, several peasants, led by the frequently punished Vasili, plot to kill Michael. Peter Mikhayeff opposes them, insisting that violence will only create greater evil and that God will punish the superintendent in due course. When the serfs are forced to plough on Easter Monday, most curse Michael, but Peter works peacefully while singing hymns, with a small candle miraculously continuing to burn on his plough despite the wind and movement.

Hearing of Peter’s conduct, Michael is overcome with fear and guilt, declaring that Peter has conquered him. Persuaded by his wife, he rides out, apparently intending to release the peasants from work. On the way, his horse is startled, and Michael falls onto a sharp fence picket and dies. Peter alone calmly retrieves the body. After learning of Michael’s cruelty, the nobleman frees the serfs in exchange for a small land rent. The peasants conclude that true power lies not in violence or revenge, but in goodness.
