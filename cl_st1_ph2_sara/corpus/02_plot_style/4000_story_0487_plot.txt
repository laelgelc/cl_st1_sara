Jean Rasteaux, a huge, good-natured Breton fisherman, is conscripted into an Alpine regiment far from his beloved sea. Lonely and mocked for his dialect, he patiently endures relentless bullying from soldiers and officers until a humiliating incident finally provokes him. Rather than strike the small soldier who insults him, Jean knocks down two larger tormentors. When an officer lashes him across the face during the inquiry, Jean lifts the officer overhead and hurls him to the ground.

Imprisoned to await court-martial and almost certain execution, Jean breaks his bonds and escapes up a snow-covered mountain, apparently hoping to cross into Italy. Soldiers gather below, confident that sheer cliffs and an overhanging ridge will force him to surrender. Realizing he is trapped, Jean deliberately throws himself down the slope. His rolling body gathers snow and triggers a massive avalanche, which sweeps away the waiting soldiers and officers, drowning or burying them. When the bodies are recovered, the victims’ faces are frozen in terror, while Jean lies dead and peaceful, as though he had finally returned to the waters of Brittany.
