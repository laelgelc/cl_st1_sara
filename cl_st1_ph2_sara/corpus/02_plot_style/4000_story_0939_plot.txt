A bitter, overworked sergeant leads a corporal and fourteen soldiers to occupy an isolated house far ahead of their regiment. After berating two hungry privates for stealing fruit and complaining about army discipline, he fortifies the stone building and declares it nearly impregnable.

An unseen enemy soon attacks. Under the sergeant’s calm but harsh command, the soldiers defend the house despite mounting fear and casualties. Men are killed or wounded one after another, yet the sergeant repeatedly insists they can hold the position and drives even terrified soldiers back to their posts. With no orders or reinforcements arriving, he continues fighting until a bullet mortally wounds him. His final words urge the regiment on.

When enemy troops finally enter the blood-soaked house, their officer is astonished by the tiny force, having believed from the intensity of its defense that at least a hundred men were inside.
