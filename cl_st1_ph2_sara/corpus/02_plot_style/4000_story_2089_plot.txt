A boy learns that Father Flynn, an elderly priest who mentored him in Catholic doctrine and ritual, has died after a third stroke. Though disturbed by the death and by an acquaintance’s insinuations that the priest was an unhealthy influence, the boy also feels an unexpected sense of freedom.

He visits the priest’s house with his aunt, views the imposing corpse, and sits with the priest’s sisters, Nannie and Eliza. During their conversation, Eliza reveals that Father Flynn had become mentally troubled by the burdens of his vocation. His decline began after he accidentally broke a chalice; afterward, he withdrew from others and behaved strangely. He was eventually found alone in a dark confessional, awake and softly laughing to himself, confirming that something in him had gone profoundly wrong.
