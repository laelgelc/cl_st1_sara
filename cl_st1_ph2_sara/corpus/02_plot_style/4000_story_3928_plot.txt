Stage driver Billy Knapp and a curious tenderfoot are held up on the road to Deadwood. The stage’s armed messenger is killed, and the lone robber escapes with the strongbox. In Deadwood, the newly elected sheriff, Alfred, refuses a posse and rides alone into a snowstorm, reasoning that the fugitive must ultimately head east to Pierre to exchange the stolen, traceable banknotes.

Alfred finds the robber’s trail, surprises him asleep beside a creek, captures him, and recovers the money. While escorting his prisoner back, they encounter a band of Sioux pursuing a pony the robber stole. Alfred releases and arms the outlaw so they can defend themselves together.

The two men fight off repeated mounted charges, then endure a prolonged siege by warriors crawling toward them through sparse grass. Their ammunition dwindles until both appear to run out just as the attackers prepare to overwhelm them. A rescue party from Deadwood arrives in time, driving the Sioux away.

The outlaw immediately tries to escape on the last surviving horse, but Alfred shoots the animal from under him. Alfred reveals that he secretly saved two cartridges—the very shots that might have stopped the final Indian advance—because, as sheriff, recapturing his prisoner took precedence over using every round in the fight.
