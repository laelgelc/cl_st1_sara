A radio-obsessed lawyer and cemetery trustee is hired by wealthy, devout Remington Solander to arrange an unusual provision in his will. Solander endows a station to broadcast his collection of sermons, hymns, and uplifting writings, while a permanently installed radio in his mausoleum will play those broadcasts throughout the cemetery. The equipment and wavelength must never be altered, and his heirs must maintain the tomb or lose their inheritance.

After Solander dies, the broadcasting tomb becomes a sensation, attracting visitors and greatly increasing demand for cemetery plots. The trustees expand the cemetery in anticipation of huge profits. Then the government reassigns radio wavelengths: Solander’s station moves, but his unalterable tomb receiver remains fixed on the old frequency, now occupied by a station broadcasting jazz, popular songs, comedy, and risqué jokes. Legally unable to adjust or silence the radio, the trustees watch as families remove every other body from the scandalous “jazz cemetery.” The cemetery is left deserted and worthless, with only Solander remaining in his perpetually noisy tomb.
