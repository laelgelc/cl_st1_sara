A woman waits desperately for a promised telephone call from a man whose affection appears to be fading. As the hours pass, she obsessively prays, counts, watches the clock, and invents excuses for his silence. Her thoughts swing between hope and despair, tenderness and rage, self-restraint and the urge to call him herself. She reflects on the affair, fears divine punishment, imagines hurting or even possessing him through death, and recognizes that he would call if he truly cared. Still unable to relinquish hope, she bargains with God and herself, finally deciding that if he has not called by the time she finishes counting to five hundred, she will telephone him. The story ends with her counting, trapped in anxious uncertainty.
