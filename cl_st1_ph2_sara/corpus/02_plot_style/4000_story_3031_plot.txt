Sylvia Seltoun triumphantly persuades her indifferent husband, Mortimer, to leave London and settle at his remote country estate, Yessney. She soon finds the place oppressively wild and sinister, while Mortimer becomes absorbed in its woods, streams, and farm. He warns her that Pan and the old nature gods still hold power there.

While exploring, Sylvia hears mysterious boyish laughter and later discovers a statue of Pan with grapes laid before it as an offering. Irritated, she removes the grapes, then glimpses a beautiful, malevolent boy watching her from the undergrowth. Mortimer cautions that woodland gods punish interference and advises her to avoid the woods and horned animals. Frightened, Sylvia decides she wants to return to London, but Mortimer ominously predicts that she never will.

During a later walk, Sylvia hears eerie piping that seems to agitate the animals. A stag fleeing a hunt unexpectedly turns toward her, driven by the mysterious music. As it charges, she sees the strange boy nearby and begs him to help, but he remains motionless. Her final moments are filled not only with terror of the stag’s antlers but with recognition of the supernatural figure, whose mocking laughter echoes as the animal strikes.
