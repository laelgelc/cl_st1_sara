Penniless Jacques de Beaune comes to Tours hoping to restore his disgraced father and win advancement at court. He follows an elegant woman to her mansion and, when a servant accidentally empties a vessel from a window, pretends to have been killed so that he will be carried inside. There he discovers that the woman is Madame de Beaujeu, the Regent. Revealing his ruse, he charms her, identifies himself as the son of her former steward, and asks for his father’s pardon.

Attracted to Jacques, the Regent secretly takes him to Amboise and accepts his boast that he can make love to her twelve times in one night. She promises him his father’s pardon, the manor of Azay, and further preferment if he succeeds. Jacques completes eleven encounters, but the twelfth attempt fails. The next morning, the Regent claims he has lost the wager. Jacques submits the dispute to her council through an allegory about knocking twelve walnuts from a tree, the last of which proves empty. The councillors rule that all twelve nuts nevertheless fell, so the wager must be paid.

Impressed by his wit and abilities, the Regent pardons Jacques’s father, grants Jacques the manor of Azay, and advances him to important financial offices. He becomes wealthy and powerful, builds a splendid château in the Indre, and rises to become Baron of Samblançay. Years later he recounts to King Francis I how his fortune began with that extraordinary night, though his eventual ascent through royal favor ends in betrayal and execution before the château is completed.
