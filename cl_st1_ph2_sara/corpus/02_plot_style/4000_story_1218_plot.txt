In a tidy New England village, the impoverished and disreputable Maddox family lives contentedly in a dilapidated house, redeemed by a magnificent blooming apple tree. Across the road, the industrious, exacting Sarah Blake forces her elderly husband, Edison, to help with relentless housecleaning. When Edison falls through a cane-seated chair and becomes dizzy, Sarah summons the idle but kindhearted Sam Maddox, who frees him and helps lay him down.

Sarah then demands Sam’s help shaking a heavy rug. Sam refuses payment but willingly works for her all day out of generosity. Secretly sympathizing with Edison, he urges the old man to remain on the couch and pretends to Sarah that her husband is still unwell, giving him a day’s rest.

That evening, aching from unfamiliar labor, Sam returns to his family. As he and his wife sit beneath their fragrant apple tree, they contrast their peaceful happiness with the Blakes’ constant work and anxiety. They conclude that gratitude and thanksgiving need not wait for a special day: despite their poverty and disorder, the beauty of the world and their contentment give their lives richness and grace.
