Count Antoine Joseph Van Horn, a young Flemish nobleman with a volatile temperament, is cashiered from the Austrian army and confined by his brother in a family castle. The castle’s brutal governor mistreats him so severely that he becomes insane. After escaping, Antoine is restored to relative sanity through his brother’s care, though he remains dangerously sensitive to contradiction.

Chafing under supervision, Antoine secretly leaves for Paris, where his noble relatives receive him cautiously because of his troubled past. He falls into gambling, dissipation, and questionable company, and may also incur the Regent’s jealousy through an affair with one of the Regent’s favorites. Despite warnings that he is in danger and should leave Paris, he remains.

Antoine and two companions, Lestang and Laurent de Mille, become implicated in the murder and robbery of a Jewish stockbroker involved in Law’s Mississippi scheme. Lestang escapes, while Antoine and De Mille are arrested. Accounts differ: one depicts all three as conspirators; another claims Antoine merely wounded the broker during a quarrel after being defrauded, while De Mille delivered the fatal blows and stole the broker’s pocketbook. Antoine insists he never intended murder and tried to protect the victim, but De Mille implicates him.

Antoine’s aristocratic relatives mobilize to save him, first seeking to have him confined as insane and then attempting to influence the judges. Their efforts are opposed by powerful figures invested in the Mississippi scheme, who want the broker’s murder punished severely. Antoine and De Mille are convicted and sentenced to be tortured and broken alive on the wheel.

Dozens of Antoine’s illustrious relatives petition the Regent for clemency, emphasizing his hereditary insanity and the disgrace his punishment would bring upon their interconnected noble houses. The Regent refuses a pardon, declaring that disgrace lies in the crime rather than the punishment. After further pleading, however, he solemnly promises to commute Antoine’s sentence to beheading, a death considered less dishonorable for a nobleman.

Antoine rejects poison offered by relatives as an escape from public execution and maintains his innocence of premeditated murder. Nevertheless, the Regent’s promise is broken: Antoine is tortured and exposed on the wheel alongside De Mille. After his death, his noble relatives personally remove his mutilated remains and carry them away for burial. The execution provokes outrage among the aristocracy of France, Flanders, and Germany, while Antoine’s brother refuses the confiscated property offered by the Regent and condemns him for violating justice and noble rights.
