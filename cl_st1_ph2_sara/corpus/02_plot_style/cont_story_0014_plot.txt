A struggling writer drives his father to a hospital for unexplained arm pain. As they navigate the hospital’s indifferent staff and maze-like corridors, the narrator worries about his father’s mortality and tries to write, compelled by a supernatural pact with the Twelve-tongued God. Years earlier, the god gave him creative power in exchange for his tongue, branding his back and demanding that he continually transform suffering into stories.

Disguised as a nurse, the god appears and challenges him to use his power boldly rather than create a safe, happy ending. Although the narrator glimpses a future in which his father suffers from cancer, he rewrites the immediate diagnosis so that his father is declared healthy. He then attempts to heal everyone in the hospital, proclaiming that sickness has ended and forcing a happy resolution onto the grieving and ill.

Instead, his intervention makes the patients’ “affliction” become flight: they float briefly when leaving the hospital, then crash to the ground, injured or helpless. Seeing the consequences, his father asks what he has done. The narrator can answer only that he has made a story about a hospital where people can fly.
