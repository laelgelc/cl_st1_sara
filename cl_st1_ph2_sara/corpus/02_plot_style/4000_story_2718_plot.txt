A visitor to an asylum is shown a severely emaciated madman tormented by violent, erotic, and macabre delusions. The doctor gives the visitor the inmate’s diary, which reveals that he was once a wealthy, contented collector obsessed with antiques and the women who had owned them.

After buying a seventeenth-century Italian cabinet, he discovers a secret drawer containing a long coil of golden-red hair. Fascinated by the unknown dead woman to whom it belonged, he becomes increasingly attached to the relic. He repeatedly touches, caresses, kisses, and sleeps with it until his obsession turns into love. Eventually, he believes the dead woman has returned bodily to him each night. He begins taking the hair—or, in his delusion, the woman—out in public as his companion, leading to his arrest and confinement when others discover his behavior and confiscate it.

As the visitor finishes the diary, the inmate’s anguished howls echo through the asylum. When asked whether the hair truly existed, the doctor produces it. Its touch fills the visitor with both revulsion and desire, underscoring the doctor’s conclusion that the human mind is capable of anything.
