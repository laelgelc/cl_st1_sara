A young golfer, disgusted by a disastrous round, announces that he is abandoning the game because it has no practical value. The club’s oldest member counters with the story of Cuthbert Banks.

In the divided suburb of Wood Hills, golfers and members of the local literary society regard one another with hostility. Cuthbert, an accomplished golfer, falls in love with Adeline Smethurst after accidentally driving a ball into a literary gathering. Adeline rejects his proposal because she considers him an unambitious nonentity and admires the pretentious novelist Raymond Parsloe Devine instead. Hoping to impress her, Cuthbert joins the literary society and endures its lectures while watching her idolize his rival.

At a reception, the celebrated Russian novelist Vladimir Brusiloff publicly dismisses the Russian writers whom Raymond claims as his influences, destroying Raymond’s intellectual reputation and Adeline’s admiration for him. Brusiloff then asks about famous Englishmen whose names no one recognizes. Cuthbert realizes that he means renowned golfers Abe Mitchell and Harry Vardon and reveals that he has played with them and won the French Open. Brusiloff recognizes Cuthbert, enthusiastically praises his golfing ability, and treats him as the only truly important person present.

Seeing Cuthbert acclaimed by the famous author, Adeline suddenly recognizes his distinction and asks to accompany him to the golf course, effectively accepting his renewed proposal. They marry, and she becomes an enthusiastic golfer. Convinced by the story that golf can indeed have practical benefits, the disillusioned young man urgently demands his clubs back.
