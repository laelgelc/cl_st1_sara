After planting a frog in his breakfast and exposing the adults’ confident error about its existence, Nicholas is punished by being excluded from a seaside outing. His domineering aunt also forbids him from entering the gooseberry garden and spends the afternoon guarding it, convinced he will disobey. Nicholas encourages this suspicion as a diversion, then secretly unlocks the forbidden lumber-room, where he delights in hidden treasures, especially a tapestry depicting a huntsman threatened by wolves.

While searching the gooseberry garden for him, the aunt falls into an empty rain-water tank and calls for help. Nicholas refuses to enter the garden, citing her own prohibition, and mischievously pretends her voice belongs to the Devil tempting him to disobey. A kitchenmaid eventually rescues her. Meanwhile, the seaside excursion proves miserable because the tide has covered the sands and one child’s tight boots ruin his mood. At tea, the humiliated aunt and disappointed children sit in silence, while Nicholas contentedly imagines how the tapestry’s story might end.
