After a drunken, impoverished mother dies, her three children are left to the mercy of the village. The two older children are quickly taken into other homes, but no one wants Maggie, the youngest, because she is bedridden from a spinal injury. The villagers suggest sending her to the poorhouse and abandon her alone in the family’s ruined hut.

Joe Thompson, a kindhearted wheelwright, cannot leave the terrified child behind. He carries Maggie home, despite expecting resistance from his irritable, childless wife, Jane. Jane initially demands that Maggie be sent away, but Joe persuades her to shelter the child for one night. As Jane feeds and cares for Maggie, the girl’s patience, sweetness, and gratitude awaken her long-dormant tenderness.

Jane soon asks to keep Maggie a little longer, and the Thompsons never contact the poorhouse authorities. They adopt her into their home, where caring for her transforms Jane’s bitterness into love and brings warmth, happiness, and purpose to the once-miserable household.
