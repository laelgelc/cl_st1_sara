Seventeen-year-old Mary Wylie lives in an isolated, impoverished bush homestead with her anxious mother, violent father, and troubled siblings. Returning home one evening, she discovers that her father has stolen and slaughtered a branded steer. He burns the hide to conceal the evidence, but mounted troopers find the remains and arrest him for livestock theft.

Mary’s brother Jack disappears, while her formerly wayward brother Tom returns and briefly supports the family before lapsing into unstable, suspicious behavior. Mary’s hope of escaping the bush with her drover sweetheart is destroyed when she learns that he has been arrested for armed robbery. When troopers later arrive looking for Tom, the accumulated shock and shame kill Mary’s mother.

After his release, Mary’s father returns with another woman. They drink, abuse Mary, and throw her out. Desperate and mentally broken, Mary begs a passing hawker to take her away from the bush. She then hallucinates approaching troopers, flees toward the gully, and throws herself from a cliff into a deep waterhole.
