Before attending the opera, Sir Henry reads from an old Virginia justice’s diary to illustrate the role of intuition in solving crimes.

Justice Pendleton encounters a distraught young woman who has been expelled by her guardian and uncle, Hambleton Dillworth. Dillworth claims her inheritance has been lost through a legally sanctioned but defective land purchase, leaving him unwilling to support her. He also tells her that her father, David, is alive and fled years earlier after stealing hidden gold and a horse.

Pendleton confronts Dillworth and exposes the truth. Indigo-stained gold coins recently used by the mysterious claimant seeking the girl’s land reveal that Dillworth secretly financed the lawsuit to seize her remaining property. Pendleton then reconstructs David’s disappearance: after David accidentally uncovered a small cache of gold, Dillworth shot him, invented the robbery story, placed the body on a horse, and dumped both man and animal into an abandoned cistern. He concealed the bullet hole behind a newly moved picture and pretended to have been assaulted.

Having taken possession of Dillworth’s pistol, Pendleton forces him to deed the family lands to David’s daughter. He then gives Dillworth until dawn either to flee through the mountains or join his victim in the cistern. Dillworth signs the deed and rides away into the night.
