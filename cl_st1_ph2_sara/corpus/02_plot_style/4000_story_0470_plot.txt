Nineteen-year-old Tom Collins, nearly penniless and repeatedly rejected for newspaper work, applies to the city editor of a major Washington paper. The editor initially dismisses the inexperienced youth, but Tom’s spirited demand for a fair chance earns him a nearly impossible test: obtain from Senator August the secret result of a political conference that every experienced reporter has failed to uncover.

Learning that the senator has left by train, Tom races to the station, boards the departing express, and finds him. Senator August refuses to reveal anything, but Tom’s honesty, courtesy, and determination win his sympathy. During the journey, an urgent telegram arrives warning that continued silence about the conference is politically damaging. Forced to release the news immediately, the senator dictates the official statement to Tom before he leaves the train at the next junction.

Tom returns to Washington and delivers the story after the regular edition’s deadline. Because the senator will not issue the statement elsewhere until later, the paper can publish an extra with the year’s biggest exclusive. Impressed by Tom’s resourcefulness and success, the city editor hires him as a reporter, instructing him to report promptly at eight the next morning.
