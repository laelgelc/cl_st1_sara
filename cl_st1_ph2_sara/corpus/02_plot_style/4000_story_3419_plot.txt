A timid guest at John Hinckman’s country home longs to propose to Hinckman’s niece, Madeline, but fears both rejection and her irascible uncle. While Hinckman is away, the guest encounters a ghost who looks exactly like him. The apparition explains that it was mistakenly appointed Hinckman’s ghost when Hinckman was wrongly believed dead, and now lives in terror of the still-living man while seeking a transfer to someone actually deceased.

The ghost offers to help the guest win Madeline in exchange for information about a likely “ghostship.” Instead, its unseen interruptions repeatedly sabotage his attempts to declare his love, even causing Madeline to mistake words addressed to the ghost as a cruel dismissal of her. The next day, the guest tries to repair the misunderstanding without revealing the ghost’s existence and again approaches a proposal.

The ghost suddenly appears to announce that Hinckman is returning and that it has finally been transferred to a recently murdered Russian nobleman. As it departs, the despairing guest cries that he wishes the ghost were his. Believing the declaration addressed to her, Madeline tearfully answers that she is his, accidentally bringing his courtship to a happy conclusion.
