Two men discuss the folklore and ghost stories told at private schools. One recalls an unexplained series of events from his schooldays involving a new master, Mr. Sampson, and his friend McLeod.

During a Latin exercise, McLeod inexplicably writes, “Remember the well among the four yews,” despite not knowing why the phrase came to him. Sampson reacts with visible alarm. Weeks later, during another exercise, an extra sheet appears among the boys’ papers, written in unfamiliar red ink: “If you do not come to me, I will come to you.” Sampson flees the classroom upon seeing it, and the writing later vanishes from the paper.

That night, McLeod wakes the narrator after seeing a deathly thin, soaking-wet figure perched outside Sampson’s window and apparently beckoning to him. By the next morning, Sampson has disappeared without a trace. The two boys never tell anyone what they witnessed, feeling mysteriously unable to speak about it, and the narrator concludes that something from Sampson’s hidden past came to claim him.
