Jennie, an unemployed Scottish immigrant, wanders through wintertime Chicago starving and penniless. After staring bitterly at an extravagant fruit display featuring unfamiliar “maymeys from Cuba,” she unsuccessfully seeks directions, charity, and help. In a department-store grocery, she pretends to be a customer so she can sample tiny pieces of cheese and meat, but these scraps only intensify her hunger. At a Scotch bakery counter, the sight of familiar scones, oatcakes, and meat pies overwhelms her, and she steals a hot scone. A woman catches her, and Jennie collapses from starvation, still clutching it. As she awaits an ambulance, her faint words are misunderstood: instead of recognizing her reference to the “maymeys from Cuba” that symbolized the city’s cruel excess, a worker concludes that Jennie’s name is Mamie and that she comes from Cuba.
