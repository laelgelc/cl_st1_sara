On a bitter winter night, the narrator visits his aspiring-author friend, Oberon, who has grown disgusted with his unpublished tales after repeated rejection by booksellers. Convinced that the stories have poisoned his happiness and contain something demonic, Oberon decides to destroy them. Despite briefly recalling the inspiration and imaginative pleasure behind his work, he drunkenly throws the manuscripts into the fire.

As the pages burn, Oberon sees his characters and visions in the flames, including the fiend he had created. He immediately regrets annihilating years of work and fears an aimless, forgotten life. Then the burning embers fly up the chimney and ignite the building’s roof, provoking a rapidly spreading fire alarm throughout the wind-lashed wooden town. Realizing that his manuscripts caused the blaze, Oberon’s despair turns to manic triumph: though no publisher would print his work, his imagination has literally set the town on fire.
