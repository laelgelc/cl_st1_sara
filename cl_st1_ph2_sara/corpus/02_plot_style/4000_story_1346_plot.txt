Nine-year-old Mary Foulkes, the motherless daughter of a teamster, treats five strange homemade dolls as her children. The rough but kindly men along her father’s route indulge her imagination, rescuing, repairing, and caring for the dolls as though they were alive. When progress brings a railroad and Mary is required to attend school, she may take only one doll with her, while the others are “adopted” by her adult friends and visited on weekends.

Mary is eventually sent away to a convent school. Though she writes affectionately with instructions for her dolls’ care, she remains gone for three years. Her friends faithfully preserve her makeshift children and bring them to welcome her home, only to find that education and adolescence have transformed her into a polished young lady. Embarrassed, they hide the dolls and realize that while Mary has returned, the imaginative little “mother” they loved has disappeared.
