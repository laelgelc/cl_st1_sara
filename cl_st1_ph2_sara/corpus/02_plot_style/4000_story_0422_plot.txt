During Napoleon’s Egyptian campaign, a young French soldier captured by Arab fighters escapes into the desert with weapons, provisions, and a horse. After riding the horse to death, he reaches a small oasis but despairs at the immense, deadly solitude surrounding him. He finds dates, water, and a cave, giving him hope that he can survive until rescuers pass nearby.

One night he awakens to discover a female panther sleeping beside him. Afraid that any attack might fail, he waits for morning and then cautiously wins the animal over by stroking her. The panther responds affectionately, and he names her Mignonne after a jealous former sweetheart. Although he repeatedly considers killing her, gratitude and growing affection prevent him.

Over the following days, the soldier and Mignonne become inseparable. She follows him, plays with him, obeys his voice, and even saves him from quicksand. Her companionship transforms the desert from a terrifying emptiness into a place of beauty and wonder. He studies her moods and movements, while she displays attachment and jealousy toward him.

Their bond ends through a fatal misunderstanding. When Mignonne catches his leg gently in her teeth, the soldier assumes she is attacking and stabs her in the throat. She dies looking at him without anger. French soldiers, having noticed his improvised signal flag, arrive to find him grieving as though he has murdered a person. Long afterward, despite witnessing wars across Europe, he remembers the panther and the desert as an unmatched experience of divine immensity untouched by humanity.
