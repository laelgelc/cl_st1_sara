Three penniless wanderers returning from Western Australia share an overcrowded steerage with men burdened by failure, injury, and loss. Among them is an elderly digger whose wife died in Sydney while he was working far away. A telegram warning of her illness went astray, and the notice of her death reached him late, leaving him tormented that he neither returned when he sensed trouble nor saw her before she died.

The digger tells the others how he and his wife struggled together for more than twenty years. Just as his claim began to prosper and he hoped to give her some comfort, he unknowingly sent an encouraging letter after she had already been buried. Though he has lost heart, he intends to return west to support his children.

His fellow passengers quietly care for him: they listen to his grief, give him a berth, and make sure he is served first at meals. When the ship reaches Sydney, he sees his children waiting in mourning clothes, including a baby he has never met and a little girl whose pity finally overwhelms him. He retreats below to regain control, followed discreetly by Mitchell.

Once ashore, the father embraces the little girl while Mitchell carries his luggage and baby and guides the family to privacy. Asked why he followed the grieving man below, Mitchell hides his compassion behind a joke, claiming he merely wanted a drink and thought the digger would not want to drink alone.
