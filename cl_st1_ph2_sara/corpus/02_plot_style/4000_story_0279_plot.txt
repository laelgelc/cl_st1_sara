A poor nineteen-year-old who once worked as a racehorse groom attends a racing meet in Sandusky dressed as a prosperous gentleman. Drawing on his insider knowledge, he advises a friendly young man and his two female companions to bet on a horse that will deliberately lose its first heat and then win. Attracted to the young man’s sister, Lucy, he impulsively claims to be the wealthy horse owner’s son and invents an elaborate privileged background.

His prediction proves correct, and the group wins heavily. They celebrate with dinner and an evening trip to Cedar Point, where the narrator and Lucy fall deeply in love and kiss. Lucy expects them to exchange letters, but the narrator has given her a false name and address. Too ashamed to confess before she leaves by train, he loses his only chance to continue the relationship and is left bitterly regretting the boastful lie that made him appear worthy of her but ensured they could never meet again.
