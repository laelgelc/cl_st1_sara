A traveler journeys westward through the rapidly developing American interior. Arriving by skiff at Burlington on Lake Champlain, he observes its imitation of a seaport, its busy foreign trade, and its diverse population, especially the many Irish emigrants laboring around the waterfront.

He later visits Rochester, where the diminished Genesee Falls prompt reflections on Sam Patch, the famous daredevil who died leaping from the cataract in pursuit of glory. Turning from the falls, the traveler surveys Rochester’s astonishing growth, crowded streets, commerce, courts, hotels, lotteries, and restless activity, contrasting the new city with a bewildered armed frontiersman who seems overtaken by civilization.

Continuing toward Detroit by steamboat, he is delayed at night by mechanical trouble near Lake Erie. While the vessel is repaired, Irish laborers gather firewood by a huge blaze onshore. Against the surrounding darkness, their figures appear and disappear in the flames, transforming an ordinary work scene into an eerie vision of supernatural beings tending an infernal fire.
