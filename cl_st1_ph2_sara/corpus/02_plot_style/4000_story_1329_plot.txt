Manager Delaney’s Rochester Stars are tied with the Providence Grays in a crucial pennant race. His greatest strength—and greatest headache—is an exceptional red-haired outfield: eccentric slugger Red Gilbat, vain showman Reddy Clammer, and phenomenally fast all-around star Reddie Ray.

During the game, Gilbat alternates between brilliance and absurdity. He mishandles a ball because he refuses to drop his peanuts, humiliates Clammer with a baby-carriage prank, then bats a home run while wearing a long duster. When spectators taunt him about his red hair, however, he charges into the bleachers and is removed from the game. Clammer then tries to cover the depleted outfield, making flashy plays until he crashes into the fence and is carried away injured.

With only seven players left, Ray must patrol the entire outfield alone. His extraordinary speed keeps Rochester in the game, though Providence scores three runs in the ninth and takes a 6–4 lead. In the bottom of the ninth, the Stars load the bases with two outs and Ray comes to bat. He drives the ball past the right fielder and races around the bases, overtaking the runner ahead of him and completing an inside-the-park grand slam. Rochester wins 8–6, fulfilling Delaney’s prediction that, after Gilbat and Clammer nearly lost the game, Ray would ultimately win it.
