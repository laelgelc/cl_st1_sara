In 1552, the young Tzar Ivan leads a modern, artillery-based siege of the Tatar stronghold of Kazan. Prince Andrej Kourbsky, one of his most trusted commanders, helps defeat invading Crimean Tatars and later plays a decisive role in Kazan’s capture. After fierce street fighting, Khan Yediguer surrenders and eventually converts to Christianity. At this stage Ivan appears brave, devout, merciful, and destined to be a great ruler.

Following a severe illness and the death of his gentle wife Anastasia, however, Ivan becomes increasingly suspicious and cruel. Learning that Ivan intends to execute him, Kourbsky escapes to Livonia with his loyal serf and stirrup-bearer, Vasili Shibanoff. Kourbsky then enters the Polish king’s service and writes Ivan a letter renouncing his allegiance. Knowing the danger, Vasili volunteers to deliver it. Ivan has him tortured from morning until night for information about possible conspirators, but Vasili reveals nothing, praises his master, and dies praying for him. Even Ivan is forced to admire the servant’s steadfast loyalty.

Ivan’s tyranny worsens through the murderous Oprichnina. Hoping for a respected churchman, he appoints the holy monk Feeleep as Metropolitan of Moscow. Feeleep accepts only in the hope of restraining him and initially succeeds, but Ivan soon resumes his atrocities. During a cathedral service, Feeleep publicly refuses to bless the disguised Tzar and condemns the innocent bloodshed committed under his rule.

Ivan retaliates by arranging false testimony against Feeleep. The Metropolitan is degraded during worship, stripped of his vestments, imprisoned, and shown the severed head of a relative. He remains calm and continues urging mercy for Russia. Eventually Ivan’s agent Maluta Skouratof visits his cell and smothers him after Feeleep refuses to bless the Tzar’s evil actions.

Neither Feeleep’s martyrdom nor other rebukes reform Ivan. His violence culminates in his fatally striking his own son, and he dies in 1584. Against the darkness of his reign, the story emphasizes the courage and fidelity of Vasili and Feeleep, whose loyalty, faith, and willingness to die rather than betray their principles stand in sharp contrast to Ivan’s cruelty.
