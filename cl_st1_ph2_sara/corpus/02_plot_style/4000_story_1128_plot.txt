Twenty years earlier, newlywed barrister James Murray becomes lost on a remote northern English moor during a snowstorm. Facing death from exposure, he finds shelter in the isolated farmhouse of a reclusive scholar who was driven from scientific society for defending belief in supernatural phenomena. Learning that Murray’s anxious wife is twenty miles away, the scholar sends his servant to guide him toward a crossroads where he can catch the night mail.

Left alone on an abandoned coach road, Murray sees an approaching mail coach and boards it. Inside, he discovers that the vehicle is decayed and its silent passengers are corpses. As the coach reaches a broken parapet, he realizes it is reenacting a fatal accident that occurred there nine years before. The spectral coach plunges over the precipice, and Murray loses consciousness.

He awakens later under his wife’s care. Shepherds had found him injured in a snowdrift below the exact site of the old crash. Although his surgeon dismisses the experience as a feverish dream, Murray remains certain that he rode with the dead as the coach’s fourth passenger.
