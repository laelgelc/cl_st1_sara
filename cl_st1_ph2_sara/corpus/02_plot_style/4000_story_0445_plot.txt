At the Council of Constance, the young, poor, and inexperienced priest Philippe de Mala becomes envious of the luxurious and licentious lives enjoyed by powerful churchmen. Fascinated by the city’s courtesans, he boldly enters the house of the celebrated Imperia and, despite his awkwardness, charms her with his sincere desire. She invites him to return the following evening.

After confessing his infatuation to his archbishop, Philippe spends his meager savings preparing for the encounter. Imperia receives him warmly, but their supper is interrupted first by the Bishop of Coire and then by the formidable Cardinal of Ragusa. The cardinal privately forces Philippe to choose between death and a wealthy abbey. Philippe accepts the abbey but cleverly advises the cardinal to frighten away the bishop by claiming that Philippe’s master has contracted the plague.

Pretending to depart, Philippe secretly hides behind a mirror. The cardinal uses Philippe’s ruse to drive off the terrified bishop, but Imperia then turns the same supposed danger against the cardinal, insisting that he may be infected and ordering him out despite his threats and pleas. Believing Philippe has abandoned her, she laments her perilous and unhappy life. Philippe then reveals himself, proving that he chose both safety and her company. Delighted by his wit and devotion, Imperia welcomes him as her true lover and resumes their intimate supper.
