An adult narrator recalls the Christmases of his childhood in a snowy Welsh seaside town, where memory blends many holidays into one enchanted day. He remembers waiting with his friend Jim to throw snowballs at cats when a fire breaks out in the Protheros’ house. The boys summon the fire brigade, whose work is met by Miss Prothero’s absurd offer of something to read.

Prompted by a child’s questions, the narrator continues describing Christmas morning: postmen arriving through the snow, useful and useless presents, sweets, noisy toys, visiting relatives, an enormous dinner, sleeping uncles, tipsy aunts, and games and walks with friends through the transformed town. That evening, the boys go caroling at a dark, unfamiliar house and flee in terror when a strange voice joins their singing from behind the door.

Back home, they find warmth, food, laughter, and family music. The narrator ends the day in bed, watching snow fall over the illuminated town, listening to music from neighboring houses, saying a prayer, and falling asleep.
