During a fierce battle, infantryman Fred Collins repeatedly complains of thirst and claims there is water in a well across a shell-swept meadow. When his comrades mock and challenge him, pride drives him to ask permission to fetch it. Though uncertain whether he truly wants to go, his officers allow him, and he sets out carrying several canteens under the regiment’s gaze.

Crossing the meadow amid exploding shells and rifle fire, Collins reflects uneasily on heroism and his own failings. Terrified but unharmed, he reaches the ruined house and well. Finding the canteens too slow to fill, he uses an old bucket, then runs back through the bombardment with water.

On the return journey, a mortally wounded artillery officer asks for a drink. Collins initially refuses in panic and runs past, but then turns back, gives the dying man water, and resumes his flight. His regiment welcomes him enthusiastically, but when two playful lieutenants take the bucket, their horseplay causes it to fall, spilling all the remaining water.
