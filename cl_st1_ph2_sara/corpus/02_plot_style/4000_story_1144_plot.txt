On New Year’s Eve at a luxurious New York hotel, kitchen checker Gussie Fink works through the night while nursing heartbreak over Heiny, her former sweetheart. Recently promoted from kitchen worker to waiter and now calling himself Henri, he has become distant and seemingly captivated by the glamour of the dining room. Coworkers exploit Gussie’s jealousy, especially when Henri receives enormous tips from a wealthy party that includes an apparently beautiful woman.

As the celebration ends, Gussie sees the sordid aftermath: the glamorous woman, exhausted and intoxicated, collapses, while her male companions abandon her. Henri helps carry the woman away but prevents Gussie from touching her, insisting that someone so wholesome should not soil her hands. Disillusioned by the revelers’ decadence, he admits that his new status had given him a swollen head and realizes how much he values Gussie’s clean, simple goodness.

The couple reconcile and leave together for coffee and a ham sandwich at their familiar neighborhood restaurant. On their way out, Henri also recognizes the reassuring decency of Tillie, the bedraggled scrubwoman, and warmly wishes her a happy New Year, giving her a tip.
