In 1891, Peg-Leg Eldridge and three accomplices rob a Denver and Rio Grande express train at isolated Texas Creek, dynamiting its safe and escaping with valuable gold. U.S. Marshal Bob Banks immediately pursues them with a posse and three bloodhounds across the Rocky Mountains and into New Mexico.

For two weeks, the fugitives travel day and night, avoid settlements and rail stations, change horses, and use difficult terrain to conceal their route. Banks relentlessly follows despite exhausted men, injured horses, and the eventual incapacitation of all three dogs. Although he never closes the gap, evidence and information gathered along the way allow him to identify Eldridge and deduce that he is heading toward a known refuge in the Chickasaw Nation.

Banks ends the direct pursuit, then arranges an ambush at a crowded horse race near Eldridge’s destination. While everyone is distracted by the race, he and another officer arrest Eldridge and one accomplice without a struggle. The other two robbers escape permanently. Eldridge is convicted of train robbery and sentenced to a prison term longer than his expected life. On the way to prison, he sends Banks a pearl button and a bitter message implying that, had he expected the marshal, their encounter would have ended in death rather than trial.
