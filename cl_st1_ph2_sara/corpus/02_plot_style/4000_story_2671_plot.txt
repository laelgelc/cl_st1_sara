Monica Tyrell, a nervous and self-dramatizing woman, is outraged when her lover Ralph telephones her during the morning hours he knows she finds unbearable. Convinced that this proves he does not truly understand her, she rejects their lunch plans and exults in a sudden sense of freedom.

She impulsively visits her hairdresser, where the usually warm staff seem strangely distant. George, the young man who styles her hair, finally reveals that his infant daughter died that morning. Confronted with genuine grief, Monica’s romantic vision of freedom collapses into pity and sorrow. She rushes to meet Ralph at the restaurant, imagining an anonymous gift of white flowers for the dead child from “one who understands,” but passes the flower shop before she can stop the taxi.
