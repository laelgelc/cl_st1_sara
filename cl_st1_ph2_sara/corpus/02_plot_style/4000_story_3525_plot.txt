Alyosha, a cheerful, obedient peasant boy accustomed to relentless labor, is sent at nineteen to work as a merchant’s servant. Though the household exploits his willingness by burdening him with nearly every task, he performs his duties without complaint while his father collects his wages.

For the first time, Alyosha experiences genuine personal affection when Ustinia, the cook, cares for him not merely for his usefulness but for himself. They grow close and consider marriage, but the merchant’s family objects to married servants, and Alyosha’s father orders him to abandon the idea. Accustomed to obedience, Alyosha submits, though both are heartbroken.

Soon afterward, Alyosha falls from a roof while clearing snow and is fatally injured. As he dies, he thanks Ustinia and consoles himself that their prevented marriage has spared her greater suffering. Accepting death with the same humility with which he accepted life, he prays, marvels quietly at what awaits him, and dies.
