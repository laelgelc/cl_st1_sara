Mr. McWilliams recounts how he installed an expensive burglar alarm at his wife’s urging, only to discover that it protected just the first floor. Burglars repeatedly entered through unprotected upper stories, forcing him to pay the alarm company to extend the system. Once fully installed, the enormous alarm began waking the household whenever the cook opened the kitchen door. Attempts to correct this flaw instead allowed burglars to disable the system and even lodge in the house undisturbed.

Further modifications bring temporary peace, but false alarms soon cause McWilliams and his coachman to shoot a nurse and injure McWilliams accidentally. After years of repeated false alarms and costly repairs, the family stops responding and gradually disconnects every room. Burglars then steal the entire alarm. While the company is reinstalling it with supposedly improved equipment, the workers leave for the summer, allowing burglars to occupy and empty the house.

A new automatic clock proves equally unreliable, activating the alarm by day and disabling it by night despite years of replacements. After nine years of expense, sleeplessness, injuries, and burglaries, McWilliams concludes that the device has protected only the criminals. With his wife’s consent, he trades it for a dog—and then shoots the dog.
