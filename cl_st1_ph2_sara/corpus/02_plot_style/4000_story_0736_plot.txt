Federigo, a noble and accomplished Florentine, squanders his fortune trying unsuccessfully to win the virtuous Monna Giovanna. Reduced to poverty, he retires to a small farm with only his prized falcon for companionship and support.

After Monna Giovanna is widowed, she moves near Federigo’s farm with her young son. The boy becomes gravely ill and asks for Federigo’s falcon, believing it will restore his health. Monna Giovanna reluctantly visits Federigo to request the bird. Delighted by her unexpected arrival but having no suitable food to offer her, Federigo sacrifices the falcon and serves it to her for dinner.

After the meal, Monna Giovanna explains her request. Federigo tearfully reveals that she has unknowingly eaten the falcon. Though initially dismayed, she recognizes the nobility and generosity that poverty has not diminished in him. She returns home without the bird, and her son dies soon afterward.

Later, pressured by her brothers to remarry, Monna Giovanna chooses Federigo, declaring that she would rather have a worthy man without wealth than wealth without a worthy man. They marry, and Federigo, now more prudent with money, lives happily with her for the rest of his life.
