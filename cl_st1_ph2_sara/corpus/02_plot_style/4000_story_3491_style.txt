The story has a reflective, lyrical, spiritually philosophical style, combining realist village observation with parable-like moral inquiry. It is narrated in the first person by an educated, self-conscious writer who regards himself with gentle irony. His voice is urbane and contemplative, moving easily between comic self-deprecation, precise social observation, sensuous descriptions of nature, and solemn reflections on devotion, grief, guilt, and the apprehension of God. The narrator often presents his own intellectual sophistication as faintly ridiculous, especially beside the Devotee’s instinctive certainty. This creates a subtle tension between abstract doctrine and lived spiritual experience.

The diction is formal, literary, and somewhat archaic. Expressions such as “made obeisance,” “with some unction,” “divine draught,” and “fraught with mystery” give the prose a devotional, early twentieth-century character. Religious vocabulary appears throughout, but it is integrated with ordinary material details: food, flowers, bare feet, socks, caste rules, writing papers, village roads, and bathing places. Elevated language is therefore repeatedly grounded in domestic and rural life. Occasional paradoxes and ironic formulations—such as the narrator’s “unpopularity” reaching “the nadir of its glory”—lend the prose wit and intellectual playfulness.

Sentences are generally long, balanced, and rhythmically composed, often developing through parallel clauses, qualifications, and semicolons. The syntax suits a narrator who thinks by elaboration and comparison. At moments of emotional crisis, however, the prose becomes shorter and more repetitive. The account of the child’s drowning uses simple declarations and recurring phrases—“blow upon blow”—to convey shock and remorse. This variation in sentence rhythm allows the narrative to shift from leisurely meditation to concentrated tragedy without abandoning its formal tone.

Imagery is abundant and closely tied to weather, light, vegetation, and the rhythms of village life. Mist is compared to eyelids after weeping; dawn roads emerge from shadow; the sun sits among the villagers like a kindly grandsire; devotion fills a house like incense in a shrine. Nature is not merely decorative but spiritually responsive, frequently mirroring states of mind. Light in particular functions as a recurring symbol of revelation, beauty, temptation, and divine presence. Flowers, food, feet, water, and the human voice likewise become sacramental objects through which the invisible is made tangible. Personification is common and gentle, giving the landscape a humane, almost sacred animation.

The narrative is framed as a series of encounters between the narrator and the unnamed Devotee. The first half proceeds episodically: she visits, speaks, worships, challenges him, and departs, while he reflects on the meaning of her actions. These scenes accumulate intimacy and philosophical depth rather than advancing through conventional external plot. The second half shifts into her extended first-person testimony, producing a story within the story. This embedded confession radically deepens her character, revealing that her religious wandering arose from maternal guilt, bereavement, betrayed reverence, and a refusal to live amid falsehood. The transition is marked typographically by the intrusive “II.”, reinforcing the two-part construction.

Dialogue is stylised, direct, and aphoristic. The Devotee’s speech is emotionally intense yet logically incisive. She answers theological abstractions with personal spiritual truth, as when she distinguishes between a God who may be everywhere in doctrine and the God she can actually find and worship. The narrator’s replies tend to be ironic or speculative; hers are immediate, embodied, and absolute. Their exchanges therefore function as compact philosophical debates, but remain vivid because they arise from concrete matters such as leftovers, caste pollution, flowers, servants, and covered feet.

Characterisation depends more on gesture, speech, and spiritual attitude than on extensive physical description. The Devotee is defined by her penetrating eyes, bowed body, fearless manner, repeated acts of prostration, and insistence on transforming common things into offerings. Her namelessness gives her an archetypal quality, though the specificity of her personal history prevents her from becoming merely symbolic. The husband is portrayed through silence, simplicity, endurance, and reverence; the Guru through beauty, authority, and a single devastating breach of trust. The narrator remains both participant and interpretive witness, receiving the Devotee’s worship while repeatedly clarifying that she venerates not his individual self but the divine presence she perceives through him.

The tone moves from satirical and pastoral to mystical, confessional, and tragic. Humour appears in the narrator’s comments about critics, editors, servants, socks, and flowers arranged like punished schoolboys. This humour prevents the devotional material from becoming uniformly solemn. As the Devotee recounts her past, the levity recedes, and the tone acquires the gravity of penance and renunciation. Even then, emotion is controlled rather than melodramatic: suffering is conveyed through recurring images, restrained dialogue, silence, and remembered physical gestures.

The story’s central stylistic principle is the embodiment of spiritual truth. Ideas are rarely left as abstractions; they are tested through touch, hunger, shame, work, bereavement, social exclusion, and sensory perception. The Devotee finds God in food received as alms, in discarded flowers, in a human voice, in a child, in a husband’s feet, and in the narrator’s physical presence. The prose repeatedly contrasts this concrete devotion with institutional religion, caste privilege, scriptural learning, and polished moral instruction. Its overall effect is meditative and humane: a quiet rural narrative gradually becomes an exploration of how love turns into worship, how worship can be corrupted, and how grief may drive a person toward uncompromising spiritual truth.
