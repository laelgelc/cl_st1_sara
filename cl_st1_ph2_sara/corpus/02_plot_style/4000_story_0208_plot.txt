On a cold, stormy day, Lizzie, a poor orphan working as a milliner’s errand girl, delivers a hat to wealthy young Belle. While waiting in Belle’s home, Lizzie is tempted to steal a rose so she can copy it and learn to make artificial flowers. Belle treats the soaked, shabby girl kindly, giving her boots, food, and flowers. Overcome by this compassion, Lizzie confesses the theft. Belle forgives her and encourages her ambition.

Soon afterward, Lizzie slips on icy steps, ruins an expensive bonnet, and is badly injured. Her employer dismisses her, and the accident leaves her permanently lame. After a difficult recovery, she learns to support herself by making artificial flowers.

Five years later, Belle urgently needs a wreath of forget-me-nots for her first ball but cannot find one anywhere. She is directed to Lizzie, whom she initially fails to recognize. Lizzie remembers Belle’s kindness, explains how it helped keep her honest through later hardships, and promises to create the wreath in time. She works through the night and delivers a beautiful garland just before the ball.

Moved by Lizzie’s gratitude and devotion, Belle offers to help her. Lizzie asks only to make the flowers for Belle’s wedding, and Belle agrees. Their reunion grows into a lasting friendship that brightens Lizzie’s life and teaches Belle the enduring power of small acts of sympathy and kindness.
