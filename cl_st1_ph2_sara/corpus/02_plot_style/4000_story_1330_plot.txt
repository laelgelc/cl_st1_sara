A baseball manager’s promising Worcester team collapses after injuries, suspensions, poor pitching, and a star hitter’s slump. The team falls to fifth place, threatening the pennant, the manager’s business prospects, and his dream of buying a cottage for the woman he loves. Desperate, he challenges his players to stop quitting and searches for a new pitcher.

He discovers Whitaker Hurtle, an awkward but extraordinarily gifted country pitcher with tremendous speed, varied breaking pitches, and remarkable control. The manager immediately signs him, but Worcester’s players ridicule the newcomer and nickname him “Rube.” In his first professional game, stage fright makes Rube disastrously wild, and league-leading Buffalo scores eight runs in the opening inning.

Following advice that anger improves Rube’s performance, the team captain taunts him and calls him “Pogie.” Enraged, Rube vows to prove himself. At the same time, Worcester’s star hitter breaks his slump with a huge home run, reviving the team’s confidence. Rube then becomes nearly unhittable, overpowering Buffalo while Worcester steadily rallies.

In the ninth inning, Rube begins the decisive rally with a hit and scores as Worcester completes a comeback victory. After surrendering only one scratch hit following his terrible first inning, he finishes with fourteen strikeouts. His performance restores the team’s spirit, renews the fans’ enthusiasm, and revives the manager’s hopes for the pennant and his future. Still furious over the insults that awakened him, Rube ends by threatening to fight the manager as well.
