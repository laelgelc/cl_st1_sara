On Christmas Eve, two children insist that their father tell them a story before bed. He invents a tale about an orphaned Pony Engine raised by its injured mother at a Boston depot. Ambitious to become a passenger locomotive, the little engine ignores its mother’s warnings and challenges the Pacific Express.

The Pony Engine races westward ahead of the Express, growing increasingly frightened as it passes through a tunnel, crosses the country, leaps the Mississippi, flees wolves and a monstrous locomotive nightmare, and bounds over the Sierra Nevada. Despite its efforts, the Express steadily gains. Near San Francisco, the terrified Pony Engine crashes through the depot, plunges into the Pacific Ocean, and disappears.

The father declares the moral to be that children should obey their parents, adding that the mother engine suffers a breakdown from grief. The children question the story’s Christmas connection and impossible timeline, then chase their father when he gives evasive answers. Later, the boy proudly notes that, unlike his sister, he did not cry.
