Uncle Peter, intrigued by a fierce dispute among nature writers, attempts to contribute as an unnoticed peacemaker by composing a sentimental parody. His story follows Little Red Tom, a young tomato growing under the protective care of his mother plant. She teaches him first to grow large, then to ripen red, and warns him of predators and gardeners, giving him signals to droop from birds and snuggle close when humans approach.

Tom successfully escapes a hungry robin and survives when the gardener harvests his brothers. However, his mother postpones teaching him a third danger signal. That evening, a young woman enters the garden, spots the ripe tomato, tears him from the vine, and uses him in a salad. She and her family eat Tom alongside other plants and animals without considering their suffering. The parody’s unfinished moral satirizes excessively sentimental nature writing that attributes human thoughts and emotions to plants and animals.
