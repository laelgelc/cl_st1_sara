An elderly narrator inherits an old New York house whose faded peacock-and-rose wallpaper reminds him of its former owner, Jimmy Rose. Once wealthy, handsome, generous, and celebrated for lavish entertainments, Jimmy loses his fortune through business failures, extravagance, and the wreck of two ships. Abandoned and condemned by former friends, he hides in the empty house, half-mad and fearful, even threatening the narrator when he tries to enter.

Twenty-five years later, the narrator finds Jimmy living in extreme poverty but restored to sanity. Sustaining himself on a tiny income and meals obtained from old acquaintances, Jimmy retains his courtly manners, cheerful smile, rosy cheeks, and habit of complimenting women despite the humiliation and indifference he endures. During his final illness, a compassionate young woman cares for him in his attic and closes his eyes when he dies. Sitting in the old parlor, the narrator compares the wallpaper’s faded peacocks to Jimmy’s ruined grandeur and its enduring roses to the kindness, dignity, and warmth that survived his fall.
