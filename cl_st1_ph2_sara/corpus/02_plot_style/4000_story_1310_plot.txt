An eighteen-year-old stranger arrives in a town penniless, hungry, and homeless. Wandering through the deserted wharf district on a cold, rainy autumn evening, he meets Natasha, a bruised young woman digging beneath a market stall in search of food. Together they break into the stall, find a loaf of bread, and take shelter beneath an overturned boat.

As they endure the freezing night, Natasha recounts how her abusive lover, Pashka, beat her, destroyed her clothes, took her money, and betrayed her with another woman, leaving her unable to return to the brothel where she lives and works. Although she bitterly condemns men and feels that death may be preferable to her miserable life, she notices that the narrator is suffering from the cold. She embraces him to warm him, speaks to him tenderly, comforts him as he weeps, and promises to help him find work.

At dawn they return to town and part as friends. The narrator never sees Natasha again despite searching for her for six months, but he remains profoundly affected by the selfless kindness of a battered, outcast woman whom society—and, until then, he himself—had considered insignificant.
