The story uses an assured third-person omniscient narrator who combines psychological access with dry, judgmental commentary. The narration immediately defines Jacob Kent through his ruling vice—cupidity—and treats his greed, paranoia, superstition, and sleepwalking as interlocking mechanisms in a fatal character study. Although the narrator can enter Kent’s distorted reasoning, the perspective remains detached enough to expose its absurdity. Occasional authorial asides, generalizations about human nature, and mock-scholarly explanations give the voice a worldly, faintly sardonic authority.

The prose is formal, expansive, and lexically rich, often drawing on Latinate or elevated vocabulary such as “somnambulic propensities,” “astral projection,” and “coign of contemplation.” Long, carefully subordinated sentences explain motives and causal sequences, while shorter declarations sharpen moments of danger or revelation. Lists, parallel constructions, balanced clauses, and escalating repetitions lend the narration rhetorical weight. Historical spellings, compound forms, and frequent dashes reinforce the period texture. The narrative also favors exact practical detail—weights, distances, tools, knots, weapons, trail conditions, and methods of calculation—which makes the frontier setting tangible and prepares the final mechanical twist.

Figurative language is vivid, muscular, and often ironic. Kent is compared to a predatory spider waiting for flies, the frozen Yukon becomes a rough-breasted sea, dawn lays “steel-gray fingers” on the window, and gold assumes an almost erotic radiance. Such personification turns landscape and wealth into active forces. Biblical, mythological, commercial, and maritime references coexist: Mammon, heaven and hell, Cleopatra, Shylock, robber barons, and sailors’ rigging all enlarge a crude cabin dispute into a grotesque moral fable. Hyperbole is frequent, especially in descriptions of profanity, greed, and the cursed trail, but it is controlled by concrete physical observation.

Dialogue provides a strong counter-register to the polished narration. Jim Cardegee speaks in heavily phoneticized Cockney-inflected sailor dialect, with dropped initial consonants, altered vowels, nautical commands, comic threats, and exuberant profanity. His speech is repetitive, theatrical, and aggressively rhythmic, repeatedly demanding to know “wot’s the matter” or “wot’s it to you.” Kent’s speech is plainer and more clipped, becoming especially cold once he believes his gold has been stolen. The contrast between Cardegee’s noisy verbal performance and Kent’s increasingly methodical menace creates both comedy and suspense. Maritime language is transplanted into the Klondike environment, making Cardegee seem at once out of place and vividly self-possessed.

The pacing moves from broad exposition into progressively tighter dramatic action. The opening establishes Kent’s reputation, vice, hoard, dreams, and habits at length; Cardegee’s arrival then converts psychological anxiety into immediate conflict. Suspense is built through foreshadowing—the recurrent man with the gash, Kent’s somnambulism, the unsecured gold, the old shotgun—and through delayed explanation. The execution sequence slows almost to real time, measuring the sun’s shadow, the stretching rawhide, the approaching sled, and the fractional timing of Cardegee’s escape. This meticulous countdown culminates in a sudden reversal: the gun explodes because Kent himself, while sleepwalking, loaded his gold into its barrel.

Tonally, the story blends frontier realism, black comedy, grotesque farce, and fatalistic irony. Threats of hanging and shooting remain genuinely dangerous, yet the failed hanging, Cardegee’s inventive abuse, and Kent’s solemn misuse of nautical language repeatedly turn terror into comedy. The ending delivers poetic justice without sentimentality: Kent’s possessiveness causes him to hide the gold unconsciously, his paranoia makes him accuse an innocent man, and his own treasure destroys him. The dominant style is therefore robust and ironic—a richly narrated frontier tale in which close material detail, exaggerated characterization, dialect comedy, and carefully engineered foreshadowing combine to make moral retribution feel both inevitable and absurd.
