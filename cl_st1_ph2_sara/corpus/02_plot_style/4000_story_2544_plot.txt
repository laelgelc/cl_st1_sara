Two fish patrolmen arrest Greek fishermen for using an illegal net, then unexpectedly encounter two Italians carrying prohibited sturgeon gear. The Italians flee in a skiff and repeatedly circle a large English ship, narrowly evading the patrolmen’s faster but less maneuverable sailboat. After one patrolman grapples their skiff, an Italian cuts the rope and knocks him unconscious with an oar. The fugitives eventually escape aboard the English ship with help from its crew, whose captain refuses to surrender them.

Determined to capture the men without creating an international dispute, the patrolmen begin a siege. For two weeks they watch the ship day and night, using the geometry of the ship, shore, and nearby wharf to prevent the Italians from reaching land. Friends of the fugitives attempt diversions and relay signals from shore, while a patrolman’s ruse to board the ship also fails. Neither side can gain an advantage.

The stalemate ends when an experimental high-speed yacht arrives at the wharf. With its owner’s cooperation, the patrolmen pretend not to notice when the Italians finally row toward shore. Once the fugitives are committed too far to turn back, the patrolmen board the yacht, race across the water at extraordinary speed, cut them off, and arrest them. One patrolman credits their success to the inventive imagination embodied in the yacht.
