Federal Sergeant Parker Adderson is captured while spying in Confederate uniform and brought before General Clavering. Certain that custom guarantees his execution at dawn, Adderson treats death with flippant wit, arguing that it should not be feared because the dead cannot experience loss or regret. His philosophical composure unsettles Clavering, who becomes increasingly preoccupied with his own mortality.

When Clavering unexpectedly orders the spy shot immediately, Adderson’s confidence collapses. Terrified, he begs for the delay he had assumed was his right, then seizes a knife and attacks. In the ensuing struggle, the provost-marshal is killed, Clavering is gravely wounded, and Adderson suffers a broken arm.

After regaining consciousness, Clavering repeats the execution order. Adderson, now incoherently pleading for his life, is shot by a firing squad. Moments later, Clavering calmly recognizes that he too is dying and passes away, displaying the serenity that Adderson had claimed but could not sustain when death became immediate.
