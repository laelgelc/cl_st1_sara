A lively but selfish young lamb resents sharing his mother with his twin sister and secretly wishes the farmer would take her to the bottle-fed pen. His mother warns him that selfishness leaves no room for true love, but he continues behaving rudely and impatiently.

When the flock is confined for shearing, the lambs are separated from their mothers. Frightened that he may never see his mother again, the brother regrets his cruel words and realizes how precious she is to him. After the sheep are shorn, their changed appearance makes it difficult for the lambs to recognize them. The brother finds his mother by her voice and walk, while his sister remains lost.

He briefly considers hiding their mother from his sister so she will be taken away, but remembers his own loneliness and chooses compassion instead. He leads his sister back to their mother, who praises him for finally becoming a comfort to her and teaches him that love grows stronger when it is shared.
