Four men enter the reputedly haunted Manton house to conduct a knife duel in complete darkness between Rosser and a hostile stranger, Robert Grossmith. Once the duelists are positioned, the seconds extinguish the light and leave. Rosser secretly follows them out, and the three flee toward town, abandoning Grossmith inside.

The quarrel began when Grossmith overheard Rosser and his friends discussing Mrs. Manton, whose husband had murdered her and their two children in the house ten years earlier before disappearing. After Rosser insulted him, Grossmith demanded a duel. Rosser’s friends recognized the stranger as the fugitive murderer Manton and devised the dark-room encounter as a cruel trick.

The next day, the sheriff, King, and Mrs. Manton’s brother inspect the house and find Manton dead in a corner, rigid with terror and apparently untouched. In the dust are three sets of bare footprints leading toward his body but not away: two belong to children, while the third belongs to a woman whose right foot lacks its middle toe—the distinguishing deformity of Manton’s murdered wife, Gertrude.
