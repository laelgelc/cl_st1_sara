Twenty-six exploited bakery workers endure brutal conditions in a dark cellar. Their only joy is Tanya, a cheerful sixteen-year-old maid who visits each morning for biscuits. Starved of affection and dignity, the men idealize her as a pure, sacred figure and treat their daily gifts as offerings.

A handsome, boastful former soldier begins working in the bakery’s better white-bread shop and brags about his power over women. When the cellar baker challenges him to seduce Tanya, the soldier accepts. Though the workers insist she will resist him, they become consumed by curiosity and effectively turn her into the stake of a cruel contest.

After two weeks, the men secretly watch Tanya enter a cellar with the soldier and emerge radiant with happiness. Feeling that she has betrayed the ideal they imposed upon her, they surround her in the muddy yard and viciously shame her. Tanya gathers herself, calls them miserable prisoners, rascals, and rabble, and walks away with contempt. She never visits again, leaving the men trapped in the same cellar, now without the one source of light and human connection they possessed.
