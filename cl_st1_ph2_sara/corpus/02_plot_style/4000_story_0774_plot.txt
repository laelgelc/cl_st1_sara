Percy Bixby, an honest but poorly paid bookkeeper, lies about his salary to win Stella Brown from a wealthier rival. To finance their marriage and maintain the lifestyle she expects, he secretly takes a thousand dollars from his employer and conceals the shortage by shifting false entries among company accounts.

For five years Percy remains chained to his ledgers, forgoing vacations and enduring constant anxiety while repaying only part of the money. When an audit is announced, he finally confesses to Oliver Remsen, the compassionate young head of the company. Rather than prosecute him, Remsen lends Percy enough to restore the accounts and arranges to deduct repayment installments from his wages.

Percy then admits the truth to Stella and says they must reduce their spending. Instead of supporting him, she announces that she will leave him, return to work, and become secretary to his former rival, Charley Greengay, whose ambition and extravagance better suit her tastes. Six months later, separated from Stella and living modestly in a boarding house, Percy asks Remsen to double the deductions from his pay, since his expenses are now lower. Remsen realizes that although Percy’s financial wrongdoing can be repaired, the damage caused by his ill-matched marriage cannot.
