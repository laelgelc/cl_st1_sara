On Thanksgiving evening, widowed blacksmith John Inglefield sits with his family beside the hearth, grieving both his dead wife and his absent daughter Prudence, who left home in disgrace. Prudence unexpectedly returns and takes the empty chair once occupied by her mother. Though her father, missionary brother, twin sister Mary, and former admirer Robert greet her with mingled affection, shame, and unease, her familiar presence gradually revives the household’s former happiness.

For a few hours, Prudence seems restored to her innocent self, and the family nearly forgets her fall. But when the time for evening worship arrives, she abruptly puts on her cloak and leaves. Her face reveals the corrupting force of her life away from home, and even her father’s desperate command cannot persuade her to remain. A carriage carries her into the darkness, and that same night she appears among the dissolute performers at a nearby city theater. Her brief homecoming proves to have been a guilty soul’s temporary return to memories of innocence, before sin reclaims its hold on her.
