During the Klondike gold rush, spirited American traveler Molly Travis shelters with two British men, Tommy and Dick, while a violent storm makes the trail to Dawson impassable. Frustrated by their refusal to move their supplies, she accuses them of cowardice and declares that, if she were a man, she would brave the storm herself. Determined to shame them, she sets out alone to retrieve her clothes from a cache.

As they wait, Tommy tells Dick about his late Indigenous wife, Tilly. Years earlier, he rescued her from a forced marriage to Chief George by disrupting the wedding, pretending to use white-man “magic” to make her disappear, and escaping with her by boat. Tilly proved brave and devoted through seven difficult years before dying in childbirth, after asking Tommy to marry a white woman next time so he would no longer be ostracized as a “squaw-man.”

Molly eventually returns exhausted, carrying a heavy pack that contains pots and pans rather than the clothes she had intended to save, showing that the storm had overwhelmed and confused her. Tommy believes the ordeal has humbled her, but Dick argues that her sharp, defiant spirit—though troublesome—is also her greatest virtue and the source of courage shared by men and women alike.
