A jeweled, gold-covered statue overlooking a city weeps after witnessing the poverty and suffering hidden from him during his privileged life. A migrating Swallow shelters at the statue’s feet and reluctantly agrees to deliver the Prince’s ruby to a poor seamstress and her sick child. The bird then postpones his journey to Egypt again and again, carrying the Prince’s sapphire eyes to a starving playwright and a frightened match-girl.

Now blind, the Prince asks the Swallow to observe the city for him. After hearing of widespread hunger and misery, he has the bird strip away his gold leaf and distribute it among the poor. Although winter arrives, the Swallow remains out of love and loyalty. He eventually freezes to death after kissing the Prince, whose leaden heart breaks from grief.

Seeing that the statue is no longer beautiful, the Mayor and councillors have it torn down and melted, but its broken heart will not melt and is discarded beside the dead Swallow. An angel chooses the heart and the bird as the city’s two most precious things, and God rewards them with eternal life in Paradise.
