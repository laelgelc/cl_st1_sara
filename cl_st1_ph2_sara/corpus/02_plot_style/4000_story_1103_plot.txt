During the French Revolution, a man rescues a young aristocratic woman from a patrol by pretending she is a laundress’s daughter named Solange and securing Danton’s protection. Calling himself Albert, he helps her fugitive father escape to England under General Marceau’s protection. He then obtains a passport for Solange, but she chooses to remain in Paris because she has fallen in love with him.

For three months, the lovers live cautiously under assumed names while Albert conducts scientific experiments on the bodies of guillotine victims, hoping his research will help end capital punishment. Although Solange’s father urges her to join him, she and Albert plan to marry, despite the danger that revealing her aristocratic identity would bring.

After Marie Antoinette’s execution, the couple are overcome by foreboding. Solange returns to her teaching position, and a letter from her father appears to have been intercepted. Soon afterward, while Albert works alone in a cemetery laboratory during a storm, a sack of freshly severed heads is delivered. He hears Solange calling his assumed name from within it. Reaching inside, he discovers her still-warm head; her eyes briefly open and shed tears before closing forever. Solange had been identified through her father’s letter, arrested, condemned, and executed that same day.
