After forty years of marriage, Madame de Cadour persuades her former lover, Monsieur d’Apreval, to accompany her on a secret journey from Fécamp into the countryside. Decades earlier, she had borne their illegitimate son, whom they surrendered to Norman peasants to avoid scandal. She has never seen him since infancy and is now desperate to do so before she dies.

They locate Pierre Benedict’s farm and pretend to be travelers seeking milk. Madame de Cadour struggles to conceal her emotions while questioning the suspicious farmer’s wife. When Benedict finally appears, he is a coarse, exhausted peasant preoccupied with his cow and cider, and he pays no attention to the strangers. Overcome by the painful contrast between the son she imagined and the man before her, Madame de Cadour quickly leaves, accusing d’Apreval of having made their child into this. He defensively replies that he provided Benedict with a valuable farm.

They return in silence to her unsuspecting husband, who mocks his wife’s exhausting walk. To preserve their secret, d’Apreval ironically assures him that it was delightful.
