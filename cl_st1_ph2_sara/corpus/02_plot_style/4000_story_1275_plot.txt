Freckles McGrath, the sharp-witted State-house elevator boy, supports a reform bill opposed by railroad lobbyist Henry Ludlow. On the day of the decisive vote, Freckles overhears that wavering Senator Stacy will support the bill unless Ludlow reaches him first.

When Ludlow enters his elevator, Freckles pretends the car has malfunctioned and stages a terrifying runaway ascent. Near the top of the building, he persuades Ludlow to climb out, then leaves him trapped in the locked attic while the Senate passes the bill. Freckles later returns, but when Ludlow threatens him, he abandons him for another hour and releases him only after he calms down.

Ludlow complains to the Governor and demands Freckles’s dismissal. Freckles innocently claims that the elevator ran out of control, that he closed the shaft door from habit, and that he assumed Ludlow had taken the stairs down. Delighted by the boy’s clever intervention but maintaining official propriety, the Governor postpones disciplinary action and requests an apology. Freckles apologizes only “on behalf of the elevator.” The following week, he proudly wears an expensive signet ring sent by an unnamed friend, apparently as a reward for ensuring the reform bill’s passage.
