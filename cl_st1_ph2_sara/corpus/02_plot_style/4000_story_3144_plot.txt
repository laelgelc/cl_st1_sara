A stubborn Bay Colt resents advice, gnaws his manger, and repeatedly runs away from the pasture despite warnings. After the farmer fits him with a cumbersome device to prevent him from jumping fences, he recognizes his foolishness but later becomes defiant again when training begins.

While his obedient cousin, the Gray Colt, calmly accepts the bit and learns to pull, the Bay Colt fights the farmer, leaving his mouth sore and making his own life difficult. Seeing his cousin’s success leads him to reflect on his behavior. He stops cribbing, cooperates with his training, and soon works beside his mother at the plow. Proud of his straight furrows and his place among the working horses, he realizes that accepting guidance and useful responsibility is more satisfying than stubbornness and constant play.
