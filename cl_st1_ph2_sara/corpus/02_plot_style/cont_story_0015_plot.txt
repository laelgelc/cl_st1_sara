A high school senior remembers his father’s animated Anansi stories while struggling with the man’s sudden disappearance. His father leaves for Ghana, promising to return in two weeks, but remains out of contact for months, leaving the teenager to help support his sick mother and younger sister while their family faces financial hardship.

Working long shifts unloading trucks at a home improvement store, the narrator finds camaraderie with Cato, a former track star whose injury derailed his future, and Reese, an older coworker who encourages the narrator to attend college. Despite several acceptances, the narrator fears he must abandon his plans and remain at the store to support his family.

These experiences are interwoven with his father’s tale of Anansi outwitting Lion to save three rabbit children. The story echoes the narrator’s feelings about his unreliable father and his own forced responsibility. One week before graduation, the father unexpectedly appears at the store, three and a half months late. Though the narrator feels anger, pride, relief, and gratitude, he cannot express them. When his father offers him a ride home, he simply accepts.
