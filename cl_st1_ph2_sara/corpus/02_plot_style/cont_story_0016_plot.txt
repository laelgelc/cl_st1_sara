A married couple travels to Rome to celebrate the wife’s fiftieth birthday, returning her to a neighborhood she knew as a young woman. She hopes to show her husband a traditional religious procession she once encountered there, but while waiting in the crowded piazza, the sight of local families and children revives memories of their son—his first steps and his death at age twelve after running into the street. Overcome by grief, she leaves before the Virgin appears.

Back at their rented apartment, she becomes fixated on a locked room, imagining it as the bedroom their son might have occupied had they raised him in Rome. Her distress escalates until she demands that they abandon the apartment and perhaps the entire trip. Her husband tells her that nothing lies behind the door except the grief she already carries.

As she calls a hotel, a French door slams in the wind and shatters. The practical need to clean the glass and arrange repairs interrupts her spiral. The couple acknowledges that their son has been dead nearly as long as he lived, and she apologizes for missing the procession. She then decides they should stay, turning toward ordinary plans for the next morning and accepting that they can continue exploring Rome together despite their enduring loss.
