During the American Revolution, Quaker farmer and staunch Whig John Reynolds lives with his family in a fortified farmhouse near the Hudson Highlands. Because he supported efforts to bring the outlaw Claudius Smith to justice, Smith’s former associates seek revenge. Reynolds’s twelve-year-old daughter, Phebe, keeps watch for danger and warns the family when armed marauders approach. The household bars the doors, and Phebe’s mother thwarts an attempt to enter through the chimney by burning feathers, whose smoke drives the attackers away.

Months later, the gang returns at night disguised as American soldiers searching for deserters. Despite Phebe’s warning, Reynolds opens the door and is captured. The intruders torture him, hang him from a hook in the fireplace, and ransack the house. Twice Phebe cuts her father down, even after being brutally whipped for intervening. Believing Reynolds dead, the bandits lock the family inside and set fire to the house before fleeing with the horses.

Phebe escapes through a window, carries water from the barn trough, and extinguishes the flames. Discovering that her father is still alive, she follows her sick mother’s instructions to stop his bleeding and revive him, then runs before dawn to summon help. Neighbors and a surgeon arrive, while an armed party pursues the outlaws into the mountains, killing or eventually bringing most of them to fitting ends. Under Phebe’s devoted care, her badly wounded and disfigured father recovers and lives to old age, while she remains modest despite being celebrated for her extraordinary courage.
