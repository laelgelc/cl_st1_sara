Prince Stepan Kasatsky, a brilliant and fiercely proud Guards officer devoted to Emperor Nicholas I, abandons his career and engagement after learning that his fiancée had been the Emperor’s mistress. Wounded in both love and pride, he gives away his property and becomes a monk. Renamed Father Sergius, he pursues spiritual perfection with the same ambition that once drove his military and social success, but he continues to struggle with anger, vanity, doubt, and sexual desire.

After clashing with worldly church authorities, Sergius becomes a hermit. When a rich, beautiful divorcee attempts to seduce him as part of a wager, he cuts off a finger to master his desire. Shocked by his sacrifice, she reforms and enters a convent, while his apparent holiness makes him famous. Pilgrims seek his blessing and miraculous cures, but public reverence feeds the pride he hoped to conquer.

Years later, Sergius succumbs to a young woman who is brought to him for healing. Overwhelmed by shame, he abandons his hermitage and considers suicide. A dream directs him to seek out Praskovya, an ordinary woman he knew in youth. He discovers her living in poverty, patiently supporting her troubled family through unnoticed, selfless labor. Her humility reveals that his celebrated asceticism has largely been performed for human approval rather than for God.

Sergius becomes a nameless wanderer, begging, serving others, and concealing his former identity. Arrested for having no passport, he is exiled to Siberia, where he quietly works for a peasant family, teaches children, and cares for the sick. In obscurity and humble service, he finally approaches the genuine spiritual life he had failed to attain through fame and heroic self-denial.
