Tom Chuff, a drunken poacher who terrorizes and beats his wife and children, collapses one night and appears to die. While unconscious, he experiences a terrifying vision in which he awakens beside an open grave in Shackleton churchyard, encounters the spirit of his wicked father and a monstrous black dog, falls into a seemingly bottomless abyss, and is shown the horrors awaiting him after death. A stern heavenly gatekeeper finally releases him for one last chance, warning that if he returns to drunkenness and cruelty, he will be condemned forever.

Reviving unexpectedly, Tom begs forgiveness, summons the vicar, and vows to reform. He abandons drinking and poaching, works honestly, treats his family better, and asks the vicar to ensure that he is eventually buried far from the beech tree and grave seen in his vision. Over time, however, his fear fades. He gradually resumes poaching, drinking, and abusing his family, until his brokenhearted wife falls ill and dies.

Returning home drunk on the night of her funeral, Tom crosses Shackleton churchyard and finds himself amid the exact setting of his vision: the black sky, brilliant stars, ominous silence, beech tree, and open grave. A whistling figure approaches across the moor, and Tom answers its signal. The figure is his furious brother-in-law, who has been seeking him over his mistreatment of his wife. Panicking and recoiling, Tom falls headfirst into the open grave and breaks his neck. The grave had been dug for his wife, while Tom is later buried near the churchyard’s center as the vicar had promised. Thus, despite his attempt to avoid the fate foretold in his vision, the place he feared becomes the scene of his death.
