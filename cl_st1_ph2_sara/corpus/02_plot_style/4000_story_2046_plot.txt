A narrator recounts the comic disasters caused by his friend McQuae’s extraordinary absent-mindedness. McQuae arrives a day late for a dinner, then suddenly remembers he should already be leaving for Scotland. He invites several families to lunch on his houseboat but forgets the engagement and has almost no food aboard. While driving his fiancée to Richmond, he accidentally throws their companion Hallyard from the cart at Putney and fails to notice his absence.

Despite predictions that he could never remember enough details to marry, McQuae successfully weds a woman unlikely to tolerate his forgetfulness. On a trip to Scarborough, however, he leaves their new lodgings without noting the address or taking money. Unable to find his wife again, he spends the day knocking on doors, goes hungry, assaults a laughing policeman, and ends up drenched and despairing on the beach. The narrator rescues him, provides food and shelter, and consults a solicitor. After inquiries among the town’s lodging houses, McQuae is reunited with his wife, though he refuses to reveal exactly how she reacted.
