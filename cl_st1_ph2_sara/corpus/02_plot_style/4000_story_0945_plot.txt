An observer travels aboard a high-speed express from London’s Euston Station to Glasgow, much of the time riding in the locomotive cab. The train races through the English countryside as the narrator describes the engine’s power, the demanding labor of the fireman, the calm skill of the driver, the tunnels, stations, and superior construction of the British railway. After a brief stop and engine change at Crewe, it continues to Carlisle, where a Caledonian engine takes over. The express then crosses into Scotland at night, descends through the dark hills, passes the industrial outskirts of Glasgow, and arrives majestically at the crowded station after completing its four-hundred-mile journey.
