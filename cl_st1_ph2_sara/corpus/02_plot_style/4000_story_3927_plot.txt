Lumbermen Morrison and Daly acquire a remote tract of pine and hire the brutal but effective contractor James “Rough Red” Bourke and his unruly Irish crew to log it. To protect their interests, they send FitzPatrick, a quiet, uncompromising scaler who insists on honest measurements and proper cutting practices. His strict standards bring him into continual conflict with Bourke, especially when he refuses to count a valuable log that has been badly sawed.

On Christmas Day, the drunken crew moves the rejected log and threatens to kill FitzPatrick if he culls it again. Despite a warning from the camp cook, FitzPatrick goes to work, rejects the log once more, and is savagely beaten and left in the snow. The cook rescues him, and they return to find the sleeping crew’s cabin on fire. Bitterly, FitzPatrick initially refuses to help the men who nearly murdered him, but then rushes to extinguish the blaze. He saves them not out of forgiveness, he claims, but because Morrison and Daly still need the crew to finish logging the tract that winter.
