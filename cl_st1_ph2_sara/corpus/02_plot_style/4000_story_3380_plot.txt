Miss Mahala Brooks, an elderly woman who closely watches over her rural community, becomes concerned that the minister’s mischievous son, St. John “Johnny” Perry, has inherited a dishonest nature. After catching him taking herbs and apples without permission, she warns his father that the boy may be “light-fingered,” but the distracted minister fails to act.

Years later, Miss Mahala catches Johnny examining the money hidden in her Bible, intending to take it for his mother. Instead of merely scolding him, she makes him confront how theft and imprisonment would devastate his loving parents and little sister Peace. Johnny breaks down, promises never again to steal or lie, and reveals his desire to farm. Convinced of his sincere reform, Miss Mahala offers to send him to agricultural college and eventually give him her neglected farm. She also gives him some dimes, which he shares with his siblings.

When Johnny’s father discovers the children with the coins and hears of a nearby robbery, he remembers Miss Mahala’s old warning and fears that Johnny is a thief. To protect the reformed boy and spare the minister anguish, Miss Mahala denies ever having made the accusation, insisting that the minister dreamed their conversation. Though she has always prided herself on never lying, she deliberately sacrifices that moral certainty for Johnny’s future and his father’s peace of mind. Unable to pray afterward, she remains unrepentant, trusting that God will someday forgive the lie.
