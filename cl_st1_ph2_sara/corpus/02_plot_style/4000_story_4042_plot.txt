During the Roman persecutions under Emperor Diocletian, the Christian brothers Simplicius and Faustinus refuse to renounce their faith and are tortured, beheaded, and cast into the Tiber. Their sister Beatrix secretly recovers their bodies with the help of Lucina and buries them. After seven months in hiding, Beatrix is denounced and refuses to worship pagan idols. She is strangled in prison, and Lucina buries her beside her brothers.

The story then turns to Margaret Roper, the devoted daughter of Sir Thomas More. More enjoys a loving family life and a distinguished career under Henry VIII, but resigns as Lord Chancellor when he cannot support the king’s divorce from Katherine of Aragon or his rejection of papal authority. Despite accusations, threats, and opportunities to compromise, More follows his conscience and refuses to swear that the king is supreme head of the English Church. He is imprisoned in the Tower, sustained especially by Margaret’s visits and letters, then convicted of treason and executed.

More’s body is buried at Chelsea, but his head is displayed on London Bridge. Margaret courageously removes or arranges the recovery of the head and openly admits possessing it when questioned by the royal council. She is released without punishment, has the head embalmed, and keeps it for the rest of her life. Upon her death nine years later, it is buried with her in Canterbury.
