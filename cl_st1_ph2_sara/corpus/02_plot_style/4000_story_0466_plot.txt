A bargeman and his wife, Bess, raise their beloved daughter Mary aboard their barge. At seventeen, Mary becomes engaged to Bill Jarvis, a blacksmith’s son. On the eve of their wedding, her parents discover that the bank holding Mary’s inheritance has failed. Bill then abandons Mary at the altar and joins the army, unaware that she is pregnant. Her parents lovingly support her, and her father Tom vows vengeance against Bill but is persuaded not to swear it.

A year later, Bill, now a soldier fleeing after apparently killing a man in a fight, unknowingly seeks refuge on their barge. Despite his hatred, Tom hides him from pursuing soldiers, lies to protect him, and later learns that the injured man survived. He urges Bill to surrender and face the consequences. Tom’s mercy profoundly changes Bill.

After escaping serious punishment, Bill returns and offers to marry Mary, but she rejects him as a coward unworthy of her or their child. Later, gravely ill with typhoid, Bill summons Tom and Bess. He explains that Tom’s example inspired him to abandon drink, live honorably, and become a better man. Believing himself near death, he asks to marry Mary so he can make amends. Mary agrees, revealing that she has always loved him. Her love helps him recover, and they ultimately live happily together with their child.
