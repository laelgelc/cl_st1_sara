Around 180 B.C., the Syrian king Antiochus Epiphanes attempts to force Greek religion and customs upon Judea. He desecrates the Temple in Jerusalem, suppresses Jewish law, destroys sacred writings, and has faithful Jews tortured and killed.

When royal agents order the inhabitants of Modin to sacrifice to an idol, the elderly priest Mattathias refuses. After killing an apostate who approaches the altar and leading an attack on the Syrian officials, he flees into the hills with his five sons and other faithful Jews. Before dying, Mattathias charges his sons to defend their ancestral covenant, naming the mighty Judas as military leader and the wise Simon as counselor.

Judas Maccabeus gathers a small rebel force and defeats successive Syrian armies despite being greatly outnumbered. Through courage, faith, and clever tactics, he wins victories over Apollonius, Seron, Nicanor, Gorgias, and Lysias. He then retakes Jerusalem’s ruined Temple, purifies and rededicates it, and establishes an annual festival of remembrance.

War resumes under Antiochus’s successors. Judas’s brother Eleazar sacrifices himself while attacking a war elephant, but the Jews are forced back into Jerusalem. Political conflict among the Syrians briefly brings religious freedom, yet a new king soon invades and installs the accommodating high priest Alcimus, dividing the Jewish people and depriving Judas of Jerusalem.

Facing twenty thousand Syrians with only eight hundred loyal men, Judas refuses to retreat or dishonor the cause. He and his followers die fighting at Eleasah, but their sacrifice strengthens Jewish resistance. His brothers Jonathan and Simon continue his struggle, ultimately securing Jewish freedom, restoring the Temple, and ensuring the survival of their ancestral faith.
