A mentally exhausted man tours a futuristic, synthetic house in the city, where glass walls resemble doors, doors resemble walls, and nearly every object is made from a material pretending to be something else. A woman guides him through the house, calmly reciting its modern features, while its deceptive design intensifies his confusion and estrangement.

He compares himself to laboratory rats driven insane after being trained to jump through a marked door for food, only to have the door unexpectedly become solid. He sees his life as a series of similarly changed “doors”: religion, science, money, romantic love, and other promises once seemed to offer meaning but ultimately failed him. Remembering a poet destroyed by pursuing an unnameable ideal and a New Jersey homeowner who began tearing apart his property after suffering a breakdown, he fears that repeated frustration will lead him through convulsions and exhaustion into passive madness—or even a lobotomy.

At last, he cautiously approaches a glass door that opens automatically. Instead of rediscovering any former source of hope, he finds an escalator and descends to the street among other people. His disorientation remains: as he steps off, the ground seems to rise unnaturally to meet his foot.
