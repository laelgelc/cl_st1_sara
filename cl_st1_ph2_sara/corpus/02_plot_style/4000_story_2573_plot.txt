The narrator joins Kelly’s Army, a force of two thousand unemployed tramps traveling east from California toward Washington. After railroad companies thwart their attempts to seize or receive a train near Council Bluffs, the army marches across Iowa, supported and entertained by hospitable townspeople. In Des Moines, the exhausted men refuse to walk farther and consume thousands of donated meals, prompting the city to build them flatboats for a journey down the Des Moines River.

The narrator, known as “Sailor Jack,” joins nine enterprising men from Company L who race ahead of the main fleet, posing as the army’s advance boat and claiming the best donated provisions. General Kelly’s efforts to restrain them fail until mounted messengers cut off their supplies. The ten return, rebel against their captain, and form Company M with another boatload of men. Their two boats are chained together, but they turn this restriction into an advantage: by shifting everyone from one boat to the other, they can float over snags and shoals faster than the rest of the army. They repeatedly evade the police boats, secure the best food, and eventually carry Colonel Speed into Keokuk ahead of the fleet.

After traveling down the Mississippi toward Illinois and Missouri, the narrator begs successfully in Quincy. At Hannibal, his close-knit group breaks apart, and he deserts with the Boiler-Maker. His companions gradually separate while hopping freight and passenger trains, leaving the narrator to reach Chicago alone. Years later, he learns that the clever river-navigation technique his group believed it had invented had long been used by Chinese boatmen.
