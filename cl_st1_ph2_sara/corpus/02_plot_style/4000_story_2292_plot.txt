A once-celebrated actor, Kostromsky, returns to the provincial theatre where his career began to perform Hamlet. Though rumors abound that alcoholism and debauchery have ruined his talent, the performance sells out on the strength of his fame. Backstage, he drinks heavily while reassuring himself that his genius remains intact.

When the actress playing Ophelia suddenly falls ill, Yureva, a modest young student and devoted admirer of Kostromsky, agrees to replace her. Kostromsky begins the play brilliantly, but quickly exhausts his dwindling emotional powers. Meanwhile, Yureva gives an increasingly compelling performance, drawing the audience’s attention away from him. During Hamlet’s famous soliloquy, Kostromsky realizes with devastating clarity that the public is now indifferent to his acting and that his fame and talent are irretrievably fading.

Consumed by jealousy, he insults Yureva during the performance and later roughly pushes her aside after the audience enthusiastically recalls her. He then abandons the unfinished play, removes his makeup, and weeps alone as he confronts the ruin caused by arrogance, selfishness, and drink. Hearing Yureva crying backstage, he is overcome by remorse and compassion. He admits that he attacked her because her success forced him to recognize his own decline, acknowledges her genuine talent, and advises her to leave the stage as soon as she feels her artistic gift fading rather than wait for the public to reject her. He then walks away from the theatre, refusing even his payment and declaring his career finished.
