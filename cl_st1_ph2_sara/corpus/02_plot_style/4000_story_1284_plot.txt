In the final hour of his governorship, the elderly John Morrison reflects bitterly on a career spent as the weak figurehead of political boss Harvey Francis. Francis and his machine have deliberately left controversial convict-labor contracts unsigned, intending to force incoming reform governor Frank Leyman into a politically damaging decision.

Morrison initially considers signing the contracts to redeem his own compromised legacy, but rejects the act as selfish, last-minute repentance. Then, inspired by Leyman’s integrity and determined to give him a fair chance against the corrupt machine, Morrison finds a selfless reason to act. With only minutes left in office, he signs and seals the contracts, removing the trap set for his successor. He leaves for the inauguration knowing he may be condemned as a traitor by his former allies, but feeling genuine joy that his final official act has helped a man in whom he believes.
