A brilliant American chemist, Rodman, develops formulas that could make precious stones as cheaply and abundantly as glass, threatening to destroy the wealth of Asian jewel owners. While Rodman is in India, a huge Oriental named Lal Gupta appears, claiming to be a monk sent to serve and protect him. He brings Rodman a valuable bronze depicting a man carrying off a woman and warns him cryptically against yielding to bodily temptation.

At Rodman’s isolated home, Gupta later claims that the bronze becomes supernaturally enlarged and produces a living, naked woman. According to his testimony, Rodman yields to the apparition and burns his completed formulas, after which he is found dead on the hearth with nearly every bone crushed. Because no one could have entered the locked room, Gupta is judged insane rather than guilty of murder and is confined to an asylum.

Scotland Yard investigator Sir Henry Marquis exposes the truth by heating Gupta’s written report, revealing a hidden message announcing that both Rodman and his work have been destroyed and India’s treasures saved. Gupta is actually a highly skilled agent hired by Asian jewel merchants. He used the bronze and an elaborate supernatural story to conceal his scheme, induced Rodman to destroy the formulas, and then crushed him to death in his powerful arms.
