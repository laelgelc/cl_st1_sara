Thomas Olney, a weary philosopher vacationing with his family in the old seaport of Kingsport, becomes fascinated by an ancient house perched atop a seemingly inaccessible cliff. Ignoring local fears, he climbs the crag and meets its mysterious, ageless, black-bearded inhabitant, who welcomes him inside and recounts secrets of lost civilizations, sea gods, and primordial ages.

After the host refuses entry to a sinister unknown visitor, a second, friendly company arrives from the mist: Neptune, tritons, nereids, and Nodens, Lord of the Great Abyss. They carry Olney and his host away in a vast shell on a supernatural journey through the heavens. Olney returns to Kingsport the next day unable to remember the experience, but the Terrible Old Man believes that only part of him came back, while his true spirit remained in the house or the mist.

Thereafter, Olney lives a dutiful but joyless and unimaginative life. Meanwhile, laughter, music, brighter lights, thicker mists, and strange auroras begin to surround the cliff-top house, suggesting that his lost spirit has joined its otherworldly revels. Kingsport’s elders fear that young men, drawn by those joyful voices, will also climb to the house and return emptied of desire and will, swelling the supernatural chorus and perhaps inviting ancient gods—and darker entities—into the mortal world.
