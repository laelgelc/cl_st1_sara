Willard, a refined but physically slight traveler, journeys across the Arctic with Pierre “Feroce,” a powerful, reckless guide who mocks Willard’s belief that mental willpower matters more than physical strength. After repeated storms delay them, their sled dogs’ feet become badly injured, Pierre’s food cache is found stolen, and both men begin starving. Willard suffers a frozen foot, while Pierre becomes snow-blind.

A violent windstorm overtakes them in a mountain pass, leaving them exposed, freezing, and increasingly delirious. When they finally struggle toward a distant roadhouse, Pierre collapses and resigns himself to death. Willard buries him beneath the snow for protection and continues alone, driven by sheer determination. Nearly dead, he reaches the roadhouse and tells the occupants where Pierre lies before collapsing.

Both men are rescued and survive. During their recovery, Pierre—now convinced by their ordeal—boasts that educated men like himself and Willard are best able to endure hardship, ironically adopting the argument he had once ridiculed.
