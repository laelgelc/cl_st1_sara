Lady Muriel, an aristocrat who has spent years selling wartime secrets through the foreign agent Hecklemeir, finds herself penniless when that trade abruptly ends. Seeking a loan from her estranged uncle, biologist Bramwell Winton, she overhears him discussing a map and watercolor that reveal the location of mysterious creatures in an African lake—an apparent discovery that could bring fame and fortune.

Believing the documents lead to treasure, Muriel steals the watercolor from Winton’s safe. When he catches her, she instinctively seizes a pistol and accidentally shoots him dead. She escapes and offers the document to Hecklemeir for cash and a share of the supposed fortune. He immediately recognizes that the image depicts legendary aquatic elephants and dismisses the discovery as worthless to him.

As Muriel leaves, she learns that the gloves she dropped have been returned by a plainclothes detective. Remembering that she removed them while opening Winton’s safe—and that Scotland Yard records identifying laundry marks—she realizes the gloves may connect her directly to the murder.
