A professional Rochester baseball team reluctantly travels to Guelph, Canada, for a lucrative exhibition game during a scheduled day off. Expecting an easy victory over local amateurs, the players are surprised by the Canadians’ exceptional speed, powerful throwing, and brilliant fielding. Rochester remains scoreless while Guelph takes a 2–0 lead.

Desperate to secure his share of the gate receipts, manager Merritt secretly introduces the “rabbit,” an illegal rubber-centered baseball whose unpredictable, increasingly violent bounces make it nearly impossible to field. Rochester exploits it to score fourteen runs. However, the alert umpire insists that Guelph also be allowed to bat with the same ball.

The scheme backfires spectacularly. The powerful Canadians hammer the rabbit all over the field, injuring and humiliating the professionals as they erase Rochester’s lead. Merritt replaces the injured pitcher and tries unsuccessfully to substitute a normal ball, but the umpire catches him. Guelph ties the game, then wins after a line drive strikes Merritt and sends him writhing to the ground. Delighted by his poetic punishment, the Rochester players celebrate while Merritt angrily throws the rabbit into a swamp.
