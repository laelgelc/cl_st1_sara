A feared Bulgarian brigand joins Alexander Ipsilanti’s anti-Turkish revolt with his companions, motivated less by politics than by the prospect of plunder. After the insurgents are defeated near the Pruth, he is wounded but escapes into Russian-controlled Bessarabia, where he lives peacefully among other refugees.

At the request of the Turkish authorities, Russian police arrest him and extradite him to Jassy despite his plea that he has committed no crimes on Russian soil. As he is taken away in chains, he tearfully asks an official to protect his wife and child. The Pasha condemns him to be impaled, but while awaiting execution, he tells his seven guards that he buried a treasure outside the city. Hoping to claim it, they take him to the supposed location, remove his shackles, and eventually give him a dagger so he can help dig. He kills one guard, seizes pistols, and frightens the others into fleeing.

Free again, he resumes brigandage near Jassy and extorts five thousand levs from the governor by threatening to burn the city and kill him.
