Stranded alone at college on Christmas Eve after illness cancels a planned holiday visit with his roommate’s family, wealthy student Satherwaite sinks into self-pity. While tidying his room, he finds a notebook borrowed from Doyle, a hardworking student outside his affluent social circle, and decides to return it.

Satherwaite discovers Doyle hosting a modest Christmas party for six students unable to go home. Though his arrival initially makes everyone uncomfortable, he chooses to stay and gradually wins the group over with humor, humility, and his willingness to join their simple festivities. The men share memories, light a tiny tree, exchange inexpensive comic gifts, and ask Satherwaite to play Santa Claus. Touched when they improvise a gift for him—a cheap red memorandum book—he secretly adds a paper knife he has just bought and presents it to Doyle.

The evening ends with songs and Christmas greetings at midnight. Satherwaite leaves wearing their cranberry garland and carrying a borrowed old pipe, now accepted as a friend. A playful snowball exchange follows, and he walks away no longer lonely, having found genuine warmth and fellowship in an unexpected place.
