The wind recounts the downfall of the proud nobleman Waldemar Daa and his three daughters. After his wife dies, Waldemar squanders his fortune first by felling an ancient forest to build a warship that the king refuses to buy, and then through obsessive attempts to manufacture gold. He briefly believes he has succeeded, but drops and shatters the vessel containing his supposed treasure. With his estate mortgaged and his wealth exhausted, he and his daughters are evicted from Borreby Hall and reduced to living in a poor cottage.

Waldemar dies in obscurity. His eldest daughter, Ida, marries a peasant, deeply wounding her father’s aristocratic pride. The defiant Johanna disguises herself as a boy and works aboard a ship, but is blown overboard and drowned before her sex is discovered. The gentle Anna Dorothea survives for decades in extreme poverty, living in a ruined hovel preserved only because of a stork’s nest on its roof—a possible repayment for her childhood act of saving a stork’s nest. She eventually dies there alone, bringing the family’s long decline to an end as their graves and names are forgotten.
