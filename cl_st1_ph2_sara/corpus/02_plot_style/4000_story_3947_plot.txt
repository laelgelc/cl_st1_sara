Reggie Pepper recounts how his chronically forgetful friend, Bobbie Cardew, marries Mary Anthony, a strong-willed former nurse determined to reform him. After Bobbie forgets their first wedding anniversary and ruins Mary’s planned evening at the opera, his continued lapses strain their marriage.

Mary eventually leaves home, promising to return if Bobbie can wish her happy returns on her birthday. Although Reggie knows the date, Mary has made him promise not to reveal it, forcing Bobbie to work it out himself. Desperate to win her back, Bobbie searches his memory, consults astrology books, traces a theatre performance they attended, and finally checks an old cheque to determine that Mary’s birthday is May eighth. The ordeal awakens his powers of concentration and teaches him to take his wife’s feelings seriously.

When Reggie tells Mary that Bobbie has succeeded after weeks of anguish, she turns on him for allowing her husband to suffer rather than breaking his promise and supplying the answer. Bobbie and Mary reconcile, but both thereafter treat Reggie coldly, leaving him to rue the consequences of acting with good intentions.
