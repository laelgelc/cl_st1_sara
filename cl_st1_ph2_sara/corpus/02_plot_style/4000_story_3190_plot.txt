Ellison, a gifted and fortunate young man, inherits an immense fortune accumulated for a century by a distant ancestor. Believing happiness depends on outdoor activity, romantic love, freedom from ambition, and devotion to a spiritual purpose, he rejects conventional uses of wealth and dedicates himself to creating unprecedented beauty through landscape gardening.

Accompanied by his friend and supported by his devoted wife, Ellison spends four years searching for the ideal location near civilization but sufficiently secluded. He eventually finds a suitable estate and transforms it into an immense, carefully designed paradise. Its rivers, ravines, flowering hills, forests, architecture, and concealed mechanisms blend art with nature so perfectly that the domain seems fashioned by supernatural beings rather than humans.

By personally overseeing this lifelong artistic project, Ellison fulfills all his conditions for happiness and satisfies his consuming love of beauty. After his death, the estate becomes accessible to select visitors, and the narrator describes their wondrous journey through its seemingly enchanted landscape as testimony to Ellison’s achievement and unusually happy life.
