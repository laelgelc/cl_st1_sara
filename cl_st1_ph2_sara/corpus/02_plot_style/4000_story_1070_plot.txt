Osip Mihalitch Polzunkov recounts how he tried to blackmail his corrupt superior, Fedosey Nikolaitch, with incriminating documents after being pushed aside as a suitor for Fedosey’s daughter, Marya. Fedosey paid him fifteen hundred roubles, then manipulated his guilt and renewed his hopes of marrying Marya, whose cavalry-officer lover had abandoned her while she was pregnant.

Believing himself welcomed back into the family, Polzunkov jokingly submitted a resignation letter on April Fools’ Day. Fedosey pretended to accept the joke, encouraged the engagement, and burdened Polzunkov with repairing the department’s disordered accounts before an inspection. Feigning illness and financial distress, he then persuaded Polzunkov to return nearly all the blackmail money and to sign a report admitting he had taken the remaining fifty roubles in advance.

Fedosey subsequently used Polzunkov’s supposedly comic resignation as an official request, had him dismissed, ended the engagement, and revealed that he had bought a house from the employee whose financial shortfall he had claimed to cover. He returned Polzunkov’s gift to Marya with a note reading only “April 1st.” Though Polzunkov later intended to denounce Fedosey as a scoundrel to his face, he could not bring himself to do it.
