Widow Amelia Bowman tells her fiancé, Nathaniel Clark, that Charles Tucker, her youthful sweetheart, has resurfaced after twenty-five years. Tucker arrives claiming he remained single for Amelia’s sake and spent his adventurous life sustained by her memory. His charm and romantic stories captivate her, while Clark becomes increasingly jealous and unwelcome.

Tucker prolongs his visit, spends nearly every day with Amelia, and gradually displaces Clark in her affections. When Amelia delays their wedding and admits that her feelings have changed, Clark dramatically ends the engagement and leaves, apparently heartbroken. Amelia and Tucker then begin planning a future together.

However, Amelia discovers a newspaper advertisement in Tucker’s pocket showing that Clark had deliberately tracked him down. Tucker confesses that Clark wanted freedom from the engagement and recruited him to lure Amelia away, believing she was too attached to Clark to release him willingly. Tucker also reveals that Clark is interested in another woman.

Furious at having been manipulated and humiliated, Amelia visits Clark the next morning and forces a reconciliation. She publicly escorts the miserable Clark to have their marriage banns posted, while dismissing Tucker with a pointed threat that she might advertise for him again. Realizing he has lost Amelia and incurred Clark’s hatred, Tucker quickly leaves town.
