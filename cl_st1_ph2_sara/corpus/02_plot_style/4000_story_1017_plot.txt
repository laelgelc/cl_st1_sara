A journalist travels by steamer from London to Oldcastle to report on the second meeting of a grand scientific association. He records the voyage with inflated seriousness, turning cramped berths, drunken crewmen, seasickness, lost luggage, and the petty behavior of pompous professors into supposedly momentous events.

At Oldcastle, the town is overwhelmed by visiting intellectuals. The reporter sensationalizes a minor arrangement to have the local beadle keep boys away from the meeting, portraying it as an attack on public liberty. When the conference begins, its learned members present absurd proposals and discoveries: restoring dancing bears and organ-grinder monkeys at public expense, investigating whether bears’ grease makes young men behave like bears, exhibiting a dubious “Shakespeare” twig, promoting a mechanized pocket-picking device, and constructing an enclosed playground where reckless aristocrats can indulge in destructive behavior without troubling the public. Through the reporter’s credulity and the scholars’ solemn debates, the proceedings expose the vanity, triviality, and social irresponsibility hidden beneath claims of scientific and civic progress.
