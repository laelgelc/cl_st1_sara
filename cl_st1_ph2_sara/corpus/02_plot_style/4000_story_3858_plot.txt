Jane, a talkative young maid, becomes engaged to William, a respectable draper’s porter whose growing success fills her with pride. As he earns promotions and adopts increasingly gentlemanly clothes and manners, however, he begins to consider himself above her. He abandons Jane for an older milliner who plays the piano, despite Jane’s determined attempts to confront the couple and reclaim him.

Jane attends their wedding carrying a bag of old shoes, intending to pelt the newlyweds. She throws one boot at the bride but accidentally gives William a black eye, then returns home pretending not to care. Her bravado finally collapses, and she sobs over the happy future she had imagined. Comforted by her sympathetic mistress, she gradually loses her bitterness, and the possibility of a new romance hints that her heartbreak will heal.
