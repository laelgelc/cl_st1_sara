Gertie, a young department-store clerk from Beloit, lives alone in a shabby Chicago boarding house. Exhausted by the constant effort to remain attractive for her job and overwhelmed by loneliness, harassment, and homesickness, she breaks down crying one night. Her unseen neighbor, Gus, hears her through the thin partition and invites her to share a midnight snack on the front stoop.

They discover that both left small towns seeking better opportunities in Chicago, only to find city life expensive, alienating, and disappointing. Feeling an immediate connection, Gus impulsively proposes that they marry and share a small apartment while continuing to work. Gertie gently rejects him, explaining that she has decided to return to Beloit and marry a dependable man she once considered too dull. She now longs for the familiar community, home, and garden she left behind. Hurt, Gus abruptly ends their brief dawn meeting and returns to bed.
