In 1884, the crew of the steamer George Washington encounters an enormous, hideous sea serpent while sailing from Liverpool to New York. Initially terrified, they discover that the creature is timid and easily won over with bread, pork, and other provisions. The serpent follows the ship for several days, and the captain and mate devise a plan to lure it to New York, capture it with shark hooks and wire rope, and exhibit it for profit. To avoid frightening it away, the captain even forbids the use of the ship’s horn and bells.

The captain then insults Joe Cooper, an exceptionally ugly and sensitive crewman, accusing his face of frightening the serpent. Deeply offended, Joe pretends that the insult has driven him into a series of fits. On the night before the planned capture, he stages another fit while at the wheel and deliberately clutches the foghorn line, causing a tremendous blast. The timid serpent flees and never returns. The crew loses its chance to capture and prove the existence of the creature, all because the captain cruelly humiliated Joe.
