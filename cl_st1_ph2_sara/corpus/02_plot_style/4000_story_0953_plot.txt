William receives a shilling from his aunt and spends half on cheap green sweets and half on an afternoon at the cinema. Enthralled by films about criminals, romance, comedy, and a drunkard’s tragic child, he spends the rest of the day imitating their plots.

Pretending to flee imaginary pursuers, William charges into his father, who angrily throws his sweets into the garden. After recovering the now-dirty sweets, William shares them with Joan next door. Inspired by a romantic film, he adopts a husky, dramatic voice; when Joan assumes he is seriously ill, he encourages her belief that he has weak lungs. The sweets make her sick, and her mother later contacts William’s bewildered family.

William next misinterprets a conversation between his sister Ethel and Jack Morgan. Believing they secretly love each other but are too proud to admit it, he visits Jack, gives him a crushed rosebud supposedly from Ethel, claims she is wasting away with love, and summons him to a private meeting at seven. Jack arrives during the family’s dinner hour, causing an awkward interview with a mortified Ethel.

Finally, copying a painter he saw in a comedy, William uses a flame and penknife to strip paint from his bedroom door. His already irritable father punishes him and declares him insane, while William regards all the resulting disasters as unfair misunderstandings. Remembering the film about the drunkard and his forgiving child, William casts himself as the tragic victim and begins offering his father melodramatic forgiveness before wisely fleeing.
