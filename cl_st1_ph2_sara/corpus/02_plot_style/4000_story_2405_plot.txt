Terry Neil, a successful bone-setter and tenant on an Irish estate, is required to spend a night guarding an old castle believed to be haunted by the deceased squire. He brings whiskey and holy water and is joined by the elderly steward, Lawrence Connor, who soon falls asleep.

Left effectively alone, Terry watches the squire’s portrait come to life. The ghost steps out of the frame, drinks from Terry’s whiskey, and explains that in the afterlife he must carry cold water to thirsty souls in a hot place. His old broken leg makes the work painful, so he orders Terry to set it, threatening to break every bone in Terry’s body if he refuses.

As Terry pulls on the ghost’s leg, the squire reaches for another drink but accidentally takes the bottle of holy water. The moment it touches him, he screams and his leg comes off, throwing Terry across the room. At daybreak, Terry awakens holding the detached leg of an old chair while Lawrence remains asleep. Deeply shaken, Terry becomes devoted to confession and Mass, and the ghost is never seen walking in the castle again.
