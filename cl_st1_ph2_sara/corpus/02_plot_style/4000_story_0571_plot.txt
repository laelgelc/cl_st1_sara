After delivering cattle to Chicago, Montana cowboy “Bitter Root” Billings befriends Murdock, a striking tugboat fireman, and learns that labor leader Oily Heegan has paralyzed the riverfront with a violent strike. Murdock and an accomplice then rob Billings, while Heegan gives false testimony that results in Billings serving twenty-six days in the workhouse.

Bent on revenge, Billings answers an advertisement for nonunion labor and joins shipping magnate Badrich and two others in attempting to tow a vessel through the strikebound river. As mobs bombard the tug and its tow from bridges and docks, the tow’s crew abandons the wheel, threatening disaster. Billings spots Heegan directing the strikers, lassos him from the tug, and drags him into the river. Once aboard, Heegan is forced to work as a fireman under Billings’s rope-backed supervision.

The tug’s successful passage proves the boats can operate despite the strike. When it returns, Billings presents the exhausted Heegan to the waiting strikers and announces that their leader has “arbitrated” and the strike is over, making Billings a celebrated accidental labor arbitrator.
