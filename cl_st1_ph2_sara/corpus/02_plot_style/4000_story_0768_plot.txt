On Christmas Eve in Chicago, a starving, destitute twenty-four-year-old estranged from his family concludes that he has failed at every pursuit and contemplates suicide. Driven by hunger, he decides to become a thief. After instinctively returning a dropped package to a young woman, he slips into a wealthy home during a party and steals jewelry from an upstairs bedroom.

While searching the room, he discovers the silver cup he used as a child and realizes he has unknowingly entered his own family’s new home. His mother finds him, recognizes him immediately, and embraces him despite his ashamed confession that he came to rob the house. She insists he cannot steal what is already his and persuades his stern father to accept him back. After speaking privately with his son, the father agrees that he may stay.

His mother feeds him, comforts him, and refuses to demand an account of his years of wandering and failure. She tells him that love requires neither pardon nor explanation. Resting beside the fire in familiar surroundings, he feels safe for the first time in years and begins to believe that his ruined life may yet be redeemed.
