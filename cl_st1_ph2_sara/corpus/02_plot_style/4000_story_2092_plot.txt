A professional hunger artist once draws enormous crowds by fasting publicly in a cage. Though constantly watched, he is frustrated by suspicions that he cheats and by his impresario’s rule that every fast must end after forty days. Because fasting comes effortlessly to him, he longs to continue indefinitely and surpass every previous achievement, but each forced conclusion turns his art into a spectacle that leaves him misunderstood and dissatisfied.

As public interest in fasting fades, the artist leaves his impresario and joins a circus. His cage is placed near the animal stalls, where visitors mostly hurry past him on their way to see the beasts. Eventually the circus staff neglects him, stops recording the days, and forgets he is there. He finally achieves the limitless fast he always desired, but no one notices or appreciates it.

When a supervisor eventually discovers him dying beneath the straw, the artist confesses that he should never have been admired: he fasted only because he could never find food he liked. After his death, he is buried with the straw. A vigorous young panther replaces him in the cage, and its appetite, vitality, and apparent freedom immediately captivate the crowds.
