An elderly farm woman, Mrs. Peet, boards a train for the first time after being forced from her longtime home. Her nephew Isaiah has exploited her husband’s trust and her own exhausted confusion to gain possession of the farm, leaving the recently widowed woman with little money and no secure place to live. Proud and determined not to become a burden, she travels to Shrewsbury hoping to stay with her nieces and earn her keep through housework and childcare. During the journey, she mourns her lost home, worries about her reception, and carries a few household remnants and her old cat as her last links to her former life.

Months later, the narrator learns that Mrs. Peet’s move proved unexpectedly happy. Her nieces welcome her warmly, and she delights in Shrewsbury’s concerts, lectures, and other opportunities, even claiming that Isaiah ultimately did her a favor. Soon afterward, however, she suffers a sudden fatal stroke. Isaiah arranges her funeral while minimizing both the farm’s worth and his wrongdoing, leaving the narrator grieving that Mrs. Peet’s long life of hardship was followed by only a brief period of freedom and happiness.
