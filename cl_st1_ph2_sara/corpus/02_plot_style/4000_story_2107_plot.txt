Reggie Burke, the capable but unconventional manager of an Indian bank branch, clashes with Silas Riley, an arrogant, sickly accountant who considers himself indispensable. When Riley becomes terminally ill, Reggie learns that the bank’s directors have decided to dismiss him. To spare Riley the distress and preserve his hope, Reggie conceals the dismissal, intercepts his mail, invents and forges flattering letters from the directors, pays Riley’s salary from his own pocket, and pretends that a newly arrived replacement is merely a guest.

Despite Riley’s continued criticism and condescension, Reggie abandons his social life, manages the bank, and patiently cares for him. When the doctor advises providing a mental stimulus to prolong Riley’s life, Reggie fabricates a substantial salary increase, which briefly revives his spirits. Riley eventually realizes he is dying and passes away still believing that the directors value him and that he could have accomplished great things for the bank. Reggie is left regretting only that he did not deliver one final forged letter of praise soon enough to encourage Riley to live another day.
