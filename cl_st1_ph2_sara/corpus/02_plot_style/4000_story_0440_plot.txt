While King Francis I is imprisoned in Madrid by Emperor Charles V, he grows despondent, partly from being deprived of female companionship. The French court sends his sister, Queen Marguerite of Navarre, to comfort him, securing papal indulgence for any sins that might result.

Before she arrives, a poor Spanish captain hopes to win the king’s favor by proving that Spanish women are superior lovers. He wagers against the valuable manor of Ville-aux-Dames and first arranges an encounter with a passionate marchioness. Francis remains unimpressed, criticizing Spanish ardor as too frenzied. Granted another attempt, the captain sends a gentler, more alluring woman, who delights the king so thoroughly that Francis concedes the wager and awards him the estate. The captain then reveals that the woman was his own wife.

The captain becomes lord of Ville-aux-Dames, founding a French family descended from him. Queen Marguerite subsequently reaches Francis, who is ready to return to French customs.
