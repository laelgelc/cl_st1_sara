After opposing the appointment of a new Congregational minister, the stubborn Marcus Woodman vows never to enter the church and spends ten years sitting on its steps during services. His refusal makes him a public laughingstock and causes Esther Barney, the woman he loves, to break off their engagement.

Years later, Esther’s apprentice, Margy, quarrels with her fiancé, George, because he insists that his mother live with them after marriage. Margy eventually yields, and her willingness to compromise restores their engagement. Her example makes Esther reconsider her own inflexibility toward Marcus.

When Marcus’s mother dies, leaving him unable to care for himself, Esther visits him and offers to marry him despite his continuing compulsion to sit outside the church. They wed, and on their first Sunday as a married couple, Marcus again feels unable to pass his customary place on the steps. Esther declares that she will sit there beside him, even before the watching congregation. Moved by her loyalty and sacrifice, Marcus finally masters his stubborn will and enters the church with her, appearing to the congregation like a conqueror.
