A country gentleman builds a north-facing piazza to enjoy the distant mountains. From it, he repeatedly glimpses a mysterious spot high among the hills, illuminated by autumn sunlight, a rainbow, and flashes from what seems to be a cottage window. Imagining it as fairyland inhabited by a joyful maiden, he eventually journeys there.

After a difficult ride and climb through lonely woods and rough mountain paths, he reaches a decaying cottage and meets Marianna, an orphan living there with her younger brother, who spends his days and nights cutting wood and burning charcoal. Isolated, sleepless, and exhausted by monotonous sewing, Marianna has become intimately familiar with the passing shadows outside her window. The beautiful view offers her no comfort; instead, she longs to visit a distant white house that appears to her like a happy palace.

The narrator realizes that the idealized house she admires is his own farmhouse—the same place from which he had romanticized her bleak dwelling. Unable to reveal himself as the “happy” inhabitant she imagines, he returns home and abandons further quests for fairyland. Though his piazza’s scenery remains beautiful, Marianna’s weary face now haunts it, reminding him that distance and illusion conceal suffering and make each person imagine happiness resides elsewhere.
