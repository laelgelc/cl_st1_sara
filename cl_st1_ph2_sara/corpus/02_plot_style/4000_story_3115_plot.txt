During a reckless high-speed drive through the countryside, chauffeur Alfred tells his passenger about Henery, a motoring enthusiast who became driver to a wealthy, cautious, and shortsighted man known as John Bull. Sent to England to buy a modest car, Henery secretly acquires a ninety-horsepower racing machine, disguises it as an ordinary twenty-horsepower touring car, and alters its speedometer so that his employer believes they are traveling at half their actual speed.

Henery uses the car to outrun and torment every rival on the road while John Bull remains unaware of its true power. One day, Henery sees enormous wheel tracks and hears locals describe the vehicle ahead as having “three elephant-power.” Determined to catch it, he races dangerously down a mountain road. The tracks belong not to a motorcar but to a circus wagon carrying an elephant with sore feet, pulled and pushed by two other elephants.

Henery crashes the car’s hot radiator into the rear elephant. Enraged, the animal seizes him with its trunk and throws him over a cliff, killing him. John Bull mourns his supposedly steady, trustworthy chauffeur and advertises for someone to drive his “twenty-horsepower” car. Another driver, Scotty, takes the job and preserves the deception, enjoying the fastest car in the country while carefully avoiding circuses.
