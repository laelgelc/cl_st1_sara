On a train to Eastbourne, a narrator becomes fascinated by an unhappy-looking woman whose nervous twitch and guarded expression seem to conceal profound suffering. The narrator invents an elaborate life for her, imagining her as Minnie Marsh, an impoverished, guilt-ridden woman visiting a domineering sister-in-law and enduring humiliation, loneliness, religious fear, and awkward encounters with family and strangers.

When the train arrives, the narrator expects the imagined drama to culminate. Instead, the woman calmly meets a young man who is her son, overturning nearly everything the narrator has supposed. Shaken by this revelation, the narrator recognizes the impossibility of truly knowing other people, yet follows the mother and son into the city with renewed wonder at the mystery and vitality of human life.
