Gebhard Knopfschrank, a Pomeranian farm boy turned struggling artist in London, frequents a Soho restaurant and displays bizarre, unsold paintings of deserted London landmarks overrun by wild animals. The restaurant’s self-styled Bohemian critics cannot decide whether he is a genius or merely eccentric, but none is willing to risk buying his work.

As Knopfschrank falls into poverty, he longs to return home but cannot afford the journey or pay his debts. One evening he unexpectedly arrives in a celebratory mood, ordering an extravagant meal and repeatedly playing American patriotic music. Assuming a wealthy American has bought his masterpiece, several diners hastily purchase his sketches before his prices rise.

Knopfschrank then reveals that he has sold nothing. A rich American motorist crashed into his family’s herd of pigs in Pomerania and, being in a hurry, paid generous damages. His parents have sent him enough money to settle his debts and return home permanently. He plans to abandon art and burn his enormous unsold painting, leaving the diners embarrassed and resentful at having been tricked by their own assumptions.
