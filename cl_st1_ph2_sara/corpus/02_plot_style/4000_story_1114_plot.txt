M’sieu Fortier, an elderly violinist in the French Opera’s orchestra, lives modestly with Ma’am Jeanne and their white cat, Minesse. He treasures his exquisite violin and repeatedly refuses to sell it to Courcey, a wealthy admirer.

When new American management takes over the opera, Fortier is replaced by a younger orchestra. His declining skill as a cigar-maker leaves the household increasingly poor, yet he continues attending performances until he can no longer afford admission. Desperate and hungry, he finally sells his violin to Courcey for fifty dollars.

Fortier immediately feels as though he has sold his own child. Unable to eat, sleep, attend the opera, or use the money, he returns six days later and begs to undo the sale, declaring that he would rather starve than live without his instrument. Recognizing that the violin is merely a whim to him but a profound passion to Fortier, Courcey returns it and lets the old musician keep the money as well.
