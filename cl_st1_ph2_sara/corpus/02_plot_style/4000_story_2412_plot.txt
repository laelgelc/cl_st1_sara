In a future ruled by an industrial oligarchy, workers are kept enslaved and illiterate so they cannot challenge their masters. A clandestine storyteller urges laborers to learn to read, then recounts how the powerful Vanderwater family descended from a slave who betrayed his own class, crushed rebellions, and earned wealth and status for his descendants.

At Roger Vanderwater’s textile factory, known as Hell’s Bottom, overseers Clancy and Munster steal a worker relief fund and brutally suppress complaints. After Tom Dixon loses an arm in a factory accident and is denied aid, a literate worker—already dying from a beating—writes a petition detailing the abuses. Disguised as a farm laborer delivering prized strawberries to Vanderwater’s palace, he presents the fruit with Dixon’s severed arm laid among it, the petition clenched in its fingers.

Shocked into reading the workers’ grievances, Vanderwater investigates and confirms them. He spares the conspirators, punishes and mutilates the corrupt overseers, and temporarily restores honest management of the fund. The storyteller then reveals that the courageous messenger was his father, who secretly taught him to read before dying. Rather than become an overseer, he became a wandering storyteller who teaches slaves literacy, believing that knowledge of history and the printed word will empower them to abolish both slavery and mastery.
