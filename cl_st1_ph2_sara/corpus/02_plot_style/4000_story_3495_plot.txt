A young postmaster from Calcutta is assigned to the remote village of Ulapur, where he feels isolated and out of place. His only meaningful companion is Ratan, an orphan girl who performs household tasks for him. During their evenings together, they share memories of their families, and he begins teaching her to read. Ratan grows deeply attached to him, regarding him as an older brother and becoming devoted to his care.

When the postmaster falls ill during the rainy season, Ratan nurses him with maternal tenderness. After recovering, he decides the village is unbearable and requests a transfer. When the request is denied, he resigns and prepares to return home. Ratan asks him to take her with him, but he laughs off the idea. His offers to have his successor care for her and to leave her money only deepen her hurt, and she refuses both.

As the postmaster departs by boat, he briefly considers returning for Ratan but instead consoles himself with philosophical thoughts about life’s inevitable separations. Ratan, unable to detach herself so easily, remains near the post office in tears, clinging to the hope that he may return.
