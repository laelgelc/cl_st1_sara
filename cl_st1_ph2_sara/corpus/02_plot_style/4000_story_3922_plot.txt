Happy Peter, a gentle and trusting prospector, repeatedly discovers valuable mines in the American West, only to be cheated out of them by shrewd businessmen. He persists because he hopes to earn enough money to return to the Vermont woman who promised to wait for him.

After years of labor in Wyoming, Peter finally proves that four claims are immensely rich. Realizing from his gray hair that both he and his beloved have grown old, he becomes desperate to sell quickly and return home. He publicizes the discovery, triggering a rush of prospectors, but then fears that dishonest men will swindle him again. His anxiety grows into paranoia and mental collapse.

When a joking prospector pretends that a minor wording error invalidates Peter’s claim and says he will seize it, Peter takes the threat seriously and shoots him dead. At his trial, the killing is judged a brutal, unprovoked murder.
