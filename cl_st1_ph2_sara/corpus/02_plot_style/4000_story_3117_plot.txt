At the vast, understaffed Buckalong station, the stern manager Sandy M’Gregor distrusts drovers and horse traders. Yet when a fever-stricken, impoverished drover offers him a worn-out-looking pony for fifteen pounds so he can afford to return home, M’Gregor sympathizes with the man’s illness and buys the horse. Before leaving, the drover twice insists that the pony is “white when he’s wanted”—dependable when it matters.

At first the horse seems lazy, rough, and nearly worthless. But during a hunt for wild horses, he reveals extraordinary speed, endurance, agility, and instinct, outperforming every other mount and becoming celebrated throughout the district. About a year after the horse mysteriously disappears, a traveler reports seeing him far beyond the effective reach of the law, once again ridden by the scarred drover, who falsely claims to have bought him from Buckalong. The station hands realize that the recovered drover has returned and taken back his exceptional old companion, whom they are unlikely ever to see again.
