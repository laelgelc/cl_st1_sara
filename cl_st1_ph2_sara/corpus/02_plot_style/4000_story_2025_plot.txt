In 1690, Sir Matthew Fell of Castringham Hall testifies that Mrs Mothersole secretly cut twigs from an ancient ash tree and repeatedly transformed—or appeared to transform—into a hare. Convicted of witchcraft, she is hanged after ominously declaring, “There will be guests at the Hall.”

Soon afterward, Sir Matthew sees a many-legged creature climbing the ash. The next morning he is found dead and blackened in his bedroom beside the tree, with mysterious punctures on his chest. Over the following decades, livestock left outdoors at night also die inexplicably.

Years later, Sir Matthew’s grandson, Sir Richard, orders Mrs Mothersole’s grave disturbed while enlarging the church. Her coffin is found empty and burned. In 1754, Richard moves into the long-abandoned room where his grandfather died and decides to cut down the ash. After hearing unexplained scratching at his window, he too is found dead and blackened.

A cat investigating a hollow in the tree is dragged inside and killed. When the hollow is searched, a lantern falls and sets the ash ablaze, driving out enormous, hairy spiders—the “guests” responsible for the deaths. Beneath the burned tree, the investigators find their underground den and the preserved corpse of a woman dead for roughly fifty years, evidently Mrs Mothersole.
