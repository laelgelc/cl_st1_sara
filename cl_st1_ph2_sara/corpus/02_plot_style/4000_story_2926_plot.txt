A mother and daughter, Mary, spend two summers in a seaside cottage at Golden Gate, where they befriend Jesse Boyd, an elderly former sailor. Kindly, cheerful, and gifted at storytelling, Jesse shares vivid accounts of his adventurous life and shows them a roughly written “life-book” recording his voyages. Beneath his good humor lies lifelong grief for Margaret, his youthful love, who drifted out to sea in a dory fifty years earlier and was never found. Jesse believes he will reunite with her after death.

Mary recognizes the literary potential of Jesse’s memoirs and later introduces him to her writer cousin, Robert Kennedy. Enthralled by Jesse’s tales, Robert transforms the life-book into a novel, using the lost Margaret romance as its central thread and crediting Jesse as collaborator. The project gives the aging sailor renewed happiness and a reason to live until publication.

As Jesse’s health steadily fails, he waits eagerly for the finished book. When it finally arrives, he spends the night reading it. The next morning Mary finds him dead by the window, peacefully clasping the book to his heart. She imagines that he died at sunrise, as he had wished, and sailed spiritually beyond the Golden Gate to reunite with Margaret.
