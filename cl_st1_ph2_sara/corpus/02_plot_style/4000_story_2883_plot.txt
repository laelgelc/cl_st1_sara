Aboard a schooner sailing from Pensacola to Vera Cruz, passenger Samuel Warriner decides to celebrate Thanksgiving with a traditional plum pudding. He supplies the cook, Cornwallis Jones, with a recipe and brandy, but the pudding arrives decorated with rooster feathers instead of holly. When it is set alight, it erupts dangerously because Cornwallis has replaced the brandy with kerosene.

Suspecting that the cook drank the brandy, the captain and boatswain Prince frighten the superstitious Cornwallis by pretending that a nautical manual is a Bible prescribing drowning as the test and punishment for brandy thieves. Faced with being thrown overboard with a grindstone around his neck, Cornwallis confesses that he drank the liquor, blamed the devil for tempting him, and substituted kerosene so the pudding would still burn.

The crew laughs at his elaborate excuse. Warriner then conducts a mock funeral for the ruined pudding, solemnly throws it overboard, and marks its sinking with a blast from the foghorn before everyone returns to work.
