In an Icelandic settlement in North Dakota, the aged and clairvoyant Urda Bjarnason is revered for her wisdom and storytelling. She gathers the settlers around her and tells of Jon and Loa, two Icelandic children whose loving mother dies. Their new stepmother starves them, overworks them, and refuses to replace their ragged clothes or inadequate blankets.

After Loa says their mother would have woven cloth for them, the stepmother awakens at night to see the dead mother’s ghost weaving a beautiful blue-and-white fabric. The ghost wraps the cloth around her, terrifying and paralyzing her. The vision returns the following night, convincing the stepmother to change her ways. She spends her hoarded money on warm cloth and woolen blankets for the children and thereafter allows them comfort, songs, and stories, fearing another visitation from their mother’s spirit.
