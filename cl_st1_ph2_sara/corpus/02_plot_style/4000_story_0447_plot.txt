The lecherous Duke of Orleans becomes obsessed with Bertha, the beautiful and steadfastly virtuous wife of his loyal follower Raoul d’Hocquetonville. After she rejects him, he traps her in the queen’s chambers, but she threatens suicide rather than submit. Seeking revenge, the duke secretly confines her in a dark bedroom, invites Raoul and several companions to a drunken supper, and persuades Raoul that an exceptionally skilled courtesan awaits him nearby. Unable to reveal herself without risking disgrace, Bertha is forced into an encounter with her own husband, who remains unaware of her identity.

Humiliated and emotionally devastated, Bertha warns the duke that his scheme will cost them all dearly. Raoul later leaves the duke’s service for that of the Duke of Burgundy and, after learning the truth, kills the Duke of Orleans with a club. Bertha dies young within the year, while Raoul mourns her deeply. The tragedy presents the duke’s death as the eventual price of his cruelty and abuse of two honorable people.
