Arthur Kimberlin, a destitute young gentleman starving in rainy San Francisco, takes shelter beside a strangely pale man who invites him into a saloon. The stranger gives him money to buy absinthe, then persuades him to play dice. Although inexperienced, Kimberlin repeatedly wins as the stakes rise until he has amassed seventy-four thousand dollars. They wager everything on one final throw, and the stranger wins back the entire sum.

From a neighboring booth, Kimberlin overhears detectives discussing a notorious bank robber who recently stole $148,000, is an unbeatable dice cheat, has shaved off his beard, and suffers from heart disease. Kimberlin realizes this is his companion, who now sits unnaturally still, staring ahead and cold to the touch. Believing the man dead, Kimberlin takes the money and leaves. Suddenly exhilarated, he imagines buying an immense feast and feeding every hungry person in the city, but perversely delays eating as his thoughts become increasingly grandiose and hallucinatory.

The detectives later return and break into the locked booth. They find the stolen money still arranged on the table and both men seated across from each other, dead—the robber from his failing heart and Kimberlin from starvation and the effects of absinthe. Kimberlin’s apparent escape with the fortune was only a dying vision.
