An elderly woman named Isabel confesses to a clergyman that, as a young woman, she and her cousin Lilian both encountered Edgar Linley, an injured artist who took refuge at their uncle’s farm. Isabel secretly fell deeply in love with Edgar, but he and Lilian became engaged. When scandalous rumors led their uncle to banish Edgar and threaten to disinherit Lilian if she married him, Isabel’s jealousy intensified.

Left alone with Lilian one night, Isabel set fire to the farmhouse beneath Lilian’s bedroom, intending to kill her. Watching the house burn, she was overcome with remorse and rushed inside to save her cousin, suffering severe burns herself. She later learned that Lilian had already fled to marry Edgar and was unharmed. Everyone believed the fire accidental and regarded Isabel’s attempted rescue as an act of devotion.

Isabel concealed the truth for the rest of her life. After inheriting her uncle’s money, she devoted herself to helping the poor, yet remained tormented by guilt and uncertainty over whether even divine mercy could forgive her crime.
