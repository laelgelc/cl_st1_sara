In the oppressive heat of an Indian night, Captain Gadsby keeps vigil as his twenty-year-old wife, Minnie, lies delirious and apparently close to death after a severe illness. At first she cannot recognize him, instead wandering through confused memories and accusing him of cruelty. The doctor administers medicine to force a final rally, warning Gadsby that her words may be painful.

Minnie briefly regains clarity and realizes she is dying. Terrified, she begs her husband to save her, asks forgiveness for past quarrels, and makes him promise not to forget her. They struggle against the thought that their marriage vows end at death, insisting their love must continue beyond it. She then appears to die in his arms.

However, the ayah notices that Minnie has begun to sweat after wisely stopping the punkah. The doctor recognizes this as a possible breaking of the fever and announces that she now has a strong chance of survival. Overcome with relief, Gadsby breaks down while a chaplain rushes to fetch another physician.

Five weeks later, Minnie is recovering at breakfast. She remembers the terrible night and says the ordeal has brought her and Gadsby closer. Grateful to the ayah who helped save her, she decides to reward her, while she and her husband resume their affectionate domestic life.
