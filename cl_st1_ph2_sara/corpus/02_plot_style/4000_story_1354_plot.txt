A stray yellow dog named Bones lives at Rattlers Ridge, where the miners refuse to claim him yet boast of his intelligence and exploits. Mocked for his color, uncertain breed, and troublesome behavior, he shows a peculiar devotion to drunkards, even imitating the decline of the camp drunk, Uncle Billy, until the two are forcibly separated.

Bones’s life changes when Pinkey Preston, the beloved daughter of a county judge, visits the settlement. She treats him tenderly and calls him a “good dog,” inspiring his immediate devotion. He repeatedly follows her home, and the miners gradually become proud of the once-disreputable animal. After Bones memorably disrupts a church service because he mistakes the minister’s Bible for a clod about to be thrown at him, Judge Preston asks permission to keep him.

Bones settles into a comfortable, respectable life with Pinkey and her father, eventually ignoring his former companions when they visit. A year later, he dies outside Pinkey’s bedroom door. The miners offer to erect a gravestone, choosing as its inscription the words they believe transformed him: “Good Dog!”
