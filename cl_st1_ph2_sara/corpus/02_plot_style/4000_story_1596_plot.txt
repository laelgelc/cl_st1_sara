John Atwood, the American consul in the tropical town of Coralio, drinks heavily while trying—and failing—to forget Rosine, the woman who once rejected him. When a postmaster from his Alabama hometown asks whether a shoe store would prosper in Coralio, where almost everyone goes barefoot, John and his friend Billy Keogh jokingly reply that the town desperately needs one.

The letter is taken seriously. Rosine’s impractical father sells everything he owns, invests the money in shoes, and arrives in Coralio with Rosine and an enormous stock. Horrified by the consequences of his joke, John realizes he still loves her and fears she will despise him once she learns the truth. Billy rallies the few shoe-wearing foreigners to buy what they can, but their purchases cannot save the business.

After thinking all night and giving up liquor, John devises a scheme. He cables his former romantic rival, Pinkney Dawson, offering an inflated price for five hundred pounds of dry cockleburrs, intending to create an urgent local need for footwear among Coralio’s barefoot inhabitants and thereby rescue Rosine’s father from ruin.
