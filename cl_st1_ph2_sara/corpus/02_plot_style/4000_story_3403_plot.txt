A modestly successful writer, newly inspired by marriage, produces an exceptionally moving story that becomes a sensation. Instead of advancing his career, its success raises editors’ expectations so high that they reject all his later work as inferior. His income disappears, and he fears poverty.

A destitute former writer named Barbel reveals that he suffered the same fate after creating one extraordinarily popular joke. Reduced to grinding pinpoints, Barbel deliberately discards any unusually fine work lest it establish another impossible standard. His warning convinces the narrator of the danger posed by exceptional success.

A sympathetic editor suggests publishing the narrator’s ordinary stories under a pseudonym. The plan restores his income while preserving his celebrated name’s reputation. Later, the birth of his son inspires another period of emotional exaltation, and he writes a story equal to his earlier masterpiece. Terrified that publishing it will again ruin his livelihood, he seals the manuscript inside nested, locked metal boxes, throws away the key, and resolves that it may be released only after his death, when its success can no longer harm him.
