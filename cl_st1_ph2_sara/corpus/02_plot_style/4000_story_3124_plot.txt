After serving twenty years in prison, David Culross emerges emotionally broken and terrified by freedom. On the train to Chicago, he recalls his bleak youth as an isolated clerk and his love for Zoe Le Baron, whose wealthy father forbade him from seeing her. Devastated by the rejection, David entered a saloon, became drunk for the first time, and killed a man during a quarrel, resulting in his long sentence.

Early in his imprisonment, Zoe wrote that she loved him, blamed herself for his downfall, and intended to devote herself to securing his release. Wanting to spare her from wasting her life on him, David coldly claimed he had forgotten her, trusting that she would eventually despise him and find happiness elsewhere. He sustained himself through the years by imagining her living a joyful life.

Upon arriving in Chicago after his release, David feels utterly alone and directionless. At the station, however, Zoe—now middle-aged—meets him. She reveals that she never believed his rejecting letter and has waited for him throughout his imprisonment, then leads him home.
