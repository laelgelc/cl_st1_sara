Court poet Shekhar secretly idealizes Princess Ajita, whom he has never seen but imagines through a shadow and the sound of golden anklets behind the screened royal balcony. Though his playful friendship with the princess’s maid, Manjari, inspires gossip and songs, his deepest devotion remains fixed on the unseen princess. His simple, heartfelt poetry is beloved throughout the kingdom.

A renowned and arrogant poet named Pundarik arrives and challenges Shekhar to a poetic contest. Shekhar moves the audience with songs expressing loyalty, love, and spiritual longing, but Pundarik counters with dazzling wordplay, scriptural learning, and metaphysical analysis. The scholars and crowd mistake intellectual complexity for poetic superiority, and King Narayan awards Pundarik the victory.

Humiliated and convinced that his work is worthless, Shekhar returns home, burns his manuscripts, drinks poison, and prepares to die among flowers and lamps. Princess Ajita then comes to him in person, declares that he was the true victor, and crowns him with her own garland. Having finally seen the woman who inspired him and received her recognition, Shekhar dies.
