Herminia, a young Cajun woman, rides her aging pony, Ti Démon, through the pine woods to sell eggs and vegetables at a planter’s summer home. When the pony develops a painful limp, she ties him to a tree and continues on foot, partly hoping to attract the attention of young Prospere Labatier. Her visit proves humiliating: the wealthy guests barely notice her, and Prospere gives her only a passing greeting.

Meanwhile, Ti Démon’s pain subsides. He unties himself, struggles unsuccessfully to remove his saddle, and returns home, then wanders to a neighboring farm. Solistan, a young farmer, finds him disheveled with the saddle hanging beneath him and fears that Herminia has been injured. His frantic search makes him realize that he loves her. He finds her unharmed but distressed by the pony’s disappearance and brings her home on his horse.

Solistan offers Herminia a safer mare and, indirectly, proposes marriage by suggesting that she take both him and the horse. She postpones her answer but later marries him. Although Solistan promises to care for Ti Démon, the pony remains haunted by Solistan’s earlier joking suggestion that he should be shot. He eventually runs away toward the Indian Nation, where he was born, and is later found dead on a road in Texas. Herminia mourns him as a good and faithful horse.
