An observer describes the harsh, unprofitable world of Australian cattle raising, where droughts, floods, disease, ticks, and the animals’ unpredictable behavior repeatedly defeat optimistic investors. The account explores the distinctive habits of bullocks, cows, and calves, as well as the danger and skill involved in managing them. An anecdote contrasts boastful, inexperienced sheep-men—who throw a quiet herd into chaos—with seasoned Queensland drovers, who calmly separate the required cattle. The story concludes with a vivid cattle-camp scene in which a skilled stockman and his responsive horse expertly cut a white steer from the restless mob.
