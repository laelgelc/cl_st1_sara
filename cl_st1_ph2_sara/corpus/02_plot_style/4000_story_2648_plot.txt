In late Republican Hispania, a Roman quaestor urges the provincial authorities to protect Pompelo from ancient mountain people whose biannual rites are associated with disappearances and supernatural horrors. After several of the hill people’s traders are killed in town, ominous drumming begins, but no villagers vanish as usual—suggesting that the entire settlement may be marked for vengeance.

Despite military objections, the proconsul sends a cohort into the mountains on the eve of the autumn Sabbath, accompanied by the quaestor and other officials. As they climb toward the ritual fires, their horses scream, their terrified local guide kills himself, the air turns unnaturally cold, and enormous winged shapes blot out the stars. Monstrous beings appear against the flames while a sentient wind coils around the soldiers, leaving the cohort screaming in darkness. The proconsul recognizes the force as an ancient evil that has finally arrived.

The narrator then awakens, revealing the expedition to have been an extraordinarily vivid dream. No historical record explains the cohort’s fate, though Pompelo itself survives into modern times, implying that the soldiers’ destruction may have spared the town.
