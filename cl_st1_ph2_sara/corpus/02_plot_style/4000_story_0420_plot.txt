Orphaned and impoverished, young Dick Whittington travels to London after hearing fanciful tales that its streets are paved with gold. He finds only hunger and hardship, but a wealthy merchant, Mr. Fitzwarren, takes pity on him and gives him work in his household. Though tormented by the cruel cook and by rats in his attic, Dick buys a cat with his only penny, and the cat quickly clears away the vermin.

When Fitzwarren invites his servants to invest goods in an overseas trading voyage, Dick has nothing to offer except his cat. Reluctantly, he sends her aboard the merchant’s ship. Later, driven away by the cook’s abuse, Dick begins to leave London, but the bells of Bow Church seem to call him back and foretell that he will become Lord Mayor three times. Encouraged, he returns to his work.

The ship reaches a foreign kingdom plagued by rats and mice. Dick’s cat eliminates the pests, and the delighted king and queen pay an enormous fortune for her. When the ship returns, the honest Fitzwarren gives all the proceeds to Dick. Now wealthy, Dick generously rewards those around him, even the cruel cook. He later marries Fitzwarren’s daughter Alice, becomes a prominent merchant, serves as sheriff and three times as Lord Mayor of London, and is knighted by King Henry V.
