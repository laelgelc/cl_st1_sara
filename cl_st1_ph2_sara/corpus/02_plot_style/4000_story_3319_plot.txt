At a girls’ school, the senior class must quickly replace a canceled play for its traditional Friday celebration. Class president Maine organizes an informal costume ball and plans to dress as October using brilliant scarlet leaves she found nearby. Chicago, an unpopular newcomer, secretly overhears the committee disparaging her, including Maine’s accidental remark likening her to a snake. Seeking revenge, Chicago steals all the scarlet branches, forcing Maine to improvise a yellow-and-brown flower costume.

Chicago wears the stolen leaves herself as “Indian Summer,” ignoring a warning that they may be poison dogwood. During the ball, the leaves begin to irritate her skin, and she soon becomes severely swollen and ill. Although Chicago’s suffering results from her own spite and theft, Maine feels partly responsible because she failed to recognize the poisonous plant. She gives up the school’s climactic birthday festivities to sit in a darkened room with Chicago, comforting and entertaining her—an act of compassion that leaves Maine’s friends admiring her character.
