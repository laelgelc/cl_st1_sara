In a park, the narrator and his companion Zimina see a severely disabled young man. When the narrator questions whether an intellectually disabled person can truly be considered human, Zimina recounts an experience from his youth.

While in St. Petersburg taking the General Staff Academy examinations, Zimina visited a poor relative, Alexandra Ivanovna, and met her intellectually disabled adult son, Stepan. Though unable to speak beyond a few sounds, Stepan became deeply attached to Zimina. Zimina tried unsuccessfully to educate him, but soon failed his own examinations and found himself stranded, penniless, starving, ashamed, and contemplating suicide.

Unable to borrow money elsewhere, Zimina asked Alexandra Ivanovna for help, but she had nothing to spare. Stepan, who jealously guarded a small collection of coins, sensed Zimina’s distress and voluntarily offered him his treasured money. The unexpected act of compassion moved Zimina to tears and restored his desire to live. Ever since, he has regarded intellectually disabled people as fully human, capable of love and sympathy even when those qualities are hidden beneath their limitations.
