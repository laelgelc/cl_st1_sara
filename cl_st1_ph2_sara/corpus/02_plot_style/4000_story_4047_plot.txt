After years of Roman conquest, the Gallic tribes revolt under the young Arvernian chieftain Vercingetorix, who unites them in a final struggle for independence. He adopts a scorched-earth strategy to deprive Julius Caesar’s army of supplies, though he reluctantly spares Avaricum at its inhabitants’ request. The Romans capture and massacre the city, but Vercingetorix continues resisting and temporarily forces Caesar to retreat from Gergovia.

The Gauls are eventually trapped at Alesia, where Caesar constructs immense fortifications both to contain the defenders and repel a relief army. Despite coordinated attacks from inside and outside, the Gauls cannot break the Roman lines. With supplies exhausted and rescue defeated, Vercingetorix offers himself up so that his 80,000 followers may be spared. The Gauls surrender their weapons, and he is taken prisoner.

Vercingetorix remains captive for six years before being paraded in chains during Caesar’s triumph in Rome. After the celebration, he is taken to a prison beneath the Capitoline Hill and executed, his self-sacrifice standing in moral contrast to Caesar’s pursuit of conquest and glory.
