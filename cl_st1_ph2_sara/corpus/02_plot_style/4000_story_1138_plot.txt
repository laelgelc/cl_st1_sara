Aspiring writer Mary Louise Moss has left her teaching job in Michigan and spent nearly all her savings trying unsuccessfully to break into New York’s magazine world. While struggling to make a lifeless romantic hero convincing, she impulsively washes her hair and asks the janitor for a sunny place to dry it. He lets her onto the roof, where she brings parsley to evoke the scents of her country home.

There she encounters a rude, collarless man who questions her ambitions and quickly recognizes her as a struggling writer. Mary Louise admits that she may soon have to return to teaching, though she remains determined to succeed. The man advises her to stop imitating conventional love stories and instead write authentically about the domestic, small-town experiences she describes so vividly. He tells her to submit such work to the prestigious editor Cecil Reeves and confidently predicts that she will remain in New York.

Afterward, the janitor reveals that the stranger is Cecil Reeves himself, a reclusive writer and the owner of the building, suggesting that Mary Louise has unexpectedly gained the attention—and perhaps the opportunity—she needs.
