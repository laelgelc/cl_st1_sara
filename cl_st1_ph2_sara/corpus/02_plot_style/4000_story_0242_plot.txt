A 365-year-old oak overlooking the sea pities the Ephemera, tiny flies that live for only one day. One fly insists that its life is not melancholy because its countless joyful moments make it as full as the oak’s centuries; they merely measure time differently.

During its winter sleep at Christmastime, the oak dreams of summer and relives the people, animals, and events it has witnessed. In the dream it grows upward beyond the clouds toward light and glory, but longs for every tree, flower, creature, and beloved memory to rise with it. When they all join it, the oak feels its roots loosen and joyfully believes it is ascending with everything it loves.

At that same moment, a violent storm tears the oak from the ground. It falls after 365 years, its long existence now seeming as brief as the Ephemera’s single day. On Christmas morning, sailors mourn the loss of the tree that had served as their landmark, while a carol about redemption and eternal life accompanies the oak’s peaceful end.
