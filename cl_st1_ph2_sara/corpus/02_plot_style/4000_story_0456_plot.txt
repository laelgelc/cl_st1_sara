A cunning former laundress marries a wealthy older silk dyer. When Carandas, her husband’s deformed mechanician friend, persistently courts her, she humiliates him twice: first by luring him to her door on a freezing night, where he is drenched and falls into a sewer, and then by hiding him in a cramped linen chest throughout a night with her husband. Carandas leaves Tours cured of his desire but consumed by hatred.

Years later, he returns wealthy and discovers that the dyer’s wife is secretly involved with a handsome priest, who is likely the father of her two children. Seeking revenge, Carandas reveals the affair to the unsuspecting husband, gives him a supposedly poisoned sword, and leads him to catch the lovers together. As the enraged dyer prepares to kill them, his wife stops him by declaring that the priest is the father of his children. Stunned, the dyer drops the sword onto Carandas’s foot, and its poison kills the spiteful schemer instead.
