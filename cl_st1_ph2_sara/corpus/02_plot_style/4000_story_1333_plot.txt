Worcester baseball manager Frank Connelly discovers that his star pitcher, the shy country-bred Whit “Rube” Hurtle, is pitching poorly because he is in love with flirtatious fan Nan Brown, who has turned her attention to the handsome new pitcher Henderson. When Whit asks to leave the team, Connelly persuades him to stay by explaining that Worcester cannot win the pennant without him.

Connelly and his fiancée, Milly, try to bring Whit and Nan together, while Connelly releases Henderson. Their matchmaking briefly succeeds: inspired by Nan’s promise of a kiss, Whit pitches a shutout. But Whit follows Connelly’s reckless romantic advice, embraces Nan forcefully, and declares that she belongs to him. Offended by his behavior and his failure to apologize, Nan resumes seeing Henderson, who joins Providence. Whit’s heartbreak causes another losing streak.

Before Worcester plays Providence, Connelly convinces Nan that Whit is the better man. She sends Whit a message that she will choose the best pitcher based on the coming game. Whit pitches courageously against Henderson, but errors, bad luck, and remarkable defensive plays defeat Worcester despite a dramatic ninth-inning rally. Whit believes he has lost both the game and Nan. Afterward, however, Nan tells him that although he lost, he is still the better pitcher, revealing that she has chosen him after all.
