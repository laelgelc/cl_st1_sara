During a plague outbreak in medieval London, two drunken sailors, the tall, gaunt Legs and the short, stout Hugh Tarpaulin, flee an alehouse without paying. Pursued by the landlady, they climb a barricade into a quarantined, disease-ridden district and wander through its abandoned streets.

They enter an undertaker’s shop, where six grotesquely costumed drunkards are feasting among coffins, skulls, and a suspended skeleton. Their leader calls himself King Pest and presents the others as his royal family, claiming they are conducting solemn research into the city’s alcoholic treasures in service of Death. He punishes the sailors’ intrusion by ordering each to drink a gallon of strong liquor or be drowned in a barrel of ale.

Legs refuses, while Tarpaulin exposes the supposed king as a stage actor named Tim Hurlygurly. The enraged group cries treason, and the “queen” throws Tarpaulin into the ale barrel. Legs retaliates by knocking the king into the cellar, attacking the company with the hanging skeleton, and overturning the barrel to rescue his companion. The resulting flood wrecks the room, kills or sweeps away several members of the mock court, and throws the rest into chaos. Legs and Tarpaulin then escape toward their ship, each carrying off one of the women.
