While sailing aboard the Harvester, first mate Morgan recounts how he received the scar on his forehead as one of only two survivors of the ship St. Lawrence.

Years earlier, the St. Lawrence was loaded with coal under the supervision of a drunken second mate, Ike Summers, who failed to trim the cargo properly. Despite Morgan’s warning, Captain Fairley sailed from Nanaimo with his wife and seventeen-year-old daughter, Marion, aboard. Soon after departure, a sudden squall caught the ship under full sail. As the vessel heeled, its loosely stowed coal shifted, throwing it onto its side. The mainmast then broke through the deck, allowing water to flood the hull.

Before the crew could launch the lifeboats, the ship sank. Morgan was struck unconscious by wreckage, but the carpenter, Simms, rescued him and pulled him onto a fragment of the poop deck. Everyone else, including Captain Fairley and his family, perished. The following morning, an English ship bound from Tacoma to Hamburg rescued the two men.

Unable to return them to shore, the rescuing vessel carried Morgan and Simms on a 190-day voyage to Hamburg. Upon arrival, Morgan read in a maritime newspaper that the St. Lawrence had been posted as missing and was presumed to have sunk with everyone aboard, the world still unaware that two men had survived.
