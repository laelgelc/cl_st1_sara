Frances returns home from Liverpool heartsick after learning that Jimmy, the man she has loved for years despite his lukewarm interest, is engaged. While resting on the common with her practical younger sister Anne, she watches Anne capture and then abruptly kill a mole that bites her. The creature’s death shocks Frances but also hardens her, replacing her acute sadness with weary indifference.

The sisters later meet Tom Smedley, a local man who has long been willing to court Frances. Since Jimmy is no longer available, Frances decides she will accept Tom as a second choice. Their flirtatious but tense conversation centers on whether she is capable of killing moles, and Tom’s challenge becomes an implicit test of her willingness to enter his world and accept him.

The next day Frances deliberately hunts down and kills another mole, then brings its body to Tom as proof. Recognizing her gesture as an invitation, Tom asks her to go out with him and says they must tell her mother. Frances agrees without enthusiasm, yet feels a troubling pleasure in surrendering to this new relationship and in letting her former hopes die.
