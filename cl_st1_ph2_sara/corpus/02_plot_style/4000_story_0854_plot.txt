Loka, an impoverished mixed-race Indigenous girl who has fled an abusive, lawless life near Bayou Choctaw, is placed by a charitable women’s group with the hardworking Padue family. Madame Padue treats her as a servant and constantly criticizes her slow, indifferent manner, but Loka forms a deep bond with the family’s baby, Bibine.

Left alone with Bibine one day, Loka becomes overwhelmed by homesickness for the freedom of the woods and prepares to run away. Unable to abandon the baby, she instead takes him into the woods and later returns with him unharmed, after the family has spent hours fearing the worst. Madame Padue threatens to send her away, but Loka explains that her love for Bibine kept her from returning to her former life. Monsieur Padue defends her, arguing that she is not bad but has been treated too harshly, and insists that Bibine is the “guardian angel” who may help her build a better life.
