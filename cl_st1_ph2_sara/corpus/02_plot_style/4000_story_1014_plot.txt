On a bitterly cold, pitch-dark night, the narrator accompanies Thames Police officer Pea on a patrol of the London river. After an unsuccessful attempt to catch river thieves near Southwark Bridge, they travel to Waterloo Bridge and interview a veteran toll collector. He recounts incidents involving attempted and completed suicides, eccentric regular travelers, madmen, toll evaders, robberies, and violent assaults he has faced while performing his duties.

The narrator and Pea then rejoin the police galley and drift downstream. Pea explains how the Thames Police prevent crime through concealed patrol boats and describes the methods used by various river criminals, including thieves who raid sleeping ships, dockworkers who hide stolen goods in their clothing, smugglers, and dredgers who throw property overboard and retrieve it later.

Although the patrol catches no criminals, it concludes at the Thames Police station in Wapping. There the narrator inspects the charge books, equipment rooms, a cell holding a drunken man, and lifesaving supplies for drowning victims. Impressed by the force’s success in preventing crime, he parts from Pea and runs toward Tower Hill to warm himself.
