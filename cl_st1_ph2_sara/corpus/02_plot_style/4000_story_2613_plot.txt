After inheriting the papers of his great-uncle, Professor George Angell, a skeptical narrator discovers evidence of an ancient worldwide cult devoted to Cthulhu, a monstrous extraterrestrial being lying dormant beneath the sea. Angell had investigated a young sculptor whose dreams of a cyclopean city and a tentacled creature coincided with earthquakes, mass hysteria, and disturbing visions among artists around the world in 1925.

Angell’s notes also describe Inspector Legrasse’s 1907 raid on a murderous Louisiana cult. Its members worshipped the “Great Old Ones,” who had ruled Earth before humanity and communicated through dreams while awaiting the stars’ alignment. An identical cult and idol had previously been found among Greenland’s Inuit, revealing that the religion spanned cultures and centuries.

The narrator later connects these accounts to a maritime disaster in the South Pacific. Norwegian sailor Gustaf Johansen and his shipmates encountered the risen island-city of R’lyeh, accidentally opened an immense portal, and awakened Cthulhu. Most of the crew were killed or driven mad. Johansen escaped by ramming the creature with a ship, though it immediately began reforming as R’lyeh sank beneath the ocean again.

After Johansen dies mysteriously, the narrator concludes that both he and Angell were probably murdered by cultists protecting the secret. Now convinced that Cthulhu and the other ancient beings are real, he fears his own death and dreads the day when the stars align again, R’lyeh rises permanently, and humanity is destroyed or driven mad.
