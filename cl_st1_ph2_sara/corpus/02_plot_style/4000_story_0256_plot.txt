A young scholar visiting a scorching southern country becomes fascinated by a mysterious house where Poesy seems to dwell. Jokingly, he sends his shadow inside to investigate, but it does not return. A new shadow eventually grows, and the scholar goes home to write about truth, goodness, and beauty.

Years later, the original shadow returns as a wealthy, worldly man. It claims that its proximity to Poesy transformed it, after which it gained power by spying on people’s secrets and exploiting their fear of exposure. While the scholar remains poor and ignored, the shadow prospers. When the scholar becomes ill, the shadow takes him traveling, reversing their former roles and treating him as its servant.

At a resort, a sharp-sighted princess notices that the shadow appears to cast no shadow of its own. The shadow deceives her by presenting the scholar as its unusually humanlike shadow. Impressed by the scholar’s wisdom, she assumes his supposed master must be even wiser, falls in love with the shadow, and agrees to marry it.

The shadow offers the scholar wealth and comfort if he will permanently pretend to be a shadow. The scholar refuses and threatens to reveal the truth, so the shadow has him imprisoned and tells the princess that its “shadow” has gone mad and believes itself human. The princess orders the scholar quietly killed. She then marries the shadow while the true man dies unheard and forgotten.
