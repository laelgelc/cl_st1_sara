Subha, the mute youngest daughter of a Bengali family, grows up believing she is a burden and disgrace to her parents. Unable to communicate through speech, she finds companionship in nature, the family’s cows, and Pratap, an idle village boy beside whom she quietly sits while he fishes. As she matures, her parents become increasingly anxious about arranging her marriage and take her away from her beloved village to Calcutta.

There, they conceal her muteness from a prospective bridegroom, who mistakes her frightened tears for evidence of a tender heart and agrees to marry her. After a painful farewell to her animal companions and the river, Subha is married and taken west by her husband. Within ten days, her new family discovers that she cannot speak. Isolated among strangers who do not understand the language of her eyes, she is left grieving silently for the home and familiar beings she has lost.
