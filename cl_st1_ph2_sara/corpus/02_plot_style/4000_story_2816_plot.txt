A young Parisian woman enters a practical marriage with Henry de Parville and moves to his gloomy, freezing Norman château. Isolated, bored, and unable to endure the damp winters, she repeatedly asks her contented, insensitive husband to install a furnace or take her to Paris, but he dismisses her complaints.

Desperate to make him understand, she deliberately exposes herself barefoot and nearly naked to the snow. She contracts pneumonia, finally prompting Henry to install the furnace, but her lungs are permanently damaged. Sent to Cannes, she falls in love with its warmth, sunlight, and sea. Terrified of recovering and being returned to Normandy, she secretly exposes herself to cold whenever her health improves.

Now terminally ill, she sits beside the Mediterranean, calmly accepting death because it will spare her another Norman winter. After reading of snow in Paris, she returns home coughing, reads a letter from her husband, and takes ironic satisfaction in knowing that she succeeded in having her furnace installed, even though the illness she induced to obtain it is killing her.
