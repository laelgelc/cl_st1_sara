The story uses a brisk, omniscient third-person narration that combines comic detachment with occasional access to characters’ intentions. The narrator is conspicuously present as a stylistic intelligence, interpreting events through ironic comparisons, mock-heroic commentary, and knowing asides. Ingenious misconduct is described with amused admiration rather than moral severity, giving the tale the atmosphere of a genial confidence trick. Even the closing catalogue of sounds is shaped as a punch line, juxtaposing Keogh’s laughter, the newly shod footsteps, and the timeless wash of the sea.

The prose alternates between efficient narrative summary and elaborately ornamented comic description. Sentences are often balanced or cumulative, with lists used to convey abundance, escalation, and absurdity. The catalogue of shoes bought by Keogh, the procession of townspeople injured by the burrs, and the repeated descriptions of hopping, limping, and stumbling all create rhythm through accumulation. Elevated formulations such as warriors examining their arms, generals planning victory, and Satan sowing tares are applied to petty commercial deception. This mock-epic disproportion is one of the style’s central sources of humor.

The diction mixes formal literary English, American slang, commercial terminology, political language, and Spanish local color. Phrases such as “necessity for a demand,” “business proposition,” and “stock of cockleburrs” give the comedy a pseudo-economic logic, while colloquialisms such as “nothing doing,” “up your sleeve,” “whoop things up,” and “genteel graft” establish the characters as enterprising American adventurers. Spanish words and titles lend geographical texture, though their use is filtered through a period American perspective. The descriptions of local people rely on broad ethnic and physical types, reflecting the exoticizing and sometimes demeaning conventions of the era.

Dialogue is highly characterizing and frequently carries the wit. Johnny speaks with quick confidence and rationalizes trickery through the language of political economy. Keogh’s speech is more extravagant, metaphorical, and slang-heavy, presenting him as a born schemer with a taste for theatrical phrasing. Mr. Hemstetter’s measured commercial language makes him an effective straight man, while Pinkney Dawson’s practical misunderstanding drives the final reversal. Speech patterns distinguish social position and temperament without much psychological exposition.

Characterization is caricatural but vivid. People are defined by a dominant trait, verbal mannerism, physical emblem, or occupational role: Johnny is the ingenious manipulator, Keogh the philosophic grafter, Hemstetter the respectable merchant, and Dawson the literal-minded supplier. Minor figures are introduced through exaggerated names, costumes, ancestry, weight, or gestures. Dona Maria’s majestic emergence and humiliating crawl, and Don Ildefonso’s fall “like a crumpled cathedral,” exemplify the fusion of visual comedy and mock grandeur.

The plot is tightly constructed around a commercial problem, an ingenious solution, escalating consequences, and a final ironic complication. Early details about the lack of demand are converted into the governing joke: demand cannot be created directly, but the necessity for it can. The cockleburr scheme then unfolds with mechanical clarity through preparation, execution, public reaction, profit, confession, and repetition. After the apparent resolution, the arrival of two shiploads of burrs supplies an additional twist, retrospectively enlarging the scheme and giving Keogh a new opportunity. The ending favors comic openness over moral closure.

The overall tone is buoyant, mischievous, and satirical. Commerce, diplomacy, romance, and imperial adventure are all treated as varieties of performance and opportunism. Moral judgment remains deliberately light: the narrative recognizes the fraud but admires its invention, and Keogh’s insistence that he wants no “widows’ and orphans’ chips” gives the rogue a selective code of honor. The result is a fast-moving comic adventure whose signature features are verbal exuberance, mock-heroic analogy, colloquial dialogue, exotic setting, entrepreneurial trickery, and a neatly engineered final reversal.
