A crowded coachload of travelers journeys through freezing rain toward a remote wayside pub. On arrival, the landlord insists they remain quiet because his wife is gravely ill. He offers them only rum and milk, then claims the replacement horses are missing, forcing the passengers to pay for drinks and makeshift beds while they wait. The travelers even silence two poor men desperate to catch a train, believing their protests might disturb the sick woman.

After leaving, the passengers grow suspicious. Under questioning, the driver admits the landlord’s wife left him years ago, no woman lives at the pub, and the horses may never have been missing. Realizing the landlord likely fabricated the crisis to delay and profit from them—and that the driver may have shared in the scheme—the humiliated travelers continue to Dead Camel in bitter silence and mutual distrust.
