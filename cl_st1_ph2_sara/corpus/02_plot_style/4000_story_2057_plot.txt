Charles Seabohn and Mivanway Evans meet on the Cornish coast, fall quickly in love, and marry while still very young. Their isolated honeymoon and inexperienced married life expose their immaturity. Petty quarrels deepen, worsened by Charles’s resentment over Mivanway making him give up smoking. After Charles strikes her during an argument, their attempted reconciliation becomes another bitter dispute. He leaves for South Africa, and she returns to her family.

Charles’s ship sinks, and he is reported dead. Although rescued, he lets the report stand, believing Mivanway never loved him and would prefer her freedom. In South Africa he builds a successful career but gradually realizes that he still loves her and regrets his behavior. After dreams that convince him she has died, he returns to the Cornish cliffs where they first met.

Mivanway, still alive and still mourning Charles, has also returned to the area. When they encounter each other at twilight, each assumes the other is a ghost. For two evenings they silently observe one another, afraid to speak. On the third, they confess their enduring love, ask mutual forgiveness, and reveal how deeply they have suffered.

As Mivanway bends to bless the man she believes is Charles’s spirit, she notices his dropped pipe. His guilty attempt to hide it proves that he is physically alive. Overwhelmed by joy and absurdity, she laughs until she faints. Charles carries her home, where her sister initially mistakes him for a ghost as well. Once Mivanway recovers, Charles goes to her, leaving the bewildered sister to accept the impossible reunion.
