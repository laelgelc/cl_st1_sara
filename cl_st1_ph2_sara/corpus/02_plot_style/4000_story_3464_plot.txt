In 1776, the Ward family eagerly supports the American struggle for liberty while Captain Ward endures hardship at Valley Forge. Inspired by his sacrifice, every member of the household contributes clothing, blankets, and supplies to the army, even giving up treasured possessions. When her children ask what she has offered, Mrs. Ward replies that she has given everything—her husband and children—to the cause of freedom.

In 1850, George, a formerly enslaved man, lives peacefully in Boston with his wife and children, supporting them through honest labor. Despite having lived free for ten years, he is arrested under federal law as the claimed property of a Georgia enslaver. The courts return him to bondage, and he is publicly auctioned while politicians and other respectable citizens approve in the name of preserving the Union. The story contrasts the sacrifices made to establish American liberty with the nation’s later sacrifice of Black freedom and families to slavery.
