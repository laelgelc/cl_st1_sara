After dismissing a newspaper report of a bizarre accidental death as an obvious hoax, a drunken, self-satisfied skeptic is visited by the Angel of the Odd, a barrel-shaped being who claims responsibility for humanity’s improbable mishaps. The narrator mocks and insults him, prompting the angel to threaten revenge.

The narrator then oversleeps and misses an appointment to renew his home insurance because a raisin stem has stopped his damaged clock. That night, a rat carries away his candle and sets the uninsured house on fire. During his escape, a hog knocks over the rescue ladder, breaking his arm. Further absurd accidents ruin two prospective marriages: his wig becomes entangled in a widow’s hair, and a speck in his eye causes him to ignore another fiancée.

Driven to despair, he attempts suicide, but a drunken crow steals an essential piece of his clothing. While chasing it, he runs off a cliff and survives only by catching a rope hanging from a balloon. The Angel of the Odd appears in the balloon and demands that he acknowledge both the angel’s existence and the reality of improbable accidents. Although the narrator submits, he cannot perform the angel’s final command because of his broken arm and missing clothes. The angel cuts the rope, sending him down his rebuilt house’s chimney.

He awakens on his dining-room floor amid ashes, broken dishes, bottles, and an empty liquor jug, suggesting that the entire chain of fantastic disasters may have been a drunken dream—yet presenting it as the Angel of the Odd’s revenge.
