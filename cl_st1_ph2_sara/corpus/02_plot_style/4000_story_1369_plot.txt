A writer living beside shabby McGinnis’s Court becomes fascinated by Melons, a mischievous, independent seven-year-old known for acrobatics, pranks, and luring wealthier children into forbidden adventures. While procrastinating over his literary work, the writer develops a silent companionship with the boy.

After the writer hangs a bunch of bananas outside his window to ripen, the fruit disappears, and several neighborhood boys are seen eating bananas. Convinced that Melons stole and distributed them, the writer confronts him. Melons tries to appear innocent, distracts him with talk of a circus, and blames another boy, but the writer delivers a stern, mock-legal accusation of felony.

Melons immediately vanishes and is never seen again. The writer later regrets frightening him away, searches unsuccessfully for him, and wonders whether the boy fled, died, or perhaps existed chiefly to provide the subject that finally filled the writer’s literary “void.”
