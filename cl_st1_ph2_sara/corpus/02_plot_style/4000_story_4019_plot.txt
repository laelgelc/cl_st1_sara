Puritan soldiers led by Sir Frederick Hamilton attack the Abbey of the White Friars, massacre the monks, and set the building ablaze. Before dying, the abbot curses those who harmed the faithful to wander among uncontrollable shadows and fires.

Hamilton sends the five troopers who initiated the slaughter to intercept two Irish messengers seeking reinforcements. In the dark woods, the soldiers encounter ominous apparitions: a crowned serpent and an old woman washing corpses that bear each trooper’s face. After losing their way, they meet a strange piper who agrees under threat to guide them.

The piper leads them onto a mountain, where stars resemble the abbey’s holy candles and his torch evokes its flames. Claiming the messengers are escaping, he drives the soldiers’ terrified horses into a headlong gallop. Unable to stop, the five troopers plunge over a precipice and die, fulfilling the abbot’s curse.
