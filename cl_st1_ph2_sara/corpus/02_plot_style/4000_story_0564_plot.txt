An elderly woman expects a peaceful retirement after raising four daughters, but each daughter dies and leaves her children in the grandmother’s care. She repeatedly enlarges her unusual house until it holds sixteen grandchildren, making it resemble a giant shoe. Despite the burden of feeding, clothing, and managing them, she raises them devotedly.

When a baker suggests sending the children to the poorhouse, they disguise themselves as warriors and frighten him away, accidentally leaving the household without bread. Their grandmother punishes them with broth without bread, whippings, and an early bedtime. The children behave better afterward and eventually grow up, find work or marry, and establish homes of their own. They remain grateful to the grandmother who cared for them.
