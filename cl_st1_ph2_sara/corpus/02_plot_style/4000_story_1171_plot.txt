Death roams eternally across the world on a pale horse, destroying people, empires, navies, and civilizations. Exhausted by endless slaughter and condemned to immortality, Death longs for creation to cease so that both Death and the horse may finally find oblivion.

Satan appears and argues that Death is fortunate because Death will perish after annihilating the universe, while Satan must survive to spread chaos elsewhere. Satan describes his own ceaseless labor: inspiring ambition, lust, hatred, war, and crime throughout human history, unloved in heaven, hell, or earth.

A procession of resurrected skeletons then arrives, led by Nero, who deliriously relives his imperial triumphs, sensual excesses, cruelty, and plan to burn Rome. When news comes of revolt and defeat, Nero realizes he must die. He begs to retain his pleasures and unfinished ambitions, but Death refuses. At Satan’s urging, Nero faces his fate, and Death wraps him in a shroud and carries him away.
