A skeptical young man visits an old castle to spend the night in a room reputed to be haunted, despite ominous warnings from its three elderly caretakers. Determined to disprove the legends, he carefully searches and secures the room, lights a fire, and fills it with candles to drive away its oppressive shadows.

After midnight, the candles begin going out inexplicably, one after another. As he frantically tries to relight them, the darkness steadily overwhelms the room. When even the fire dies, panic destroys his reason. He attempts to flee but, disoriented in the darkness, crashes repeatedly into the furniture and knocks himself unconscious.

The caretakers find him injured at dawn. Upon recovering, he concludes that the room is haunted not by the dead, but by an even more terrible presence: primal, irresistible fear itself—a force that extinguishes light, silences reason, and overwhelms anyone who enters.
