In the rough mining settlement of Red Gulch, prim schoolteacher Miss Mary encounters Sandy, a habitual drunk, passed out beneath an azalea bush. After she orders him to bathe, he drunkenly runs toward the river. Sobered and ashamed, Sandy later begins leaving azalea blossoms on her desk and quietly helping her, and the two gradually become close. During a school picnic, they share their pasts: both are orphans, and Sandy claims he is trying to reform. Their mutual affection deepens.

As Miss Mary prepares to leave Red Gulch, the mother of one of her pupils, Tommy, begs her to take the boy away and raise him beyond the settlement’s corrupting influence. She then reveals that Tommy’s absent father is Sandy. Shocked by his concealed past, Miss Mary agrees to care for Tommy but orders the woman to tell Sandy that he must never see or follow either of them. The next morning, Miss Mary departs by stagecoach with Tommy, pausing only so he can cut a branch from the azalea bush associated with her first meeting with Sandy.
