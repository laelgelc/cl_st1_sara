Three American soldiers in a French dugout debate whether German language and culture should be eliminated after the war. Phipps-Herrick and Rosenlaube argue for abolishing German, while Mitchell insists that a language and its useful literature should not be condemned because of Germany’s rulers and wartime brutality.

That night, all three volunteer for a dangerous listening patrol in No Man’s Land to investigate suspected German mining. Near the enemy lines, they discover a live German telephone receiver. Only Mitchell understands the conversation, learning that the Germans have redirected their mine and are sending out a twelve-man raiding party.

During their return, the patrol is attacked. Rosenlaube is badly wounded and left temporarily behind, while Mitchell is shot in the knee. Knowing Mitchell alone possesses the crucial intelligence, Phipps-Herrick carries him back through the gunfire. Mitchell’s report enables the Americans to counter the German plans, capture roughly two hundred soldiers, and seize three machine guns. Rosenlaube is later rescued, and all three men are honored. In the hospital, Rosenlaube admits that knowing German can indeed prove invaluable.
