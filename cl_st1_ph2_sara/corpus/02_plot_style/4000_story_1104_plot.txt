Robert Dixon returns to his Southern hometown after college and several years of ministerial work in the city. His father, Abram, an elderly, unschooled preacher who values emotional, spontaneous sermons, doubts the practical, restrained approach Robert learned at school. When Robert preaches in Abram’s church, his quiet sermon on applying religion to everyday life disappoints both his father and the congregation, who expected passionate oratory.

Nearly a year later, Abram visits Robert in the city and witnesses the realities behind his son’s ministry. Robert serves homeless Black children, rescues a hungry boy accused of theft from imprisonment, feeds and shelters him, and teaches neglected street boys with kindness and simplicity. Seeing the children’s trust in Robert, Abram realizes that his son’s practical service embodies the gospel.

Invited to preach in Robert’s church, Abram humbly confesses his former pride and misunderstanding. Taking “A little child shall lead them” as his text, he delivers a gentler sermon and declares that his visit—and his son’s example—have converted him to a deeper understanding of faith.
