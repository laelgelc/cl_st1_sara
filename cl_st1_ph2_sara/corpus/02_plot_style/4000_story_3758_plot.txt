A father and his fifteen-year-old daughter, Dorothea, leave their fellow tourists at Watchet for an unplanned day exploring Somerset’s Quantock Hills. After touring the harbor town and recalling Coleridge’s “The Ancient Mariner,” they hire a dog-cart, which Dorothea drives toward Bridgewater.

Along the way, they enjoy the countryside and seek places associated with Wordsworth and Coleridge. They visit Alfoxton, where Wordsworth once lived with his sister, wander through its woods and grounds, and reflect on the poet’s creative recovery there and his eventual departure after suspicious neighbors branded him a possible revolutionary. They then travel to Nether Stowey, where they lunch, climb the remains of a Norman castle, and inspect the humble cottage in which Coleridge lived during his most productive year and composed many of his greatest poems.

As evening approaches, father and daughter leave the Quantocks, drive through the surrounding villages to Bridgewater, and take a train to rejoin their companions in Bristol, ending a day devoted to landscape, literary history, and shared discovery.
