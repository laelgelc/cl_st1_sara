After the violent death of his brother Harry during a friendly mission abroad, a grieving man loses faith in divine goodness. Unable to accept the apparent waste of Harry’s life, he becomes convinced that the world’s pervasive suffering reveals a supremely intelligent but deliberately cruel God.

One night, Harry’s spirit appears outside the man’s home with a saddled horse and leads him on a moonlit ride into an unfamiliar country. They reach an immense amphitheater whose walls depict countless forms of torture. Inside, masked, faceless figures torment men, women, and children in cages before a curtained imperial throne.

At first, the man believes the scene confirms his vision of God as a monstrous spectator. Harry then reveals that every sufferer bears the same face—the face of God’s Son—and that the throne’s shaking curtains conceal not cruel delight but divine sobbing. The water flowing from the throne is God’s tears, washing the blood away and encircling the earth. Harry teaches him that no suffering occurs without ultimately being endured by God. For an instant, the empty tiers fill with white-robed figures who bow and weep.

As they leave, the supernatural landscape abruptly becomes the gate of the man’s own estate. Though he cannot explain the vision, it permanently restores his spiritual calm: suffering still troubles him, but he now believes it is shared by a grieving God rather than inflicted for divine pleasure.
