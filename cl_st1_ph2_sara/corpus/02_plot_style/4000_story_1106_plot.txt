As Washington’s school year nears its end, Bud works hard in the High School Cadet Corps, while his widowed mother, Hannah, sacrifices to provide his uniform and support his education. He, Hannah, and his sickly little sister eagerly expect his company to win the competitive drill.

During a difficult maneuver, Bud drops his bayonet. Though he continues marching without breaking formation, he believes his mistake has disgraced his company, which loses the competition. Devastated, he avoids his comrades and fears disappointing his family, but his mother and sister assure him that they remain proud of him.

The next morning, Bud is ashamed to return to school, but Hannah reminds him that courage means refusing to retreat from hardship. At school, the officer who judged the competition publicly praises Bud’s discipline and self-control: by continuing without pausing to retrieve the bayonet, he prevented the entire formation from falling into confusion. The officer shakes his hand, and his classmates cheer him. Restored in confidence and pride, Bud returns home and prepares to wear his uniform again.
