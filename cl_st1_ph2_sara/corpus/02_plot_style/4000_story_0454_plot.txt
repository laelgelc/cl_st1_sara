During the French Revolution, Madame de Dey, an aristocratic widow living cautiously in Carentan, awaits her only son, Auguste, an émigré soldier who has been captured after returning to France. His secret letter says he hopes to escape and reach her within three days; if he does not, she is to assume disaster has overtaken him.

Her unusual seclusion and preparations for his arrival arouse the suspicions of the townspeople and revolutionary authorities. To protect herself, she resumes her customary salon on the third evening, concealing her terror while friends and officials scrutinize her. The public prosecutor guesses the truth but promises to protect her, provided her son leaves before a planned search the next morning.

A young recruit is then billeted at her house. Because he closely resembles Auguste, Madame de Dey initially mistakes him for her son, only to be devastated when she realizes he is a stranger. Still hoping Auguste may arrive, she spends the night listening to every sound, but he never comes. By morning she is found dead. At the exact hour of her death, Auguste is executed by firing squad in Morbihan, suggesting a mysterious bond between mother and son that transcends distance.
