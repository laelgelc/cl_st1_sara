A poor butler is accused of murdering and robbing his reclusive employer, an elderly banker. During the trial, the butler’s unscrupulous lawyer demands more money from the prisoner’s exhausted wife and threatens to abandon the defense when she cannot pay.

Wandering the streets in despair, the wife is rescued by a celebrated Swiss opera singer. After hearing the case, the singer draws the crime scene and realizes that the position of the victim and his left-side wound indicate that the killer must have been left-handed. Learning that the banker’s nephew, Percy Thompson, is pressing for conviction, she visits him under a flirtatious pretext. By dropping a glove, she confirms that he instinctively uses his left hand; while pinning a rose to his coat, she also feels a bulky packet in his pocket.

The next morning, the lawyer withdraws in court and insinuates that his client is guilty. The singer unexpectedly intervenes, has Thompson raise his hand under oath, and exposes his left-handedness. She explains that only a left-handed attacker approaching along the wall could have inflicted the fatal wound. She then accuses Thompson and reveals that he is carrying the stolen money. Police recover it from his pocket, clearing the butler and exposing the nephew as the murderer. Watching the miraculous rescue, the corrupt lawyer recalls his earlier taunt that only Cinderella could save the accused and is forced to question his cynical faith in money and power.
