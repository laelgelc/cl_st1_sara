At a seaside hotel, a young writer befriends Miss Sylvia, a beloved sixty-year-old woman who prefers the company of young people. After hearing one of his stories about a man who leaves the woman he loves without confessing his feelings, Sylvia reveals that the same thing happened to her forty years earlier. She loved a sickly young man who abruptly departed, later went to California for his health, and died. Because he never declared his love, she has spent her life wondering whether he cared for her.

Months later, while sorting through the belongings of his long-dead uncle, Alan Blair, the narrator discovers that Alan was Sylvia’s lost love. Alan’s journal reveals that he loved her deeply but, knowing he had less than a year to live, left without confessing so she might be spared grief. He continued loving her until his death.

The narrator brings the journal to Sylvia, finally giving her the certainty she has long desired. Overjoyed to learn that Alan truly loved her, she declares that her old pain can no longer hurt her, and the narrator embraces and kisses her on his uncle’s behalf.
