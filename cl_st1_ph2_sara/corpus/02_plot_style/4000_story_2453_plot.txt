After five brutal years in the far North, Jan snaps when John Gordon insults him and shoots Gordon in the neck. Believing Gordon dead, their companions—Red Bill, Lawson, and Mr. Taylor—decide to administer frontier justice by hanging Jan. He resists ferociously, escapes briefly, and is recaptured with the help of their dogs.

Jan then exploits the group’s improvised legal reasoning, arguing that a hanging requires a man, a rope, and something from which to suspend him. Because the barren landscape has no trees or structures, he believes he has escaped execution. Lawson, however, constructs a makeshift gallows from two boat oars. The men overpower and bind Jan, place the noose around his neck, and prepare to raise the gallows.

At the last moment, Gordon emerges from the collapsed tent alive: the bullet merely grazed his skull and stunned him. The execution is halted, but Gordon challenges Jan to fight or apologize. Still defiant, Jan refuses to repent and threatens to thrash Gordon and all the others.
