A seafaring narrator offers a satirical “ethnographic” account of the people sailors contemptuously call “’Gees,” descendants of Portuguese settlers and Africans on the island of Fogo. He describes how American whaling captains recruit them as cheap labor, paying them largely in biscuits and subjecting them to abuse, while sailors disparage them for their race, inexperience, and willingness to work for less.

The narrator explains how captains inspect prospective recruits as if judging livestock and recounts cautionary examples: one supposedly agile man is discovered after hiring to suffer from elephantiasis and remains an unusable passenger for a three-year voyage; Captain Hosea Kean avoids such deception by secretly observing recruits at home, where they cannot conceal their condition. The sketch closes by noting that those who survive and settle in American ports become harder to identify, and by directing anyone seeking further information to experienced whaling captains such as Kean, presently somewhere in the Pacific.
