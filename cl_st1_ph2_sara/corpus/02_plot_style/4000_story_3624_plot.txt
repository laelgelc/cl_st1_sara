A traveler with a ruined appetite visits a Bohemian health resort run by Professor Haimberger. The professor offers him a menu of revolting dishes, locks him in his room, and refuses him ordinary food. As the traveler goes without eating, books about shipwrecks and starvation intensify his hunger. After sixty hours, he becomes willing to eat even the menu’s grotesque offerings. The professor then serves him chicken, followed by steak, potatoes, bread, and coffee, which he devours with delight, proving that his appetite has returned.

Haimberger explains that he developed this cure after surviving a shipwreck. During the voyage, passengers had lost interest in the ship’s monotonous meals, but severe rationing after the wreck made them ravenous enough to relish raw ham and even softened sailor boots. Once rescued, however, they resumed regular eating and soon lost their appetites again.

From this experience, Haimberger concludes that people should eat only when genuinely hungry and should skip meals whenever food ceases to taste satisfying. He argues that conventional health resorts secretly achieve results through restricted diets rather than water, baths, or other advertised treatments. He advises the traveler to return to his former habit of one and a half meals a day, since adding an unnecessary luncheon caused his condition, and recommends brief fasting as a remedy for minor illnesses.
