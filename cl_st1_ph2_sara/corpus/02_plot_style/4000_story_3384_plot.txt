A mysterious mansion suddenly appears on Great Hill, inhabited by a dark, beautiful woman who repeatedly drives across the surrounding countryside as though searching for something. Known locally as the Mad Lady, she eventually orders her chauffeur onto a marshy causeway, crying that she has found what she seeks. The automobile crashes into quicksand, killing them both. Afterward, the mansion is found empty and strangely unstable in appearance, with parts visible from the village but absent up close.

Years later, Mary Solewise and her poet lover discover the abandoned house and decide to repair its east wing as their home. They marry, raise children there, and fill the once-haunted building with celebrations, work, music, and village life. The poet also restores the Mad Lady’s automobile.

When the couple finally drive the car, Mary hears the dead woman’s voice through its speaking tube, giving frantic directions toward the marsh. Following the instructions, they dig beneath a lightning-struck tree and uncover a locked box containing a lock of hair, a gold ring, and an illegible document resembling a marriage certificate—apparently evidence the Mad Lady had desperately needed to recover.

Alarmed that the mansion and everything associated with it may be only a supernatural projection, the poet considers leaving. Their friends and Mary’s father reassure them that, even if the house began as the Mad Lady’s dream or force of will, the couple’s love, children, and happy life have made it real and permanent. Mary remains uneasy, but their home is understood to have been stabilized by the life they brought into it.
