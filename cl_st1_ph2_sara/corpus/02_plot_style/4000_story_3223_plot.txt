An American woman engaged to Tony, a British soldier serving in France, is confronted by his aristocratic parents over reports that she has been secretly spending time with Arthur Meadows, an unknown American businessman. She admits that she deliberately approached Meadows in London, befriended him, and accompanied him alone on long nighttime drives along England’s east coast, apparently with the approval of Sir Henry Marquis, head of the Criminal Investigation Department.

She then explains that Sir Henry invited them to investigate an apparent murder at an abandoned country house. The evidence—a broken door, a vanished corpse, bloody footprints, and a burned candle—leads Sir Henry to propose that foreign criminals killed an accomplice. Meadows, however, systematically proves that the scene was staged: the door was broken by an amateur, the witness’s description was impossible in darkness, the candle had never burned where it stood, the “blood” was red ink, and the footprints had been made by Sir Henry’s hand.

When Meadows accuses Sir Henry of fabricating the crime, Sir Henry reveals that the entire mystery was a trap. Meadows’s brilliance confirms that he is actually Karl Holweg Leibnich, a renowned German criminologist and spy who has been using his car’s powerful lights to signal German submarines from the coast. The woman had agreed to risk her reputation by drawing close to him so Sir Henry could expose him. Realizing that her seemingly scandalous behavior was an act of wartime courage comparable to Tony’s service, his parents honor and accept her.
