At an isolated hunting camp on Skeleton Lake, Rushton arrives exhausted after a canoeing disaster with his companion, Jake. He claims their canoe overturned in deep water, that they clung to it for hours, and that Jake broke away during a quarrel over whether to swim toward an island, then drowned. Rushton says he survived, searched unsuccessfully for Jake, and spent two harrowing days reaching the camp.

Although his account is largely plausible, Rushton's frantic insistence, contradictions, and haunted behavior make everyone suspect that he is concealing the truth. He first says Jake sank silently, then recalls hearing Jake’s final cry. That night, Rushton talks violently in his sleep, while an examination of the canoe reveals that the holes supposedly cut while the men clung to it were too small for their arms and must have been made afterward.

Hank and Silver Fizz later search for Jake and find his body in shallow water near the island. A severe wound in the back of his head confirms that he did not simply drown: Rushton murdered him and fabricated the canoe story to disguise the crime.
