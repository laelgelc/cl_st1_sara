A teenage immigrant girl welcomes a family renting the rural property where her father works as caretaker. Over the course of their weeklong vacation, she quietly observes the parents and two daughters delighting in country life—visiting the beach, exploring nature, and enjoying a closeness that contrasts with her own family’s isolation and hardship.

During the father’s birthday dinner, a guest mentions a city bakery, stirring painful memories for the caretaker. He once sold flowers in the same piazza, until three men brutally attacked him in a racist assault, shattering his teeth and impairing his speech. Traumatized, he moved his family to the secluded countryside, where he could work without speaking and feel safe, while urging his daughter to study and eventually escape their limited life.

The vacationing family leaves refreshed and nostalgic, unaware of how different the place feels to those who live there year-round. The girl cleans the house for the next guests and keeps the small things they have left behind, including traces of the mother’s writing, which suggest that the mother has been observing and writing about the caretaker’s family just as the girl has been observing hers.
