A talkative five-year-old girl, Mini, befriends Rahmun, an Afghan fruit seller. Though initially frightened by his imposing appearance and large bag, she soon delights in his gifts, patient attention, and playful jokes. Their friendship reassures her father but worries her suspicious mother. Rahmun, who has a daughter of his own in Afghanistan, becomes deeply attached to Mini.

Before returning home to his country, Rahmun quarrels with a debtor who denies buying a shawl and stabs him. As the police lead him away, he and Mini exchange one last familiar joke about going to the “father-in-law’s house,” a euphemism for prison. Rahmun is sentenced to several years in jail, and Mini gradually forgets him as she grows up.

Eight years later, Rahmun is released and visits Mini’s home on her wedding day, expecting to find the same little girl. Mini’s father initially tries to send him away, but Rahmun reveals that he has always carried the inked handprint of his own distant daughter. Moved by their shared love as fathers, Mini’s father summons the bride. Rahmun is stunned to see Mini grown and realizes that his own daughter must also have changed during his absence. Mini’s father gives him money to return to Afghanistan, sacrificing some wedding luxuries so that Rahmun can reunite with his child.
