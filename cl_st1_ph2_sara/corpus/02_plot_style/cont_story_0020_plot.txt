A Nigerian American woman returns to her late grandmother’s home in Nigeria after eighteen years and reunites with her cousin Dozie. The visit revives memories of the last summer she spent there, when her grandmother openly favored her brother Nonso as the male heir. Jealous of the attention he received and feeling that his existence left no room for her, the ten-year-old girl persuaded Nonso to climb high into a weak avocado tree, then frightened him by shouting that a deadly snake was nearby. He fell and died while Dozie silently watched.

Afterward, she falsely told her mother that the grandmother had ordered Nonso to climb, scared him, and then failed to help him. The lie intensified the family’s divisions and helped sever her mother’s ties to Nigeria and to the girl’s father. In the present, Dozie reveals that he has long dreamed about Nonso and that, as a child, he cared only about fulfilling what she wanted. Unable to confess her enduring guilt and pain, she watches him walk away and remains beneath the avocado tree, weeping.
