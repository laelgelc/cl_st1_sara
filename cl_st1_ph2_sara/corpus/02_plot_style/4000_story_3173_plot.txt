Two aristocratic Hungarian families, the Metzengersteins and Berlifitzings, have maintained a bitter feud for centuries, reinforced by a prophecy that the mortal Metzengerstein will triumph over the immortal Berlifitzing. After inheriting immense estates at fifteen, the cruel Baron Frederick Metzengerstein indulges in debauchery and violence and is suspected of setting fire to the Berlifitzing stables.

During the fire, Frederick sees a tapestry depicting a gigantic horse belonging to a defeated Berlifitzing ancestor. The horse’s image mysteriously moves and assumes a threatening, human expression. Soon afterward, an identical fiery-colored horse emerges from the burning stables, branded with the initials of Count Wilhelm von Berlifitzing, though the Berlifitzing household denies owning it. The count dies in the blaze while trying to rescue his horses, and part of the tapestry inexplicably disappears.

Frederick claims the strange horse and becomes obsessively attached to it, withdrawing from society and riding it constantly despite its demonic behavior. Although he appears devoted to the animal, witnesses sense that he fears it and may not truly control it, suggesting that it embodies the dead count or the vengeful spirit of the Berlifitzing line.

One stormy night, Frederick rides into the forest. While he is gone, his own château catches fire. The horse returns carrying the terrified baron, who struggles helplessly to stop it. It leaps through the gates and into the burning palace, killing both horse and rider. As the storm suddenly ceases, smoke rises above the ruins in the immense shape of a horse, fulfilling the ancient prophecy through Berlifitzing’s supernatural revenge.
