At a packed public lecture, a pedagogue condemns conventional school discipline before defending corporal punishment as a Russian tradition. He unveils his invention: an automated, supposedly impartial flogging machine designed for schools, prisons, the military, workplaces, and even families. He explains that weighted coupons or coins determine the number of strokes, while the machine restrains offenders and cannot be stopped without special keys.

During a demonstration with a leather dummy, the inventor predicts that the machine will eradicate crime, idleness, and financial problems. As he displays a heavy commemorative chronometer, it slips from his hand into the machine’s receptacle. The apparatus activates, traps him, and begins flogging him relentlessly. The keys cannot be found, and attempts to stop or damage the machine fail as the stroke counter climbs into the thousands.

Eventually, the fire brigade destroys the machine after 1,650 strokes, ending both the apparatus and enthusiasm for the idea. The inventor survives but becomes permanently calmer, gentler, and melancholy. He returns to teaching, becomes beloved by his pupils, and never revisits his invention.
