A gloomy coffin-maker, Adrian Prokhoroff, moves with his daughters and servant into a long-desired new house. Soon afterward, his jovial German neighbor, the shoemaker Gottlieb Schultz, invites the family to celebrate his silver wedding anniversary.

At the feast, the guests drink to the health of those for whom they work. A watchman jokingly urges Adrian to toast his own customers—the dead. Humiliated by the company’s laughter, Adrian returns home drunk and angrily declares that, instead of inviting his neighbors to a housewarming feast, he will invite all the corpses he has buried.

He is awakened before dawn by news that a wealthy shopkeeper’s wife has died. After spending the day arranging her funeral, he returns home at night and finds his house filled with the dead, who claim to have accepted his invitation. Among them is a skeleton who accuses Adrian of having cheated him years earlier by selling him a cheap pine coffin instead of an oak one. Adrian recoils, knocks the skeleton apart, and is threatened by the outraged corpses until he collapses unconscious.

Adrian later wakes in bed and learns that the death, funeral arrangements, and supernatural gathering were all a drunken dream. Relieved that the shopkeeper’s wife is still alive and that no corpses visited him, he cheerfully asks for tea and his daughters.
