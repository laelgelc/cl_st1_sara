A traveler recalls meeting Simeon Virin, a humble postmaster, and his beautiful, capable fourteen-year-old daughter, Dounia. Years later, he returns to find Virin aged and broken. Virin explains that a young hussar, Captain Minsky, once stopped at the station, pretended to be ill, and gained their trust. When he departed, he offered to take Dounia as far as church but instead carried her away to St. Petersburg.

After recovering from the shock, Virin tracked them down. Minsky admitted his guilt but insisted that Dounia loved him and would be happy, then tried to pay Virin off. Virin later found Dounia living luxuriously with Minsky, but she fainted upon seeing her father, and Minsky violently expelled him. Convinced that Dounia would eventually be abandoned and ruined, Virin returned alone to his station and descended into grief and drink.

On a later visit, the traveler learns that Virin has died. A village boy tells him that a beautiful, wealthy lady had recently arrived with three children, wept upon hearing of the postmaster’s death, and lay for a long time on his grave. The traveler understands that she was Dounia, who had prospered but returned too late to reconcile with her father.
