Rollo Finch, a cheerful but dim-witted heir, devises a rigid six-week system for courting chorus girl Marguerite Parker. His valet, Wilson, doubts the elaborate plan and recalls the simpler romantic customs of his hometown, Market Bumpstead.

Rollo learns that his wealthy uncle, Andrew Galloway—whose fortune Rollo expects to inherit—has also fallen for Marguerite and intends to propose within days. Panicking, Rollo abandons his schedule and sends her flowers, jewelry, and an invitation to supper. She never arrives, and he returns home humiliated.

There he discovers Marguerite celebrating her marriage to Wilson. She and Wilson were once engaged but had separated after a misunderstanding; when Rollo sent Wilson to deliver flowers to her, they reconciled and quickly married. Rollo realizes the marriage both removes his romantic rival and prevents his uncle from marrying her, thereby protecting his inheritance. Delighted, he gives the couple the gifts intended for Marguerite and concedes that Wilson’s direct Market Bumpstead approach is wiser than his own system.
