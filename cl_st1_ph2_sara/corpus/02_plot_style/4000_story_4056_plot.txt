After Roman ambassadors from the Fabii family violate diplomatic custom by fighting alongside Clusium and killing a Gallic chief, the Gallic leader Brennus demands their punishment. Rome refuses and even appoints them military tribunes, provoking the Gauls to march against the city. The hastily assembled Roman army is routed at the River Allia, leaving Rome nearly defenseless.

Most inhabitants flee, while the remaining soldiers fortify the Capitol. Eighty elderly senators stay behind, offering themselves in atonement for Rome’s wrongdoing, and are killed when the Gauls enter, sack, and burn the city. Unable to capture the Capitol, the Gauls besiege it and try to starve its defenders.

Meanwhile, the exiled general Camillus defeats a Gallic raiding force at Ardea. Scattered Romans rally around him, but he refuses command without lawful authorization. Pontius Cominius secretly swims the Tiber, climbs the Capitol, and obtains a Senate decree appointing Camillus dictator. The Gauls discover the route and attempt a nighttime ascent, but Juno’s sacred geese awaken Marcus Manlius, who raises the alarm and repels them.

With both sides suffering from hunger and disease, the Romans agree to pay Brennus a ransom in gold. When he uses false weights and throws his sword onto the scales, declaring “Woe to the conquered,” Camillus arrives with a Roman army, cancels the agreement, and proclaims that Rome must be saved with iron, not gold. He drives the Gauls from the city, pursues them, and destroys their army. Camillus is honored as Rome’s second founder, Manlius for defending the Capitol, and the sacred geese for giving the warning that saved it.
