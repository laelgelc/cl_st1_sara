Mary “Molly” Ludwig, a strong, hardworking young woman with dreams of becoming a soldier, marries John Hays, who joins the Continental Army during the American Revolution. While staying near her family, Molly aids wounded soldiers after the Battle of Princeton, fires an abandoned cannon to delay approaching British troops, and carries an injured soldier to safety.

At the Battle of Monmouth, Molly repeatedly brings water to the overheated soldiers and their cannons, earning the nickname “Molly Pitcher.” When John collapses from heat exhaustion while operating his cannon, she takes his place, loading and firing the weapon for the remainder of the battle. Her courage helps the Americans repel the British and earns the admiration of the troops. George Washington honors her with the rank of captain and recommends that she receive military half-pay for life. After the war, Molly returns to Carlisle, where she lives quietly, but her battlefield heroism secures her lasting place in American history.
