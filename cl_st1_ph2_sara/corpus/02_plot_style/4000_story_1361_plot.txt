After winning a difficult case for the Eureka Ditch Company through sheer theatrical eloquence, the flamboyant Colonel Starbottle is approached by a widow and her daughter, Zaidee Hooker. They want him to bring a breach-of-promise suit against Adoniram Hotchkiss, the austere president of the company he has just defended.

Zaidee claims Hotchkiss courted her through words marked in hymnals and prayer books, a stamped peppermint declaring love, and birdlike signals exchanged near her home. Although there are no letters, witnesses, or physical displays of affection, she insists that he promised marriage and then abruptly abandoned her. Starbottle initially dislikes such suits and recognizes that the evidence is nearly nonexistent, but Zaidee’s beauty, perceptiveness, and admiration of his courtroom powers captivate him.

Starbottle confronts Hotchkiss in hopes of arranging a settlement. Hotchkiss angrily denies any courtship, accuses Zaidee of blackmail, and nearly provokes a violent encounter with the dueling-minded lawyer. Offended on Zaidee’s behalf and confident that his rhetoric can overcome the weak facts, Starbottle commits himself to the case. Encouraged by Zaidee’s faith in his ability to tell the story persuasively and make Hotchkiss pay, he begins legal proceedings, and news of the sensational lawsuit spreads through town.
