Eddie Houghton, a handsome, well-liked small-town drugstore clerk, longs for a more ambitious life. Enticed by a naval recruiter’s glamorous promises of travel, education, and opportunity, he enlists despite leaving behind his devoted mother and Josie Morehouse, the girl who cares for him.

Naval life proves radically different from the advertisements. Eddie is surrounded by hardened, vicious sailors whose cruelty, obscenity, and exploitation of others horrify him. Because he refuses to become like them, they torment and mistreat him. Overwhelmed by homesickness and disgust, he deserts, intending simply to return to his mother and his former decent life.

On the train home, a stranger explains that desertion will lead to imprisonment and that the authorities will find him if he goes home. Realizing he cannot reclaim his old life, Eddie leaves the train, checks into a cheap hotel, and kills himself with carbolic acid. His hometown learns of his death and understands his suffering through his letters. Thereafter, the narrator reacts with fury whenever he sees recruiting posters that falsely portray military service as an idyllic path to travel and advancement.
