A narrator befriends Jacob Settle, a melancholy, solitary laborer known for quiet acts of kindness. Finding Jacob terrified by a recurring nightmare, he stays with him through the night and persuades him to reveal its cause.

Jacob confesses that as a young schoolmaster he loved a woman named Mabel. When a gentleman seduced her and contemptuously admitted he had no intention of marrying her, Jacob killed him in a fit of rage and concealed the body. He later learned that Mabel had become pregnant and died in disgrace, making his crime seem useless. Ever since, guilt has convinced him that Heaven is closed to him. In his recurring dream, he approaches its gates wearing a white robe, only to discover that the robe and his hands are stained with his victim’s blood; angels then prepare to strike him down.

The narrator assures Jacob that mercy and atonement remain possible and urges him to live among others and serve them. Soon afterward, Jacob disappears. Two years later, the narrator visits a Glasgow hospital after an industrial accident. Two workers had fallen into a flooded gasometer, and one sacrificed himself by standing underwater and holding his companion above his head until rescuers arrived. The dead hero is Jacob. Though his body is stained purple by gas and tar, his hands have been blanched white by the water. The narrator takes this as a sign that Jacob’s courageous self-sacrifice has cleansed his guilt and opened the gates of Heaven to him.
