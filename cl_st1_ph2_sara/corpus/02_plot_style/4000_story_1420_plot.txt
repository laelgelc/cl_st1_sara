A narrator investigates the mysterious, silent man who has accompanied him throughout life. This figure resembles him exactly, copies his clothes and expressions, shares his illnesses and emotions, appears in mirrors, polished objects, and water, and follows him everywhere while remaining impossible to touch. The narrator gradually recognizes the companion as his own reflection and contemplates their inseparable fates: they have aged together, and the figure will disappear when he dies.

Once a source of youthful vanity, the reflection now confronts the narrator with aging, disappointment, and wasted years. He resolves to use it for honest self-examination and moral improvement, while reflecting on identity, mortality, spiritual existence, and humanity’s tendency to seek ultimate wisdom in images of itself. When the figure appears before him again, it merely imitates his questioning silence, leaving life’s deepest mysteries unanswered.
