An elderly, isolated widow, Mrs. Manstey, lives in a New York boardinghouse and finds her chief happiness in the view from her back window: neighboring gardens, flowering trees, household servants, animals, and a distant church spire. When she learns that a neighbor, Mrs. Black, plans to build an extension that will block this cherished world, she pleads with her and offers a large portion of her savings to stop the construction. Mrs. Black pretends to agree but begins work as planned.

Desperate, Mrs. Manstey ventures out at night and sets fire to construction materials beneath Mrs. Black’s wooden balcony. The fire causes little damage, but exposure to the cold leaves her fatally ill with pneumonia. Before dying, she asks to be placed by her window and sees that work has temporarily stopped, leaving her beloved view intact. She dies smiling in the spring dawn, and construction resumes later that day.
