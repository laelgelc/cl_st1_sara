Nelly, a compassionate young girl inspired by her wounded brother Will’s stories of military hospitals, decides to create a hospital for sick and injured animals. With her mother’s approval and the help of Tony, the gardener’s son, she cleans an old summer-house, studies how to care for different creatures, and equips it with suitable beds, food, and supplies. Will helps transform her toy cart into a miniature ambulance modeled on the Sanitary Commission’s work.

On her first expedition, Nelly collects several suffering creatures, including a trapped fly, an ailing caterpillar and beetle, an injured snake, a wounded young bird, two field mice, and a turtle. Although she fears the snake, she treats it kindly after reflecting that compassion should extend even to one’s enemies. Tony secretly places some animals in her path so she will not be disappointed. Nelly and Tony house and treat the patients, though the mice and snake die and are tenderly buried.

The hospital becomes a daily charitable enterprise. Nelly nurses animals, continues preparing lint for wounded soldiers, and learns about nature from books, Tony, and Will. Her example influences neighborhood children to care for animals, persuades rough boys to stop harming them, and reminds adults of the larger humanitarian work being done for wounded soldiers.

The project also helps Will recover emotionally. Interested in Nelly’s patients and ashamed to complain when they endure pain patiently, he becomes cheerful and brave again. Before returning to duty, he decorates the hospital with a flag, a humorous picture of Nelly and Tony tending a procession of animals, and a motto affirming that true devotion is shown by loving all creatures, great and small.
