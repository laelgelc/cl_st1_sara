Hugh Walker, a mathematics professor trapped in an emotionally barren marriage, feels detached from his wife, children, work, and ordinary life. Though materially comfortable, he experiences marriage and family as a prison of mutual belonging and tries to recover a sense of reality through solitary nighttime walks.

His interest is revived by Mary Cochran, a young college student whom he invites into his household. Mary becomes close to his wife and children, while Hugh observes her as a free, still-unformed person who does not belong to him. Her presence temporarily reconnects him to life, but as she becomes a familiar part of the family, he grows disturbed that she, too, seems to be entering his private prison. His frustration erupts when he snatches a book from her and throws it into the fire, condemning books, thought, and his own failure to live fully.

After Mary returns to the household, Hugh intercepts her one evening, kisses her, and orders her never to come back. He takes grim satisfaction in awakening adult desire and fear in her while refusing any continuing relationship. Convinced that Mary will eventually become imprisoned by love and belonging, he comforts himself with the belief that he will not be the one who builds that prison for her.
