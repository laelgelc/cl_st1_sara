Boori Ma, an elderly refugee displaced to Calcutta after Partition, earns shelter beneath a building’s letterboxes by sweeping the stairs and informally guarding the entrance. She repeatedly tells the residents elaborate, contradictory stories about the wealth and family she lost, but they tolerate her exaggerations, value her work, and regard her as a capable gatekeeper.

When Mr. Dalal receives a promotion, he installs a new basin in the shared stairwell. The improvement sparks envy among the other residents, who begin their own renovations. With workers and strangers constantly entering the building, Boori Ma can no longer monitor the gate and retreats to the rooftop, waiting for the Dalals to return from vacation with promised new bedding. Restless and increasingly neglected, she wanders through local markets, where someone steals her savings and keys.

While she is away, thieves remove the stairwell basin. The residents accuse Boori Ma of abandoning her duty and helping the robbers, refusing to believe her denials because of her history of embellishment. Seeking someone who can protect their newly improved building, they throw her belongings into the street and expel her. Boori Ma leaves with only her broom, still protesting her innocence, while the residents prepare to hire a “real” gatekeeper.
