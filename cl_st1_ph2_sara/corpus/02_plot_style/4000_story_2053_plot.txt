A train passenger notices a bald man carrying seven newspapers that each provide railway-accident insurance. When a horseshoe the man picked up for luck falls from the luggage rack and strikes his head, he explains that supposedly lucky things have always brought him misfortune.

Born on the allegedly fortunate day of Wednesday, he received no help or inheritance because his relatives assumed he would prosper naturally. A lucky black cat killed a canary entrusted to him by a young woman, ruining their relationship. A lucky piebald horse repeatedly dragged him into public houses, leading to alcoholism and the loss of a good job.

His worst experience began when an employer unexpectedly gave him a large Christmas goose. Having no use for it, he spent the evening trying to sell, raffle, give away, or abandon it. Each attempt failed, while repeated drinks left him drunk and disorderly. Pursued by police, he finally threw what he believed was the goose into a canal. When the recovered parcel proved to contain a dead baby, he was arrested and detained over Christmas. Though eventually cleared of the serious suspicion and convicted only of drunken disorder, he lost both his job and his fiancée.

As the train reaches its destination, the swelling from the “lucky” horseshoe prevents him from putting on his hat, confirming his conviction that luck has never served him well.
