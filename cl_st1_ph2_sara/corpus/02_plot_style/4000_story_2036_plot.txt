Three men aboard a ship discuss how repeated behavior makes virtue, vice, and even unpleasant tastes effortless. One man illustrates the point with his acquired fondness for dreadful cheap cigars and wine, then tells of a widower so accustomed to his first wife’s incessant nagging that he remarries solely to recover the abuse he needs for comfort and sleep.

A quiet stranger joins in with a supposedly true story about a rigidly punctual American businessman who has followed the same daily schedule for twenty-five years. After inheriting a London business, the man moves to England but inexplicably wakes at one in the afternoon, becomes hungry at one in the morning, and cannot sleep until five. His work and health suffer until his family realizes that his body is still obeying his old schedule according to the time in his hometown.

Unable to change habits stronger than his response to local time, he forces his London life to conform to them. He works from afternoon until late evening, rides at night, dines after midnight, plays cards until dawn, wakes his family for prayers, and then sleeps during the day. Though mocked and deprived of conveniently timed Sunday worship, he accepts the arrangement. The listeners find the tale so incredible that the man who wagered he would believe it silently pays the stranger and leaves.
