Lawyer Quince, a village shoemaker famed for his amateur legal advice, is consulted by Farmer Rose about a young man he has locked in his stable for courting his daughter Celia. Quince declares that Rose may keep the trespasser imprisoned and even pretend to lose the key. He later discovers that the prisoner is his own son, Ned, and realizes Rose has tricked him into publicly authorizing the confinement.

Quince tries to retract his advice, but Rose follows it to the letter, claiming the key is lost and keeping Ned on bread and water. Ned refuses to renounce Celia and cheerfully treats his imprisonment as preparation for becoming Rose’s son-in-law. Rose therefore plans to send Celia away.

Before her coach departs, Celia hides in Quince’s tool shed and deliberately hints, while pretending to sleep, that he should lock her inside. Quince understands her plan and does so. When Rose discovers where she is, Quince mirrors the farmer’s tactics by claiming to have lost the key and saying only Ned knows where it is. Forced to release Ned, Rose brings him to the shed, where Ned produces the key and frees Celia.

Defeated by Quince and the lovers’ scheme, Rose angrily disowns Celia in the moment, but Quince and the miller persuade him to enter the village inn, accept the joke, and make peace.
