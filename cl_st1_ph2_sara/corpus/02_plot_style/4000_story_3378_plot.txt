Bedridden Mrs. Dacre, once the energetic and influential center of her village, uses the new rural telephone to eavesdrop on her neighbors and remain involved in community affairs. Her invalidism began when her beloved daughter Nancy insisted on loving Saul Manley, the hardworking son of a family Mrs. Dacre considers socially inferior. Haunted by her own youthful attraction to Saul’s father and fiercely possessive of Nancy, she forbids the marriage, while Nancy refuses to abandon her mother and Saul vows lifelong fidelity.

A neighbor finally warns Mrs. Dacre that the villagers resent her listening in on their calls. Mrs. Dacre then overhears what she believes is a serialized story about a cherished young woman whose marriage is blocked by a selfish old relative, leaving the girl in danger of dying from consumption. Recognizing herself and Nancy in the account, she is horrified by the harm her possessiveness may cause. She immediately rises from the bed she has occupied for months, dresses, and calls the church elder to bring Saul. Declaring herself restored and determined that Nancy will not waste away, she prepares to attend Nancy and Saul’s wedding.
