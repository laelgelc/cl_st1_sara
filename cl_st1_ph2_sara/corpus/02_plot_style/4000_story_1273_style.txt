The story uses a reflective, psychologically intimate realist style, with its emotional force arising less from outward action than from gradual shifts in consciousness. The narration remains in the third person but is closely aligned with the elderly professor’s point of view, frequently passing into free indirect discourse. External details—a spring breeze, wandering student eyes, library shelves, twilight on the campus—are filtered through his memories and anxieties, so that setting and thought continually merge. The narrator is sympathetic without becoming overtly sentimental, allowing the professor’s dignity, self-deception, hurt, and eventual consolation to emerge incrementally.

The prose is measured, lyrical, and gently formal. Its diction belongs to an early twentieth-century literary register, evident in constructions such as “to-day,” “class-room,” and “out-of-doors,” as well as in abstract moral terms including “intellectual honesty,” “spiritual dignity,” “fulfilment,” and “the consciousness of life.” Concrete sensory language softens and animates this philosophical vocabulary. May is experienced through breezes, birds, lilacs, sunlight, loosened hair, laughter, and fragrance, while age is associated with fatigue, heat, stooping, shadows, enclosure, and failing powers. The effect is contemplative rather than ornate: natural imagery carries conceptual meaning without losing its physical immediacy.

Sentence structure is often long and cumulative, reproducing the professor’s associative movement through observation, memory, qualification, and self-questioning. Parenthetical interruptions, dashes, semicolons, repetitions, and carefully delayed clauses create the rhythm of a mind testing its own conclusions. Short sentences appear at moments of recognition or pain—“He was tired,” “Must one always pay?”—and acquire emphasis through contrast with the more flowing narration. Ellipses and unfinished formulations suggest thoughts that are emotionally difficult or not yet fully articulable. Recurrent rhetorical questions dramatise self-examination rather than solicit answers.

A central stylistic device is antithesis. The story repeatedly sets youth against age, spring against decline, classroom against outdoors, books against lived experience, thought against feeling, belief against unbelief, and replacement against fulfilment. These oppositions are not left static. The conclusion reconciles them by presenting education as a form of continuity: the older teacher does not merely surrender to youth but survives through what youth has learned from him. Key words and ideas return in transformed form. “May,” initially the rival of philosophy, becomes part of philosophy’s meaning; “immortality,” first an abstract lecture topic, becomes a practical account of influence; and “facing” the truth evolves from a threat into a source of dignity.

Nature functions symbolically throughout. Spring represents youthful vitality and the impersonal renewal that makes the professor newly conscious of his age. The changing light measures his emotional journey: the bright afternoon exposes his exhaustion, gathering shadows accompany desolation, and twilight becomes a transitional space in which age and youth can meet. The personification of May as a playful force that undoes Anna’s hair establishes a world of exuberant life, while the later description of daylight “creeping away under protest” mirrors the professor’s reluctance to relinquish his place. Such correspondences are suggestive rather than allegorically rigid.

The pacing is deliberately gradual. A minor classroom unease develops into the overheard library conversation, which serves as the story’s quiet crisis. Thereafter, the narrative slows and expands into memory, self-assessment, and grief. The professor’s resignation is written quickly, but his inward acceptance takes much longer. The final conversation with Gretta supplies the emotional and philosophical resolution, yet it does so through hesitation, silence, and a revision of language rather than through dramatic reversal. The movement is from unconscious foreboding to humiliation, from humiliation to honest recognition, and from recognition to serenity.

Dialogue is sparse and strategically placed. Most speech is plain, restrained, and weighted by context. The overheard remarks wound precisely because they are practical rather than malicious. Gretta’s speech has greater warmth and rhetorical elevation, especially when she replaces “replaced” with “fulfilled.” That verbal correction crystallises the story’s argument and reveals her role as both student and spiritual successor. Her refusal to offer false reassurance is treated as a higher form of compassion; emotional intimacy is expressed through pauses, tears, looks, and shared silence as much as through words.

Characterisation depends on gestures and inward responses rather than physical description. The professor’s rubbing of his forehead, leaning against the books, hesitation on the steps, and reluctance to go home disclose his vulnerability. Gretta is characterised through her steady gaze, forward-leaning attention, moral directness, and capacity to understand without condescension. Even secondary students are sketched through small social details—straying eyes, tennis games, low conversation—making youth appear both thoughtless and full of promise.

The tone moves through tenderness, wistfulness, irony, desolation, and finally earned consolation. Its sentiment is controlled by honesty: the professor’s pain is not denied, and the story does not pretend that retirement is painless or that gratitude can restore his former powers. Instead, it finds meaning in transmission between generations. The closing mood is elegiac but affirmative, using the communal sounds and fragrances of evening to enlarge a private reconciliation into a broader meditation on work, mortality, and legacy. Overall, the style combines psychological realism, philosophical reflection, seasonal symbolism, and restrained emotional lyricism, culminating in a quiet epiphany rather than a conventionally dramatic ending.
