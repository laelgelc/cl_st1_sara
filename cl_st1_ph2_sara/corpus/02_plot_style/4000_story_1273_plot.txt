A seventy-three-year-old philosophy professor, weary after more than forty years of teaching, dismisses his class early on a beautiful May day, telling his students that philosophy should enrich life rather than deaden it. Troubled by the tears of Gretta Loring, his favorite and most perceptive student, he later overhears her and another student discussing his declining abilities and the school board’s uncertainty about allowing him to continue teaching.

Painfully recognizing the truth, he writes his resignation, feeling discarded by the college to which he has devoted his unmarried life. As he reflects on his years of service, his accomplishments offer little comfort against the loneliness and loss of purpose awaiting him.

That evening, he finds Gretta alone on campus and tells her of his resignation. She does not deny that it is time for him to step aside, but reframes his departure: he is not being replaced but fulfilled. The younger generation can advance because his work prepared them, and his influence will endure through those whose minds and lives he has shaped. Her insight restores his sense that his life has mattered, allowing him to face retirement with dignity and serenity.
