Don Munio Sancho de Hinojosa, a renowned Castilian border knight, captures an unarmed Moorish wedding party led by the young noble Abadil and his bride, Allifra. Moved by Abadil’s courteous appeal, Don Munio treats them as honored guests, celebrates their wedding at his castle for fifteen days, gives them generous gifts, and escorts them safely home.

Years later, Don Munio and his seventy cavaliers join the King of Castile in battle against the Moors. Before departing, they vow that after one final battle they will make a pilgrimage to the Holy Sepulchre in Jerusalem. During the fighting, they sacrifice themselves to save the king. Don Munio is unknowingly killed by Abadil, who recognizes his former benefactor only after removing his helmet.

Grief-stricken, Abadil returns Don Munio’s body to his wife, Donna Maria, and pays for a magnificent tomb in his honor. Donna Maria dies soon afterward and is buried beside her husband. Meanwhile, on the day of the battle, the spirits of Don Munio and his seventy fallen followers appear in Jerusalem, silently pray at the Holy Sepulchre, and vanish, thereby fulfilling their pilgrimage vow even after death.
