Afzal Khan, a former policeman, begs a British official to employ him as a messenger while insisting he is honest. He explains that at the remote Howli police station, he and his fellow officers secretly arranged with local bandits to avoid pursuing them in exchange for occasional scapegoats.

One night, six rifles and the station’s official record book disappear while the policemen sleep. Fearing punishment, the corrupt Havildar stages a fictitious bandit attack: the officers damage the station, trample the grounds, use goat’s blood, and wound themselves. They then arrest villagers against whom they hold grudges and report a fierce raid.

Yunkum Sahib, a vigilant assistant commissioner, listens calmly to their sworn story. He then reveals the missing rifles and record book in his bathroom: he himself had silently visited the station during the night and removed them, exposing the policemen’s negligence, corruption, false testimony, torture, and fabricated arrests. The Havildar and the others are imprisoned, while Afzal escapes on the Havildar’s mare and rides it until it dies. Having finished this self-incriminating tale, Afzal continues flattering the official and confidently presents himself as a suitable new servant.
