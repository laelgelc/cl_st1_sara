On a cold, rainy election day in Dublin, several poorly paid canvassers gather in candidate Richard Tierney’s shabby committee room. While waiting for their wages, they complain about Tierney’s stinginess, discuss their canvassing, suspect one another’s political loyalties, and debate Irish nationalism, corruption, class representation, and a proposed welcome for King Edward VII.

Their bitterness briefly softens when Tierney sends them stout. As they drink, their criticism of him gives way to praise, and their political arguments turn toward Charles Stewart Parnell on the anniversary of his death. Joe Hynes, initially suspected of spying for a rival candidate, returns and recites a fervent poem mourning Parnell as Ireland’s betrayed leader. The men respond with emotional applause and drink in silence, momentarily united by nostalgia for a heroic political past that contrasts with their compromised present.
