Twelve-year-old Prince Lilimond learns that beggars throughout his father’s prosperous kingdom claim to be starving because of royal taxes. When the King dismisses his concerns, Lilimond uses a promised birthday wish to request absolute rule for one day. He plans to distribute a gold piece to every beggar and summons them from across the kingdom.

On the appointed day, so many people gather that the treasury cannot provide gold for everyone. Advised by the Chamberlain, Lilimond examines each claimant individually and discovers that many are healthy idlers, wealthy opportunists, or outright frauds. After he threatens punishment for deception, the impostors flee, leaving only a small number of genuinely needy people, who receive aid from a fraction of one royal coffer.

Realizing that not everyone who begs is truly destitute, Lilimond acknowledges that he misjudged his father’s councilors and returns the crown before his day of rule ends. He later becomes King after his father’s death, having learned to combine compassion with careful judgment.
