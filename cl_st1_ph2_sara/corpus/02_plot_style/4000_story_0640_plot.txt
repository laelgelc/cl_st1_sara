A man awakens near the Stone’s River battlefield believing he is still a twenty-three-year-old Union lieutenant recently struck unconscious by a bullet. In the moonlight, he watches a vast, unnaturally silent army march northward, only for it to vanish by dawn. He fears the Union has lost and seeks his command.

On the road he meets a physician, who is puzzled by his civilian clothes, aged appearance, and questions about a battle long past. The man insists he saw troops only hours earlier, then leaves angrily when the doctor cannot help him.

Growing fatigued, he notices that his hands and face are withered and concludes he must have spent months recovering in a hospital. He then discovers an old, weathered monument commemorating the Battle of Stone’s River in 1862. Looking into a rain-filled pool, he finally sees his aged face and realizes that decades—not months—have passed during his lost consciousness or inexplicable second life. Horrified, he collapses into the water and dies.
