A virtuous young laundress from Portillon attracts the attention of the king’s chamberlain, Monsieur du Fou, who hires her and then sexually assaults her. She seeks justice and demands compensation, but the judge initially dismisses her claim, arguing that a woman could have prevented the act by resisting. To prove otherwise, she uses his own needle-and-thread test against him: after repeatedly distracting and tiring him, she threads the needle when he can no longer keep it moving, demonstrating how persistence and force overcame her resistance.

Convinced, the judge discreetly presents her case at court. The king orders du Fou to pay her one hundred crowns, then several courtiers offer to supply the remainder of the thousand crowns she sought in exchange for her favors. She accepts, earns the full sum within a month, and thereafter refuses further offers. After briefly attracting the king’s interest, she is paid by his jealous mistress to leave for Orléans. Later she repents, performs penance, supports a bed in a leper hospital, and eventually marries Taschereau, becoming known for her wit, resourcefulness, and amorous escapades.
