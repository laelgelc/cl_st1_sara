Peduzzi, an impoverished and already drunken laborer, persuades a young tourist and his wife to join him for an illicit trout-fishing expedition near Cortina. He parades them and their rods conspicuously through town, borrows money for wine, and boasts that his local connections will protect them despite the fishing ban. The wife, already upset after an argument with her husband, resents the outing and eventually abandons it.

At the muddy river, Peduzzi discovers they lack the lead needed to sink the bait, so no fishing takes place. Relieved that he will not be caught breaking the law, the young man shares the wine with Peduzzi and tentatively agrees to try again the next morning. Peduzzi eagerly takes an advance to buy food, bait, and supplies, imagining that his fortunes are improving, but the young man warns that he probably will not return, leaving Peduzzi’s hopeful plans uncertain.
