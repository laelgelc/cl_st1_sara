A novelist injures his ankle while hiking and seeks help at a decaying country estate inhabited by the Van der Knoope family. There he meets young Wilhelmina, her blind great-uncle Peter, and his brother Melchior. Peter proudly speaks of his grandson Fritz, a naval officer whom Wilhelmina is expected to marry, and shows the visitor one of Fritz’s letters. Clues reveal that the letter was fabricated.

The next day, Wilhelmina takes the novelist to the family church and shows him Fritz’s memorial. Fritz drowned years earlier, but Peter has never been told because the truth might kill him. Melchior and Wilhelmina have sustained the illusion by inventing Fritz’s naval career and correspondence, though they struggle to write convincing letters.

Recognizing that the novelist can preserve their deception, Wilhelmina asks for his help. He agrees to assume Fritz’s voice and sends Peter a letter every month until Peter dies. Even after the letters are no longer needed, the narrator continues to imagine himself as Fritz and remembers Wilhelmina waiting amid the estate’s ruins.
