During the Civil War, Captain Parrol Hartroy commands a remote, fortified Union post in Tennessee. He recognizes a supposed civilian carrying a forged pass as Dramer Brune, a Confederate spy. Hartroy arrests him and discovers detailed intelligence about the fort and its defenses, making Brune liable to immediate execution.

The next morning, Hartroy reveals that Brune was once a Union soldier who deserted after being denied a discharge based on changed convictions. Years earlier, while awaiting execution, Brune had been guarded by Hartroy, who fell asleep at his post—an offense punishable by death. Brune could have escaped and left Hartroy to face execution, but instead compassionately remained and woke him before the relief guard arrived, saving Hartroy’s life at the apparent cost of his own.

Although overwhelmed by gratitude and guilt, Hartroy concludes that duty requires him to enforce the sentence against Brune as a deserter and spy. He orders Brune shot before the assembled troops. As the execution volley sounds, Hartroy kills himself, unwilling to retain the life Brune once gave him after ordering Brune’s death. Both men are buried without military honors in forgotten graves.
