Camille, an orphan of unknown parentage raised in a New Orleans convent, refuses adoption at fifteen because she distrusts the prospective father. Seeking safety and belonging, she takes the veil and becomes Sister Josepha, but soon finds convent life monotonous, oppressive, and ill-suited to her youth and passionate nature.

At a grand cathedral celebration, she is dazzled by the color and excitement of the outside world. A brief, compassionate glance from a handsome young soldier awakens romantic longing, and she becomes consumed by thoughts of him and of freedom. She plans to slip away after Mass and disappear into the city.

On the night before her escape, however, she overhears two nuns discussing how vulnerable she would be outside the convent: she has no surname, family, nationality, friends, or protection, while her beauty could expose her to exploitation. Recognizing the dangers and uncertainty awaiting her, she abandons her plan. After Mass, she pauses at the convent entrance and looks longingly toward the city, then tearfully follows the other sisters back inside.
