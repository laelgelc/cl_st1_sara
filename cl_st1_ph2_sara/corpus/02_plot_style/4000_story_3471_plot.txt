A devoted rural couple allows their stubborn, nearly six-year-old son to choose his own religion. After observing several denominations, he clearly prefers Episcopal services, but his fear of baptism—confused in his mind with vaccination—keeps him from being christened. His parents have already endured being revaccinated, along with their servant and several animals, in a failed attempt to persuade him to accept a vaccine.

When a splinter causes the boy’s foot to swell, his terrified parents fear lockjaw and summon an Episcopal rector for an emergency baptism. The boy escapes to the top of a fragile bean arbor, where no adult can reach him. To coax him down, his parents are rebaptized as Episcopalians, but he remains aloft and demands that others be baptized too. Finally, the rector performs the ceremony from the porch while rain falls on the boy as baptismal water. Asked to name himself, the child insists on taking his father’s full name, including “Senior.”

Once the rector leaves, the boy climbs down and promises that next time he will submit properly. His father explains that baptism cannot be repeated, prompting the boy to wonder what will happen if his baptism “doesn’t take,” just as a vaccination might not. His parents fondly conclude that, judging by his sweet behavior, it has already begun to take effect.
