A small-town barber recounts the life of Jim Kendall, a drunken, neglectful bully celebrated locally for his cruel practical jokes. Jim torments his family, humiliates a mentally disabled young man named Paul, and pursues Julie Gregg despite her rejection. Jealous of Julie’s affection for the new doctor, Ralph Stair, Jim imitates the doctor’s voice to lure her to his empty office, where he and his friends publicly mock and chase her.

Paul tells the doctor what happened. Enraged, Stair remarks that anyone capable of such cruelty ought not to be allowed to live. Soon afterward, Jim takes Paul duck hunting and lets the inexperienced young man handle his gun. Paul shoots and kills Jim, claiming it was an accident. As coroner, Stair accepts this explanation without an inquest. The barber continues to remember Jim fondly as a great comedian, apparently oblivious both to Jim’s viciousness and to the possibility that Paul deliberately killed him after interpreting the doctor’s words literally.
