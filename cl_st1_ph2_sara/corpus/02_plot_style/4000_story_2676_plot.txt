An aging former colleague visits a prosperous businessman and mentions that his daughters, while visiting war graves in Belgium, found the grave of the businessman’s only son near that of their own brother. After the visitor leaves, the businessman shuts himself in his office, intending to weep. He recalls how his son had been destined to inherit the business before being killed in the war, but discovers that six years later he can no longer summon the overwhelming grief he once felt.

His attention shifts to a fly struggling in his inkpot. He rescues it and watches admiringly as it cleans itself, then repeatedly drops ink onto it to test its courage and persistence. Though the fly recovers several times, the final drop kills it. Suddenly seized by wretchedness, the businessman disposes of the body, summons his messenger for fresh blotting paper, and then realizes he has forgotten what he had been thinking about.
