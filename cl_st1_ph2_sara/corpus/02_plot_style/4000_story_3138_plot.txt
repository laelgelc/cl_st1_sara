During a brutal smallpox winter in a frontier settlement, the resilient Father de Smet tends the sick alongside Ninon, a worldly Frenchwoman known for her beauty, cooking, and unconventional life. Though the priest worries about her soul, he comes to respect her courage and compassion as they nurse dying trappers together.

As Easter approaches, Ninon offers two rare lilies for the neglected chapel altar, along with her mother’s lace to decorate the Virgin. Already showing signs of illness, she helps the community clean and restore the chapel but later collapses from smallpox. Summoned to her cabin at midnight, Father de Smet administers the last rites while her final concern is that he not forget the lilies. By morning, Ninon is dead, and the two white lilies bloom on the wilderness altar.
