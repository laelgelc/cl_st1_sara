Joe Larrabee, an aspiring painter, and Delia Caruthers, a promising pianist, meet in New York, fall in love, marry, and pursue their artistic studies while living happily in a small flat. When their savings run out, Delia claims she has found a well-paid music pupil so Joe can continue studying. Soon afterward, Joe says he has begun selling paintings to a buyer from Peoria.

Each secretly brings home wages while sustaining the fiction that their artistic career is succeeding. When Delia returns with a badly burned hand, she admits she could not find pupils and has been ironing shirts at a laundry. Joe then reveals that he discovered the truth because he works in the same laundry’s engine room and had sent materials to treat her burn. His supposed art buyer, like Delia’s invented pupil and wealthy family, never existed. They realize that both willingly sacrificed their artistic ambitions out of love for each other, concluding that no service is too hard not merely when one loves art, but when one loves.
