A seminary student secretly leaves a shabby boarding house because of its terrible food and poor conditions, asking his roommate, Briggs, to smuggle out his bag. When the landlady, Mrs. Betterson, catches Briggs returning, he angrily reveals why the student fled and condemns her cooking and management.

Ashamed of his outburst, Briggs overhears Mrs. Betterson lamenting her poverty and incompetence while her sickly daughter promises to help. He learns that the family is struggling to maintain the boarding house while hoping the other daughter will marry and improve their circumstances. Moved by pity, Briggs decides to remain despite the hardships. Another boarder offers to stay if his overdue bill can be settled, while Wallace, a medical student to whom the landlady already owes money, goes downstairs to comfort her and apparently provide further financial help.
