Kansas wheat farmer Sam Lewiston is ruined when speculator Truslow’s bear campaign drives wheat down to sixty-two cents a bushel, far below the cost of growing it. Sam and his wife abandon their ranch and move to Chicago.

Later, trader Hornung corners the wheat market and forces the price above a dollar. He sells Truslow one hundred thousand bushels on the condition that it be exported, but Truslow secretly diverts the grain back into Chicago and uses it to attack Hornung’s corner. Hornung’s brokers initially fear an unknown supply is flooding the market, but the grain is traced to Truslow’s railway maneuver, allowing Hornung to counter it and preserve his position.

Meanwhile, the Lewistons sink into urban poverty. Sam, once ruined because wheat was too cheap, must now stand in a charity bread line because Hornung’s corner has made wheat—and therefore bread—too expensive. The struggle between bulls and bears enriches speculators while ordinary producers and consumers suffer at either extreme.
