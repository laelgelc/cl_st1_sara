Eeldrop and Appleplex rent rooms in a quiet, disreputable London neighborhood overlooking a police station. Periodically escaping their ordinary lives, they observe arrested criminals and curious crowds in hopes of understanding people as unique individuals rather than as social types. Their observations lead them to debate how classification obscures private experience, tragedy, and the irreversible consequences of human actions. Despite their resistance to labels, Eeldrop eventually reveals that he is a suburban bank clerk with a wife, children, and garden, while Appleplex maintains extensive files for a survey of contemporary society.

On a later Sunday evening, they discuss Edith, nicknamed Scheherazade, a woman with a colorful history of abrupt marriage, divorce, theatrical work, travel, poetry, and movement through fashionable social circles. Eeldrop initially romanticizes her as a passionate seeker of experience, but Appleplex argues that her apparent mystery is calculated rather than profound. Together they conclude that Edith is neither a true artist nor a tragic figure: she consciously manages her experiences, presents herself too completely, and allows nothing genuinely unforeseen to happen to her. Their conversation ends with plans to examine social circles and society further, though Appleplex is first due to dine with their mutual acquaintance, Mrs. Howexden.
