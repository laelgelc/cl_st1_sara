Archibald Jordan, a prosperous, vain, and fastidious Islington bachelor, spends twenty-five years moving among lodgings whenever a landlady begins to treat him as ordinary, relishing his power to upset them by leaving. He finally finds perfect rooms under the efficient widow Mrs. Elderfield and resolves to stay, only to learn that she intends to move to Wood Green. Growing attached to her and fearful of losing his comforts, he marries her and follows her there.

Once married, Jordan discovers that his formerly accommodating landlady is an exacting, strong-willed wife who regulates his habits as rigidly as he once regulated those of his landladies. His efforts to assert authority end in humiliating defeat, and he feels trapped by marriage, alienated from Wood Green, and nostalgic for his old Islington freedom. After staying away from home for two nights, he returns expecting confrontation, but his wife treats his absence as irrelevant. He realizes that she may permit him freedom outside the house so long as he submits to her domestic rule, leaving him with a faint hope of recovering some part of his former independent life.
