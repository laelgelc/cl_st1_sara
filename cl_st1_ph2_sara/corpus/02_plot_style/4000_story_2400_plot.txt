A poor widow, Mary Ryan, lives with her four children near Lisnavoura, a hill believed to be haunted by fairies. One autumn evening, while she is away gathering turf and her eldest daughter Nell tends the house, the three younger children play outside until after sunset.

A magnificent old-fashioned carriage drawn by enormous white horses appears. Its malicious dwarf servants frighten the children, but a beautiful lady inside charms them and chooses five-year-old Billy, a golden-haired boy. She lifts him into the carriage while another sinister woman watches. The lady then distracts Billy’s siblings by dropping apples along the road. When they have followed as far as a crossroads near Lisnavoura, a whirlwind of dust envelops the carriage, which vanishes with Billy inside.

The terrified children return and tell their mother, who searches desperately but finds no trace of him. Over the following months, Billy occasionally appears at the cabin door, smiling and silently beckoning to his siblings, but always disappears when they approach. The visits eventually stop.

Nearly a year and a half after the abduction, Billy returns once before dawn, barefoot, ragged, pale, and starving. He silently warms himself at the dying fire, but flees when his sister awakens. He is never seen again. Neither fairy doctors nor a priest can recover him, and his family mourns him as dead, believing he remains hidden somewhere within haunted Lisnavoura.
