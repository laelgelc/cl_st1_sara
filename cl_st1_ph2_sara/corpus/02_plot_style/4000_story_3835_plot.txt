An orchid collector meets Butcher, a scarred former explorer, who recounts how he discovered perfectly preserved eggs and bones of the extinct giant bird Aepyornis in a Madagascar swamp. After his native assistants steal their canoe, Butcher shoots one and pursues the other, only to find him dead. He then drifts at sea for ten days, surviving by eating two of the ancient eggs, one of which contains a living embryo.

Butcher reaches a remote atoll with the final egg, which hatches into a young Aepyornis. He raises the bird as his only companion, while his unpaid salary continues accumulating because his employers cannot contact him. Over two years, the bird grows to an enormous size. It eventually becomes aggressive, scars Butcher’s face, and repeatedly drives him into the lagoon or up palm trees.

Unable to reconcile with or escape the creature, Butcher traps its legs with a weighted cord and kills it. He immediately regrets the act and is overwhelmed by loneliness. A passing yachtsman eventually rescues him, and Butcher sells the bird’s skeleton, which is later recognized as an exceptionally large Aepyornis specimen.
