A well-meaning but meddlesome man devotes his life to helping, instructing, and managing others, invariably making matters worse. From childhood, his attempts to teach animals, supervise other children, and assist classmates lead to injury, punishment, and confusion. As an adult, he continues offering unwanted advice in sports, travel, social affairs, illnesses, weddings, funerals, and domestic disputes.

The narrator meets him on an omnibus, where the man intervenes in a minor fare transaction and turns it into a chaotic dispute involving passengers, the conductor, and the police. He then befriends the reluctant narrator and persuades him to challenge an ordinary water-rate bill as a matter of principle. The resulting legal case costs the narrator nearly fifty pounds.

Although their friendship fades, the narrator continues witnessing the man’s disastrous benevolence: he forces tedious games on party guests, sends elderly women onto wrong trains, terrifies children, gives unsuitable food to invalids, arranges miserable excursions, mismanages weddings, and offers tactless consolation at funerals. His intentions remain unfailingly kind, but his compulsion to interfere in other people’s affairs produces universal confusion and misery.
