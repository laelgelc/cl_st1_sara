Barbara Uhler, fiercely protective of her independence, interprets her husband Herman’s reasonable expectations about managing their household as tyranny. Encouraged by friends who preach women’s rights in extreme terms, she neglects domestic responsibilities and leaves cooking and childcare to incompetent servants, while resisting Herman’s complaints.

After Barbara abandons the family dinner to attend a meeting, Herman begins mirroring her behavior. He neglects his business for drinking and socializing, delegates important work to careless clerks, and answers her objections with the same arguments she used to defend her household neglect. His business eventually fails, plunging the family into poverty.

Barbara’s friends urge her to leave Herman, but their vicious portrayal of him shocks her into recognizing her own failures. Returning home, she discovers that a servant has deserted the children and that the youngest has been burned while playing with fire. Learning that Herman wished the children had no mother so he could care for them himself forces Barbara to confront the consequences of her neglect.

Repentant, Barbara personally tends the home, prepares good meals, restores order, and devotes herself to her husband and children. Herman responds by staying home, becoming affectionate, and renewing his diligence at work. The couple reconcile, their circumstances improve, and Herman rapidly regains prosperity. Thereafter, both accept responsibility for their respective duties, creating a loving, comfortable, and mutually respectful home.
