A working-class father refuses to spend time with his twelve-year-old daughter, Lizzie, and sends her into the dangerous streets instead. Years later, Liz has become a hard-drinking young woman engaged to Kid Mullaly, who first introduced her to alcohol. When Kid takes another woman to a dance to provoke her jealousy, Liz confronts him and stabs him, then flees a pursuing mob and drowns herself in the East River.

In a dreamlike afterlife, Liz is brought to judgment for murder and suicide but is discharged. The court declares that the true guilty party is the neglectful father who chose his newspaper and rest over caring for his child, leaving her to be shaped by the streets.
