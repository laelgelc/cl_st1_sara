A baseball manager’s matchmaking succeeds when his star pitcher, the naïve but formidable Rube Hurtle, becomes engaged to the spirited fan Nan Brown. Rube’s happiness transforms his pitching, helping Worcester surge back into the pennant race. Although the manager and captain worry that a wedding and honeymoon during a difficult road trip will disrupt the club, Rube and Nan marry on July Fourth. Rube then pitches both games of a crucial doubleheader against Buffalo, including a perfect no-hit, no-run game, and carries Worcester into first place. His performance also earns the manager a lucrative agreement to sell him to a major-league team after the season.

Nan accompanies Rube on Worcester’s road trip, which becomes a celebrated traveling honeymoon. The players relentlessly prank and tease the newlyweds—stealing and rearranging their luggage, staging embarrassing encounters, separating them before trains, and switching berth numbers in a sleeper car. Nan often defeats the players’ mockery with her confidence and flirtatious wit, while Rube alternates between embarrassment, jealousy, and anger. Despite the chaos, the team plays brilliantly and maintains its lead.

Near the end of the trip, Rube and Nan have their first serious quarrel. To provoke him, Nan attends the final game with a former admirer. Opposing players and fans taunt Rube about being controlled by his wife until he loses his temper, deliberately hits batters, throws the ball at the opposing bench, and starts a brawl involving players and police. As officers remove him through an angry crowd, Nan forces her way onto the trolley carrying him away and clings protectively to his side. The crisis reconciles the couple, bringing their turbulent baseball honeymoon to an end.
