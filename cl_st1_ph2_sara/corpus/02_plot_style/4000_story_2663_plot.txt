After burying her beloved young grandson Lennie, an elderly cleaning woman goes to work at a careless literary gentleman’s filthy flat. As she cleans, memories of Lennie mingle with recollections of a lifetime of hardship: harsh domestic service, the deaths of seven children and her husband, years of poverty and caregiving, and the loss or departure of most of her remaining family.

She has always endured suffering without crying, but Lennie’s painful death finally overwhelms her. Leaving the flat in a daze, she wanders through the cold streets, desperate to find a private place where she can release all the grief she has suppressed for years. She cannot cry at home, at work, on a bench, or anywhere public without disturbing others or attracting suspicion. As rain begins to fall, she realizes there is nowhere in the world where she can grieve undisturbed.
