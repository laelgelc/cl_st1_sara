In 1510, the poor young Winzy returns to the service of the alchemist Cornelius Agrippa so he can earn enough money to marry his childhood love, Bertha. While tending one of Agrippa’s experiments, jealousy over Bertha’s apparent interest in a wealthy rival drives Winzy to drink half of a potion he believes will cure him of love. Instead, it fills him with extraordinary vitality and confidence, enabling him to reclaim Bertha from her controlling guardian and marry her.

Five years later, the dying Agrippa reveals that the potion was actually the Elixir of Immortality. Winzy initially dismisses the claim, but as decades pass without his appearance changing, his perpetual youth inspires suspicion and ostracism. Bertha ages while he remains physically twenty, becoming jealous and distressed by their growing disparity. After Winzy explains his secret and offers to leave for her protection, she chooses to remain with him. They move to France, where he faithfully cares for her through old age, paralysis, and death.

For the next three centuries, Winzy wanders alone, alienated from humanity and tormented by endless youth. He remains uncertain whether he is truly immortal or merely extraordinarily long-lived, since he drank only half the elixir. Though he longs for death, he fears suicide and cannot bring himself to involve another person in killing him. At age 323, he resolves to undertake an expedition into deadly polar conditions, hoping either to perish and gain freedom or to survive, prove his immortality, and achieve lasting fame before seeking a more decisive means of destroying his body.
