During a Civil War battle, a general spitefully orders the highly praised Captain Coulter to place a single cannon in an exposed mountain pass and engage twelve Confederate guns near a plantation house. Coulter reacts with visible anguish but obeys, repeatedly replacing destroyed guns while his crews suffer horrific casualties. His accurate fire damages the house and eventually helps force the Confederate rear guard to withdraw.

Suspecting the general’s order was motivated by a past grievance involving Coulter’s Southern wife, the colonel stops the suicidal action. After occupying the plantation, Federal officers discover Coulter in its cellar, cradling his wife and baby, both killed by artillery fire through the floor. Coulter reveals that the shelled house was his own home.
