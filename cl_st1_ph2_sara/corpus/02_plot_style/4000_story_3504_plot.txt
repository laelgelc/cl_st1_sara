At seventeen, Ariel Tabor attends a dance in a poorly altered wedding gown, hoping at last to be admired and accepted by Canaan’s fashionable young people. Instead, the other girls mock her appearance, the young men avoid her, and an unwilling partner humiliates her further when she trips during their awkward dance. She retreats to the veranda and confides in her equally ostracized childhood friend, Joe Louden, lamenting that neither of them can enjoy the easy happiness of their peers.

The evening ends abruptly when Ariel learns that her estranged great-uncle Jonas has died. He leaves a fortune to Ariel and her grandfather, enabling her to leave Canaan and live abroad.

Years later, Ariel returns transformed into an elegant, beautiful, socially accomplished young woman. Joe, recently returned and still isolated from the town, encounters her but does not recognize her until she reveals herself. As they walk through Canaan together, the townspeople stare in astonishment, and men who once ignored her become eager admirers. Ariel stays with Judge Pike, who manages her property, and immediately becomes the center of the town’s social attention, completing her dramatic rise from mocked outsider to celebrated beauty.
