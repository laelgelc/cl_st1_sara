The story uses a retrospective first-person narrator who is both participant and social observer. His voice is educated, restrained and morally alert, recording the life of the ship with the assurance of a traveller accustomed to reading class, manners and character. He generally remains at the edge of events, allowing encounters and contrasts to carry the emotional burden, but his reflective asides reveal sympathy for the dispossessed and unease about his own privileged position. The narration moves easily from panoramic description to private meditation, as when the physically divided decks become an image of rich and poor inhabiting separate worlds within the same vulnerable vessel.

The diction is formal and distinctly period-inflected, combining maritime vocabulary, imperial geography and class-specific social language. Words such as “saloon-deck,” “hurricane-deck,” “fore-companion,” “chronometer” and “crow’s-nest” give the setting practical solidity, while terms such as “propitious,” “penumbra,” “apostrophised” and “expostulatory” create a cultivated literary register. This elevated narration is offset by colloquial dialogue, especially Cockney phrasing, commercial bluntness and the millionaire’s aggressive rhetoric. Social identity is therefore conveyed through speech as much as through explicit description.

Sentence structure is often long, balanced and semicolon-rich. Clauses accumulate qualifications, contrasts and parenthetical observations, reproducing the narrator’s habit of weighing appearances against moral realities. Dashes are frequent and flexible: they mark historical asides, corrections, interruptions, emotional turns and delayed revelations. At moments of crisis, however, the prose shortens sharply. The final cries of “England!” and “England, baby—England!” derive their force from standing against the more elaborate syntax surrounding them. Repetition is used sparingly but decisively, whether in the pacing figures who are “pacing and turning and pacing again,” the repeated social distinctions between decks, or the father’s desperate invocation of England.

The dominant tone is compassionate but controlled, with irony directed chiefly at wealth, vanity and patriotic self-interest. Mr. Olstein is rendered through satirical physical detail: his perspiration, bristling hand-hairs, flashing diamonds and habitual grievances make his indignation almost grotesquely visible. His loud profession of love for the British flag is implicitly contrasted with the narrator’s memory of a soldier’s death and, later, with the baker’s deeply felt attachment to England. The story thus distinguishes performative patriotism from sacrificial or instinctive belonging without stating the judgement didactically.

Characterisation depends on selective, emblematic detail. The third-class passengers first appear collectively as a “dejected, wistful, whispering throng,” while the consumptive baker is sketched through his hollow chest, neck pits, ragged beard, blush and oversized rosette. These details disclose illness, humiliation and fragile dignity before his history is told. Mr. Olstein’s diamonds and gold-rimmed glasses similarly embody his wealth and limited moral vision. The narrator often lets gestures expose character: one passenger silently intercepts the collector after walking away; Olstein retrieves the overlooked sovereign; the baker tears his child from its cot and holds it toward the distant light.

Dialogue is naturalistic, socially marked and frequently allowed to condemn or dignify the speaker without overt authorial intervention. Olstein’s speech is expansive, argumentative and full of rhetorical questions, appeals and generalisations. The baker speaks plainly, with modest qualifications and practical concerns. His account of returning to England to die is delivered “in the most matter-of-fact way,” creating pathos through understatement rather than lamentation. The gap between what he says calmly and what the reader understands produces much of the story’s emotional power.

Structurally, the narrative is built from a sequence of contrasts and delayed convergences. It begins with the division between first-class gaiety and third-class failure, moves through the awkward sports and the baker’s personal history, then brings both classes into parallel anticipation of the Eddystone. On the saloon deck, the lighthouse is the object of a sweepstake and an occasion for Olstein’s greed; at the bow, it is the first visible sign of home. The same flash therefore means money to one man and homeland, mortality, family and hope to another. This juxtaposition supplies the climax without requiring explicit commentary.

Imagery is economical but symbolically charged. The ship is a compressed social world whose thin partitions seem absolute until the possibility of shipwreck exposes their artificiality. Darkness, waves and shared propulsion suggest a common human fate obscured by class arrangements. The Eddystone light operates both literally and symbolically: its intermittent flash marks safe navigation, the approach to England and the fragile promise of return. The final tears “baptize” the child, lending the scene a restrained religious resonance and turning the father’s patriotic cry into a blessing, inheritance and farewell.

Pacing alternates between summary and carefully staged scenes. Broad historical and social exposition establishes the voyage, after which dialogue-heavy episodes narrow the focus. The baker’s biography is compressed into a plain recital, while the lighthouse sequence expands minute by minute, using watches, guesses, distances and passing seconds to create suspense. That technical suspense is then redirected into emotional revelation: the apparent climax of Olstein winning the sweepstake becomes merely a prelude to the baker’s appearance with his child.

Overall, the style combines social realism, maritime exactness, moral irony and restrained sentiment. Its pathos arises from contrast, understatement and symbolic action rather than overt emotional instruction. The prose is humane but unsparing, exposing the cruelty of class indifference while preserving the dignity of ordinary speech and feeling. The ending is openly moving, yet its repetition, brevity and concrete action keep it from becoming diffuse: a distant light, a dying father, a protesting child and a single word carry the story’s full emotional and thematic weight.
