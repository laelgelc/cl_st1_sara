A newly married couple, Bob and Mary, rent a cheap, dilapidated London house from the harsh Mr. Gradder and begin renovating it themselves. Gradder repeatedly insists on helping with the work, but they refuse, suspecting he has a hidden motive.

Bob learns that the house’s former tenant, Giles Armer, was a wealthy miser who left everything to any surviving children who formally claimed it within one month of his death; otherwise, the fortune would pass to Gradder. Armer had hidden money throughout his two rented houses, explaining Gradder’s eagerness to search their home.

When Bob reads a copy of the will, Mary recognizes Giles Armer as her long-absent father. With the deadline only hours away, the couple immediately files the required declaration before a magistrate. They then search their house but find no hidden treasure.

Keeping Mary’s claim secret, Bob accepts £100 from Gradder to surrender the lease. While Gradder frantically tears the house apart looking for money, Bob and Mary consult a solicitor and gather evidence proving Mary’s identity and relationship to Armer. Gradder later confronts them, furious that the house contains nothing, only to learn that his suspicious persistence alerted Mary in time to claim her father’s entire fortune.
