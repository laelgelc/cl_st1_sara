Eleven-year-old Patsy Burns, a poor, neglected street boy, befriends a group of Harvard athletes practicing the shot put and becomes their devoted helper. He especially idolizes champion shot-putter “Bull” Fosgill and resolves to attend Harvard and become a shot-putter himself. Moved by his ambition and difficult circumstances, the athletes secretly plan to support his education.

During a crucial dual meet, a fouled shot hurtles toward the unsuspecting Fosgill. Patsy pushes him out of its path but is struck in the chest and fatally injured. Harvard wins the meet, and Patsy briefly regains consciousness in the hospital. Reassured that Fosgill won, that he himself could have become a great shot-putter, and that his sacrifice helped Harvard triumph, Patsy dies peacefully after saying goodbye to his hero.
