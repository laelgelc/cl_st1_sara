After his shiftless father dies, fourteen-year-old Ellis Duncan must support himself and his frail mother. Though strong, educated, and eager to work, he is rejected by employers because of his father’s reputation. His wealthy, hardhearted uncle Timothy also refuses to hire him, assuming Ellis is equally lazy and unreliable.

Desperate for employment, Ellis accepts a poorly paid job clearing an overgrown pasture of deeply rooted elderberry bushes. He labors through the summer with remarkable persistence. Halfway through, he receives a far better offer but refuses it because he promised the landowner he would finish the pasture.

Unbeknownst to Ellis, Timothy observes his hard work and learns of his refusal to break his word. Once Ellis completes the difficult job, Timothy admits he misjudged him and offers him steady employment, along with a home for Ellis’s mother. Ellis accepts on the condition that Timothy never again insult his dead father. Timothy agrees and suggests that, if Ellis remains industrious and dependable, he may eventually regard him as his own son.
