Colonel Voznitsin, a forty-year-old officer increasingly haunted by aging and death, revisits the Moscow places of his childhood, hoping to recover the bittersweet emotions of youth. Instead, he finds both the city and himself changed, and his inability to feel the expected nostalgia convinces him that old age has begun.

Continuing toward Crimea, he boards a nearly empty steamer at Odessa on Easter Eve. There he meets Elena Lvova, whom he gradually recognizes as Lenotchka Yurlof, his childhood friend and first love. They exchange news of the intervening decades: her brother and mother are dead, her sister has become a doctor, Elena is respectably married with a daughter, and Voznitsin has remained single.

Their reunion revives Voznitsin’s memories of falling passionately in love with Lenotchka as a schoolboy. He recalls their youthful games, her sudden transformation into a young woman, his shy adoration, and an Easter night when he confessed his love and kissed her beneath the trees near her family’s home. Though she afterward avoided being alone with him, the moment remained sacred in his memory.

Elena’s daughter, also named Lenotchka and now the same age her mother was that Easter, joins them on deck. Seeing the younger woman’s beauty and vitality, Voznitsin finally experiences the tender connection to the past that had eluded him in Moscow. He realizes that life continually renews itself: individuals die, but something of them survives through later generations, memories, thoughts, and actions. Comforted by this vision of continuity, he and Elena exchange affectionate farewells, smiling through tears.
