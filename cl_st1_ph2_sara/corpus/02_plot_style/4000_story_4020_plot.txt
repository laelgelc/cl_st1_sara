An aging, wandering poet named Hanrahan returns to the villages beneath Slieve Echtge, where he feels increasingly drawn toward an unseen supernatural world. One evening he encounters Winny Byrne, an ancient beggar believed to have lost her wits to the Sidhe, proclaiming that she is eternally young and beautiful. Compelled to follow her, Hanrahan falls into a bog drain and reaches her isolated cabin badly injured.

While resting outside, he dreams or witnesses four old women playing cards and quarreling, their suits symbolizing courage, power, knowledge, and pleasure. Winny later finds him, tends his wounds, and shelters him. Over several days Hanrahan grows steadily weaker as music, joyful voices, pale lights, and shadowy beings fill the cabin.

Near death, he mistakes the clashing of supernatural swords for heavenly music. Ordinary objects in the cabin—a pot, stone, knife, and staff—appear to him as the mystical Cauldron, Stone, Sword, and Spear, and he realizes he has finally asked a long-awaited question. Winny then transforms in his vision into one of the immortal Voices dwelling among the broken, dying, and mad. She claims him as hers forever and surrounds him with lights resembling both wedding torches and funeral candles.

Hanrahan dies in the cabin. At sunrise Winny resumes her wandering song and never returns. Two days later turf cutters discover Hanrahan’s body, and the local people give the great poet an honorable wake and burial.
