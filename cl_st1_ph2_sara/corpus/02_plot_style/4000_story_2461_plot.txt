Subienkow, a Polish exile and adventurer, is captured after the Indigenous people of Nulato revolt against the brutal Russian fur traders who enslaved, whipped, and extorted them. Having spent years fleeing across Siberia and Alaska in hopes of returning to Europe, he now watches his companions suffer prolonged torture and realizes that he will be next. He accepts that he cannot escape death but desperately wants to avoid dying in agony and humiliation.

He tells Chief Makamuk that he possesses a secret medicine capable of making skin impervious to blades. In exchange for the formula, he bargains for his freedom, supplies, dogs, an armed escort, and the chief’s daughter. To prove the medicine works, he proposes rubbing it on his neck and allowing Makamuk to strike him three times with an axe. He then concocts a meaningless mixture and performs a false ritual, making his captors believe in its power.

Subienkow places his neck on a log and challenges Makamuk to strike with all his strength. The axe severs his head in one blow. The captors realize that the medicine was a trick: Subienkow never expected to survive but manipulated Makamuk into granting him a swift, clean death instead of torture. The people laugh at the humiliated chief, whose failure earns him the lasting name “Lost Face.”
