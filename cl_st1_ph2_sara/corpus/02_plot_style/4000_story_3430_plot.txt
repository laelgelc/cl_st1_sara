Joshua Considine and his friend, Dr. Gerald Burleigh, visit a nearby gipsy camp to test its fortune-telling. A gipsy queen examines Joshua’s palm and warns him to leave his beloved wife, Mary, forever. When he dismisses her warning, she predicts that he will murder Mary, with blood flowing through the broken circle of a severed ring.

Despite Gerald’s advice to keep the prophecy secret, Joshua jokingly tells Mary. She faints and becomes terrified. Hoping to disprove the prediction, Mary secretly visits the gipsy queen, only to be told that she will lie at her husband’s feet while his hands are stained with blood. Her fear deepens, and she secretly blunts every knife in the house.

When Joshua discovers what she has done, he is hurt by her lack of trust and decides to confront the superstition. He retrieves a razor-sharp Ghurka knife to cut roses. Seeing him carrying it, Mary panics and faints. Joshua throws down the knife and tries to catch her, but she falls onto the blade, cutting her hand and severing her wedding ring. Although the accident closely fulfills the prophecy’s imagery, Mary concludes that this minor fulfillment means the predicted murder will never happen, restoring her peace and trust in her husband.
