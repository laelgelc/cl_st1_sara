A lonely boy named Ernest, stifled by the well-meaning discipline of his aunts, becomes fascinated by a marble child supporting the font in church. He pities the figure, imagines playing with it, and gradually sees it move, first reaching toward him during a stormy service and later appearing alive in the woods. In dreams and at church, the marble child becomes his secret companion.

Desperate for friendship, Ernest runs away to the church, intending to live with the marble child. There it comes alive and offers to keep him forever, but warns that lightning will soon destroy the church and kill both the pew-opener and Ernest’s approaching aunt. Though Ernest loves the marble child more than anyone, he chooses to leave and warn them, knowing this means losing his companion.

Ernest leads his aunt and the pew-opener outside just before lightning strikes and the church collapses. He becomes gravely ill from the shock, while the marble child is presumed crushed to powder. Yet the pew-opener insists she saw a radiant, unearthly child holding Ernest’s hand as they escaped, and a white marble finger is later found clenched in Ernest’s hand.
