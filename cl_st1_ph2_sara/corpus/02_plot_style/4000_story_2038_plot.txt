A narrator becomes acquainted with the cynical, fashion-conscious aristocrat William “Blasé Billy” Drayton, a man bored by nearly everything. To his friends’ surprise, Billy genuinely falls in love with Gerty Lovell, a fashionable but seemingly shallow society woman with no fortune. Marriage transforms them both: Billy abandons much of his detached pose, while Gerty becomes happier and more affectionate, and their relationship deepens into sincere devotion.

When Billy falls gravely ill during the London social season, Gerty loses all interest in society and remains at home to help nurse him. Billy, however, worries that his illness is depriving her of the fashionable life she loves. To ease his mind, Gerty follows the nurse’s suggestion and pretends to attend dinners, balls, and concerts. Each day she dresses elaborately, departs by carriage, secretly returns home, waits alone, and later reenters as though coming back from an engagement. She then invents cheerful accounts of her supposed social triumphs, making Billy happier in his final days.

Outsiders repeatedly see her leaving and returning in evening dress and conclude that she is heartlessly enjoying herself while her husband is dying. The narrator discovers the truth, but Billy dies before his return to London, with society believing Gerty had to be summoned from a ball. When the narrator offers to defend her reputation by revealing her sacrifice, Gerty refuses, unwilling to publicize the intimate secret of their love. No longer ruled by social opinion, she says that what others think does not matter.
