Archbishop Baldwin of Treves, having subdued the nobles along the Moselle, rides with a small escort toward his summer palace at Cochem. He intends to force the widowed Countess Laurette von Starkenburg to marry one of his loyal knights so that her strategically placed castle will come under his control.

On the road, forty armed retainers surround Baldwin’s party and compel him to accept the countess’s invitation to dine. At Starkenburg, his attempt to send a captain secretly to Treves for an army is thwarted, leaving him and his followers trapped. Mindful that one of his predecessors died mysteriously in the castle, Baldwin initially distrusts his hostess, but she proves composed, courteous, and fearless.

The countess asks permission to build a castle near Trarbach for her son, a pledge that Baldwin will not interfere with its construction, and a thousand gold pieces from his treasury to finance it. When Baldwin angrily accuses her of holding him for ransom, she calmly admits she cannot release him unless he agrees. She explains that his many defeated enemies would gladly support his permanent confinement, while his army, deprived of its commanding leader, would quickly disintegrate.

Impressed by her courage, strategy, and accurate judgment of his power, Baldwin grants all her requests. He even expresses romantic admiration for her, lamenting that Church law prevents him from seeking her hand. The new castle is consequently built with the archbishop’s money rather than the Starkenburg family’s.
