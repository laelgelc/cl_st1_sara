In wartime Berlin, Frau Ebermann takes to bed with influenza, obsessively ensuring that every object in her room remains perfectly arranged. Feverish, she begins seeing five dirty, exhausted children whom her servant Anna cannot perceive. The children say their villages were destroyed by German troops and that they have come to Berlin to wait for their dead families, along with thousands of other displaced or murdered children.

Frau Ebermann refuses to accept their account, repeating official claims that few children were harmed and blaming any deaths on careless children running beneath military horses and guns. When she finally sees the children’s wounds, they politely leave to wait elsewhere. Anna later finds Frau Ebermann deliriously scrubbing the floor with the radiator’s lace cover, convinced it is stained with their blood, while insisting there could have been only five victims and trying to prepare the room for God’s arrival.
