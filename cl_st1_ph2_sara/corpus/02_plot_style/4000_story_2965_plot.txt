After her aunt’s death, sheltered and naïve Alethia Debchance travels to visit her distant cousin Robert Bludward, a parliamentary candidate. Influenced by melodramatic novels, she overhears hostile voters and reads a partisan newspaper article denouncing Robert as corrupt and wicked. She interprets ordinary political insults and threats literally, becoming convinced that Robert is a dangerous villain plotting against his opponent, Sir John Chobham.

Hoping to warn Sir John, Alethia visits a local reading room, only to find another partisan newspaper portraying him as equally depraved. Concluding that both candidates are murderous scoundrels and that the outside world is full of relentless villainy, she invents an urgent summons and flees home. Rather than recognizing the exaggeration of electioneering rhetoric, she remains grateful to the local press for supposedly exposing the danger and returns to her sensational novels with greater conviction.
