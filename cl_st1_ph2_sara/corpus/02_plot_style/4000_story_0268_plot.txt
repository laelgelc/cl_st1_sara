A duck hatches an unusually large, awkward-looking bird that is mocked and abused by the farmyard animals, his siblings, and eventually even his mother. Miserable and convinced that his ugliness makes him unwanted, he runs away.

During his wanderings, he encounters wild ducks and geese, narrowly survives a hunters’ attack, and takes refuge with an old woman, a cat, and a hen. Because they ridicule his love of swimming and cannot understand him, he leaves again. He later sees a flock of magnificent swans and feels a powerful longing to join them, though he does not know why.

The harsh winter nearly kills him. A peasant rescues him from the ice, but the frightened bird causes chaos in the man’s home and escapes into the snow. When spring arrives, he discovers that his wings have grown strong. He flies to a garden where he sees several swans and approaches them, expecting to be killed for his ugliness.

Looking into the water, however, he sees that he has matured into a beautiful swan. The other swans welcome him, and nearby children praise him as the loveliest bird of all. After a life of rejection and hardship, he finally finds acceptance and happiness among his own kind.
