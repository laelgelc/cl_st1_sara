A wealthy invalid lives in comfort but is consumed by self-pity and irritability. She rejects her young son, dismisses her daughters before they can share good news, complains about sunlight, flowers, misplaced objects, noise, and every minor discomfort, and responds to her devoted husband only with grievances. Although her family provides everything she requests, her selfish fixation on her suffering worsens her condition and turns the household gloomy and tense.

Nearby, another sick mother lives in a poorly furnished room and suffers similar weakness and pain. Instead of dwelling on herself, she fills her thoughts with care for her children. She comforts her crying baby, warmly welcomes her daughters home from school, praises their accomplishments, and enjoys their noisy play because it reflects their happiness and health. Even nearby construction fails to trouble her because her mind remains usefully occupied. She accepts her family’s sympathy without demanding it and tries to spare them sadness, finding relief from her own pain in their affection and joy.

The contrast shows that illness need not poison a home: selfish brooding magnifies suffering and burdens others, while loving concern, useful thought, and cheerfulness allow even a frail person to comfort and bless those around her.
