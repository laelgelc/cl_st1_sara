A woman becomes intrigued by Cavanelle, an energetic shop clerk who sacrifices his comfort to support his frail sister Mathilde, whom he believes possesses an extraordinary operatic voice. Hoping to help Mathilde’s career, the narrator visits their modest home and hears her sing, only to discover that her voice is thin, unpleasant, and wholly unsuited to the stage. She initially regards Cavanelle’s faith as foolish delusion but realizes that his blindness springs from profound love.

After Mathilde dies, the narrator assumes Cavanelle will finally spend his earnings on himself. Returning two years later, however, she finds him unchanged and now devoted to caring for his impoverished, ailing aunt. Though briefly exasperated by his endless self-sacrifice, she ultimately concludes that Cavanelle is not a fool but an extraordinarily loving and generous man.
