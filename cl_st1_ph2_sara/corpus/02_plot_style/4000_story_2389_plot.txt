Thirty years earlier, a surveyor travels to the long-abandoned Barwyke Hall to divide the estate between two elderly co-heiresses. The gloomy house is tended only by bailiff Tom Wyndsour and his wife. Nearby, the surveyor encounters Dickon, a deranged wanderer who has refused to sleep beneath a roof for twenty years and repeatedly cries that the late Squire Bowes is the devil.

During his first night, the surveyor awakens to footsteps and a mysterious light. He sees his bedroom door opening and a malformed hand—with a shortened middle finger and claw-like nail—gripping its edge before vanishing when challenged. Tom identifies the hand as that of Squire Bowes, who had hated the women who inherited his property and died before he could disinherit them.

Tom explains that after Bowes’s death, his ghost was seen touching the estate’s cattle, which then sickened and died. Dickon, then a young herdsman, was brought in to protect them. One night, Dickon’s brother saw Bowes’s ghost carry the sleeping youth out of their locked room. Dickon was later found on the island in the estate’s lake, permanently insane and crying out against Bowes. After hearing this story, the surveyor sees Dickon at the window, shrieking that Bowes is behind him. Deeply unsettled, he leaves Barwyke Hall as soon as his horses arrive.
