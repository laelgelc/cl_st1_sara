Captain John tells a visiting boy how Captain Green discovered a large, abandoned foreign ship drifting near Isle au Haut in 1827. Though unnerved by its ghostly condition, Green towed it home as a valuable salvage prize. Searching its hold, he found the skeletons of enslaved Africans and realized it had been a slave ship, probably deserted by its crew after running out of water.

Rumors arose that Green himself had participated in the slave trade during his mysterious early voyages and recognized the vessel. Despite fearing it, he became obsessed with restoring it for commerce. On its first voyage under him, three sailors died in a storm, the crew abandoned it upon reaching New York, and Green grew increasingly troubled and dependent on rum.

Returning home, the ship’s old anchor cable broke during a gale. It ran aground, and while the crew escaped into the rigging, Green refused rescue, declaring that he had to go with the ship. He was swept away and killed, while the vessel washed onto the harbor beach, where its hulk remained. Captain John concludes that the ship had pursued Green like the embodiment of his sins, carrying the dead he had once helped imprison and abandon, until it finally claimed him.
