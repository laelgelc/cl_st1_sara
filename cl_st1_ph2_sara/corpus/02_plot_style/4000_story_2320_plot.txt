A beautiful but emotionally distant mother lives beyond her means with her husband and three children. Their home seems haunted by a constant demand for more money. When her son Paul learns that his mother blames their financial troubles on his father’s bad luck, he becomes determined to prove that he himself is lucky and relieve her anxiety.

Paul discovers that by riding his wooden rocking horse in a frenzy, he can sometimes enter a trance-like state in which he knows the winner of an upcoming horse race. With the help of the gardener Bassett and his uncle Oscar, he secretly bets on these predictions and accumulates a large fortune. Hoping to silence the house’s demands, he anonymously gives his mother five thousand pounds. She takes the entire sum at once and spends it, but the household’s craving for money only intensifies.

Driven to desperation before the Derby, Paul rides his rocking horse until he identifies Malabar as the winner, then collapses with a brain fever. Malabar wins, earning Paul more than eighty thousand pounds, but the boy dies soon afterward. His mother is left vastly richer at the cost of her son, who sacrificed himself trying to win the luck and love he believed his family lacked.
