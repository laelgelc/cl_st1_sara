Hilda Rowbotham, a frail schoolmistress with heart disease, buys cakes for the christening of her unmarried younger sister Emma’s baby. At home, the Rowbotham family gathers under the oppressive influence of their disabled father, a former miner whose forceful will has left his children emotionally stunted and resentful.

A clergyman arrives to baptize the fatherless child, Joseph William. During the ceremony, the old man prays passionately, confessing that his possessiveness has warped his children and asking God to free and restore them. Most of the family remain emotionally closed off, while Emma broods over her shame, the absent father, and the judgment surrounding her. Her brother Laurie returns from the mine, mocks the ceremony and taunts Emma with a song about a baker’s man, revealing that the baby’s father works for the baker.

Afterward, the siblings fall back into hostility and bitterness. Though their father is physically and mentally disintegrating, his presence still dominates the household, preventing his children from becoming fully independent. The next day, his joyful praise of the daisies only makes his daughters withdraw from him in sullen discomfort.
