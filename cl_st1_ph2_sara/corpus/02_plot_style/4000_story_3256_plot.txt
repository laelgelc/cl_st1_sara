In 1863, a young army physician named Tribourdeaux rents rooms in a largely deserted building outside Orléans. After two months, a mysterious light begins appearing nightly in the neighboring apartment, while an unusual golden Angora cat enters his bedroom, sleeps beside him, and vanishes by morning.

Investigating, Tribourdeaux secretly crosses the divided balcony and sees a beautiful young woman named Linda, whose golden hair, green phosphorescent eyes, and feline movements strikingly resemble the cat’s features. Linda seems to recognize him, though she will reveal almost nothing about herself. As he grows intimate with her, the cat continues visiting him, but he can never see Linda and the animal together. He becomes obsessed with the possibility that they are two forms of the same being.

Determined to resolve his torment, Tribourdeaux poisons the cat with hydrocyanic acid. It dies with an eerily human cry, and he throws its body into the Loire. That same night, Linda disappears without taking any of her belongings or leaving evidence of her identity. Because Tribourdeaux is seen wandering near the river, he nearly faces a criminal trial, but a sympathetic magistrate accepts his private account and ends the inquiry. Years later, Tribourdeaux still refuses to say whether the events were supernatural or merely coincidence.
