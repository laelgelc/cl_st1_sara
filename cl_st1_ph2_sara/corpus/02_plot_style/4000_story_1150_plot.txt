A naïve but principled young shoe salesman from Oskaloosa moves to Chicago and finds work in a disreputable discount shoe shop. There he meets Sophy Epstein, a pretty, impoverished, streetwise clerk whose only dress is tight, shabby, and cut revealingly low. Although attracted to her, Louie is disturbed by her clothing and by the coarse attention she receives from the other men.

Louie’s respectful treatment unsettles Sophy because it contrasts with the insults and familiarity she normally expects. When he asks her to make her dress more modest, she angrily explains that poverty leaves her too exhausted and ill-equipped to maintain washable, respectable clothes, and that her appearance helps her attract attention and secure meals. Louie sympathizes with her hardship, recounting his own humiliating struggle to wash his clothes while surviving on meager wages, but he still urges her to sew a lace yoke into the dress. She dismisses the suggestion and continues wearing it unchanged.

Soon afterward, Louie encounters an old acquaintance who owns a respectable shoe store and offers him a much better job. Just before Louie is due to leave, Sophy arrives wearing the same dress with a painstakingly assembled black lace yoke covering its low neckline. Mocked by the other clerks, she pretends she altered it only for warmth and fashion. Yet her shy exchange with Louie reveals that his kindness and respect moved her to make the change, while her defiant excuses preserve her pride.
