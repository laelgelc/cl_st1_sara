After forty years away, wealthy and ruthless businessman Robert Turner returns to his childhood village to inspect the Jameson farm, whose overdue mortgage he intends to foreclose. He relishes the prospect because he has long hated the late Neil Jameson, once a schoolboy rival who humiliated him, and sees dispossessing Jameson’s widow and children as revenge.

At the shore, Turner meets Neil’s young son, Paul, who explains how the foreclosure will devastate his mother and end his hope of someday restoring the farm. Turner is disturbed by the boy’s familiar appearance and learns that Paul’s mother is Lisbeth Miller, Turner’s beloved childhood playmate and first sweetheart. Memories of Lisbeth’s affection and encouragement revive the tenderness buried beneath his hardened nature.

Realizing that his revenge would punish Lisbeth and her innocent children rather than his dead enemy, Turner abandons the foreclosure and promises Paul that the family may remain on the farm as long as necessary. He declines to meet the now-older Lisbeth, preferring to preserve his youthful memories of her, and leaves content that he has repaid the kindness she once gave his lonely boyhood.
