In 1436, King James I of Scotland spends the Carnival season at a Dominican monastery in Perth despite warnings that his life is in danger. Hated by powerful nobles for enforcing royal law and confiscating the earldom of Strathern, he becomes the target of a conspiracy led by Sir Robert Graham and aided by the king’s chamberlain. The assassins enter the monastery after the doors’ defenses have secretly been removed. James hides in a sealed vault, while Catherine Douglas thrusts her arm through the door staples to delay the attackers; they break her arm and force their way inside. The king is discovered and murdered.

The story then recounts a parallel incident at Versailles during the French Revolution. When a mob invades the palace intending to kill Marie Antoinette, a wounded royal guardsman warns Madame Auguier, who bolts the door and rushes the half-dressed queen through another passage into the king’s apartments. Guardsmen hold off the attackers, allowing the royal family to escape temporarily. Both episodes emphasize how treason and violence reveal the courage and self-sacrifice of loyal attendants who risk themselves to protect their sovereigns.
