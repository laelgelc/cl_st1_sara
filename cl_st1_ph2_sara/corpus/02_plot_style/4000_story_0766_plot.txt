Philo Gubb, an eccentric paper-hanger and correspondence-trained detective, uses part of a recent payment to reduce the debt he owes his future father-in-law, who has sold him worthless mining stock. Encouraged by news that his enormously overweight fiancée, Syrilla, is continuing to lose weight and may soon escape her sideshow contract, Gubb returns to his detective work.

He is hired by Dolly O’Hara to find her missing dog, Waffles, which is her chief remembrance of her recently deceased uncle, Haddon O’Hara. At the same time, two rival lawyers offer Gubb separate rewards to locate Mustard Bilton, a missing witness to O’Hara’s baffling will. O’Hara left two nearly identical wills written on opposite sides of the same sheet: one gives the bulk of his estate to Ardelia Doblin, while the other gives it to Sarah Kinsey. Because no one can determine which side was written last, the two women dispute the inheritance, and Mustard may be the only person able to resolve the matter.

Gubb’s microscopic examination of dog hairs and other clues proves useless, so he searches Waffles’s doghouse directly. Inside its roof he finds a hidden envelope from O’Hara giving the dog’s location and instructing that the envelope be delivered to the executor after Waffles is returned. Gubb follows the directions to an abandoned cattle shed, where he finds both Waffles and Mustard. Mustard explains that O’Hara had hired him to steal the dog after his death and wait until Gubb discovered the concealed clue.

Gubb brings Mustard and the envelope to the lawyers, collecting both offered rewards. Mustard reveals that neither side of the double-sided document was O’Hara’s final will. The envelope contains a later, simple will leaving everything to Dolly. O’Hara had arranged the entire mystery—including the conflicting wills, the dog’s disappearance, and Gubb’s investigation—as an elaborate final joke that nevertheless ensures his niece inherits his estate.
