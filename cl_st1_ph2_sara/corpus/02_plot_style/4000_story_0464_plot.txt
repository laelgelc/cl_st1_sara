Fourteen-year-old Roy Milford rides his pony, Scamp, home across the Colorado prairie as a violent storm approaches. Seeking shelter and a quicker route, he enters a deep, narrow arroyo. Heavy rain soon turns the gully’s sandy floor to mud, and a cloudburst sends a massive flash flood roaring toward them.

Roy and Scamp race for the nearest exit, but the flood overtakes them. Believing they cannot both survive, Roy abandons the saddle to give Scamp a chance and leaps toward the arroyo wall. Swept by the rising water, he manages to seize the exposed taproot of a greasewood bush. Using it like a rope, he painfully climbs the crumbling bank and escapes moments before part of it collapses.

Exhausted, Roy is later found asleep on the prairie and taken home. Scamp has also returned alive, though nearly spent. Reunited with his family, Roy recounts how the supposedly troublesome greasewood bush saved his life, prompting his grateful father to declare that he will never again willingly uproot one.
