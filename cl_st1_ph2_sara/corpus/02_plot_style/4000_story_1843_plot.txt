After the British conquest of New Amsterdam, a band of fiercely traditional Dutch settlers led by Garret Van Horne retreats to Communipaw. They sever contact with the conquered city, forbid English, preserve Dutch customs, and establish the House of the Four Chimneys as their cultural stronghold.

As the isolated community grows poor, relics belonging to former Dutch governors are accidentally discarded. Through the intervention of St. Nicholas, Walter the Doubter’s hat becomes the first enormous “Governor’s Head” cabbage, while Peter Stuyvesant’s shoe becomes the first “Governor’s Foot” oyster. Cultivating these products saves Communipaw and eventually forces its inhabitants to resume limited trade with New York, though they guard their young people and traditions from outside influence.

In later generations, their hostility shifts from the English to encroaching Yankees, who are transforming old New York. Yankee speculators attempt to buy and redevelop Communipaw, even proposing to turn its gardens, oyster beds, and historic mansion into lots, docks, and a bank. The Van Hornes drive them away, allowing the village to continue its old-fashioned life. Yet the decaying House of the Four Chimneys and the younger generation’s adoption of modern customs suggest that Communipaw’s preserved Dutch culture may finally be fading.
