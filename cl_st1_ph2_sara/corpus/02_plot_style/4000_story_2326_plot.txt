On a suffocating New Year’s Eve during a drought, Nils returns to his neglected rural home, angry at the disorder but soon concerned for his pale, overworked eldest son. The boy has spent the day caring for his younger siblings, tending animals, fetching wood, cooking, and cleaning while his mother lies in bed with a headache.

Throughout the night, the mother berates Nils, demands tea and attention, and complains bitterly about her husband and children. The sick boy, terrified by his parents’ quarrels, minimizes his illness and seeks comfort by holding his father’s hand. Troubled by strange fears of growing up and dying, he eventually falls asleep while Nils secretly watches over him.

Before dawn, Nils leaves for work five miles away. The boy rises to milk the cows, only to be ordered by his mother to bring her tea, fetch her reading, and care for the baby. The New Year begins exactly as the old one ended: with the father laboring away and the burdened child maintaining the household amid resentment, illness, and neglect.
