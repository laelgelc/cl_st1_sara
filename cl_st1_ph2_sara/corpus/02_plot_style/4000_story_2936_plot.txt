During a violent storm on Carmel Bay, a man is struck by a bottle washed ashore. Inside, he finds and deciphers a desperate account written by Amasa D. Keating.

Keating recounts how the brig *Hopewell*, carrying American settlers bound for the South Pacific, was wrecked in 1853. The survivors reached a fertile, isolated island whose apparently benevolent king welcomed them but confined them to a guarded valley. The king began removing the sick and infirm one at a time, supposedly to send them home. Suspicion grew when none returned and the colonists realized they were prisoners.

After a healthy young man named Lemuel Arthur was taken, Keating disguised himself as a native and escaped to investigate. He overheard islanders reveal that the unhealthy captives had been burned in a kiln while the remaining settlers were being fattened for ceremonial cannibalism. Arthur was to be the first victim, beaten with flails before being cooked on heated stones and divided among privileged men.

Keating interrupted the ceremony, killed the king, freed Arthur, and escaped with him in a stolen boat. Adrift without food, water, clothing, or knowledge of the nearest land, Keating wrote the account, sealed it in a bottle, and cast it into the sea in the faint hope that it might lead to the rescue of the colonists left behind.
