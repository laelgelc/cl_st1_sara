On Christmas morning in the Cherokee Strip, hundreds of cowboys organize a massive circle hunt to eliminate the gray wolves preying on local cattle. Under Bill Miller’s command, groups of riders and beaters surround the wolves’ refuge along the Cimarron River and drive the animals toward a confined horseshoe-shaped sandbar.

The carefully planned hunt repeatedly threatens to dissolve into chaos as excitable participants shoot at turkeys, chase deer and antelope, break formation, and play pranks. Nevertheless, the cordon gradually closes, trapping a large band of wolves. Attempts to kill them safely from a distance fail because of poor marksmanship. Some wolves are shot while trying to swim the river, while a huge wounded leader charges through the mounted line before being roped and killed.

When mounted men with shotguns advance, a wounded wolf attacks a horse, prompting the entire line to rush in. The hunt becomes a wild melee of gunfire, ropes, frightened horses, and fleeing wolves. Once order returns, the hunters count twenty-six dead wolves on the sandbar and seven more across the river, though several have escaped. The participants disperse, while Reese—who had earlier abandoned his post to catch a turkey—invites the others to his camp for turkey and eggnog.
