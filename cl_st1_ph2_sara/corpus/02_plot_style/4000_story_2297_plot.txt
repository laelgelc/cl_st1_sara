A man confesses in court that he deliberately murdered someone he sees as the embodiment of the “Russian intelligent”: a recurring social type whose intrusive chatter, clichés, hypocrisy, vulgarity, and disrespect have poisoned every part of his life. This figure seems to appear everywhere and in countless guises, spoiling work, travel, art, literature, nature, and romance.

His resentment finally erupts aboard a crowded train. A school official falsely claims an empty seat is reserved so his family can occupy extra space, then lectures his son self-righteously about the wickedness of lying. When the narrator exposes his hypocrisy, the official indignantly insults him and calls him an intellectual. Enraged that his tormentor has applied the same label to him, the narrator calmly shoots him. He feels no remorse, but fears that the victim’s son may grow up to embody the same detested type.
