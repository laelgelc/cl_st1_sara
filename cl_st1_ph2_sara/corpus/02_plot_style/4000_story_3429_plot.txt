A gifted artist is abducted by sea raiders and imprisoned in a king’s palace, leaving behind Aurora, his bride of only an hour. Promised freedom if he creates an unrivalled work of beauty, he pours his love, grief, and failing life into carving a crystal cup inspired by the song he once shared with her. He completes the cup but dies before the king’s grand Feast of Beauty.

The king, remorseful that his ambition has caused captivity, suffering, and death among the artists forced to compete, decrees that all competitors—living or dead—must attend the feast and that the surviving captives will be freed. The dead artist is brought to the celebration and crowned beside his masterpiece.

Aurora, herself a captive competitor, is called upon to sing for her freedom. As she performs their old song, the crystal cup answers her voice with the artist’s echo, creating music of supernatural perfection. The sound entrances the king, then reaches such intensity that the cup shatters. Aurora collapses and dies, finally reunited in death with her beloved, while the overwhelmed king also dies in the presence of the perfect beauty he had pursued.
