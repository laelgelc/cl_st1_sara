Two older travelers hiking through a remote Jura valley stop at a dilapidated farmhouse, where a silent, strangely alluring girl gives them milk. One man spits it out because of its odd taste, but his exhausted companion, Morton, drinks it all while the girl watches him intently. Afterward Morton behaves strangely, becomes obsessed with the girl and the farmhouse, and insists that she is following him. She later appears behind them in the forest.

That night at their inn, Morton leaves his bed. His companion follows faint footsteps and the smell of freshly turned earth to the village cemetery, where he finds Morton crouched among disturbed graves with the girl bending over him. Her mouth is stained with blood. When confronted, she glides away through the churchyard, leaving Morton dead. The doctor attributes the death to heart failure, but Morton has a deep pinprick wound in his throat and his heart has been almost entirely drained of blood.

The traveler then learns that the farmhouse they visited was burned down fifty years earlier. Its former occupants, an old woman and her daughter, were suspected of poisoning travelers with milk, while local legend claimed the daughter was a vampire. Disturbed graves and a trail of blood through the church further suggest that the vampire lured Morton away and killed him.
