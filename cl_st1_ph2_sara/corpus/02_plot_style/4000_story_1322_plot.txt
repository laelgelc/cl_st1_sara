Two brothers are thrilled when the adults in their household casually promise to take them to a circus. The elder narrator becomes consumed by anticipation, imagining horses, clowns, and beautiful performers, only to discover on the appointed day that the adults have abandoned the plan for a garden party. Harold loudly protests, while the narrator withdraws in silent despair and fantasizes about escaping to sea.

After a prank with a water-filled orange leads to a brief fight, the brothers reconcile and spend the day seeking distractions, but their disappointment persists. They then meet a serious yet sympathetic family friend whom they call the “funny man.” Recognizing their boredom, he abandons his errands and unexpectedly offers to take them to the circus himself.

At the circus, the narrator is overwhelmed by the spectacle. He falls instantly in love with Coralie, a radiant equestrienne, then becomes fascinated by the clown and imagines adopting his profession. Finally, he sees Zephyrine, a dramatic bareback rider, and transfers his romantic devotion to her. Returning home in a dreamlike state, he remains absorbed in the circus’s music, smells, performers, and imagined adventures, falling asleep dreaming of riding across the Sahara with Zephyrine while Coralie watches reproachfully.
