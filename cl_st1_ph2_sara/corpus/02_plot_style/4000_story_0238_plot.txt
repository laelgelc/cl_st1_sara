A wealthy merchant’s reckless son squanders his inheritance until only a dressing gown, slippers, and a few coins remain. A friend sends him a magical trunk that can fly, and he accidentally travels in it to Turkey. There he learns that a princess is confined in a high castle because of a prophecy that she will be unhappy over a lover.

Using the trunk, he enters her room through a window and claims to be a Turkish angel. Charmed by his flattering words and stories, the princess agrees to marry him, provided he returns when her parents are present and entertains them with a tale. He tells the king and queen a humorous, moral story about proud household objects—especially a bundle of matches that boasts of its noble origins before briefly blazing and going out. Delighted, the royal couple approve the marriage.

On the eve of the wedding, the merchant’s son fills his trunk with fireworks and flies over the city, convincing everyone that he truly is an angel. After landing, he leaves the trunk in the forest while he goes to hear the townspeople praise the spectacle. During his absence, a lingering spark burns the trunk to ashes. Unable to fly back to the princess, he misses the wedding. She continues waiting for him, while he wanders the world telling stories, never again creating one as entertaining as the tale of the matches.
