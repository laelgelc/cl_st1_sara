A powerful political machine expects the elderly senior senator’s impending death to create a vacancy for the state’s ambitious governor. Because the governor cannot appoint himself, railroad magnate and political boss Tom Styles devises a scheme: the governor resigns on the pretext of attending to his cotton plantation, allowing Lieutenant-Governor John Berriman to take office and later appoint him to the Senate.

After the senator dies, Styles orders Berriman to make the planned appointment. Berriman assumes he must comply until his older brother Hiram, an honest farmer unfamiliar with political manipulation, challenges him. Hiram asks why he would appoint a man he does not believe will serve the people honestly and reminds him that, as governor, his duty is to the public rather than to the party machine.

The next morning, Berriman begins to see his office and its responsibilities differently. Although another commanding telegram arrives from Styles, he asks his secretary whom the people truly want as senator. Learning that Huntington, leader of the reformist faction, is widely regarded as the best and most popular choice, Berriman appoints him. Politicians on both sides search for a hidden motive, unable to believe that Berriman acted simply because he thought the people should have the man they wanted.
