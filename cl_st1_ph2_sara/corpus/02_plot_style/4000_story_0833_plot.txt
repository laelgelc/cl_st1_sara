Bobint reluctantly attends a Cajun ball because he fears that Alcée Laballière, a wealthy young planter, will pursue Calixta, the spirited woman Bobint loves. Alcée arrives seeking distraction after a cyclone devastates his rice crop and after Clarisse, his cousin, coldly rejects his declaration of love. At the ball, Alcée and Calixta flirt intimately, reviving rumors about an earlier encounter in Assumption.

Clarisse, alarmed by Alcée’s secret departure and realizing she cannot bear to lose him, rides to the ball and insists that he return home. Alcée immediately leaves Calixta to follow her. Hurt and exhausted, Calixta accepts Bobint’s longstanding marriage proposal, though without romantic enthusiasm. On the journey home, Clarisse confesses that she loves Alcée, transforming his despair into joy and making his attraction to Calixta seem insignificant. As the ball ends, both men believe they have gained the women they love, though Calixta’s engagement appears born more from disappointment and resignation than passion.
