Roland Clewe secretly prepares a heavily equipped car to descend an extraordinarily deep shaft created by his experimental projectile. Despite the risks, he travels more than fourteen miles underground while maintaining telephone contact with his assistant, Bryce. At the bottom, he emerges into a vast, brilliantly illuminated space where his damaged projectile appears to rest unsupported in midair.

Leaving the car, Roland discovers that both the projectile and he are actually supported by a solid substance so transparent that it looks like empty space. He cautiously explores it but is overcome by terror when he senses the surface sloping toward an apparently limitless depth. At Bryce’s insistence, he returns to the car and is hauled back to the surface, where he finds Margaret waiting anxiously.

Roland privately tells Margaret that the transparent substance is diamond. He theorizes that Earth’s center is an immense diamond—the remnant of a cometary nucleus whose burning outer layers formed the planet’s crust. As proof, he shows her large fragments broken loose when the projectile struck, which he has tested and confirmed as diamonds. Although the fragments alone represent immense wealth, Roland warns that exploiting the discovery would destroy the value of diamonds worldwide. Traumatized by his experience in the transparent abyss, he vows never to descend again.
