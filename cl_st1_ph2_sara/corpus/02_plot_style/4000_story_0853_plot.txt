Every spring, the widowed Parisian performer Adrienne Farival is drawn by the scent of lilacs back to the convent where she was educated. There she finds peace from her hectic theatrical and romantic life, warmly reunites with the nuns—especially Sister Agathe—and brings valuable gifts for the chapel. After each tranquil fortnight, she returns to Paris, where her unexplained absence has distressed her manager, servants, admirers, and current lover.

A year later, the lilacs again prompt Adrienne to abandon her obligations and journey eagerly to the convent. This time, however, the Mother Superior has evidently learned of Adrienne’s worldly reputation or conduct. Adrienne is refused entry, her chapel gifts are returned, and a reproachful letter permanently banishes her. Devastated, she weeps at the locked door and leaves. Inside, Sister Agathe secretly sobs on the bed Adrienne once used, while a lay sister sweeps away the lilacs Adrienne dropped outside.
