Late at night, Ellen, a lifelong maid, brings tea to a guest and talks about her elderly, deeply religious mistress. Her rambling reminiscences reveal a childhood marked by loss and cruelty, her entry into domestic service at thirteen, and the small pleasures she had to suppress. She recalls falling in love with a florist named Harry and planning to marry him, but, fearing that her frail mistress could not manage without her, she abruptly ended the engagement and devoted her life entirely to her employer. As Ellen prepares the guest for sleep, she admits that she cannot imagine life after her mistress’s death and quickly stifles the thought, exposing both the depth of her loyalty and the emptiness awaiting her beyond service.
