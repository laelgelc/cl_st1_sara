A once-cheerful Ohio farmhand becomes ambitious after marrying a schoolteacher and having a son. Urged by his wife to rise in the world, he leaves farm labor to start a chicken farm, but ten years of disease, death, failed incubators, and financial loss leave the family discouraged. He preserves deformed chicks in jars, hoping they might someday make his fortune.

The family moves near a railroad station and opens a restaurant, hoping to prosper and give the boy a better life. Business remains meager, so the father resolves to become a cheerful, entertaining host. When a young man waits there for a delayed train, the nervous father tries to amuse him with egg tricks and his collection of malformed chickens. His demonstrations repeatedly fail, and an egg finally breaks over his clothes. Humiliated by the young man’s laughter, he erupts in anger and throws another egg at him.

Upstairs, however, the father gently sets down the egg he still carries, collapses beside his wife, and weeps. Witnessing his parents’ defeat, the son comes to regard the egg as the symbol of their frustrated ambition, suffering, and ultimate failure.
