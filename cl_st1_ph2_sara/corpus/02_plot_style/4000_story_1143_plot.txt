A small-town writer is challenged by Millie Whitcomb, a department-store clerk, to write about a truly ugly heroine. She chooses Pearlie Schultz, an overweight, homely public stenographer who longs for romance but has never experienced it. Though dieting and exercise never reduce her weight, Pearlie possesses warmth, insight, and an extraordinary talent for cooking.

Pearlie meets Sam Miller, a young traveling salesman who is lonely, homesick, and devoted to the woman he plans to marry. After they talk, Pearlie invites him to Sunday dinner with her family. Her superb home-cooked meal comforts and deeply impresses him. Before leaving town, Sam offers to do anything he can to repay her kindness.

Pearlie asks for one thing: when they reach the deep shadows outside her house, she wants him to put his arm around her and kiss her once, and afterward tell his fiancée that Pearlie requested it. Sam understands the loneliness behind the request and grants it with tenderness and reverence rather than mockery. After hearing the story, Millie decides she prefers beautiful heroines after all, particularly ones who end up married.
