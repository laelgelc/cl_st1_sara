Three starving vagrants—a former soldier, a supposed student, and the narrator—leave Perekop and wander across the Crimean steppe in search of food. At night they encounter a feverish carpenter returning to his wife and daughters. Frightened, he threatens them with a revolver but throws them some bread. Still hungry, the men rush him, seize his remaining food and weapon, and ignore his pleas while they eat.

They allow the sick carpenter to share their fire, then fall asleep. By morning, the narrator and soldier discover that the student has strangled and robbed the carpenter before fleeing. Afraid of being blamed, they abandon the corpse and continue wandering together. Years later, recounting the episode from a hospital, the narrator rejects personal guilt and concludes that brutal circumstances have reduced everyone involved to beasts.
