In 1780, a British expedition under Colonel Polson invades Nicaragua and sends a flotilla commanded by twenty-two-year-old Horatio Nelson toward Fort San Carlos. Impatient for reconnaissance, Nelson secretly enters the jungle, where he captures Donna Rafaela Mora, the sixteen-year-old daughter of the fort’s commandant, spying on the British boats. Fascinated by her beauty, he flirts with her and learns that the fort supposedly holds 340 men and has cannons loaded with clay balls packed with metal scraps.

Nelson escorts Rafaela toward the apparently deserted fort and trades one of his pistols for her stiletto. She immediately fires at him and misses, but the lack of response from the fort convinces him that it has been abandoned. The British flotilla therefore advances, only to be devastated by cannon fire. Although the garrison has fled and her father is bedridden, Rafaela alone operates the guns, killing nearly the entire force and severely wounding Nelson, who loses an eye.

Rafaela is honored by the King of Spain with a military rank and a lifetime pension. Nelson’s lost eye later contributes to his legend when he uses his blindness as an excuse to disregard a signal to retreat.
