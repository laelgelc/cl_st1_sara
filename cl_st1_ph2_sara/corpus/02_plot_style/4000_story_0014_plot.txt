On the eve of the Cherokee Strip’s opening to settlers, a group of cowboys gathers in a Hennessey saloon to discuss abandoning cattle work and claiming land. Ace Gee recounts his recent cattle drive from Texas to Montana under an inexperienced, overbearing foreman nicknamed “Bad Medicine.”

Bad Medicine repeatedly questions the crew’s accurate cattle counts, threatens seasoned hands, and loses their respect. After a stampede, the quiet but capable Californy openly rebukes him, and the men jokingly treat Pink as their true foreman. Pink gradually wins Bad Medicine’s confidence, then deliberately loses him during a hunting trip, forcing him to spend a night alone. Shaken by the experience, Bad Medicine leaves the herd at the railroad and lets Pink take command. At the Montana range, the company formally appoints Pink foreman, and he proves excellent at the job.

Ace explains that he rejected Montana ranch life because it involved too much work on foot and returned to Oklahoma as a “boomer” seeking a claim. His companions respond with a joking call for drinks, and the cowboys end the evening drinking and planning to eat together before the land rush.
