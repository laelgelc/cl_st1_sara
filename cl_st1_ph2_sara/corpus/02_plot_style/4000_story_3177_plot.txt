The narrator recounts the life of his friend Toby Dammit, whose badly directed childhood discipline supposedly turns him into a precocious sinner. As an adult, Dammit habitually punctuates his speech with the empty wager, “I’ll bet the Devil my head,” despite the narrator’s repeated warnings.

While crossing a dark, covered bridge, Dammit boasts that he can leap over a turnstile and again stakes his head on the feat. A mysterious lame old man dressed in black appears, accepts the wager, and supervises the attempt. Dammit jumps spectacularly but strikes a concealed iron brace above the turnstile and is decapitated. The old man catches the severed head in his apron and flees, revealing himself to be the Devil collecting his winnings.

Dammit soon dies despite medical treatment. After the transcendentalists refuse to pay the funeral expenses, the narrator has the body exhumed and sells it as dog meat.
