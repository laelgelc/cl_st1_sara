Mr. Smith asks his profit-minded neighbor, Mr. Jones, to help advise Mrs. Lloyd, the newly widowed wife of an old acquaintance. Jones refuses because charitable work will not make money, while Smith visits the widow and offers his assistance.

During Smith’s absence, Jones eagerly pursues two seemingly profitable deals: he buys fifty shares of railroad stock from Mrs. Lloyd’s broker and exploits a struggling merchant named Armor to obtain goods far below their value. Jones mocks Smith for missing the stock bargain, but the railroad soon defaults and its shares collapse, leaving Jones with a severe loss. His mistreatment of Armor also damages his reputation, causing a ship captain to entrust a valuable cargo to Armor instead of Jones.

Meanwhile, Smith helps Mrs. Lloyd sell her stock before its collapse and use the proceeds to purchase a suitable house with a storefront, where she can establish a small dry-goods business. His generosity brings friendship, gratitude, and warmth into his home, while Jones’s selfishness leaves him bitter and spreads unhappiness through his family. The contrasting outcomes show that ruthless pursuit of financial gain often fails even materially, while humane acts yield deeper and more lasting rewards.
