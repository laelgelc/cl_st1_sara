Newly arrived in Hanbridge after buying an old dentist’s practice, the young and inexperienced Mr Cowlishaw spends a sleepless first night at the town’s leading hotel. Unaccustomed to the industrial Five Towns, he is repeatedly disturbed by rumbling electric trams, noisy guests, blast furnaces, carts, factory whistles, workers, and an alarm clock.

On his opening day, while the town attends an important football match, his surgery remains empty. His first patient is Rannoch, Hanbridge’s celebrated centre-forward, who has had three teeth knocked out during the match. Rannoch offers to let Cowlishaw display the teeth as a publicity attraction in exchange for free replacements. Although the scheme would bring enormous attention, Cowlishaw refuses because it would violate professional etiquette, and Rannoch leaves.

Cowlishaw’s next visitor is Mrs Clowes, the influential mayoress and hotel proprietor. She insists that a painful but repairable tooth be extracted without anesthetic, despite Cowlishaw’s reluctance. Anxious, exhausted, and aware that pleasing her could establish his reputation, he attempts the difficult extraction. He successfully removes the tooth but faints from nervous strain while still gripping it in the forceps. Far from being offended, Mrs Clowes is flattered that she has made such an impression on him and becomes warmly disposed toward him, giving him the influential patronage likely to secure his professional success.
