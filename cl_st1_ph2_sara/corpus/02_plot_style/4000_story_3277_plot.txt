In 1839, twenty-one-year-old Samuel Bax is appointed minister of an Independent chapel in Troy. Among those who hear his trial sermon is Nance Trewartha, a beautiful, strong-willed fisherman’s daughter from the socially despised neighboring town of Ruan. Immediately attracted to him but aware that Troy’s wealthier young women will have an advantage, Nance resolves to act first.

On Samuel’s first night in Troy, Nance arrives by boat and falsely claims that a dying congregant urgently needs him. She carries the inexperienced minister out to sea, then confesses that no one is ill: she loves him and will not return until he agrees to marry her. Samuel is outraged, fearing that being absent overnight with her will destroy his reputation and career. Yet as they sail beneath the stars, he finally recognizes her beauty, courage, and devotion. When she embraces him, he kisses her, and she immediately turns the boat homeward.

They return unseen before dawn. Though Nance offers to release him from any obligation, Samuel admits he may not want to be released. Three months later they marry despite his congregation’s indignation. He retains his pulpit, and they share fifty years of loving marriage before dying within a week of each other.
