Jack, a young pointer, becomes separated from his cook on the way to market and is captured by municipal dog-catchers. In the cart, he joins several other seized dogs, including Arto, an old circus poodle, and a mangy, violet-stained stray. Arto explains that they are being taken to a pound where unclaimed dogs may be killed, eaten by the others, and skinned. The frightened dogs debate whether they can free themselves from human cruelty, but Arto insists that humans’ superior strength and intelligence make resistance futile and that a dog’s happiness depends entirely on having a good master.

At the pound, however, the violet-stained stray challenges Arto’s conclusion. He races toward the high fence and, despite tearing his side on the nails, leaps over it and escapes. Watching him disappear, Arto realizes that a dog’s happiness can lie in its own courage and determination rather than solely in human hands.
