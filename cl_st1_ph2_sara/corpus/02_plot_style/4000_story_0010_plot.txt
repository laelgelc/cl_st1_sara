In 1880s Texas, rancher Gray’s herd of nearly two hundred horses is seized under a revived judgment tied to old debts, though Gray insists the judgment was already paid. Rather than submit, he and his friends drive the horses into Indian Territory, beyond the Texas sheriff’s civil authority, and keep them guarded near Gray’s brother’s Kansas home.

Sheriff Ninde, worried about his reputation and his bondsmen’s liability, hires armed frontier toughs to recover the herd by force. At night, they overwhelm Gray’s son and the other herders, taking not only Gray’s horses but sixty belonging to local men. Gray, his relatives, and the other owners quickly assemble an armed rescue party and pursue them.

They catch Ninde’s group at a corral, where the sheriff is separating the horses. Gray advances with a double-barreled shotgun and declares that he will kill Ninde—and risk dying himself—rather than submit to injustice. Convinced that Gray means it, Ninde backs down and releases the herd.

Though relieved that no blood is shed, Gray is physically shaken by how close he came to killing or being killed. Ninde avoids returning to Texas, while his posse goes back humiliated. After investigating irregularities in the original proceedings, the sheriff’s bondsmen choose not to pursue the matter, and the case is dropped.
