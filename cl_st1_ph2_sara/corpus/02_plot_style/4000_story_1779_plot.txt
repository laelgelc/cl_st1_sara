Ohio’s diverse Indigenous tribes, once divided by old Iroquois-Algonquin rivalries, unite against white expansion. Among their notable leaders is Logan, a Mingo chief renowned for generosity and friendship toward white settlers. After Virginians treacherously murder his family in 1774, however, Logan vows revenge and leads deadly raids. Even then, his compassionate nature persists: he protects captives, saves Major William Robinson from execution, and continues to distinguish personal vengeance from a wider war against whites. Having exacted revenge, he sends Lord Dunmore an eloquent message lamenting that none of his family remains to mourn him. In later life, Logan becomes homeless and alcoholic as his people lose their lands, and he is eventually murdered by another Indian.

The narrative then turns to Tecumseh, a Shawnee warrior committed to resisting American settlement. Although he hates white encroachment, he opposes cruelty, prevents the torture of prisoners, and protects women and children. Assisted by his brother, the Prophet, he attempts to unite Northern and Southern tribes in a vast Indigenous confederacy. The Prophet strengthens the movement through claims of supernatural power, but attacks General Harrison at Tippecanoe while Tecumseh is absent. The resulting defeat destroys much of the Prophet’s influence.

During the War of 1812, Tecumseh allies with Britain, hoping to defend Indigenous lands. He saves American captives from massacre and condemns British officers for failing to control their allies. After the British naval defeat on Lake Erie, he recognizes that they intend to retreat and bitterly accuses them of abandoning the Indians. Forced to follow them into Canada, Tecumseh is killed at the Battle of the Thames, ending his campaign for Indigenous unity.

The Prophet survives but loses his former authority. His promotion of witchcraft accusations leads to executions, including that of the dignified Wyandot chief Leatherlips. The persecutions finally subside when a young warrior rescues his accused sister and openly declares that the Prophet’s influence has turned the Indians against one another.
