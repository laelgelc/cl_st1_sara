The sleepy town of Miltonville is stirred by the arrival of Joseph Scatters, an imposing, eloquent Black stranger claiming to represent the Cuban estate of Isaac Jackson’s long-lost brother. Scatters says the brother became wealthy and left Isaac five thousand dollars, presenting a sealed package supposedly containing the inheritance. He deposits it in a local grocer’s safe until court can authorize its release, wins the town’s admiration, and becomes a celebrated social and intellectual figure.

While living as Isaac’s guest, Scatters borrows money from Isaac and several other residents, telling each that he has only Cuban currency. When they compare stories, they grow suspicious and open the sealed package, finding only worthless paper. Scatters is arrested and tried for obtaining money under false pretenses.

At trial, Scatters admits the essential facts but argues that he merely charged the townspeople a price for their vanity, greed, and credulity. He then flatters the white judge, jurors, and local aristocrats by invoking their families and the chivalric traditions of the old South. Captivated by his eloquence and appeals to their pride, the jury acquits him without deliberation. As Miltonville’s angry Black residents gather outside, a prominent white major provides Scatters with a guarded carriage to the station, allowing him to escape safely.
