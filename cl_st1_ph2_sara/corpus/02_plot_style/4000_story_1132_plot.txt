Emily Grierson, the last descendant of a once-prominent Southern family, lives in increasing isolation after her domineering father dies. Unable to accept loss, she initially refuses to surrender his body for burial. She later begins courting Homer Barron, a Northern labor foreman who admits he has no intention of marrying. When Emily buys arsenic and Homer subsequently disappears, the townspeople assume he has abandoned her.

Emily withdraws into her decaying house, attended only by her servant, and resists the town’s efforts to collect taxes or impose modern changes. After her death decades later, townspeople force open a long-sealed upstairs bedroom. They discover Homer’s corpse lying in a bridal bed, revealing that Emily poisoned him. An indentation in the pillow beside him and a strand of Emily’s iron-gray hair show that she had continued sleeping beside his body for years.
