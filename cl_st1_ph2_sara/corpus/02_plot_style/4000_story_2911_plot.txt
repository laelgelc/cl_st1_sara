Eighteen-year-old Ursula is sent from Montreal to spend the summer with her formidable Aunt Philippa on Prince Edward Island. Her father hopes the visit will discourage her romance with Mark Fenwick, whose family he despises. Ursula has recently quarreled with Mark and worries that the distance will prevent their reconciliation.

At Philippa’s farm, Ursula enjoys country life but grows despondent when Mark does not write. Her fiercely independent aunt continually recounts examples of bad husbands and unhappy marriages, reinforcing Ursula’s fear that Mark has forgotten her. Eventually, Mark writes that he is being sent to South Africa for five years and asks Ursula to meet him in Halifax, marry him, and accompany him. Though she loves him, Ursula refuses to elope because she considers it shameful.

Unexpectedly, Aunt Philippa supports the marriage. Having investigated Mark’s character for herself, she tells Ursula to invite him to marry respectably at her home. Philippa organizes an elaborate wedding despite warning Ursula that Mark may still fail her. Mark arrives, but the local Presbyterian minister is called away at the last moment, forcing the staunchly anti-Methodist Philippa to accept the neighboring Methodist minister as officiant. During the ceremony, a fire breaks out on the kitchen roof, but Philippa waits until the couple is pronounced married before raising the alarm, and the blaze is quickly extinguished.

As the newlyweds depart, Ursula asks why Philippa helped them. Philippa reveals that she once refused to run away with the man she loved and has regretted it ever since—though she adds that she might have regretted eloping as well.
