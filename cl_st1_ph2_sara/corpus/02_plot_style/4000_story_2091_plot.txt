A country doctor must make an urgent journey through a snowstorm to visit a supposedly dying patient, but his horse has died and no one will lend him another. After he opens an abandoned pigsty, a mysterious groom emerges with two supernatural horses. The groom assaults the doctor’s servant, Rosa, and refuses to accompany him, intending to remain with her. Before the doctor can intervene, the horses carry him instantly to the patient’s distant home, leaving Rosa at the groom’s mercy.

The doctor initially finds the young patient healthy despite his plea to be allowed to die. Distracted by guilt over abandoning Rosa and pressured by the family, he examines the patient again and discovers a vast, rose-colored wound in his side, crawling with worms. He realizes the wound is fatal, but the family and villagers expect an impossible cure. They strip the doctor, sing that he must heal the patient or be killed, and force him into bed beside the dying man. The patient condemns his helplessness, while the doctor offers hollow reassurance.

The doctor escapes through a window and mounts the horses, but their supernatural speed has vanished. They carry him home at an agonizingly slow pace while he remains naked and exposed to the cold, unable to reach his fur coat. He despairs that Rosa has been victimized, his practice will be stolen, and he will never return home, concluding that answering the false emergency has irreparably ruined him.
