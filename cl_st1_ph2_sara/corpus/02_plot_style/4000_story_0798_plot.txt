Mr. Ryder, the refined and socially conservative leader of an elite Black society, plans an exclusive ball at which he intends to propose to the young, light-skinned, well-educated widow Molly Dixon. On the afternoon of the event, an elderly, dark-skinned woman named Liza Jane visits him while searching for Sam Taylor, the husband from whom slavery separated her twenty-five years earlier. Her story and an old photograph reveal to Ryder that he himself is Sam.

At the ball, Ryder recounts Liza Jane’s history and poses a hypothetical moral dilemma: should a man who has risen far beyond his former circumstances acknowledge the aging wife of his youth, even if he loves another woman and can safely conceal his identity? Molly and the other guests agree that he should. Abandoning his planned proposal and his concern for social status, Ryder brings Liza Jane into the room, identifies himself as her long-lost husband, and publicly introduces her as his wife.
