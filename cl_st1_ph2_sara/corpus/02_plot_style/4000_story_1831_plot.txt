A warship at sea is thrown into mortal danger when a carelessly secured carronade breaks loose during heavy weather. Rolling violently across the gun deck, it kills five sailors, destroys other guns, tears holes in the hull, damages the masts, and threatens to sink the vessel. The gun captain responsible descends alone with an iron bar and rope to subdue it. After a prolonged struggle, he is nearly crushed, but an elderly passenger intervenes by throwing a package beneath the cannon’s wheels. This allows the gunner to overturn and secure it.

Once the immediate crisis has passed, the crew discovers that the badly damaged ship is leaking, drifting off course near Jersey and Guernsey, and facing an approaching storm. On deck, the passenger is revealed to be a general. He rewards the gunner’s extraordinary courage by awarding him the Cross of Saint-Louis, then orders him executed for the negligence that caused the disaster. The gunner accepts his fate and is shot, demonstrating the general’s ruthless conviction that bravery must be rewarded but dereliction of duty must still be punished. His severity convinces the officers that the Vendée rebellion has found a formidable leader.
