On a stormy evening in Calcutta, a seven-year-old boy pretends to have a headache to escape his tutor. His mother indulges him, and he persuades his grandmother to tell him a fairy tale.

In the tale, a king leaves his queen and young daughter for twelve years to perform austerities in hopes of gaining a son. When he returns, he fails to recognize his now-grown daughter and impulsively decrees that the first man he sees the next morning will marry her. That person is a seven-year-old Brahman boy gathering sticks, so the princess marries him and raises him in a seven-winged palace.

As the boy grows older, his schoolmates repeatedly ask who the beautiful woman living with him is. The princess continually postpones revealing that she is his wife. When she finally promises to tell him, she prepares their room for the disclosure, but a serpent hidden among the flowers bites and kills him. Refusing to let the story end in death, the child listens as his grandmother has the body floated downriver on a banana stem and restored to life through a magician’s incantations. Comforted by the tale, the boy falls asleep, secure in a childhood world where death is only temporary and wonder matters more than factual plausibility.
