The narrator meets Begglely at a boarding house, where the young man’s fanatical devotion to tennis disrupts everyone’s peace. Begglely plays and discusses the sport incessantly, especially praising the famous player Renshaw, until even the narrator becomes exasperated.

The following year, Begglely abruptly replaces tennis with amateur photography. His remarkably unflattering portraits expose or exaggerate people’s defects, ruin vanity and reputations, embarrass friends and relatives, and even help separate a young man from his beloved. After acquiring a portable camera, he begins photographing private and compromising moments, making himself so intolerable that his acquaintances encourage him to tour Turkey in hopes that an offended local will dispose of him.

Begglely returns alive but cured of photography after encountering overwhelming numbers of tourists with cameras and locals who charge to pose. He then becomes obsessed with golf, converting lawns into makeshift courses and exhausting people he persuades to play. When the narrator later meets him in Switzerland, Begglely has already lost interest in golf and transferred his consuming enthusiasm to whist, studying card problems even during a mountain climb.
