A splendid stuffed rabbit is given to a boy for Christmas but is soon forgotten and belittled by the more sophisticated nursery toys. The wise Skin Horse explains that toys become “Real” when a child loves them deeply and for a long time, even though that love leaves them worn and shabby.

When the rabbit becomes the Boy’s constant bedtime companion, they form a close bond. After months of games and affection, the Boy declares that the rabbit is not a toy but real. The rabbit understands that love has made him Real to the Boy, and he no longer cares that his fur, whiskers, and shape have worn away.

The Boy later contracts scarlet fever, and the rabbit stays beside him throughout his illness. Once the Boy recovers, the doctor orders everything from the sickroom burned, including the germ-covered rabbit. Abandoned in a sack awaiting the bonfire, the rabbit mourns that becoming Real has ended in loss.

A fairy appears from a flower grown from the rabbit’s tear. She explains that although the Boy’s love made him real to the Boy, she can make him real to everyone. She transforms him into a living rabbit with fur and working legs, and he joins the wild rabbits in the woods. The following spring, he returns to glimpse the Boy, who notices the rabbit’s familiar markings but never realizes that it is his beloved old toy.
