While vacationing at Pass Christian, Annette repeatedly encounters a refined, mysterious fisherman named LeConte, who rescues and assists her and gradually wins her affection. Despite her cousin Philip’s jealousy and her family’s concern over the apparent social gulf between them, she trusts LeConte’s gentility. Before returning to New Orleans, she promises not to forget him, not to speak of him until they meet again, and to continue her vocal studies.

At the opening of the New Orleans opera season, Annette discovers that the fisherman is actually a celebrated tenor who had been living incognito while recovering his health. Encouraged by his praise and growing closeness, she works fervently toward becoming an opera singer and plans to study in Paris. She becomes jealous of Madame Dubeau, the leading soprano who frequently performs opposite LeConte, but he dismisses her fears as a misunderstanding of professional theatrical relationships. Just before Annette is to leave for Paris, she learns that LeConte and Madame Dubeau have secretly married. Heartbroken, she abandons her ambition to go abroad.
