The narrator recounts the scandalous reputation of the nuns at Poissy, largely attributed to the excesses of a former abbess. During her rule, younger sisters secretly gather at night to feast, gossip, joke, and mock convent life. They initiate a seemingly innocent novice by giving her an elaborate, bawdy lesson on catching and “converting” fleas, only to discover that she understands their sexual innuendo and has already had romantic experience. The evening continues with a grotesquely comic tale about the ascetic Sister Petronille before the nuns retire.

After the convent is reformed, a charitable archbishop intends to send a worn pair of breeches to an impoverished noblewoman named Mademoiselle de Poissy for mending. His servant misunderstands and delivers them to the nuns of Poissy instead. Initially horrified, the sisters decide the garment is a spiritual test against idleness. They lavishly repair and decorate it, fill it with sweets, and return it as a gift.

Meanwhile, the archbishop finds the impoverished woman a suitable noble husband and celebrates their wedding at his palace. The decorated breeches arrive during the feast and are placed on the table as a centerpiece. When they are accidentally revealed beneath the sweets, the guests erupt in laughter, and the bridegroom jokes that the nuns have aptly presented both the pleasures and the essential instrument of marriage.
