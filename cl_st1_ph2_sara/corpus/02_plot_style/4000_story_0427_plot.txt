A gifted but impoverished young Florentine sculptor, Angelo Cappara, works at King Charles VIII’s court in Amboise. Though admired by the court’s women, he is painfully shy, inexperienced, and frustrated by unfulfilled romantic longing.

A beautiful married noblewoman invites him to visit her privately. Angelo falls passionately in love, but she repeatedly excites and kisses him while withholding full intimacy, often using her husband’s imminent return as an excuse. Convinced that the husband is the only obstacle, Angelo arranges for friends to detain him and asks the lady to signal by placing a light in the window once the household is asleep. Instead, she immediately displays the light, revealing that she values avoiding danger more than fulfilling her declarations of love.

Feeling cruelly deceived, Angelo threatens to disfigure her so she will always remember the lover whose life she has made miserable. Although she finally offers herself to him, he concludes that she loves her beauty more than she loves him and slashes her cheek before fleeing.

Her husband reports him, and Angelo is captured and sentenced to hang. A noblewoman who admires his passionate courage persuades the king to pardon him and give him into her care. Angelo refuses her, declaring himself still devoted to his former mistress. He enters the Church, eventually becomes a cardinal and renowned scholar, and in old age continues to live on the bittersweet memory of his brief, tormented romance.
