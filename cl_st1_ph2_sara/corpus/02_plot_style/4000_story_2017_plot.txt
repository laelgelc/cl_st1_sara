In 1883, Cambridge antiquarian Dennistoun visits the cathedral at St Bertrand de Comminges, where he notices that the elderly sacristan behaves as though haunted by an unseen pursuer. The sacristan later offers him an extraordinary volume assembled by Canon Alberic de Mauléon from rare illuminated manuscripts. At its end are a treasure-seeker’s plan and a terrifying drawing of Solomon confronting a murderous, half-human demon.

The sacristan sells the priceless book for a suspiciously low sum and is visibly relieved to be rid of it. His daughter gives Dennistoun a silver crucifix for protection. That night, while examining the book alone at his inn, Dennistoun removes the crucifix and sees the demon from the drawing rising behind him, preparing to attack. He seizes the crucifix and screams; servants rush in, feel an invisible presence force past them, and find him unconscious.

The sacristan admits that he has seen the creature twice and felt its presence countless times. Writing on the drawing reveals that Canon Alberic summoned or encountered the demon while seeking treasure in 1694 and remained persecuted by it until his sudden death in 1701. Dennistoun keeps the manuscript collection but photographs and burns the drawing, later arranging prayers for Alberic’s soul.
