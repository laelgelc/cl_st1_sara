At a remote outback pub, the drunken landlord, his domineering wife, and the cold, attractive barmaid Alice exploit itinerant workers, encouraging them to drink and gamble until their cheques are gone. Old Danny, a station hand nearing the end of his money, is tolerated while he spends freely but humiliated once he becomes unprofitable. After he makes a crude joke with a piece of Alice’s underwear, she orders him out. Yankee Jack escorts him away, then rebukes Alice’s contempt by declaring that Danny is as good as she is.

A newly arrived bushman’s cheque fuels another day of drinking, and Alice soon resumes treating Danny kindly. By midnight nearly everyone lies unconscious on the verandah. Alice secretly places a cushion beneath Jack’s head before retiring to her room. A melancholy young jackeroo, who has lost his last shilling and is infatuated with her, awakens, hears her crying, and quietly leaves across the plain with his swag and dog. The next evening Danny asks Jack what happened with Alice, but the others dismiss him as too drunk to have heard anything, leaving the incident unexplained amid the larger cycle of degradation caused by drink.
