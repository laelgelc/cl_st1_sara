A white traveler persuades Palitlum, an aging drinker, to recount the death of the renowned Thlinket chief Ligoun. Once a formidable warrior, Ligoun came to advocate peace after seeing how unity strengthened white society. He taught the young Palitlum—then called Olo—the tribal law of vengeance: blood for blood and rank for rank, with greater honor gained by killing a higher-ranking foe.

At a great feast hosted by Chief Niblack, the chiefs drink quass together. A young Yukon chief, Opitsah the Knife, deliberately insults Ligoun, then taunts and repeatedly stabs him. Ligoun refuses to kill Opitsah because the younger man is beneath his rank. Instead, supported by Olo, he crosses the room toward Niblack, the only chief of comparable status. The confrontation ignites old feuds, and the assembled chiefs begin slaughtering one another. Despite being stabbed and shot, Ligoun reaches the terrified Niblack and cuts his throat, thus obeying the law of rank for rank. He then sings his death song before another chief shoots him dead.

Olo avenges Ligoun by killing Opitsah, Ligoun’s killer, and several other high-ranking chiefs. Years later, though drink has diminished him and earned him the name Palitlum, he still treasures Ligoun’s knife and the honor of having supported him in his final battle. He refuses to trade the knife even for an ocean of liquor, though after finishing his tale he eagerly claims the promised bottle.
