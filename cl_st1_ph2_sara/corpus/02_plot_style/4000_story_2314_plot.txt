During a trade depression in Nottingham, a weary country girl arrives late at the fair with twelve geese, one of them lame, and fails to sell them. Elsewhere, the refined Lois expects her lover, Will Selby, for dinner, but he claims he must guard his family’s factory against disgruntled workers. That night the factory burns down, and Lois, believing Will may have committed arson, resolves to conceal their last conversation while inwardly deciding their relationship is over.

The next morning, Lois visits the ruins and encounters Will and her brother Jack, both dishevelled after spending the night in jail. Jack reveals that they never reached the factory: they drunkenly tormented the country girl and her geese, provoked a brawl, and were arrested, with the girl giving Will a black eye. Relieved that Will is innocent of the fire but disgusted by his behavior, Lois tells him that everyone suspects him of arson. Yet she does not abandon him; instead, they walk away together, with Will submitting to the judgment and punishment of the woman who remains both his partner and his conscience.
