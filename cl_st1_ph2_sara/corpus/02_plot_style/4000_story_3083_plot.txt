Businessman Cyrus Ryder acquires an American claim to Paa, a South Pacific island covered in valuable guano. Learning that Norwegian captain Jens Petersen is preparing to seize it, Ryder orders three trusted sailors—Hardenberg, Strokher, and Ally Bazan—to sail the schooner Idaho Lass there first.

While fitting out the schooner, the men repeatedly see a shrouded figure climb the foremast at night and raise the topsail and jib. The apparition appears only when Bazan sleeps in the forecastle, though he alone never sees it. Convinced the vessel is haunted and doomed, Bazan frightens the others into abandoning the expedition. Petersen consequently reaches Paa, secures Norway’s claim, and makes a fortune.

Two years later, the narrator catches a sheet-wrapped Bazan raising a sail while asleep. Bazan admits that he used to sleepwalk in exactly that fashion, revealing that he had unknowingly been the “ghost” aboard the Idaho Lass. The narrator keeps the truth from the three sailors, whose superstition cost Ryder the island.
