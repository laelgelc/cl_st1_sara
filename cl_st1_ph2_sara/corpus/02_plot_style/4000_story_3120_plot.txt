A mysterious foreign woman named Elizabeth Astrado settles in a small Iowa town, where she buys an upland meadow, plants clover and mignonette, keeps bees and livestock, and lives with striking simplicity despite evidence of concealed wealth. Though the villagers gossip about her unknown past, reserved manner, locked room, and unopened foreign trunks, she gradually wins their affection through kindness, charity, and contented participation in ordinary rural life.

John Hartington, a strong, innocent young college graduate, returns to town and is immediately drawn to Elizabeth. Their attraction deepens into an idyllic courtship centered on nature, music, farm work, and simple pleasures. They marry and live happily in her modest cabin, ignoring the villagers’ suspicions about her age and past. When Elizabeth becomes pregnant, they prepare a cradle and anticipate the child’s arrival. She offers to reveal her former life, but John chooses to postpone hearing the painful story.

The baby dies during childbirth, and Elizabeth dies soon afterward. Following her wishes, John buries her among the mignonette and destroys the cradle. He then opens the locked room and discovers only a private Catholic altar. In the cellar trunks, he finds sumptuous clothing, jewels, rare books, and documents revealing that Elizabeth had once been a wealthy, titled woman. Rather than investigate further, he remains grateful that she escaped the burdens of her former life, found peace in simplicity, and shared her final happiness and love with him.
