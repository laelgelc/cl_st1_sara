A solitary traveler who believes natural beauty is best appreciated alone discovers a secluded river island at sunset. Its western half is radiant with flowers, sunlight, and life, while its eastern half lies in grave-like shadow and suggests sorrow and death.

As he watches, the traveler imagines—or witnesses—a fairy circling the island in a fragile canoe. Each passage from light into darkness seems to represent a year of her life: her shadow is absorbed by the black water, and she returns to the light increasingly sorrowful, weak, and indistinct. After repeated circuits, the sun sets completely, and the nearly spectral fairy enters the dark side once more. She does not reappear, leaving the traveler uncertain whether she has vanished or died.
