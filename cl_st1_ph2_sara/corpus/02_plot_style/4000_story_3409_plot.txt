A man and his wife leave their country home for a three-week business trip, entrusting their garden, animals, and house to their capable young servant, Pomona. Returning a day earlier than expected, they find the gates locked, movable objects missing, and a notice claiming the property is to be sold for unpaid taxes. Pomona assures them everything is fine and explains the strange circumstances through a melodramatic “novel” she has written.

While alone, Pomona defended the house from a lightning-rod salesman who mistakenly tried to install rods on the wrong property. She trapped him on the roof with a bulldog, sent his horse and wagon down the road, and eventually forced him to leave. The agitated bulldog then barred her from the house for three days until she pacified it with meat. To ensure that the bulldog and the household dog could work together, she deliberately let them fight until one established dominance. A minister witnessed the fight, and Pomona’s attempt to protect her employer’s reputation led her to claim that he wished to become a church vestryman, prompting the minister to consider him for a vacancy.

Finally, to deter a persistent tree salesman, Pomona made the property look deserted and posted the false tax-sale notice. Her scheme worked, but because her employers returned early, she had not yet restored everything or removed the sign. Though overwhelmed by her unconventional methods, the couple are relieved to find their home in excellent condition and resolve not to leave it again for a long time.
