Paul Boielle, an impoverished London waiter and aspiring painter, dreams of selling his masterpiece so he can buy a cigar shop and marry his fellow waitress, Jeanne. Jeanne dismisses his proposal because of his low wages but becomes interested when she hears that his painting might make money. Paul repeatedly tries and fails to sell it.

After Paul attacks their employer for flirting with Jeanne, he is fired, and Jeanne angrily rejects his attempt to defend her. Despairing over his lost job, failed artistic ambitions, and unrequited love, Paul tries to kill himself by stepping in front of a car. The American driver stops in time, hears his troubles, and examines his painting.

The American, a wealthy advertising manager, recognizes that the picture can be adapted to promote his company’s suspenders. He offers Paul three thousand dollars for it and a lucrative long-term job as an illustrator. Newly prosperous, Paul returns to the restaurant as a customer and humiliates his former employer by proving he can afford to dine there. When Jeanne suddenly becomes affectionate after learning of his success, Paul coolly rebuffs her and treats her merely as a waitress.
