A married couple rejects the idea that a honeymoon must immediately follow a wedding and instead resolves to seek a new “honeymoon” each year through shared travel. During a six-week journey across Norway, they travel from Christiania to Throndhjem by train, steamer, carriage, boat, and foot, wandering wherever scenery, fishing, or curiosity leads them.

Along the way, the narrator fishes rivers and mountain lakes while his wife, Graygown, accompanies him, arranges lodging, waits with her crochet work, and turns unfamiliar inns and farmhouses into temporary homes. They experience dramatic fjords, glaciers, waterfalls, valleys, villages, and mountain storms, while meeting hospitable Norwegians and enjoying both successful and frustrating fishing expeditions. Recalling these episodes years later, the narrator concludes that Norway gave them one of the happiest periods of their marriage: although they missed the midnight sun, they unmistakably found the honeymoon they had been seeking.
