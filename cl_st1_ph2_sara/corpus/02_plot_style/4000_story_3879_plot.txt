Confined by illness to a dim room overlooking a river, Bailey becomes absorbed in the passing boats and people, treating the view as entertainment. One morning he sees an armed group pursuing a Malay servant from a nearby estate who has gone mad and attacked several people. The chase repeatedly passes beyond Bailey’s limited field of vision, frustrating him.

After swimming across the river under gunfire, the fugitive disappears into the woods near Bailey’s house. Bailey’s terrified housekeeper returns and reports the attacks, only for the wounded Malay to climb a tree onto Bailey’s balcony and enter the room with a bloodstained knife. Unable to stand, Bailey throws a medicine bottle at him. As the Malay climbs inside, young Fitzgibbon shoots him from outside, and the dying man falls across Bailey’s legs and reaches for his knife. Bailey finally strikes him in the face with a second bottle, stopping him. When Fitzgibbon regrets killing the man, Bailey replies that it was just as well.
