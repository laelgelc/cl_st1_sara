A priest is summoned before dawn to the squalid home of Pat Connell, a notorious drunkard believed to be dying. Connell appears dead after a strange seizure, but while the priest leads the family in prayer, he suddenly revives when a delayed flow of blood from a physician’s lancet restores him.

Days later, Connell tells the priest that during his apparent death he dreamed he sank through the stair landing into an immense cavern resembling hell. There he sat among countless condemned souls beneath rolling balls of red fire and confronted a gigantic, terrifying ruler. When Connell begged to leave in God’s name, the figure allowed him to depart only after he promised to return. Convinced that he has pledged away his soul, Connell despairs, but the priest persuades him to treat the vision as a warning and seek salvation through repentance.

Connell reforms completely, abandoning his drunken companions, returning to steady work, paying his debts, and supporting his family. Still terrified by the dream, he reinforces the landing through which he imagined himself falling. His improved life continues for months, until a chance meeting with an old friend tempts him into a public house. Despite intending to drink moderately, he relapses and is brought home insensible.

That night, Connell’s wife awakens to see him leaving the room alongside a mysterious second figure. Moments later she hears a crash. Connell has fallen headfirst down the stairs, broken his neck, and died instantly on the very landing connected with his vision. The identity of his companion and whether his dream foretold his death remain unresolved.
