A ragged boy named Mamouche arrives at Doctor John-Luis’s home on a stormy night. The elderly bachelor feeds and shelters him, then discovers that Mamouche is the grandson of his old friends Théodule Peloté and Stéphanie Galopin. The connection revives fond memories of the doctor’s youth, but Mamouche secretly leaves the next morning.

Feeling lonely, Doctor John-Luis decides he wants to adopt and educate a worthy boy, yet none of the children his servant Marshall introduces seems suitable. Meanwhile, the doctor offers a reward for whoever has been removing gates and committing other troublesome pranks around the community.

Mamouche is eventually captured as the culprit. He freely confesses to numerous acts of mischief but insists he is not a thief. The doctor believes him, pays the promised reward, and recognizes that the boy fled earlier because he felt ashamed after receiving such kindness. Seeing in Mamouche both the lively spirit of his grandfather and the intelligence of his grandmother, Doctor John-Luis offers to raise him as his own child, while leaving him free to depart. Mamouche chooses to remain and begins trying to resist further mischief through prayer, guidance, and work.
