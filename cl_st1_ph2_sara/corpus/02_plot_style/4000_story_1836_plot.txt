An Oxford undergraduate named Lykeham tells his skeptical friend that, during a night in London, he met an extraordinarily beautiful woman at the theater. They immediately believed each other to be divine, exchanged kisses, spent the night together, and speculated that they might be Apollo and Daphne.

Two weeks later, while walking near Oxford on a moonlit night, Lykeham suddenly recognizes the woman and races across fields and woods toward her. She appears in a glade along a path of moonlight, welcomes him, and embraces him. The narrator realizes she is Cynthia, the moon goddess, but cannot initially recall her divine lover. Consulting a mythology book afterward, he discovers the humiliating truth: the vain Lykeham is not Apollo but the goat-footed god Pan.
