A traveler meets a genial former congressman on a train who recounts being stranded with twenty-three other passengers in a prairie snowstorm. After seven days without food, the starving men adopt elaborate congressional procedures to elect which passengers will be killed and eaten, debating candidates according to their size, toughness, and nutritional value. The stranger vividly describes the resulting meals and claims that many passengers were consumed before rescuers arrived.

Disturbed by the account—and by the stranger’s suggestion that he likes him almost as much as the first victim—the traveler asks the conductor about him. The conductor explains that the man really was once trapped in a snowbound train and nearly starved, but the ordeal left him with a harmless monomania: whenever he tells the story, he imagines methodically eating the entire group. Relieved, the traveler realizes he has heard a madman’s fantasy rather than a cannibal’s confession.
