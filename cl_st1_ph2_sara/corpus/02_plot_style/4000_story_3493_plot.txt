During a delayed nighttime train journey, two travelers meet a remarkably confident and learned stranger, who tells them about his former post as a cotton-duty collector in Barich. Despite warnings from an old clerk, Karim Khan, he takes up residence in a deserted marble palace once used by a ruler and his Persian courtiers.

The palace gradually exerts a supernatural fascination over him. He hears invisible women bathing in the river, senses vanished revelers in its halls, and experiences vivid nightly visions of fountains, music, perfumes, servants, and royal luxury. He becomes obsessed with a beautiful Persian woman who seems trapped within the palace’s violent past. As the visions grow stronger, his ordinary work and modern identity appear unreal, while the spectral world becomes his true existence.

He briefly moves out but is irresistibly drawn back. During a storm, he senses the Persian woman’s anguish and hears the recurring warnings of Meher Ali, a madman who circles the palace crying that everything is false. Karim Khan explains that the palace’s stones have absorbed centuries of frustrated passion, sorrow, and cruelty and now consume anyone who stays there for three consecutive nights. Meher Ali alone escaped, but only by losing his sanity.

Karim Khan is about to reveal the tragic history of the Persian woman and the difficult means of escape when the stranger’s train arrives. He leaves before finishing the tale. One listener dismisses the story as a fabrication, while his theosophist companion believes it, and their ensuing dispute permanently destroys their friendship.
