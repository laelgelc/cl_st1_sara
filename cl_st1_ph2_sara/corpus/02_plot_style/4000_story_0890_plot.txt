Ambrose Pottle, a shy, middle-aged barbers’ supply salesman, falls in love with the widowed Blossom Gallup, who insists that her next husband must be a cultured, well-read man. Just as Ambrose tries to propose, the confident Mr. Deeley arrives and impresses Blossom with an endless stream of facts, jokes, and supposed intellectual expertise. Unable to compete, Ambrose leaves heartbroken, but after noticing that Deeley discusses only subjects beginning with the letter A, he realizes the man has merely memorized one section of an encyclopedia.

Ambrose spends a week studying everything under both A and B. On his next visit, he outshines Deeley with facts about obscure animals, countries, and historical figures, then exposes the limits of Deeley’s knowledge. Humiliated, Deeley leaves. Ambrose admits to Blossom that both men were bluffing: Deeley sells a book on hog farming and memorized the A entries, while Ambrose memorized A and B solely to win her. Touched by his devotion, Blossom confesses that what she truly wants is a strong, caring man. The two embrace and plan a future together.
