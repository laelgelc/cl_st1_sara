After his wife’s death, Parson Carryl relies on the young and capable Huldy to manage his household, despite local women insisting that he needs an older, more experienced housekeeper. When he tries to supervise domestic affairs himself, his ignorance leads to comic disasters: he attempts to make a tom-turkey hatch eggs, crushing them in the process, and later accidentally throws a pig into the well after mistaking the new well-curb for a pigpen.

Recognizing Huldy’s superior judgment, the Parson increasingly trusts her. Under her management, the home, gardens, finances, and preparations for a ministers’ gathering all flourish, frustrating the meddlesome village women. Their jealousy turns into gossip that Huldy is improperly pursuing the widowed minister. Distressed by the rumors, Huldy considers leaving. The Parson tells her how much her presence has helped him and proposes marriage as the surest way to silence the talk. Huldy accepts, and they are married the following week.
