Horace Nimms, a timid but gifted cashier for the Amalgamated Soap Corporation, has spent twenty-one years contentedly working with figures while privately developing a cost-accounting system that could save the company money. Too intimidated by company president Oren Hammer to present it, he remains unnoticed until an arrogant efficiency expert, S. Walmsley Cowan, administers dubious personality tests and decides Horace’s hands are better suited to mechanical work. Cowan transfers him to elevator duty and replaces him with an elevator operator, who promptly steals six hundred dollars.

While operating the elevator, Horace overhears that Hammer is furious because no executive can determine the true cost of producing and selling the company’s soap. Inspired by Cowan’s own speech urging employees to act boldly, Horace traps Hammer between floors and finally explains his detailed knowledge of the business and his accounting plan. Impressed, Hammer summons him the next morning, appoints him to install the new cost-accounting system, and dismisses Cowan, sending the efficiency expert to the cashier’s cage to collect his final salary.
