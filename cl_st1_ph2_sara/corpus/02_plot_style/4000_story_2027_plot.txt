Mr. Williams, curator of a university art museum, receives an unremarkable mezzotint depicting an unidentified English manor house. As he and several acquaintances examine it over the course of a weekend, the engraving inexplicably changes: a shrouded figure appears at its edge, crawls across the lawn, enters through a window, and later emerges carrying a child before vanishing.

Williams documents each stage with witnesses and photographs, then identifies the house as Anningley Hall. He learns that its owner, amateur engraver Arthur Francis, completed the mezzotint after his infant son mysteriously disappeared in 1802. Local history reveals that Francis had engineered the execution of Gawdy, a dispossessed poacher and last member of an ancient family. The changing picture implies that Gawdy’s ghost abducted Francis’s child in revenge, ending Francis’s family line as Francis had ended his.

The engraving is placed in a museum and examined for trickery, but no explanation is found, and it never changes again.
