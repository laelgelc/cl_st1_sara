A magazine editor named Brown champions strict realism and discovers an ideal contributor in Robert Severne, a humorless young writer who insists on personally witnessing or experiencing everything he describes. Severne’s equally serious fiancée, Lucy Melville, prefers romantic fiction and breaks their engagement, promising forgiveness only if he publishes an imaginative story. Refusing to betray his artistic principles, Severne retreats to Wyoming to brood and gather realistic material.

While writing a Western, Severne realizes he has never experienced being chased by armed men. He hires several cowboys to pursue him with blank cartridges, but another group mistakes the chase for the pursuit of a horse thief and joins in with live ammunition. They kill his horse, capture him, dismiss his truthful explanation as an extravagant lie, and nearly hang him before the original cowboys arrive and rescue him.

Severne uses the ordeal to complete what he considers his most realistic masterpiece. Brown rejects it because the chase and the hero’s terror seem impossibly exaggerated and imaginative, but another magazine publishes it. Lucy reads the story without initially noticing the author’s name and concludes that Severne has written a brilliant romance to win her back. She returns, moved by what she believes is his loving sacrifice of principle. Though Severne knows the story is entirely realistic, he abandons the impulse to correct her when their reconciliation becomes romantic.
