In socially proud Oldcastle, the widowed Mrs Ebag and her unmarried sister secure the post of church organist for Carl Ullman, a talented but impoverished and habitually gloomy musician. They invite him to stay in their home until he finds lodgings, but he remains for months, helping them influence church affairs and attracting local gossip about his relationship with both women.

During a violent storm, the family cat, Goldie, becomes trapped by the tail in the rooftop weather vane. Carl climbs onto the roof to rescue him, then falls with the cat into a rhododendron bush; both survive unharmed. Mrs Ebag interprets his bravery as an act performed for her sake and nearly inspires him to propose. The following morning, Miss Ebag likewise claims Goldie as especially dear to her and almost wins Carl’s affection before her sister interrupts.

The situation changes when Mrs Ebag’s young stepdaughter Edith arrives from London. Though dismissed by the older women as frivolous, she draws Carl into playing waltzes and awakens his own suppressed lightheartedness. Edith then declares that Goldie is actually her cat and thanks Carl for saving him. Carl falls in love with her, and they marry despite having little money. Mrs Ebag reluctantly supplements their income, while she remains a widow and Miss Ebag unmarried.

Years later, after Goldie dies, the bedridden patriarch Caiaphas insists that the cat had always belonged solely to him, revealing that each family member had privately claimed ownership of the animal whose rescue brought about the marriage.
