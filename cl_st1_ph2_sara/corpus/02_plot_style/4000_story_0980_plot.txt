Impoverished twins Violet and Jerry Trelevan have lost their fortune in a failed railway investment. Convinced that Violet cannot support herself, Jerry pressures her to marry the wealthy Dick Kenyon, despite her aversion to him. For Jerry’s sake, she reluctantly accepts Dick’s proposal, while Dick treats her with unexpected patience and gentleness.

When the railway shares suddenly rise, apparently restoring their fortune, Jerry tries to end the engagement on Violet’s behalf. He crudely suggests compensation, but Dick angrily refuses to deal through him and insists that Violet speak for herself. Before she can do so, the shares collapse again. Ashamed that she accepted Dick for his money, Violet writes confessing the truth and asks to be released.

During their meeting, Dick assures her that the shares will soon rise permanently and offers to free her so she can marry the “right man.” Confronted with losing him, Violet realizes that Dick himself is the man she loves. Jerry then arrives with news that the railway has revived because Dick is the capitalist financing it. Dick reveals that he allowed the uncertainty to test whether their relationship could yield something more valuable than money—and Violet’s love proves that it has.
