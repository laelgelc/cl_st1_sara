Augustus Teak learns that his frugal wife has secretly saved more than three hundred pounds and hidden it somewhere in their house. Unable to search without arousing suspicion, he persuades his friend Alfred Chase to become their lodger, locate the money, and help him obtain it in exchange for twenty pounds.

Their efforts repeatedly fail. Teak digs up the garden, while Chase searches the house whenever Teak manages to take his wife out. During one search, bad weather brings the couple home early, and they catch a soot-covered Chase emerging from their bedroom chimney. He claims he was rescuing a trapped bird, but the incident alarms Mrs. Teak.

Later, Mrs. Teak reports that her £322, hidden in mustard tins behind the fireplace, has been stolen after someone broke the washhouse window. Teak assumes Chase has successfully taken it and refuses to involve the police. When Chase returns, however, each man believes the other has the money. Their argument becomes a fight, and Chase leaves the house.

A month later, Teak discovers the truth: after seeing Chase in the chimney, Mrs. Teak suspected a plot, removed the money herself, staged the robbery, and deposited the savings in a bank. Teak is left unable to get any of it.
