Wealthy bachelor Reggie Pepper is asked for advice by his friend Archie Ferguson, an unsuccessful avant-garde painter engaged to the intensely artistic poet Eunice Nugent. Archie secretly earns his living by drawing the popular slapstick comic strip about the Doughnut family, but fears Eunice would despise him for commercializing his talent. On Reggie’s advice, he conceals the truth and claims his income comes from railway investments.

After their marriage, the railway stock collapses. Believing them ruined, Eunice moves with Archie into cheaper lodgings and supports them with what she calls her private means. Archie continues receiving substantial comic-strip income but cannot spend it without exposing his secret. Reggie eventually suggests that Archie pretend to have sold his modernist painting, “The Coming of Summer,” to the wealthy American art collector J. Bellingwood Brackett for two thousand pounds.

Eunice enthusiastically sends the supposed sale to the newspapers. Brackett’s representative then arrives and demands a public denial, explaining that Brackett is fighting an insanity case and that purchasing such a bizarre painting would be used as evidence against him. Cornered, Archie confesses that he invented the sale, secretly draws the Doughnut comics, and used his earnings to buy his own painting.

Instead of being horrified, Eunice finds the comics hilarious and reveals an equivalent secret: her supposed private income actually comes from writing advertisements for coffee substitutes, baby food, sausages, and other products. Relieved that neither has to maintain an artistic façade, the couple embraces while Reggie quietly leaves them alone.
