Mrs. Abercrombie begins the day irritated because a new pair of gaiters does not fit. Her sour mood unsettles her sensitive husband and spreads to their children, turning a harmonious household into one filled with quarrels. In anger, she hastily punishes the wrong children, then treats the real offenders too severely. After reflecting on her conduct, she regains control and restores peace through tenderness.

Mr. Abercrombie carries the gloom from home into his workplace. Irritable and impatient, he dismisses Edward Wilson for arriving late without listening to the boy’s explanation, then needlessly refuses financial help to another man. He soon regrets both actions but is too proud to reverse the dismissal.

That evening, Mrs. Abercrombie tells her husband that Edward is the sole support and caretaker of his sick, impoverished mother. Troubled by guilt, Mr. Abercrombie spends a sleepless night reflecting on the harm he caused. The next morning, his wife’s cheerful mood brightens the family, and he goes to work, summons Edward back, and restores him to his position under better terms. The day’s events reveal how a trivial irritation can spread through many lives and cause lasting pain.
