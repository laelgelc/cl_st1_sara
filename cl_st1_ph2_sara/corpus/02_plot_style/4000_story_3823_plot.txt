Toinette Girard marries Prosper Leclere and develops an absolute, almost worshipful love for him. Though they have no children, they are happy together and prosper as shopkeepers. During Prosper’s business trips, however, Toinette suffers deeply from loneliness. Her superstitious aunt warns that loving a person too much may provoke a supernatural “countersign of death,” causing Toinette to fear for Prosper whenever he is absent.

Four years into their marriage, Prosper travels to Ottawa for an extended stay. While Toinette anxiously awaits him, Prosper becomes irrationally jealous, suspecting that her friendliness toward male customers—especially a commercial traveler—may conceal infidelity.

One morning, Toinette appears to enter a strange, radiant church crowded with unseen worshippers. A hooded collector tells her that the Mass is for the souls of dead people who were loved too much and accepts only gold. Having no gold except her wedding ring, she surrenders it. She later finds herself kneeling beside her bed, fully dressed, with the ring truly missing. Terrified that the vision foretells Prosper’s death, she searches the house in vain.

Prosper returns unexpectedly, already consumed by suspicion. Seeing her disordered room and bare ring finger, he accuses her of misconduct. Toinette explains the supernatural Mass and says she sacrificed the ring because she feared he was dead. When she reaches for the crucifix to swear that she is telling the truth, she discovers the ring resting beneath it.

Convinced of her innocence and overwhelmed by shame, Prosper recognizes that the problem was not Toinette loving him too much, but his own failure to love and trust her enough. They reconcile.
