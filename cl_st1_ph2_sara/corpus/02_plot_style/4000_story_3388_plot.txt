An aging narrator prepares to take leave and reflects on life as a long, imperfect service ending in death. He argues that people should accept their inevitable failures without despair, focus less on prohibitions and self-righteous moral judgment, and devote themselves instead to the difficult daily work of honesty, kindness, patience, cheerfulness, and making others happy. While individuals should forgive injuries done to themselves, they should courageously defend others from harm, preferably with sympathy rather than anger or denunciation. Looking back on a life of good intentions, limited effort, and frequent failure, the narrator concludes that one may still die honorably as a “faithful failure,” meeting death peacefully like sunset after a long day’s work.
