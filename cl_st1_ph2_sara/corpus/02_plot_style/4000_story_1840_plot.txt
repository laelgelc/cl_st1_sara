A galley carrying a cruel, possessive young Spanish nobleman and his mistress stops near a North African town, where an elderly Christian missionary, Ramon Lully, has been stoned for preaching. Believing him dead, the opportunistic captain plans to recover the supposed martyr’s body and return it to Christendom as a sacred relic. He recounts how Lully abandoned a dissolute youth after discovering that the married woman he desired had a breast ravaged by cancer, a revelation that drove him toward religion, alchemy, and missionary work.

The sailors retrieve Lully but discover that he is still alive, though mortally wounded. Laid on the bier prepared for his corpse, he speaks to the young couple about corruption, lust, hatred, and humanity’s failure to transform its innate goodness into spiritual perfection. He describes his life as an attempt to refine both metals and human souls into incorruptible “gold,” while doubting whether he has accomplished anything.

As dawn arrives, Lully falls asleep near death. Unmoved by the substance of his teaching, the captain worries chiefly about whether the impending relic will bring greater prestige and profit in Minorca or Genoa, revealing that the greed and corruption Lully condemned persist even among those who honor him.
