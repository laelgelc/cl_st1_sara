At a winter ball, cultured novelist Aston Tyrrell and young artist Topsy Garrick retreat to a tropical conservatory. Each is accompanied by personified aspects of the self: Aston’s crude lust, Cain, and cynical intelligence, Sir Jasper; Topsy’s sensuality, Belle, and frightened innocence, Henrika. They initially converse through dummies representing their conventional social masks, but soon abandon them and discover a shared interest in literature. Aston offers to critique Topsy’s poetry, while their hidden impulses comment on and manipulate the flirtation.

As they explore the conservatory’s grotesque, animate flowers, intoxicating music, heat, perfume, and a chloroform-producing plant weaken Topsy’s resistance. Aston supports and tenderly kisses her, but Cain then embodies the predatory desire behind his cultivated behavior by forcing a kiss on her. Topsy’s innocent aspect recoils in terror, and Aston, suddenly ashamed, drives Cain away and begs forgiveness. Topsy coldly ends the incident and insists it be forgotten.

Unable to confront what has happened, Aston and Topsy recover their dummies and resume empty small talk as they return to the ballroom. Their inner companions follow, while the sinister flowers mockingly chuckle after them.
