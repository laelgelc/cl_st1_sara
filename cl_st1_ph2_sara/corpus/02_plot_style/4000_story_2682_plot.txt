John Hammond waits impatiently and anxiously at a wharf for his wife, Janey, to return after ten months abroad. When her delayed ship finally docks, he is overwhelmed with relief and affection, eager to escape with her to a hotel where they can be entirely alone. Yet Janey’s composure and slight emotional distance awaken his fear that she can never belong to him as completely as he desires.

In their hotel room, Hammond presses her for affection and asks why the ship was delayed. Janey reveals that a young male passenger died of heart failure during the voyage—and that she alone held him in her arms as he died. Though she insists the event has nothing to do with their marriage, Hammond is devastated by the intimacy she gave a stranger, especially because he feels she has never surrendered herself so fully to him. He embraces her and hides his anguish, realizing that the dead man’s presence will remain between them and that he and Janey will never truly be alone together again.
