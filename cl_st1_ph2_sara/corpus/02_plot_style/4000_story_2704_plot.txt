Baron d’Etraille accidentally discovers his young wife kissing the Marquis de Cervigne at a party. Determined to avoid scandal and ridicule, he arranges an amicable separation, ordering her to preserve appearances while living independently. For six years they do not meet, and the baron distracts himself with travel, hunting, and restoring his estate, only to grow older and increasingly bored.

While traveling by train to Nice, he unexpectedly shares a carriage with his estranged wife, Bertha. She has matured into a fuller, more self-assured, and far more alluring woman, rekindling his desire. Although she treats him with cool indifference and bluntly observes that he has deteriorated, he becomes fascinated and finally asserts his legal right as her husband to take her back.

At Marseilles, Bertha reveals that she deliberately arranged for them to be seen traveling together. Friends are waiting at the station and will spread the news that the estranged couple spent the night in the same railway carriage. She then tells him she may be pregnant, implying that their apparent reconciliation will protect her reputation and make him seem the possible father. Leaving him stunned, she joins her friends and departs. He never sees her again and never learns whether her claim was true or merely a calculated deception.
