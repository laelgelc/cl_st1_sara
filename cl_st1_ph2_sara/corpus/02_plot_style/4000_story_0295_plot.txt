Max, a passionate and idealistic young man, believes his love is uniquely profound until he discovers his beloved kissing his trusted friend. His ensuing suicide attempt fails, but the betrayal leaves him emotionally numb and destroys his faith in personal love and friendship.

Eventually, Max revives with his faith in humanity intact. Renouncing personal happiness, he dedicates himself to ending human suffering, convinced that people’s misery arises from irrationality and evil will and that wise guidance can establish peace. He becomes an influential, controversial public figure—at times a peaceful teacher, at times a militant leader—praised, condemned, imprisoned, and persecuted.

After years of struggle, the people misunderstand his efforts and blame him for the very panic and destruction he has tried to prevent. Their rejection causes another spiritual collapse, and Max is condemned to lifelong solitary confinement in a narrow, permanently illuminated cell. Deprived of movement, darkness, human contact, and any apparent reason to live, he nevertheless preserves his body and mind rather than surrendering to death. Though love, faith, and freedom appear lost, he continues living because he still hopes.
