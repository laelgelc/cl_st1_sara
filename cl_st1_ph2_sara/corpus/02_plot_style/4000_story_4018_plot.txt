Cumhal, a wandering gleeman, seeks shelter at an abbey near Sligo but is given damp fuel, filthy water, mouldy bread, and a flea-ridden blanket. Enraged, he loudly curses the friars and their abbot in verse. Fearing that his satire will spread and ruin the abbey’s reputation, the abbot orders the friars to seize and dunk him in the river, then decides to crucify him as a dangerous, unruly remnant of pagan culture.

The next morning, Cumhal is forced to make and carry his own cross. Along the way, he delays the journey by juggling, telling jokes, and singing a tragic old tale. Though the younger friars are captivated, they ultimately reject and beat him because his performances stir forbidden desires and memories.

At the execution site, Cumhal asks to eat the food in his wallet. He declares himself the poorest of all because his outwardly wretched life has never matched the beauty and love he carries within, but he nevertheless throws a portion of his food to the surrounding beggars. While they fight over it, the friars crucify him and leave. As evening falls, the beggars also depart, angrily pelting him with stones when he calls them outcasts and begs them to protect him. Birds descend to peck at his body and wolves devour his feet, leaving him to lament that even fellow outcasts have turned against him.
