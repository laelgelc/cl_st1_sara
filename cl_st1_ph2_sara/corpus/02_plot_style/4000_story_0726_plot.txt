A practical, emotionally stolid businessman is suddenly overwhelmed by uneasiness while attending a London theater, feeling that he has forgotten an important engagement. He returns to his flat, where his distress intensifies into the sense that an unseen presence is nearby. At the same time, he becomes newly receptive to the beauty of the moonlit night.

Gradually he recalls Straughan, an artistic friend from his youth who loved beauty and with whom he had once made a pact: whoever died first would, if possible, appear to the other. His fear gives way to wonder as he experiences the night’s beauty with profound, transformative intensity. Three weeks later, a letter from India informs him that Straughan died at nine o’clock that same evening, revealing that the friend had fulfilled their promise by returning through the beauty he loved.
