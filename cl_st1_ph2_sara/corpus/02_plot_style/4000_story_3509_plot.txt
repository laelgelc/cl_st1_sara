Orphaned Harry Esmond finds the affection he has long craved in Lady Castlewood and becomes devoted to her and her family. Happy in their household, he tutors and plays with her children, Frank and Beatrix, and fiercely defends Lady Castlewood’s honor.

After visiting the village blacksmith’s family, Harry learns that a child there has smallpox. Fearing he has exposed Frank, he warns Lady Castlewood, who lashes out at him in panic and proposes sending him away to college. She soon apologizes, but Harry has indeed carried the infection home. He, Frank, and Lady Castlewood contract the disease and recover, though Lady Castlewood’s beauty is scarred. Harry’s blunt acknowledgment of the change hurts her, but his sincere assurance that she remains dear and beautiful to him restores their closeness.

Harry subsequently becomes tutor to Lady Castlewood and her children, enjoying some of the happiest years of his life. When Lady Castlewood inherits money from an aunt, she dedicates her share to Harry’s education. At sixteen, he leaves for Cambridge, bidding an emotional farewell to the family and the village before riding out into the wider world.
