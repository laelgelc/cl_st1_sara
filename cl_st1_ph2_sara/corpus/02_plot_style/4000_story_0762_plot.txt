On Christmas morning, the placid widow Mrs. Gratz discovers that someone has broken into her locked chicken coop and stolen nine hens and a rooster. In the muddy yard, however, she finds a lost purse containing nine hundred dollars. Amused by the exchange, she decides that Santa Claus has taken her chickens but left her an extraordinarily generous present.

A nervous, thin man soon arrives and repeatedly tries to gain access to the yard. He first claims to be a poultry buyer, then a municipal chicken-house inspector investigating ravenous “tuberculosis bugs,” and finally a police detective seeking clues to the theft. Each time, Mrs. Gratz calmly exposes or plays along with his absurd stories. His obsessive searches and eventual admission that the missing clue is a pocketbook containing exactly nine hundred dollars confirm that he is the chicken thief who dropped the money.

Mrs. Gratz refuses to return the purse or call the police, partly because the law might force her to surrender it. She chooses to believe that Santa Claus gave her the money, while blaming the missing chickens on imaginary bugs and the broken padlock on unruly reindeer. Contentedly, she plans to put the nine hundred dollars in the bank.
