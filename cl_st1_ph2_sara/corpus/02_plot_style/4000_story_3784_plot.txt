Alice Mackaye, the daughter of a wealthy and controlling sugar merchant, falls in love with Will Hermann, a poor but self-supporting artist. Her father refuses to permit the marriage unless Will enters the family business, and for years keeps the couple apart. Desperate but unwilling to create scandal, Alice and Will seek advice from a trusted friend, who persuades them that Alice may honor her father without surrendering control of her own life. Willing to forfeit her inheritance, Alice marries Will. Despite early hardships, they build a happy family, eventually receive some inheritance after her father’s death, and establish a home on a dangerous but beautiful point on the Maine coast.

Their home and grounds are decorated with salvaged pieces of wrecked ships, reflecting their passion for rescuing whatever can be saved. Will recounts their most memorable case: an unmanned motorboat mysteriously crosses several offshore ledges under its own power and runs aground. The Hermanns recover it and advertise for its owner.

A thirteen-year-old boy named Johnny arrives and identifies the boat as belonging to his widowed father, who had departed on a fishing trip with two other men and never returned. The boat is Johnny’s only inheritance. Instead of demanding a salvage fee, the Hermanns charge him one cent, pay for the repairs, and help him take the vessel home so he can continue a seafaring life while hoping his father may return. Years later, Johnny serves as a boatswain’s mate aboard an American destroyer during the First World War. The fate of his father remains an unresolved mystery.
