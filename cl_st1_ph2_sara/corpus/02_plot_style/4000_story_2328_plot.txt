Steelman and Smith pose as naïve bush workers enjoying a holiday while a professional swindler shadows them, hoping to lure them into a fraudulent gold-share scheme. Knowing he is eavesdropping, Steelman stages a conversation in which suspicious Smith reluctantly agrees to test the man with a small investment before risking their supposed savings.

They give the swindler ten pounds. To win their confidence and induce them to invest a hundred, he returns twenty-one sovereigns as their share of a fabricated profit. Steelman keeps the money, reveals to Smith that he has deliberately turned the confidence trick against the sharper, and refuses to proceed with the larger investment.

Realizing he has been outwitted, the swindler eventually offers Steelman a partnership. Steelman coldly declines, saying he is not taking pupils.
