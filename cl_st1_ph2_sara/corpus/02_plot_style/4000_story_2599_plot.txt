An unnamed physician recounts his years assisting Herbert West, a brilliant but amoral scientist convinced that death is merely a mechanical condition reversible by chemical injection. As medical students in Arkham, they secretly exhume a freshly drowned laborer and administer West’s serum. Believing the attempt has failed, they leave the corpse unattended, only to hear it awaken with inhuman screams. They flee and accidentally burn down their laboratory; the revived man apparently escapes and claws at his own emptied grave.

During a typhoid epidemic, West obtains the body of Dr. Allan Halsey, the medical-school dean who had opposed his research. Reanimation restores only bestial life, and Halsey attacks West and the narrator before escaping. After murdering and partially devouring several people, he is captured and confined in an asylum, unrecognized by most as the formerly respected physician.

West and his companion establish a medical practice near a burial ground and continue experimenting. They attempt to revive Buck Robinson, a boxer killed in an illegal match, but see no result and bury him. He later returns as a murderous, child-eating creature, forcing West to empty his revolver into him. West subsequently secures an exceptionally fresh corpse by murdering a traveling salesman. The reanimated man briefly regains enough intelligence to accuse West of killing him, proving that immediate treatment can preserve memory and personality.

During the First World War, the two doctors experiment on fresh battlefield casualties. West reanimates the decapitated Major Eric Moreland Clapham-Lee, separately treating his body and severed head. Both display awareness, but an artillery explosion destroys the laboratory, and West assumes they have been annihilated.

Years later in Boston, West becomes increasingly paranoid. Clapham-Lee has survived and gathered several of West’s other reanimated victims, including Halsey and Robinson. The dead creatures enter West’s home through an underground passage, seize him, tear him apart, and carry away the pieces under Clapham-Lee’s direction. No trace of West remains, and the authorities refuse to believe the narrator’s account of the undead vengeance that caused his disappearance.
