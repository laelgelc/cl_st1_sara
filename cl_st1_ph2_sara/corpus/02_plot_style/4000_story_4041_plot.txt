During the First Punic War, the Roman consul Regulus invades Carthaginian territory in North Africa. After early victories, he rejects Carthage’s peace proposals with harsh demands. The Carthaginians then place their forces under the Spartan commander Xanthippus, whose elephants and cavalry crush the Roman army and capture Regulus.

After two years of imprisonment, Regulus is sent to Rome with Carthaginian envoys to negotiate peace and a prisoner exchange, having sworn to return if the mission fails. Putting Rome above his own safety, he advises the Senate to continue the war and refuse the exchange, arguing that Carthage is weakening and that he himself is too sick to be useful. Though offered release from his oath, he insists that honor requires him to keep his word. He returns voluntarily to captivity and dies soon afterward, although later stories of his torture may have been exaggerated. His wife, believing those accounts, cruelly retaliates against Carthaginian prisoners until the Senate intervenes. Regulus is remembered for sacrificing his life rather than betray his country or break his oath.
