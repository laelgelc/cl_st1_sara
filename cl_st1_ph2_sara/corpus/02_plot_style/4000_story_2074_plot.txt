Mrs. Trimble and Rebecca Wright, returning from a church event, stop at a remote, dilapidated farmhouse to visit Ann and Mandana Bray, two elderly, disabled sisters who have been forced to depend on the town after losing their home and possessions. They discover the sisters confined to a cold, sparsely furnished room, neglected by the family paid to house them and unable even to obtain proper shoes, firewood, or transportation to church.

Despite their hardship, Ann welcomes the visitors joyfully and shares the sisters’ meager food, including the last of their preserved peaches, while trying to minimize their suffering and even expressing sympathy for their unhappy caretaker. Deeply ashamed that the community has allowed such dignified women to live in misery, Mrs. Trimble resolves to persuade the town to return them to their former home, provide rent and firewood, and help repurchase their auctioned furniture. Rebecca promises to return the sisters’ cherished cups and seek medical help for Ann’s injured arm and Mandana’s failing eyesight. The two women leave determined to begin restoring the sisters’ home and independence the next morning.
