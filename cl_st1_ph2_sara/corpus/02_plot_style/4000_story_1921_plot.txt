Cantankerous Mr. Wragg’s attractive niece, Miss Miller, comes to live with him, drawing the attention of local young men—especially fisherman George Gale. Desperate to get close to her despite her uncle’s hostility, George follows his friends’ scheme to stage a bicycle accident outside the house. They carry him into Mr. Wragg’s bed and persuade a doctor that he is seriously injured.

The doctor orders George not to be moved and allows him only water. Realizing the accident was contrived, Mr. Wragg takes revenge by locking George in the bedroom, withholding his clothes and food, mocking his supposed delirium, and publicizing his predicament to the neighbors. Miss Miller eventually releases and feeds the humiliated George. When he declares that he must leave town to escape ridicule, she coyly suggests there may be a way for him to avoid looking foolish and maneuvers him into proposing marriage—only to teasingly pretend he is mad for suggesting it. When Mr. Wragg returns, he concludes that both of them are mad.
