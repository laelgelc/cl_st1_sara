Farrington, a resentful and alcoholic office clerk, is reprimanded by his employer, Mr. Alleyne, for neglecting his work and taking excessively long breaks. Distracted by his craving for drink, he slips out for porter, fails to complete an urgent contract, and then publicly humiliates Alleyne with a witty retort. Forced to apologize and fearing further persecution at work, he pawns his watch to finance a night of drinking.

Farrington retells his workplace triumph to friends in several pubs, but the evening gradually turns against him. He spends nearly all his money, fails to attract a woman who catches his eye, and is twice defeated in an arm-wrestling contest by a younger, smaller man. Returning home still thirsty, humiliated, and furious, he finds his wife away and the fire nearly out. Unable to retaliate against those who shamed him, he turns his rage on his young son, brutally beating the child as the boy begs for mercy and promises to pray for him.
