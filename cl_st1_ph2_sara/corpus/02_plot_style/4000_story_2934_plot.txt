A sensitive young soldier named Gratmar, tormented by a harsh officer, announces that he will kill himself so his spirit can take revenge. He apparently destroys himself with dynamite, leaving only unidentifiable fragments. Soon afterward, the officer becomes terrified and unstable, behaving as though an unseen presence is haunting him. He consults a spirit medium, who claims that Gratmar’s ghost intends to murder him that very night.

While returning to the garrison, the officer is found dead in his carriage with a broken neck. The narrator, who has secretly followed him, allows everyone to interpret the death as supernatural vengeance. A year later, however, the narrator receives a letter from Sweden suggesting that Gratmar could have faked his suicide by using animal remains, conspired with the medium, and personally murdered the officer while posing as a ghost. The letter is signed “Ramtarg”—Gratmar’s name reversed—strongly implying that the soldier survived and engineered the entire haunting and murder.
