During a fierce Alaskan blizzard, prospector Billy Joyce and several travelers take shelter at a roadhouse with Annie Black, a bitter, combative woman notorious for jumping mining claims. Annie has just lost a lawsuit against the Lund brothers over a rich fraction of land and openly declares her hatred for them.

An exhausted Indigenous guide arrives and reports that two men—the Lund brothers—are freezing nearby. Although the others refuse to brave the storm, Annie shames them into joining her. Her courage and knowledge lead the rescue, and she then nurses her former enemies back to health.

Soon Annie learns that her daughter, whom she has supported in boarding school while pretending to own a prosperous mine, is coming to find her. Terrified that the girl will discover her poverty and rough reputation, Annie reveals that years of abandonment, exploitation, and sacrifice hardened her. When the daughter arrives asking for Mrs. Bradshaw, the men protect Annie’s dignity by praising her. In gratitude for being saved, Lund declares that Annie owns the disputed rich claim. Annie finally steps forward, and mother and daughter reunite joyfully as the hardened men look on with emotion.
