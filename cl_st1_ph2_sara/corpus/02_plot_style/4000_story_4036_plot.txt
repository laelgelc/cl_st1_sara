A series of historical anecdotes celebrates courage, self-sacrifice, and composure in the face of deadly danger, especially danger involving fire and gunpowder.

During the Irish rebellion of 1641, Bryan Ferral saves the infant John Edgeworth from rebels by pretending to condemn him to a cruel death, hiding him in a bog, and later smuggling him through the rebel camp to his mother. John grows into an extravagant but principled man, while his fearless wife later prevents the destruction of their household by calmly removing a burning candle that a servant has stuck into a barrel of gunpowder. Their descendants continue the family tradition of courage, notably the Abbé Edgeworth, who accompanies Louis XVI to the guillotine.

The narrative then recounts later acts of heroism. In Jersey in 1804, Edward Touzel, Lieutenant Lys, and soldier William Ponteney risk certain death to extinguish a fire in a fort containing hundreds of barrels of powder and ammunition, thereby saving the garrison and the town. That same year, officers aboard HMS *Hindostan* repeatedly enter smoke-filled lower decks in an attempt to fight a shipboard fire; although the vessel is lost, the crew escapes.

After the capture of Ciudad Rodrigo in 1812, Captain William Jones and a sergeant discover a fire amid loose powder in a church used as a magazine. They carry the burning material out and avert a catastrophic explosion. In Strasbourg in 1820, French officer Mathieu Martinel forces his way through a burning barracks to prevent cartridges from igniting a powder store beneath nine helpless sick soldiers. He also saves a drowning soldier by allowing himself to be swept beneath a mill wheel while holding the man.

Years later, Martinel leads another rescue during a deadly crowd crush in Paris. He and his comrades repeatedly enter the suffocating mass to pull out victims, while mounted cuirassiers carefully divide the crowd and carry children to safety. Finally, during the Indian Mutiny, Lieutenant Willoughby sacrifices his life by blowing up the Delhi magazine so its ammunition cannot fall into rebel hands.

Together, the episodes argue that the essential virtues of chivalry—honor, mercy, loyalty, courage, and willingness to die for others—survive even after the age of knights.
