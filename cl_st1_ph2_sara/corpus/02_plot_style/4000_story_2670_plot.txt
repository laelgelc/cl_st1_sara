A thirty-year-old woman welcomes a close male friend to her studio for tea. They pride themselves on an unusually candid, intellectual friendship supposedly free from romantic passion. During the visit, however, unexpected silences and charged glances reveal a deeper intimacy neither is willing to acknowledge. Fearing that emotion will destroy their friendship, they repeatedly retreat into artificial literary discussion.

Unable to express what he feels, the man abruptly claims he must leave for another engagement. They part unhappily, each aware that they have missed an important moment. The woman returns to her studio furious and convinced that something between them has ended.

When the bell rings, she expects him to have returned, but finds an elderly, lonely female admirer bringing her a shabby bunch of violets. Initially intending to dismiss the visitor, the woman suddenly recognizes the value of simple, openly expressed affection. She embraces her friend tenderly and invites her to return. Calmed and renewed by this act, she writes to the man about their intellectual conversation and closes with the same sincere invitation: “Good night, my friend. Come again soon.”
