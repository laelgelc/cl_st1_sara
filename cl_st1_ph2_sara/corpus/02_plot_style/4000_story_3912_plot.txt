Lucius Warland, an ambitious but indebted man, learns that powerful political figure John Oberville is coming to visit. Oberville was once deeply loved by Lucius’s wife, Isabel, but gave her up because he claimed an obligation to a married woman. Realizing Oberville could secure him a coveted diplomatic secretaryship, Lucius subtly encourages Isabel to revive the old connection.

When Isabel meets Oberville after ten years, he confesses that he still regrets losing her. She initially shares his emotion, having preserved an idealized image of him as a man who sacrificed love to honor. But Oberville reveals that he abandoned her merely because he was afraid of an unpleasant scene. This admission destroys Isabel’s long-held reverence for him, and her love abruptly dies.

Though emotionally disillusioned, Isabel decides to use Oberville’s lingering feelings to advance her husband’s career. She claims Oberville’s company for the next day, arranges for the other guests to be sent away, and tells Lucius to go to Washington after all—signaling that she will secure Oberville’s political help, even as she recoils from her husband’s pleased affection.
