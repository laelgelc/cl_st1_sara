An aging businessman, Mr. Neave, walks home feeling suddenly exhausted and alienated from the springtime vitality around him. He worries that his spoiled, irresponsible son Harold cannot be trusted to inherit the successful business, despite his wife and daughters urging him to retire.

At home, his affluent family is absorbed in parties, clothes, and social gossip. Though they fuss over his tiredness, they barely attend to him as a person. While dressing for dinner, Mr. Neave dozes and envisions himself as a withered old man endlessly climbing stairs, compelled to return to the office because no one will stop or replace him. Awakening alone in the dark, he feels that his wife and children are strangers and that his life has passed him by. A memory of a loving young woman bidding him farewell makes him wonder whether his entire family life has been a mistake or a dream. When the servant announces dinner, he suppresses his distress and goes downstairs.
