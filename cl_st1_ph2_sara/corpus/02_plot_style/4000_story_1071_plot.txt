At a New Year’s Eve children’s party, an observant outsider notices that the gifts and attention given to each child reflect the wealth and status of their parents. The host’s eleven-year-old daughter, already promised an enormous dowry, receives the finest doll, while the poor governess’s timid son receives a cheap book and is excluded by the other children. The girl befriends him, and they play together privately.

Julian Mastakovich, an honored and influential guest, learns of the girl’s dowry and secretly calculates how much it will grow over the next five years. Realizing the fortune he could gain by marrying her when she is sixteen, he approaches and flatters the frightened child while trying to drive her poor companion away. When the boy resists leaving her, Julian angrily chases and humiliates him. He then refuses the host’s request to help the boy obtain a position, but eagerly ingratiates himself with the wealthy girl’s parents and secures an invitation to visit them.

Five years later, the narrator encounters a wedding and recognizes the groom as Julian and the pale, tearful, sixteen-year-old bride as the wealthy girl. Onlookers discuss her dowry, now worth five hundred thousand rubles, confirming that Julian’s cold calculations were correct.
