Aboard a ship in Calcutta, the bullying first mate repeatedly targets red-haired sailor Bill Cousins. Bill and Bob Pullin disguise themselves as Hindoos so they can provoke and beat the mate ashore without being charged with mutiny. Their plan succeeds only partly: although they injure him, he gives both men a severe beating. Too proud to admit that two Hindoos defeated him, the mate returns claiming he fought off five German sailors.

Bill and Bob then discover that the carpenter’s blacking will not wash off. Fearing the mate will recognize them and punish the entire crew, their shipmates suggest desertion, but no solution is reached. When the two are summoned on deck, they claim that five foreign sailors attacked them, knocked them unconscious, and blackened their faces. The captain concludes that they were assaulted by the same supposed Germans who attacked the mate. Unable to expose the lie without admitting he was beaten by the disguised sailors, the mate remains silent. The sympathetic captain protects Bill and Bob while their coloring gradually fades, leaving the humiliated mate to vent his anger by bullying the rest of the crew.
