In 1799, American captain Amasa Delano encounters a battered Spanish slave ship drifting near an uninhabited island off Chile. Boarding to offer supplies and assistance, he finds the vessel in disorder. Its captain, Benito Cereno, appears sick, nervous, and strangely dependent on his devoted Black servant, Babo. Cereno claims that storms, disease, and shortages have killed most of the Spanish crew and many enslaved Africans.

Throughout the day, Delano notices disturbing irregularities: the Africans move freely, armed men polish hatchets, the remaining Spaniards behave fearfully, and Cereno repeatedly gives evasive answers. Yet Delano’s optimism and racial prejudices lead him to explain away every warning sign. He assumes the Africans are naturally submissive and interprets Babo’s constant attendance as exemplary loyalty.

When Delano finally leaves, Cereno suddenly leaps into his boat. Babo follows with a dagger, revealing that the enslaved Africans had revolted, killed their owner and much of the crew, and forced Cereno and the surviving Spaniards to act out an elaborate deception. Babo had remained beside Cereno not as a servant but as his guard, directing his every word and movement. Delano’s men attack and recapture the ship.

Legal testimony later reveals that Babo organized the uprising and ordered the owner’s skeleton displayed as a warning beneath the inscription “follow your leader.” Babo is executed, and his head is publicly displayed. Although Cereno survives, he remains psychologically devastated by what happened and dies a few months later.
