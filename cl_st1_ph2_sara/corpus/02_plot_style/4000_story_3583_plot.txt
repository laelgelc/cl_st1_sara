A traveler falls ill at an inn and summons a district doctor, Trifon Ivanich. As they talk through the night, the doctor recounts a painful episode from his early career.

Years before, he was called through terrible roads to treat Aleksandra Andreyevna, a beautiful, educated twenty-year-old woman suffering from a severe fever. Though her impoverished family trusted him completely, her condition steadily worsened. Trifon remained at the house, became deeply attached to her, and eventually fell in love.

Convinced that she was dying, Aleksandra confessed that she loved him. Trifon returned her declaration and promised they could marry, although he understood that her feelings arose partly from fever, fear, and her desperation to experience love before death. During her final days, she openly claimed they were pledged to each other, exposing their intimacy to her mother. Shortly before dying, she asked Trifon to forgive her, remember her, and keep her ring.

After finishing the story, Trifon suppresses his grief and reveals that he later married an ill-tempered merchant’s daughter for her dowry. He and the traveler then play cards, and the doctor leaves pleased after winning a small sum.
