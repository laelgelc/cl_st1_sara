A captive circus ape named Romulus escapes from his cage while traveling through the Santa Clara Valley. Overwhelmed but delighted by the unfamiliar freedom of the countryside, he explores fields and trees before encountering Moses, a gentle, intellectually disabled young man confined in an asylum. Recognizing a fellow prisoner, Romulus persuades Moses to break through the fence, and the two spend a joyful afternoon roaming together and causing mischief.

Toward evening they hide in a tree above a Chinese burial ground and watch a grieving mother bury her young daughter, Wang Tai. Food is left on the grave as an offering. After the mourners depart, the hungry fugitives eat the offerings, find the hidden spades, and dig into the shallow grave.

The next morning, authorities discover Romulus and Moses asleep beside the opened grave and empty coffin—and Wang Tai alive. People conclude that the child had mistakenly been buried alive and that the pair’s grave-robbing antics rescued and revived her. Romulus and Moses are punished and returned to captivity, while Wang Tai is restored to her overjoyed mother.
