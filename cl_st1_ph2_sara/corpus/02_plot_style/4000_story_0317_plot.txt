Mr. Belknap struggles with his twelve-year-old son, John Thomas, whose sullen manner, slow obedience, and apparent forgetfulness frustrate him. He orders the boy to fetch a hammer and nails, speaking so harshly that John performs the errand reluctantly and returns without the hammer.

While John is away, Aunt Mary suggests that Mr. Belknap’s cold, commanding manner may be causing the boy’s stubbornness. She urges him to try kindness, explaining that love inspires obedience more readily than fear. When John returns and admits forgetting the hammer, his father controls his anger and responds with calm disappointment instead of punishment. Moved by this gentleness, John eagerly runs back for the missing tool and returns promptly.

Mr. Belknap praises him and politely asks him to perform another errand. John agrees cheerfully and leaves at once. His father realizes that affection and self-control succeed where threats and authority failed, and regrets not having learned earlier how kindness could bring out his son’s better qualities.
