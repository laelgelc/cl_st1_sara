American jewel dealer Douglas Hargrave encounters Sir Henry Marquis, head of London’s Criminal Investigation Department, puzzling over a coded newspaper notice about “nine hundred horses” being shipped from New York to an unknown European port. Soon afterward, a wealthy-seeming American woman calling herself Mrs. Farmingham asks Hargrave to authenticate sapphires offered by Major Mikos, supposedly a Romanian officer who must leave London immediately. The jewels are genuine, so Hargrave buys them for eighteen thousand dollars in gold, expecting Mrs. Farmingham to repay him and purchase them at a profit.

Mrs. Farmingham later receives a portmanteau containing eighteen thousand dollars in American gold coins and exchanges it for the sapphires, promising to pay Hargrave’s commission the next morning. Meanwhile, Sir Henry consults a cipher expert and learns that the coded notice identifies London as the shipment’s destination.

When Hargrave casually shows Sir Henry one of the coins, the detective discovers it is gold-plated counterfeit metal. In criminal slang, a counterfeit twenty-dollar coin is called a “horse,” meaning the mysterious shipment consists of nine hundred false coins. Hargrave realizes he has been deceived: “Mrs. Farmingham” is the notorious counterfeiter Hustling Anne, while the supposed Romanian officer is her accomplice, Dago Mulehaus. They have used genuine sapphires and elaborate disguises to exchange counterfeit money for Hargrave’s real jewels and cash.
