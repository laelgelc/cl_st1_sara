Peter Mullaney has dreamed of becoming a police officer since childhood, but he falls just short of the department’s minimum height and has limited formal education. Impressed by his strength, determination, and good character, the police commissioner grants him a conditional place in training: he must earn excellent marks in every subject.

Peter excels physically but struggles with the commissioner’s demanding general-knowledge curriculum. During field training, veteran Officer Gaffney teaches him that learning is useful, but the essential quality of a policeman is never retreating when someone needs help. Peter later fails his final examination after mistakenly writing that the Tropic of Capricorn is in the Bronx. Dismissed from the program, he receives permission to wear the uniform on patrol one last time so his fiancée can see him in it.

That night, burglars shoot Gaffney inside a house. Though unarmed, rejected by the department, and briefly tempted to walk away, Peter remembers that a true policeman never runs when needed. He enters the house, confronts both armed criminals, defeats them, and is critically wounded.

Peter survives and awakens in the hospital, where the commissioner pins the dead Gaffney’s shield on him. Declaring Peter a brave officer, the commissioner dismisses his academic mistake and accepts him onto the force.
