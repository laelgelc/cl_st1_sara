Sixteen-year-old Raymond Dale is rebellious, foul-tempered, and hostile to education after years of neglect and a succession of failed tutors. His father hires John Twigg, giving him one month and complete authority to gain control of the boy and prepare him for college.

Unlike his predecessors, Twigg remains calm and firm. He outmaneuvers Raymond’s refusals to study, imposes consequences without losing his temper, and uses Raymond’s interests in fishing, riding, shooting, golf, and adventure stories to build a relationship with him. Raymond initially spies on Twigg and tries to defy or evade him, but he gradually respects the tutor’s strength, fairness, competence, and gentlemanly code. He begins studying regularly, moderating his behavior, and imagining attending Twigg’s college.

At the end of the month, Twigg says he must leave because he cannot honestly claim complete victory. Raymond, now emotionally attached to him, asks him to stay. Twigg agrees only if Raymond accepts harder study, obedience, an end to bad companions and cigarettes, and punishment for swearing. Raymond accepts these conditions and openly asks Twigg to remain, marking the beginning of his genuine reform.
