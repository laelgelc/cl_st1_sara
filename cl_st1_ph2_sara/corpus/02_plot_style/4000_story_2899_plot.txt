During a violent mountain thunderstorm, a drenched stranger enters a man’s cottage carrying an elaborate copper staff. He refuses the hearth and insists that the center of the room is the only safe place, warning that fireplaces, walls, metal bars, bell wires, trees, animals, and even tall people can conduct lightning.

The stranger reveals that he sells lightning rods and deliberately travels during storms, using thunder and tales of fatal strikes to frighten people into buying his product. The homeowner questions his claims, exposes weaknesses in his examples, and mocks the contradiction between the salesman’s professed ability to provide safety and his own extreme terror.

As the storm clears and a rainbow appears, the homeowner rejects the rod, declaring his trust in divine providence rather than fear-driven promises of protection. Enraged, the salesman attacks him with the rod, but the homeowner seizes and breaks it, tramples it, and throws both the man and his shattered instrument outside. Despite this defeat and the homeowner’s warnings to his neighbors, the salesman continues traveling during storms and profiting from human fear.
