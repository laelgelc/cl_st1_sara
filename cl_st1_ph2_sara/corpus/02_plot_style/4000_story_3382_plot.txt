Two elderly widows and childhood friends, Julia Cairnes and Sophia Maybury, long to escape loneliness by living together. After Sophia’s husband dies, she moves into Julia’s comfortable village home, expecting their shared beliefs and old affection to create a peaceful companionship.

Their habits and temperaments soon clash. Julia is frugal, independent, socially active, fond of gossip, protective of her plants and cat, and strict in her religion. Sophia prefers varied food, sunshine, cards, quiet refinement, and memories of her husband; she dislikes Julia’s cat and plants and feels abandoned when Julia goes out. Each begins to see the other as controlling, inconsiderate, and strangely changed, yet neither feels able to end the arrangement.

When the hotel that supplied Sophia’s income burns down uninsured, leaving her penniless, Julia prepares to support her permanently. Instead, Sophia sells her watch and uses the money to enter an old ladies’ home, where she gains independence, companionship, and freedom to live as she pleases. Julia likewise restores her house and routines and delights in recovering her autonomy. Once they no longer share a home, they visit each other devotedly every day, and the village regards their friendship as ideal.
