A country-dwelling narrator observes the birds that herald spring. An unidentified wren-like songbird, the sociable Phoebe-birds, and the bluebirds bring hope and cheer as winter ends. His chief interest is the Bobolink, whose joyful music once made him envy its carefree life during his school days.

As spring turns to summer, however, the Bobolink stops singing, exchanges its striking plumage for a duller coat, and abandons its refined habits for gluttony. Traveling south in search of richer food, it grows fat on reeds and rice and becomes prized by hunters as a delicacy. Its career ends at the gourmand’s table, offering a cautionary moral against forsaking intellectual and artistic pursuits for excessive sensual indulgence.
