Madame Carambeau, an elderly New Orleans Creole fiercely prejudiced against Americans and estranged from her son Henri for marrying an American woman, withdraws from her grandson Gustave’s noisy birthday party. When a feverish little girl unexpectedly takes refuge in her lap, Madame’s skill and instinct as a nurse overcome her hostility. She cares tenderly for the child for two days and becomes deeply affected by her affection and innocence.

The encounter gradually forces Madame to question her prejudices. On Christmas morning, she shocks her household by attending an American church and then visiting Henri, whom she has not seen for ten years. There she discovers that the child she nursed is her own granddaughter. Madame reconciles with Henri and welcomes his wife and daughter into the family. They return together to the old family home for Christmas dinner, where Madame embraces the prospect of teaching her granddaughter French while learning English from her, humorously claiming that she herself has no prejudices.
