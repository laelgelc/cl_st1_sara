A narrator notices a small dark mark above the mantelpiece but remains seated rather than inspecting it. Wondering whether it is a nail hole, a leaf, a crack, or some protruding object, the narrator drifts through a long chain of reflections on memory, lost possessions, death, knowledge, self-image, social conventions, authority, freedom, nature, and the uncertain nature of reality. The mark repeatedly anchors these wandering thoughts, offering a sense of solidity amid uncertainty. Finally, another person enters, complains about the war, and casually reveals that the mysterious mark is a snail on the wall.
