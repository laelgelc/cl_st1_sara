Antoine “Tonie” Bocaze, an awkward but highly capable fisherman, becomes instantly infatuated with Claire Duvigné, a wealthy young woman from New Orleans, after hearing her play the organ at church. He begins spending nearly all his time at Grand Isle simply to watch her socialize, dance, swim, and play games with her many admirers.

During a rare boat trip alone together, Claire realizes that Tonie loves her. Flattered and mildly sympathetic but unaware of the depth of his obsession, she coquettishly gives him a silver chain. Tonie is overwhelmed by desire and later regrets not embracing her and drowning with her at sea, resolving that he would do so if they were ever alone again. They never are.

Separated from Claire after the summer, Tonie grows sick with hopeless longing and contemplates death. Months later in New Orleans, he learns that Claire has died after catching a cold. Unexpectedly, the news releases him from despair, and his health and vitality return.

Back home, Tonie explains to his shocked mother that while Claire lived, he was tortured by the certainty that she would marry another man and have a family. In death, however, she can belong to no earthly rival. He believes that in the afterlife, where social distinctions vanish, Claire’s soul may finally recognize that he loved her best. Comforted by this hope, he resumes his life and returns affectionately to his duties toward his mother.
