Years after a violent expedition in Africa, adventurer Stuart McGregor remains terrified of his former partner, Farragut Hutchison. The two men had drunkenly desecrated and robbed a Bakoto fetish, prompting the tribe’s warriors to capture them and demand that one be sacrificed by prolonged torture. Allowed to choose the victim themselves, the partners settled the matter with a hand of poker. McGregor, an experienced cardsharp, secretly dealt himself four aces, condemning Hutchison, who was taken away to die.

Although Hutchison’s remains were later discovered, McGregor is haunted not by remorse but by fear that his betrayed partner will somehow take revenge. Ten years later, an antiques dealer sends him an African signal drum covered with white human skin bearing Hutchison’s distinctive eagle tattoo. Delighted by final proof that Hutchison is dead, McGregor embraces the drum. A venomous African snake concealed inside, revived by the New York heat, bites and kills him. As he dies, McGregor sees the tattooed eagle appear to wink and believes Hutchison has finally reached him from beyond the grave.
