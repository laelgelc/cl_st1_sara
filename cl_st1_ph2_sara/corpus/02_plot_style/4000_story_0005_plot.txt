After drunkenly desecrating and robbing a Bakoto fetish in Africa, adventurers Stuart McGregor and Farragut Hutchison are captured by the tribe. The Bakoto chief decrees that one must undergo a slow, torturous sacrifice and orders the two men to choose the victim themselves. McGregor proposes deciding by a single poker hand, then secretly deals himself four aces, condemning Hutchison to death.

For the next ten years, McGregor is haunted not by remorse but by fear that Hutchison will somehow take revenge, even from beyond the grave. He becomes obsessed with African artifacts and repeatedly recalls the sinister wink of the eagle tattooed on Hutchison’s back.

One day, an antiquities dealer sends McGregor an African signal drum covered with white human skin bearing Hutchison’s distinctive tattoo. Delighted by this apparent proof that Hutchison is truly dead, McGregor embraces the drum. A venomous African snake concealed inside bites him, having revived in the New York heat. As McGregor dies, the tattooed eagle seems to wink, convincing him that Hutchison has finally killed him from beyond the grave.
