A young boy wakes consumed by the desire for books, colored pencils, and, above all, an immediate lesson in writing numbers. His uncle, unwilling to go into town or teach him that day, falsely claims it is a holiday and insists the lesson must wait until tomorrow. Frustrated and impatient, the boy grows increasingly unruly, eventually jumping, stamping, and shrieking during the adults’ tea. When he defiantly refuses to stop, his uncle loses his temper, slaps him, and sends him from the room.

The humiliated boy cries desperately, but his mother, grandmother, and uncle suppress their sympathy in the name of discipline. Even after he falls silent, they maintain their coldness, urging him to apologize and warning that nobody loves disobedient children. The boy stubbornly declares that he will never love his uncle again, but his grandmother finally breaks his resistance by reminding him that only his uncle can teach him the coveted numbers.

Exhausted but still longing to learn, the boy timidly asks forgiveness and insists that he loves his uncle. The uncle relents, and they sit together at the table. Overjoyed and eager to please, the child carefully traces the numbers while his uncle gently corrects him, restoring their affection after the painful struggle.
