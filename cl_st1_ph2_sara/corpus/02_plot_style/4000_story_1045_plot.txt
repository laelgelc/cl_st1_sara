A jovial German baron grows bored with hunting and revelry, so he marries the daughter of a neighboring nobleman. His domineering wife gradually forces him to dismiss his companions and abandon everything he enjoys. Burdened by twelve children, an interfering mother-in-law, mounting debts, and the prospect of a thirteenth child, he decides to kill himself.

Before doing so, he retreats with wine and a pipe to a private room, where he encounters the grotesque Genius of Despair and Suicide. Their conversation inadvertently exposes the absurdity of suicide: people kill themselves from both poverty and wealth, present troubles may yet be remedied, and death offers no guarantee of improvement. Recovering his humor and resolve, the baron rejects suicide, frightening the apparition away with laughter.

He then confronts his wife and in-laws, returns to hunting, and lives for many more years as a happy, though not wealthy, man while raising his large family.
