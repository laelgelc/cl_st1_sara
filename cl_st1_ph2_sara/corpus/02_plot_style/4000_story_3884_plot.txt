A healthy young woman travels home from Colorado with her terminally ill husband, troubled by how his illness has estranged them and transformed her from wife into reluctant caretaker. During the night, he dies unnoticed in his sleeping-car berth. Terrified that railway officials will remove her and the body at an unfamiliar station, she conceals his death, telling the porter and inquisitive passengers that he is merely sleeping or too sick to rise.

Throughout the day, she guards the closed berth while enduring unwanted advice, mounting suspicion, and increasingly vivid hallucinations of her husband’s dead face. She plans to pretend surprise when the body is discovered in New York, but the strain of maintaining the deception nearly overwhelms her. As the train finally enters the city and the porter suggests waking her husband, she loses consciousness and falls against his berth.
