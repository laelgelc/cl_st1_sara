Vasantasena, the daughter of a defeated southern king, is brought as a captive slave to the court of the young King Vikramavati. Enchanted by her beauty, Vikramavati frees her, showers her with riches, and offers her complete devotion. Yet Vasantasena cannot return his love, believing it selfish because he focuses on his own passion without seeking hers.

Instead, she loves Madusadan, the older captain of horse who rescued her during battle. His patience, restraint, strength, and understanding awaken her desire. When Vikramavati discovers them embracing, he condemns them to a lingering death. Madusadan and Vasantasena insist that their love will survive both death and rebirth.

The king’s prime minister proposes a more subtle punishment: chain the lovers together and confine them in luxurious isolation for three days, expecting constant proximity and monotony to turn passion into disgust. When the king and minister listen at the end of the confinement, however, they hear the couple declaring that their love has only deepened.

Their physical chain becomes a symbol of the qualities that make love enduring: passion, patience, fulfillment, friendship, tolerance, understanding, tenderness, forgiveness, service, and humor. The tale concludes that lasting love also requires vigilance and must never be allowed to grow complacent through excessive security.
