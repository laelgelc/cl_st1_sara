A sailor-narrator recalls visiting the barren, volcanic Encantadas, portraying them as drought-stricken, treacherous islands inhabited chiefly by reptiles and surrounded by dangerous currents. While cruising near Albemarle, his crew captures three enormous tortoises. Their ancient, melancholy appearance inspires awe and nightmarish visions, but the sailors soon eat them and turn their shells into serving vessels.

The narrator then visits Rock Rodondo, an isolated sea tower crowded with penguins, pelicans, albatrosses, gulls, petrels, and other seabirds, while unusually abundant and fearless fish swarm below. After a difficult ascent, he surveys the surrounding ocean and islands, using the view to reflect on the remote island groups off South America and on the hazardous voyages through which Spanish sailors first encountered them.
