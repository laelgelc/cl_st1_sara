In 1878, a cattle crew driving a herd up the Chisholm Trail hires an inexperienced, inquisitive young cowboy nicknamed Lucy. Curious about why two nearby trail routes separate and later rejoin, Lucy asks the foreman, who directs him to the amiable, boastful Stubb as part of a prank.

Stubb solemnly claims that he created the newer route five years earlier while leading a cattle drive. According to his elaborate tale, another trail boss repeatedly blocked his herd and refused to let it pass. Stubb finally drove his cattle cross-country alongside the rival herd, creating a new trail. The rival outfit tried to stampede Stubb’s cattle by tying a dry hide to a steer’s tail, but Stubb’s roper killed the steer before it reached the herd. A gunfight followed, leaving the rival boss and two of his men dead, several horses killed, and others wounded. Afterward, Stubb’s men helped the defeated outfit bury its dead in the nearby sand hills and continued north, where their employer praised them for defending his interests.

Satisfied that he has explained both the alternate trail and the three graves Lucy noticed, Stubb ends his dramatic account and refuses to tell any more stories, leaving the gullible newcomer to accept the embellished tale as truth.
