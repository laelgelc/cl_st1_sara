In Montpont, truffle hunting is both a cherished sport and a source of civic pride. On the final Sunday of the season, close friends and rival champions Monsieur Bonticu, an undertaker, and Monsieur Pantan, a veterinarian and apothecary, enter the preserve tied for first place. Guided by their prized pigs, Anastasie and Clotilde, they remain evenly matched until both animals point to the same enormous truffle.

Each man claims the find, and their attempt to dig it up simultaneously ends with the truffle torn in half. Their ceremonious disagreement escalates into personal insults. When Bonticu calls Pantan a “puff-ball,” an unforgivable slur among truffle hunters, Pantan challenges him to a duel to the death. Bound by Perigord’s severe dueling code, Bonticu accepts, though both men understand that the coming combat—with pistols, sabers, and daggers—is likely to be fatal. Afterward, the shaken undertaker returns to his shop, sits among his coffins, and stares fearfully at one that appears large enough for himself.
