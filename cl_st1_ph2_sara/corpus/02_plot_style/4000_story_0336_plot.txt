Mrs. Cartwright persuades her compliant husband to spend his carefully saved money on a new parlor carpet so they will appear prosperous when her cousin Sally visits. The carpet costs more than planned and leads to further decorating expenses, exhausting money intended to repay a friend’s loan. When the friend unexpectedly asks for repayment, Mr. Cartwright cannot pay and receives an angry letter accusing him of buying luxuries instead of honoring his debt.

Sally arrives seeking renewed emotional intimacy, but Mrs. Cartwright’s constant concern with appearances disappoints and alienates her. Before leaving, Sally explains that she came to see her cousin, not to judge her furniture. Afterward, Mrs. Cartwright discovers the creditor’s letter and realizes that her vanity has damaged both her husband’s friendship and her own relationship with Sally.

When a neighbor admires the new carpet, Mrs. Cartwright immediately sells it for fifty dollars, restores the old carpet, and gives the money to her husband for its original purpose. The couple resolve thereafter to live according to their means and values rather than other people’s opinions.
