Thirteen-year-old Sashka lives in poverty with a violent, alcoholic mother and a sick, broken father. Rebellious and embittered by constant punishment and humiliation, he is expelled from school and responds to hardship by bullying others and defying authority.

On Christmas Eve, wealthy family friends invite him to their celebration. Surrounded by privileged children, Sashka feels alienated and resentful until he notices a small wax angel hidden among the branches of the Christmas tree. The figure awakens in him an unfamiliar sense of beauty, tenderness, and hope. Desperate to possess it, he humiliates himself by kneeling before the hostess and begging until she gives it to him.

Sashka takes the angel home and shares it with his father, who associates it with his lost love and the better life he once knew. As father and son contemplate the angel, their shared wonder briefly overcomes the emotional distance between them, giving both comfort and a sense of spiritual connection. They hang the angel near the stove so they can see it while they sleep, but during the night its wax body melts from the heat and falls into a shapeless mass. Morning arrives while father and son remain unaware that their brief symbol of beauty and hope has been destroyed.
