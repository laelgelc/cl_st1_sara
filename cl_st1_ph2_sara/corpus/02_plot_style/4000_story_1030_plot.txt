A seasoned English traveler describes repeated visits to a French seaside resort. After humorously recounting the seasickness, customs inspections, and confusion that make arrival humiliating, the narrator explores the town’s attractions: its picturesque old quarter, lively markets, coastal scenery, fishing community, and inexpensive public entertainments.

The central portrait is of the narrator’s landlord, M. Loyal Devasseur, a former soldier, town councillor, property owner, and ardent admirer of Napoleon. Loyal is practical, hospitable, unfailingly optimistic, and deeply generous, once allowing an impoverished family to remain rent-free in one of his houses for a year. The narrator also describes sea bathing, village festivals, charitable celebrations, English boarding-house residents, and the mingling of French and English communities.

Rather than following a conventional dramatic plot, the story unfolds as an affectionate, comic tour of the resort and its people, ending with praise for the hardworking, temperate, cheerful inhabitants and for the mutual understanding that has developed between the two nationalities.
