James Duffy, a rigid and solitary Dublin bank cashier, lives an orderly life without friends, intimacy, or emotional commitments. At a concert he meets Mrs. Emily Sinico, a married woman whose sea-captain husband is frequently absent. Their repeated meetings develop into a deep intellectual and emotional companionship, and Duffy gradually shares with her the private thoughts he has withheld from everyone else.

When Mrs. Sinico passionately presses his hand to her cheek, Duffy recoils from the physical and romantic implications of their relationship. Convinced that attachment inevitably brings sorrow, he ends their friendship despite her distress and returns to his isolated routine.

Four years later, Duffy reads that Mrs. Sinico has been killed by a train after developing a dependence on alcohol. He initially reacts with disgust, judging her decline and reassuring himself that he was right to leave her. As he reflects, however, he recognizes how lonely she must have been and begins to feel responsible for having denied the only person who loved him. Walking through places they once visited, he realizes that his self-protective moral rigidity has excluded him from love and life. Unable any longer to sense her presence, he is left with the devastating awareness that he is completely alone.
