While hiking across a lonely moor, Martin becomes confused by an apparently inaccurate map and signpost and chooses the unfamiliar route over Litacy Hill. Along the way, he encounters two suspicious German-speaking tramps and suddenly realizes they are disguised criminals. They attack him, cut his throat, and steal something hidden beneath his clothes.

The experience abruptly vanishes, and Martin finds himself back at the signpost, unharmed, as though he has witnessed a detailed psychic vision intended for someone else. He flees to a nearby village, where he recognizes the two attackers—now respectably dressed—in the inn.

The next morning, an elderly tourist with gold-rimmed glasses asks for directions to Litacy Hill. Martin instinctively warns him and offers to accompany him, but the stranger declines. When a tramp approaches and asks the time, mirroring Martin’s vision, Martin understands that the warning concerned this man. Nevertheless, convinced that he has no right to interfere based on mysteriously acquired knowledge, he leaves.

Later, Martin reads that the tourist was murdered on Litacy Hill, his throat cut and a large sum of money stolen from a belt beneath his clothes. Police are pursuing two German tramps.
