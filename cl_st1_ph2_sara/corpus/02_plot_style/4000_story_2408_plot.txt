While traveling through rural Ireland, a priest meets a local farmer who tells him the tale of Terence Mooney and his unusually affectionate old gander. When neighbors suggest the bird may be supernatural, Terence consults Jer Garvan, a fairy doctor, who claims the gander contains the wandering soul of Terence’s father. To force the spirit to reveal itself, they announce that the bird will be taken to market and sold for food.

That night, a drunken Terence accidentally falls asleep in the poultry hamper prepared for the gander. At dawn, two farm boys place the bird in the same hamper and set off for Tipperary, unaware that Terence is buried beneath the hay. When the jolting cart awakens him, his muffled protests convince the terrified boys that the gander is speaking and has identified itself as Terence. They rush to Father Crotty, who orders the hamper suspended over running water while he performs an exorcism.

The hamper’s bottom breaks, dropping Terence and the gander into the river; the startled priest and his horse tumble in after them. Everyone survives, but no one accepts the simple explanation: Terence and his wife deny his drunken mistake, while the priest declares the affair a mystery. Terence thereafter becomes even more devoted to the gander and eventually dies at a great age, leaving the bird and his large family behind.
