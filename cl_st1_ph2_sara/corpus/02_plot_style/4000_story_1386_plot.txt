In 1797, a storm drives Yankee trader Peleg Scudder into the isolated Spanish settlement of San Carlos. Commandant Hermenegildo Salvatierra grants him shelter, and the two spend a drunken evening exchanging news, gossip, and trinkets. By morning, Scudder’s schooner has vanished, and the one-eyed commandant discovers that a lifelike glass eye has been placed in his empty socket.

The community declares the new eye miraculous, and Salvatierra conceals the mundane truth. Yet its cold, unblinking stare terrifies everyone, making soldiers, servants, children, and priests feel scrutinized and guilty. Their fearful behavior makes Salvatierra increasingly suspicious and severe until his personality comes to match the eye. Social trust collapses, religious authority weakens, drought afflicts the land, and unrest spreads among the Indigenous people.

On the final evening of 1798, Salvatierra’s young pupil Paquita tells him she still recognizes his old voice and touch but fears the eye because it is not truly his. That night, mutineers enter his room to murder him, but the glass eye remains open while he sleeps and frightens the would-be assassin. Salvatierra awakens, fights off the attackers, and loses the artificial eye in the struggle. With it gone, his former nature and the settlement’s harmony return, the rains resume, and San Carlos recovers—while Scudder continues trading such “Boston notions” as glass eyes elsewhere.
