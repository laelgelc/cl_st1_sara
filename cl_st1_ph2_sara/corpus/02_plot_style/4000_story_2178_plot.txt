After Pinecoffin sells Nafferton a dangerously bad-tempered horse and mocks him for being thrown, Nafferton vows revenge. Exploiting Pinecoffin’s enthusiasm for agricultural administration, he pretends to be developing a government-backed scheme to raise pigs as food for the British Army in India. He draws Pinecoffin into an ever-expanding bureaucratic inquiry covering pig breeds, distribution, husbandry, diet, acclimatization, economics, religious objections, bristles, hides, and related industries.

For more than a year, Pinecoffin exhausts himself producing elaborate reports while managing his regular official duties. When he finally refers Nafferton back to an earlier, irrelevant report, Nafferton complains to the government that Pinecoffin has been unhelpful and flippant. Pinecoffin is officially reprimanded, and Nafferton sends his work and frustrated response to a newspaper, which publicly ridicules him as pompous and impractical.

Humiliated and unable to explain how he was manipulated, Pinecoffin confronts Nafferton. Nafferton reveals that the entire pig inquiry was calculated revenge for the horse: just as Pinecoffin resented neither the sale nor the danger as much as the ensuing mockery, Nafferton has arranged for Pinecoffin’s lasting punishment to be public ridicule.
