Mrs. Bates receives a beautiful Parisian head-dress and plans to debut it at an upcoming party. Her vain acquaintance, Mrs. Tarleton, repeatedly asks for the pattern, but Mrs. Bates declines, even offering to surrender the original rather than allow matching versions to appear. After borrowing the head-dress under the pretense of showing it to her sister, Mrs. Tarleton secretly has a milliner copy it.

The milliner inadvertently reveals the deception to Mrs. Bates, who quietly chooses another head-dress for the party. Mrs. Tarleton arrives wearing the copied design and initially enjoys the admiration it attracts, but realizes she has been exposed when Mrs. Bates appears in something different and greets her with polite coldness.

Feeling offended and anxious about her social standing, Mrs. Tarleton circulates a distorted account that minimizes her wrongdoing. Mrs. Bates explains that although the material injury is trivial, Mrs. Tarleton’s dishonesty and betrayal of confidence make continued intimacy impossible. Mutual acquaintances nevertheless characterize the quarrel as a misunderstanding in which both women share the blame. The story concludes that Mrs. Bates is justified in ending the friendship because Mrs. Tarleton’s conduct reveals an untrustworthy character.
