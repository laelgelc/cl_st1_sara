Elizabeth Rundle cares for her dying uncle, Ebenezer Transom, at his isolated farm. On the night he dies, his estranged son William unexpectedly returns from the army. Elizabeth once loved William, but he had abandoned her years before. He now behaves coarsely, assumes he will inherit the farm, and hints that Elizabeth might become its mistress with him.

Elizabeth soon discovers that William has secretly brought a wife, Maria. When she leads Maria upstairs, they catch William searching his dead father’s chest for money and papers. There he finds a will leaving the entire estate to Elizabeth in gratitude for her care. Although she knew nothing of it, William accuses her of scheming to rob him and buy his affection. Humiliated by her former love for such a man and believing his disinheritance unnatural, Elizabeth burns the will, allowing William to inherit, then leaves to work for the neighboring Hoopers.

William and Maria take possession of the farm, while Elizabeth avoids them and closes herself off emotionally. Maria dies a little over a year later. Five months afterward, William proposes to Elizabeth, presenting marriage as a way to restore her share of the property and end his loneliness. Elizabeth refuses. She explains that her experiences have exposed both his character and her own misplaced love, and that he has wounded her pride too deeply. William departs angrily, and Elizabeth remains unmarried for the rest of her life.
