Ivan Vasilievich recalls how, as a carefree young student, he fell deeply in love with the beautiful Varinka. At a lavish ball, he dances with her and is moved by the affectionate bond between her and her father, a distinguished colonel whose kindness and modest appearance inspire his admiration.

Unable to sleep afterward, Ivan wanders outside at dawn and encounters a brutal military punishment. A Tartar deserter is being driven between rows of soldiers and beaten while begging for mercy. Supervising the torture is Varinka’s father, who angrily strikes a soldier for hitting the prisoner too gently and demands fresh sticks.

Horrified by the contrast between the colonel’s tenderness at the ball and his cruelty on the parade ground, Ivan cannot reconcile the violence with the authority that sanctions it. The experience destroys his desire to enter military or civil service. It also gradually extinguishes his love for Varinka, since she continually reminds him of her father’s brutality. Ivan concludes that this single chance encounter redirected his entire life.
