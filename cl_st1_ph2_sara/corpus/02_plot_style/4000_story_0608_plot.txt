Alderman Keats, a self-made wealthy man, cultivates the image of an old-fashioned English gentleman by proudly exaggerating his gout, drinking port, and carrying a loaded revolver. After accidentally appearing to shoot through a keyhole, he gains a reputation as an expert marksman, though the bullet actually misses the doors and pierces an upstairs ceiling. Frightened by the weapon but unwilling to lose his prestige, he secretly disposes of his live ammunition, buys blanks, and stages regular shooting practice.

After a drunken civic feast, Keats encounters an emergency involving two fighting circus tigers. Asked to lend his supposedly loaded revolver to their trainer, he first claims to have forgotten whether it is loaded, then admits that it contains only blanks rather than let the trainer risk his life. His cousin and companions mock him, destroying his carefully constructed reputation. The tigers are separated without the gun, and the humiliated Keats never again boasts about his gout.
