Bill Burtenshaw, a deeply superstitious former sailor, drinks heavily and squanders his wages and household possessions, leaving his wife impoverished. When his old shipmate Silas Winch visits, Mrs. Burtenshaw tells him about Bill’s alcoholism and recalls the two men’s pact that whichever died first would return as a ghost. She and Silas devise a plan for Silas to impersonate his own drowned ghost and frighten Bill into sobriety.

Silas appears beside the drunken Bill’s bed covered in luminous paint. Convinced that his supposedly dead friend has returned, Bill swears never to drink again. Exploiting the deception, Silas also makes him promise to send fifteen shillings each week to Silas’s alleged widow. Bill quits drinking and faithfully sends the money, but his wife becomes furious that so much of their income is going to Silas and cannot expose the trick without risking Bill’s relapse or anger.

She eventually stages a second ghostly visitation, pretending that Silas’s ghost has appeared to her because a payment was lost. She claims the ghost ordered her to deliver the weekly money personally. Bill thereafter gives the fifteen shillings directly to his wife, believing she is taking it to the widow. He remains sober, while she keeps the money and uses it to buy the clothes and hats she had previously been unable to afford.
