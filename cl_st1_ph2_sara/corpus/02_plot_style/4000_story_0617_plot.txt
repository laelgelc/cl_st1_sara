Oliver Bowman, a delicate, sheltered man who dislikes real people and physical experience, prefers to enjoy life through books, embroidery, sensory cues, and imagination. His vigorous sister Alice, who values direct action, provides a sharp contrast to him. Realizing that he can use everyday sights, sounds, and smells to simulate adventures without enduring their risks, Oliver begins roaming London and transforming shop displays into elaborate private fantasies.

He becomes infatuated with a beautiful mannequin in an orange dress, and the imagined romance makes him healthier, more active, and more confident. When the mannequin disappears, he searches desperately for her, only to return home and discover Alice wearing the mannequin’s orange dress as a new tea-gown. Devastated, he renounces serious emotional attachments and returns to lighter fantasies.

Oliver then discovers a vast department store where countless departments can supply every stimulus his imagination needs. He spends nearly all his time there, imagining himself traveling, playing sports, conducting diplomacy, performing music, pursuing romances, and dining luxuriously. Gradually, however, he loses control of his imagination and becomes wholly subject to whatever external objects suggest.

One day he accidentally enters the store’s funeral department. Confronted by mourning clothes, wreaths, and a coffin, he is unable to distance himself from the suggestion of death. Overcome with terror, he collapses and dies.
