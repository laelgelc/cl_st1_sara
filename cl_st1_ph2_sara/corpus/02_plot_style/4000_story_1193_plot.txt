Twelve-year-old Ann Mary lives with her loving but absent-minded grandparents and helps supervise the preparations for Thanksgiving dinner. Disappointed that her cousin Lucy will not be visiting, she delivers a plate of food to an elderly neighbor. While she is away, her grandparents receive word that a relative is ill and leave abruptly, telling Ann Mary to eat the prepared dinner and seek company from her teacher, Loretta.

Ann Mary returns to an empty house and discovers that the turkey and plum pudding have mysteriously vanished. Loretta’s family is away, and the elderly neighbor cannot hear her knocking, so Ann Mary prepares to spend the night alone, comforted only by Loretta’s cat. Her grandparents return late that evening after learning the supposed emergency was minor, but even her grandmother cannot remember where she hid the missing food.

Several days later, the family discovers that they celebrated Thanksgiving a week early. Lucy and her parents announce that they will come for the actual holiday, forcing the grandmother to prepare another turkey and pudding. On Thanksgiving morning, Ann Mary and Lucy find the original meal hidden beneath the parlor sofa, where the grandmother had placed it for safekeeping. With Lucy beside her, Loretta expected that evening, and a fresh feast waiting at home, Ann Mary feels that her loneliness, disappointments, and mysteries have all ended in genuine thanksgiving.
