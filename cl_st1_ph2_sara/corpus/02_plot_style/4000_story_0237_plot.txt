A young fir tree grows in a pleasant forest but is never content with the present. He longs to become tall, envying the great trees cut down for ship masts and the younger trees taken away to become splendidly decorated Christmas trees. Ignoring the sun, wind, birds, children, and other joys around him, he impatiently waits for a grander future.

Eventually, he is cut down and placed in a wealthy home, where he is adorned with candles, sweets, toys, and a golden star. For one glorious evening, children dance around him and take his gifts while listening to a fairy tale. The fir assumes that even greater happiness will follow, but the next morning he is dragged into a dark loft and forgotten.

There, mice visit him, and as he tells them about the forest and Christmas Eve, he finally realizes that those earlier times were happy. Still, he continues hoping to be brought out and restored to glory. When he is eventually removed from the loft, he discovers that his branches are dry and yellow. The children call him ugly, strip away his golden star, and trample his branches. Regretting that he never appreciated happiness while he had it, the fir is chopped into firewood and burned, bringing his life and his hopes to an end.
