At a Boxing Day gathering near Putney, wealthy financier Sir Leopold Fischer presents his goddaughter, Ruby Adams, with three celebrated diamonds known as the Flying Stars. Ruby’s socialist suitor, John Crook, openly criticizes Fischer’s wealth, making himself an obvious suspect if anything goes wrong.

Ruby’s supposed Canadian uncle, James Blount, enthusiastically organizes an improvised Christmas pantomime. During the performance, a man believed to be the famous French actor Florian arrives dressed as a policeman. Blount, playing harlequin, attacks, tosses, and drugs the “actor” as part of the comedy, while the audience applauds. Soon afterward, Fischer discovers that his diamonds have vanished.

Father Brown dismisses suspicion of Crook, reasoning that a genuine thief would not advertise himself through radical rhetoric. When he learns that Blount arrived only after Mrs. Adams’s death and therefore could not have been recognized by her, he realizes that the supposed uncle is actually the master criminal Flambeau. The unconscious performer is not Florian but a real police officer who had come to arrest Flambeau and was subdued in full view of the amused audience.

Father Brown pursues Flambeau into the garden and explains the scheme: Flambeau stole the diamonds while pretending to attach a paper tail to Fischer’s coat, concealed them among the imitation jewels on his harlequin costume, and used the pantomime to neutralize the approaching policeman. Father Brown then appeals to his remaining honor, warning that a romantic criminal life will inevitably decay into cruelty and degradation and pointing out that he has unfairly cast suspicion on Crook and endangered Crook’s relationship with Ruby. Moved by the appeal, Flambeau returns the diamonds and escapes, making this his final crime.
