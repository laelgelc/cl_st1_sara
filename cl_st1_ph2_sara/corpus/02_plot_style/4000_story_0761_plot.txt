Amateur detective and paper-hanger Philo Gubb is hired by the miserly Uncle Gabriel Hostetter—at Gubb’s own expense—to investigate what Hostetter calls his “slow murder.” Hostetter claims someone repeatedly shoots him with a pea-shooter and suspects his son-in-law, bank cashier Farrington Pierce, who might kill him for his fortune and control of the bank. Gubb accepts the case and agrees to shadow Pierce and submit daily reports.

While watching the bank, Gubb overhears townsmen revealing that Hostetter actually wants evidence of Pierce’s alleged affair with a widow so that Hostetter’s daughter can divorce him. Realizing the supposed murder plot was merely a trick to obtain free detective work, Gubb angrily abandons the investigation and returns to paper-hanging. Later that day, however, he learns that Pierce has shot and killed Hostetter with a tiny pistol likened to a pea-shooter. Clinging to Hostetter’s theory, Gubb insists that the death was not instantaneous but the culmination of a long, gradual murder.
