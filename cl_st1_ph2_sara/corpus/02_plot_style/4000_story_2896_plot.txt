After his poem is harshly criticized, the proud and despondent Helmstone goes out and joins his friend Standard and the cheerful, unassuming Hautboy at a circus. Helmstone is fascinated by Hautboy’s unaffected delight, sound judgment, and contentment, but assumes these qualities arise from a lack of genius or ambition.

Later, Hautboy invites them to his modest rooms and plays the fiddle with extraordinary skill, dispelling Helmstone’s bitterness. Standard then reveals that Hautboy was once a celebrated child prodigy who enjoyed enormous fame and wealth but is now forgotten and earns his living by teaching music. Despite losing public acclaim, he remains genuinely happy.

Humbled by Hautboy’s example, Helmstone recognizes the emptiness of his own craving for fame. The next day, he destroys his manuscripts, buys a fiddle, and becomes Hautboy’s student.
