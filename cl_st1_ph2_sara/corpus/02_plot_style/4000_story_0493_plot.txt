On a transatlantic steamer, the narrator and several companions discover that naïve young Henry Storm is losing nearly all his money at poker to a professional gambler. They form an informal society to reform him, choosing the narrator to persuade him to stop and planning to lend him money if necessary.

Instead, the narrator joins Storm at the poker table and repeatedly lends him money during two enormous hands. Storm defeats the gambler’s four aces with a straight flush, then wins again by bluffing. Having recovered everything he lost plus a small profit, Storm vows never to play poker again. The defeated gambler bitterly remarks that Storm benefited from the combined luck of two fools—his own and the narrator’s.
