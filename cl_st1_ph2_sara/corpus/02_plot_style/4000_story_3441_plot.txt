After a Sunday sermon about being accountable for every “idle word,” the solemn Miss B. argues that conversation without a directly useful purpose is sinful. Her lively cousin Helen objects that ordinary chatter about weather, flowers, fashions, and other trivial matters makes social life pleasant and encourages friendly feelings.

Their uncle mediates, explaining that harmless small talk has genuine value: it puts people at ease, strengthens affection, and provides the mental relaxation necessary for emotional and spiritual health. He warns Helen against habitual thoughtlessness but also cautions Miss B. that constant solemnity can produce gloom, exhaustion, and religious coldness.

He then interprets the biblical warning not as a prohibition against cheerful conversation, but as a warning about careless words that cause harm. Thoughtless slander, criticism, gossip, or skeptical remarks can damage reputations, weaken faith, and undermine beneficial influences, making speakers responsible for foreseeable consequences. True Christian discipline, he concludes, consists not in anxious silence but in cultivating a pure and benevolent character, so that even spontaneous conversation naturally avoids cruelty, impurity, and harm.
