Lord Murchison recounts how he fell in love with Lady Alroy, a beautiful widow whose secretive behavior made her seem entangled in some hidden affair. She insisted on discreet meetings, concealed her correspondence, and appeared fearful of being overheard. When Murchison saw her enter a rented house in a shabby street and later caught her lying about having gone out, he accused her of meeting someone there. Though she denied it, he rejected her explanation, returned her subsequent letter unopened, and left the country.

After Lady Alroy suddenly died, Murchison investigated the rented rooms. The landlady revealed that Lady Alroy had always visited alone and merely sat reading or drinking tea. Murchison’s friend concludes that she had no real secret but cultivated mystery for its own sake, enjoying the fantasy of being a mysterious heroine. Murchison remains uncertain whether this explanation is true.
