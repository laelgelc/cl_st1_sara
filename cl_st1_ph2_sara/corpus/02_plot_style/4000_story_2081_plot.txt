A young boy in Dublin becomes intensely infatuated with his friend Mangan’s sister, watching and following her from afar while investing his feelings with romantic, almost religious significance. When she tells him she cannot attend a bazaar because of a convent retreat, he promises to go and bring her a gift.

He spends the following days obsessing over the bazaar, but on Saturday his uncle returns home late and initially forgets to give him money. By the time the boy reaches the bazaar, it is nearly closed: most stalls are shuttered, the hall is dark, and the remaining vendors seem indifferent. Unable to find or afford a meaningful gift, he recognizes that his quest has been fueled by vanity and fantasy. As the lights go out, he is overwhelmed by anguish and anger at his disillusionment.
