A dreamy, unsuccessful barrister and art enthusiast encounters a familiar-looking man during a violent storm on a London steamboat. He realizes that the stranger is an artist’s model whose face, throat, and legs have appeared in countless paintings as historical, literary, religious, and romantic figures. Bitter about being repeatedly posed with stale props and costumes—and newly threatened with unemployment by changing artistic fashions—the model ominously vows to “grow one” that will haunt the narrator.

Two years later, during another thunderstorm, the model appears at the narrator’s chambers sporting an enormous beard. Calling himself the “Ghost of Art,” he demonstrates how rearranging the beard can represent severity, benevolence, death, adoration, vengeance, romance, jealousy, despair, avarice, rage, and virtually every other emotion. His beard has made him employable again under the new German-influenced artistic taste.

After the model mysteriously vanishes down the dark staircase, the narrator finds himself haunted everywhere by the same artistic gimmick: paintings that rely on manipulated facial hair to express all character and emotion. The model’s prophecy is fulfilled as his beard appears throughout contemporary exhibitions and torments the narrator without rest.
