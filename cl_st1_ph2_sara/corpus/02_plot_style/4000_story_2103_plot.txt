A fleet remains idle in a harbor so long that its captains and crews grow accustomed to inactivity. One by one, most ships eventually depart, but a peculiar old brig stays behind under a complacent captain and an equally conservative steersman. When younger crew members demand action, the captain angrily resists, then decides to demonstrate his “initiative” by setting sail.

The crew enthusiastically takes the brig to sea, but after a single large wave strikes the vessel, the frightened officers immediately order both anchors dropped near a dangerous, rocky coast. They portray this needless retreat as proof that the younger sailors’ demands were reckless and disclaim responsibility for the brig’s predicament. Conflict and resentment consume the crew, while the captain and steersman remain secure in their self-justification. No one attempts another change, and the brig remains permanently anchored in its unsafe position.
