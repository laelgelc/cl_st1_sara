A young red-headed woodpecker hatches with three sisters and grows up in a carefully protected nest. As his feathers develop, he becomes excessively vain, constantly inspecting and even tugging at them to make them grow faster. His parents try unsuccessfully to correct his self-absorption.

After learning to fly and find food, he meets an old gray squirrel who fails to recognize him and remarks that the woodpecker family’s son is a silly fellow who sees the world only through his own feathers. Stung by this outside criticism, the young woodpecker asks his father what the phrase means. His father explains that he cares so much about his appearance that he ignores everything else. The youngster resolves to limit how often he admires and preens his plumage, finally overcoming his vanity—much to his mother’s relief and amusement, since a stranger’s comment succeeded where his parents’ repeated advice had failed.
