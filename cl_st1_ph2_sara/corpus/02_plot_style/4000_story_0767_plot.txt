A prosperous new barbershop run by Hippolyte and Espérance Sergeot arouses the jealousy of their struggling grocer neighbor, Alexandrine Caille. Resentment grows when an old admirer of Alexandrine’s, now a policeman named Abel Flique, becomes a loyal customer and friend of the Sergeots. The conflict intensifies when Alexandrine’s prized white angora cat, Zut, repeatedly deserts the grocery for the barbershop, where Espérance feeds and indulges her.

An attempted reconciliation ends in mutual insults, and Alexandrine’s efforts to reclaim Zut only provoke further hostility. Flique warns her to stop harassing the Sergeots, threatening to expose her sale of inferior goods. Zut eventually begins staying overnight at the barbershop and gives birth there to five kittens, one of them pure white. Backed by Flique’s claim that the kittens legally belong to the shop where they were born, Espérance keeps the white kitten she has always wanted and returns Zut and the other four to Alexandrine with a mocking letter. Unable to protest without risking exposure of her dishonest business practices, Alexandrine is forced to accept defeat.
