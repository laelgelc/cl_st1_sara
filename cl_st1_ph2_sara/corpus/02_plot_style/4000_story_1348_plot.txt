In 1858 Fiddletown, the beautiful, sentimental, and flirtatious Mrs. Tretherick is trapped in a bitter marriage marked by her husband’s violence, her infidelity, and his drinking. After leaving him and staying at a hotel, she returns to their cottage with Colonel Starbottle’s help to collect her belongings while her husband is away.

At the house she discovers Caroline, or Carry, her husband’s neglected young daughter from his first marriage. Initially resentful and convinced the child has been brought there to replace and insult her, Mrs. Tretherick treats Carry harshly. But when she secretly watches the lonely girl reenact their encounter with a homemade doll, she is moved by Carry’s longing for a mother. Their bond deepens as Carry calls her “Mamma,” confides in her, and falls asleep in her arms. Holding the child awakens Mrs. Tretherick’s grief over a pregnancy and child she lost years earlier, stirring a powerful maternal love.

Abandoning her planned rendezvous with Colonel Starbottle, Mrs. Tretherick leaves Fiddletown with Carry and travels toward San Francisco. The town reacts with gossip, accusing her of abducting the child while ridiculing both Tretherick and the rejected colonel. Ah Fe, the family’s Chinese servant and the only witness, confirms that she departed with Carry alone. Six months later, he secretly receives Carry’s freshly washed apron, suggesting that he has remained connected to the fugitives and knows more about their whereabouts than he reveals.
