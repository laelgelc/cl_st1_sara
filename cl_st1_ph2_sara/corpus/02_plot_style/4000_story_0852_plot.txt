On a hot summer night, Manna Loulou tells Madame Delisle the tragic story of Zoraïde, an enslaved mixed-race woman raised in luxury by her mistress and godmother, Madame Delarivière. Although Madame plans for Zoraïde to marry Ambroise, Zoraïde despises him and falls in love with Mézor, an enslaved field laborer and celebrated dancer.

Madame refuses their marriage, but Zoraïde and Mézor continue meeting, and Zoraïde becomes pregnant. In retaliation, Madame persuades Mézor’s owner to sell him far away. After Zoraïde gives birth, Madame secretly sends the healthy baby to her plantation and tells Zoraïde that the child has died, hoping she will return to her former life.

Overcome by grief, Zoraïde loses her sanity and begins treating a bundle of rags as her baby. Remorseful, Madame eventually brings Zoraïde’s real daughter back, but Zoraïde rejects the child, believing the girl is part of a scheme to take away her rag bundle. The daughter is returned to the plantation and grows up without either parent’s love, while Zoraïde lives into old age in madness, forever clutching her imaginary child. After hearing the story, Madame Delisle concludes that the real child would have been better off dead.
