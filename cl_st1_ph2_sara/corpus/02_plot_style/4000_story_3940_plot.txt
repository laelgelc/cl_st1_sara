At a royal wedding, the palace prepares a magnificent midnight fireworks display. Among the fireworks is a vain, self-important rocket who insists that the marriage and celebration are being held in his honor. He boasts of his distinguished ancestry, imagines himself uniquely sensitive and important, and dismisses the other fireworks as inferior. Despite warnings to remain dry, he cries over an invented tragedy and soaks his gunpowder.

At midnight, all the other fireworks perform brilliantly, delighting the court, but the damp rocket fails to ignite. Rather than admit failure, he convinces himself that he is being reserved for a grander occasion. The next day, workers discard him into a muddy ditch. There he meets several animals, but remains too absorbed in boasting and talking about himself to recognize their indifference or criticism.

Two boys eventually find the rocket and use him as fuel for a cooking fire. Once dry, he shoots into the sky and explodes, imagining that he has astonished the entire world. In reality, the boys are asleep, nobody sees or hears him, and his remaining stick falls onto a goose. Mistaking the goose’s alarm for proof of his greatness, the rocket dies convinced that he has created a tremendous sensation.
