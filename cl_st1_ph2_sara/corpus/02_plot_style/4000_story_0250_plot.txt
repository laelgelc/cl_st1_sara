Anthony, an aging unmarried merchant’s clerk in Copenhagen, spends his lonely nights remembering his childhood in Eisenach and his love for Molly, the mayor’s daughter. As children, they planted an apple seed together, but after Molly moved away, she fell in love with another man and rejected Anthony. Soon afterward, Anthony’s family lost its fortune, forcing him to abandon his grief, support his father, and seek work abroad. Though he eventually forgave Molly, he remained unmarried and isolated.

Years later, old and helpless during a snowstorm, Anthony lies alone in his room. Near death, he envisions Saint Elizabeth and the apple tree he planted with Molly, now transformed into a heavenly source of comfort. He dies peacefully, clutching his nightcap.

The cap preserves the tears, memories, and painful dreams of his life. Decades later, anyone who wears it experiences visions of Anthony’s lost love, poverty, and suffering, while his old tears fall from it like pearls. The tale ends by warning others not to covet a bachelor’s nightcap or the sorrowful thoughts it contains.
