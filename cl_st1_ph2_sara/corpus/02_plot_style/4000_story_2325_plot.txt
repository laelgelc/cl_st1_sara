A group of migrant tradesmen working in Western Australia employ Alfred O’Briar, a quiet, unpopular laborer known to be a gifted mimic and ventriloquist. When voices—including those of women—are repeatedly heard coming from his private tent at night, his campmates suspect he is secretly entertaining a disreputable visitor and fear for their respectable camp’s reputation.

Two of the men spy on Alfred and overhear what sounds like a visit to his former sweetheart’s family. Alfred appears to return from his travels, reunite with Mary, and receive her parents’ blessing to marry her. Yet when the men enter the tent, they find Alfred entirely alone. One realizes that Alfred has been “thinking aloud,” using his ventriloquism to enact the reunion he longs for.

Recognizing the private hopes and loneliness behind Alfred’s reserved manner, the men keep his secret and begin treating him with sympathy. When he leaves soon afterward, apparently to return to his sweetheart, they see him off warmly and wish him luck.
