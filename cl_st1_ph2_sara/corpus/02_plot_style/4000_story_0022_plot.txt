A distinctive line-backed steer is born near Texas’s Nueces River and grows up beside his mother and a speckled heifer calf. He survives the trauma of branding, harsh winters, and his mother’s eventual abandonment when she bears another calf. As a two-year-old, he loses the speckled heifer to a stronger rival and, humiliated and wounded, resolves to leave his home.

He secretly joins a large trail herd moving north and travels hundreds of miles into Indian Territory. Because he is an unclaimed stray, cowboys repeatedly gamble him away in poker games, earning him the name “poker steer.” A Texas inspector briefly claims him for his original owner, but a cowboy covertly helps him escape. His brand is later altered from bar-circle-bar to a pilot wheel, and he continues changing hands through wagers while maturing into a valuable beef steer.

Eventually he is included in a herd sent to fulfill a cattle contract at the Fort Peck Indian Reservation in Montana. Chosen for slaughter, he is pursued and shot repeatedly by mounted hunters. Before dying, he turns on one of them, disembowels the hunter’s pony, and nearly gores the trapped rider. He finally collapses and dies almost three thousand miles from his Texas birthplace, with a former owner admiring his final act of defiance.
