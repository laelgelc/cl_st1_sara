A Chinese emperor learns from foreign books that the greatest wonder in his realm is a nightingale whose beautiful song is famous everywhere except at court. He orders his servants to find it, and a poor kitchen girl leads them to the plain gray bird in the forest. The nightingale agrees to perform at the palace, where its song moves the emperor to tears. Delighted, he keeps it at court, though its freedom is restricted.

Later, the emperor receives a jeweled mechanical nightingale that sings predictable tunes and dazzles the court with its appearance. The artificial bird is praised as superior, while the real nightingale escapes back to the forest and is banished. Eventually, the mechanism wears out and can be played only rarely.

Five years later, the emperor becomes gravely ill. Abandoned by his courtiers and haunted by visions of his deeds, he begs the mechanical bird to sing, but no one is there to wind it. The real nightingale, hearing of his illness, returns and sings so beautifully that Death surrenders the emperor’s crown, sword, and banner before departing. Restored to health, the grateful emperor asks the bird to remain. The nightingale chooses freedom but promises to visit, sing about the lives of his subjects, and secretly tell him what happens throughout his kingdom. When the servants return expecting to find him dead, the recovered emperor calmly greets them.
