Rising sculptor George Peel maintains an elegant London lifestyle with his wife, Mary, and their infant son, largely through a yearly allowance from Mary’s wealthy, austere uncle, Samuel. Although George has earned artistic prestige, including an international gold medal, his commissions have dwindled and his extravagance has left the family in financial trouble. When Mary asks Samuel for an additional two hundred pounds, he refuses and, after reading a society report about champagne flowing at George’s studio celebration, reduces their annual allowance from five hundred to three hundred pounds.

Humiliated and unable to sustain appearances, George devises a scheme to pressure Samuel. He takes Mary and their child to Samuel’s hometown of Bursley and lodges conspicuously at the Tiger, a respectable hotel that Samuel, a temperance advocate campaigning for public office, regards as disreputable. George then encounters Samuel in the busy marketplace, thrusts the baby carriage upon him, and disappears into the hotel. The crying infant attracts public attention and embarrassing jokes, alarming Samuel about the damage to his reputation and the appearance of a family quarrel.

George claims that financial necessity has forced him to abandon sculpture, seek industrial work, and live cheaply in Bursley. He also suggests that Mary is deeply offended by Samuel’s remarks about champagne. Anxious to end the scandal, Samuel visits Mary, takes the family into his home, and quickly reverses his decision. George returns to London assured of the original five-hundred-pound allowance and carrying the additional two hundred pounds he had requested, while Bursley continues gossiping about Samuel being seen carrying a baby through the Tiger.
