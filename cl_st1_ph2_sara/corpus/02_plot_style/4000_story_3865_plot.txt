After a bitter quarrel with his wife and her noisy Sunday guests, timid shopkeeper Mr. Coombes leaves home despairing over his unhappy marriage and failing business. In the woods, he eats a poisonous purple fungus while contemplating suicide. Instead of killing him, it produces wild exhilaration and insanity.

He returns home bizarrely dressed, carrying fungi and demanding that everyone join his merriment. When the guests resist, he terrorizes them, attacks Jennie’s fiancé, wrecks the house, drinks several bottles of stout, and eventually collapses asleep in the coal cellar.

Five years later, Coombes is prosperous and self-assured, while his wife has become a cooperative business partner. He tells his brother that his violent outburst taught her to respect him, never realizing that the fungus-induced madness gave him the assertiveness that transformed his marriage and fortunes.
