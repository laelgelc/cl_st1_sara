A once-model prisoner nearing early release is falsely accused of trying to claim an extra tobacco ration twice. Refusing to surrender his rights or accept being called a thief, he defies the prison authorities. The warden has him confined in a dark dungeon, fed only bread and water, and brutally whipped. The prisoner vows to kill the warden and consequently remains isolated for twenty-three months, emerging emaciated and gravely ill.

When the state prison directors investigate, he recounts his mistreatment but refuses to renounce his murderous intention. They move him to the hospital, and public attention leads to a pardon after another former prisoner confesses to having taken the tobacco by exploiting his resemblance to him.

Before leaving his post in disgrace, the remorseful warden meets privately with the pardoned prisoner, admits his cruelty, apologizes, and offers him a loaded revolver so he can fulfill his threat. The prisoner cannot shoot him. The warden’s sincere compassion finally breaks the defiance that punishment could not, and the prisoner forgives him. Overcome by emotion and weakened by heart disease, he dies while holding the warden’s hand.
