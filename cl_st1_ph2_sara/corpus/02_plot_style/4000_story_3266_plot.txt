Miss Emily Le Petyt rents Tresillack, a secluded cottage in Cornwall with a history of disreputable tenants, on the condition that she retain its housekeeper, Mrs. Carkeek. She soon notices that the house seems supernaturally attentive to her wishes: rooms are tidied, flowers arranged, and every comfort anticipated beyond what Mrs. Carkeek could accomplish.

After Emily unblocks a deliberately sealed water supply to the pantry, she awakens one night to hear its tap running. Investigating, she sees the disembodied hands of a little girl washing in the basin. Mrs. Carkeek reveals that they belong to Margaret, the former owner’s daughter, who died of diphtheria at seven. Margaret’s benevolent ghost has continued caring for the house and its inhabitants, even comforting and feeding children abused by previous tenants.

Rather than flee, Emily grows to cherish Margaret’s loving presence and remains at Tresillack for several happy years. Eventually the house is sold to Margaret’s uncle, Colonel Kendall, who will live there with his wife and eight children. Consoled that Margaret will have a good family and young cousins to care for, Emily prepares to leave. On her final morning, she enters the pantry, calls to Margaret, and feels two small hands briefly rest in hers in farewell.
