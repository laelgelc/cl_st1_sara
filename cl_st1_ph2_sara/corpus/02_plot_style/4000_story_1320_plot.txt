A narrator and the drunken clerk Isaiah travel by sleigh to visit Isaiah’s brother, a former teacher dying of consumption. Their difficult journey is halted at the Volga, where drifting ice has delayed the ferry. They wait with a district judge, a miller, and Kireelka, the impoverished peasant responsible for the crossing.

As they watch the ice, the travelers debate the condition and education of the peasantry. The judge and miller portray peasants as ignorant, lazy, drunken, and in need of harsh discipline, while Isaiah suggests that hunger and bad government are the real causes of their misery. Kireelka—both a heroic rescuer and a petty thief—responds evasively to their patronizing questions and silently endures the judge’s insults and blame for the ferry’s delay.

When hunger overcomes the group, they discover that Kireelka has a small loaf hidden in his shirt. The gentlemen divide and eat it, despite finding it coarse and repulsive, then continue theorizing about why peasants are poor. Once the boats arrive, the travelers cross the dangerous, ice-filled river. Left behind on the bank, Kireelka calls to a ferryman to bring him bread, explaining that the gentry have eaten his last loaf.
