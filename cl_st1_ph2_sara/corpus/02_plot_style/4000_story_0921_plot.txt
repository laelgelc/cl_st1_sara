Philadelphia policeman Dan Kerry tracks fugitive Andy Proudfoot to a mountain cave, hoping to claim the reward and use it to marry his beloved Katy and take her West. When Andy returns with a badly injured hand, however, Kerry instinctively treats the wound instead of arresting him. He remains in the cave while Andy heals, and over two weeks the men form a deep friendship. Though Kerry keeps his handcuffs hidden nearby, he becomes increasingly unable to betray his gentle, hospitable companion.

Kerry eventually reveals that he came to earn a reward so he could rescue Katy from her abusive father. Andy admits he recognized Kerry as a policeman from the beginning and knew why he was there. Grateful for Kerry’s kindness and weary of his lonely fugitive life, Andy offers to surrender so Kerry can collect the money. Kerry indignantly refuses, ashamed that he ever considered arresting a man he now loves like a brother.

Andy then explains the crime that made him an outlaw. A corrupt mining agent named Dickert cheated Andy and his new wife, Euola, out of payment for their land and insulted Euola by claiming she had once been his lover. Enraged, Andy struck Dickert harder than intended, apparently killing him, then took the money Dickert had withheld and fled after Euola promised to follow. Having heard nothing from her, Andy believes she abandoned him and has nearly lost the will to live.

Andy offers Kerry his share of the hidden money and asks him to deliver Euola’s portion if he can find her. Kerry suddenly remembers a woman in Asheville, living under an assumed name, whom police suspect is Andy’s wife and who has repeatedly tried to enter the mountains secretly. Realizing that Euola has remained faithful and has been searching for Andy, Kerry tells him the truth and proposes helping the couple evade the authorities. With enough money for a new beginning, Kerry plans for Andy, Euola, Katy, and himself to escape together and build new lives in the West.
