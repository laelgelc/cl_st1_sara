A young Student longs for a red rose to give the Professor’s daughter, who has promised to dance with him if he brings one. Hearing his despair, a Nightingale believes him to be a true lover and searches the garden for a rose. The only tree capable of producing one has been damaged by winter and can bloom only if the Nightingale sings all night with her breast pressed against a thorn, staining the flower with her heart’s blood.

Convinced that love is worth more than life, the Nightingale sacrifices herself. Her song and blood create a magnificent red rose, but she dies without the Student understanding what she has done. He finds the rose and offers it to the girl, yet she rejects it because it clashes with her dress and because another suitor has given her jewels. Enraged, the Student throws the rose into the street, where it is crushed. He then dismisses love as impractical and returns to studying philosophy, leaving the Nightingale’s sacrifice unrecognized and wasted.
