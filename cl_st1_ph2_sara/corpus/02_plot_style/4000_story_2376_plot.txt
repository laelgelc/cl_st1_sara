Two wealthy businessmen, Jones and Robinson, dine at an expensive restaurant and reminisce about their impoverished beginnings in New York. Their conversation becomes an escalating contest over who endured greater hardship: Robinson claims he slept in a drafty tar barrel and ate discarded porridge and animal feed, while Jones insists his broken piano box, meager scraps, and privations were even worse. Each also boasts that those harsh years were happier and more innocent.

When the waiter asks for their orders, pride compels both men to request cold porridge, potato peelings, and other humble fare. After a tense silence, Robinson abandons the pretense and orders an elaborate gourmet meal. Jones immediately follows suit and adds champagne. Embarrassed by the exposure of their hypocrisy, they never again mention the tar barrel or piano box.
