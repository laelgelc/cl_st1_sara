A healthy forty-one-year-old bachelor agrees to transport the remains of his deceased childhood friend by train to the man’s parents in Wisconsin. At the station, however, a mix-up causes him to take a box of rifles while another man mistakenly takes the coffin. Unaware of the error, he boards the express car with the rifle box, on which someone places a package of pungent Limburger cheese.

As the heated, tightly sealed car fills with the cheese’s increasingly powerful odor, the narrator and the expressman, Thompson, assume it comes from the supposedly decomposing corpse. They try cigars, moving the box, carbolic acid, and a foul-burning mixture to overcome the smell, but every remedy only makes conditions worse. Forced to alternate between suffocating inside and freezing on the outdoor platform, they are eventually found unconscious.

The narrator develops a severe fever and learns weeks later that the box contained only rifles and that the odor came from cheese. Nevertheless, terror and imagination have permanently ruined his health, and two years later he believes he is returning home to die.
