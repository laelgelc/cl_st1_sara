Thomas Bird, a poorly paid but kindhearted London clerk, lives a modest, uneventful life while quietly helping everyone who appeals to him. Though he needs new clothes, his salary is continually depleted by small acts of generosity and by repeated loans to Mr. Warbeck, an apparently prosperous family friend who already owes him nearly thirty pounds.

Thomas has long been secretly devoted to Warbeck’s daughter, Alma, despite believing himself too poor and socially inferior to marry her. Mrs. Warbeck summons him one evening and bluntly explains that Alma is likely to become engaged to a successful young businessman. Fearing Thomas’s shabby presence might embarrass the family or deter the suitor, she asks him to stop visiting until the marriage is settled. Deeply wounded, Thomas accepts without protest and leaves without seeing Alma.

Alma eventually marries, while her father continues borrowing money from Thomas and remains unaware of his devotion. On the wedding day, Thomas is overcome by grief and leaves work early. That evening, however, a desperate woman asks for help: her unemployed husband has abandoned an urgent envelope-addressing job to get drunk. Thomas gives the family money for food and spends three hours completing most of the work himself. Through helping others, he recovers his composure and condemns his own earlier sorrow as weakness, never recognizing the exceptional goodness and selflessness that define him.
