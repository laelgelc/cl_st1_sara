At a remote British station in India, the arrogant but eligible Saumarez courts two closely attached sisters, Maud and Edith Copleigh. Everyone assumes he intends to marry Maud, the elder and more conventionally charming sister, though both women appear drawn to him.

During a moonlight riding picnic, a violent dust storm plunges the party into darkness. Believing he is speaking to Edith, whom he has secretly loved all along, Saumarez proposes—only to discover that he has proposed to Maud. Overjoyed, Maud accepts, while Edith, having overheard the proposal, flees on horseback in despair.

Saumarez confesses his mistake to the narrator, who prevents him from immediately abandoning Maud and rides after Edith. After a dangerous chase through the storm, Edith’s horse goes lame. The narrator explains that Saumarez truly loves her and persuades her to return.

At dawn, they reach the picnic site, where Saumarez publicly kisses Edith, making his real choice clear. The assembled guests applaud, and the party rides home in newly arranged couples, leaving Maud’s humiliation and her version of events untold.
