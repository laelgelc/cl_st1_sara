In the early 1870s, a timid, frail-looking traveler named Alfred stops overnight at Billy Knapp’s rough frontier hotel while secretly carrying fifty thousand dollars to the Spotted Tail agency. His awkwardness makes him seem like a harmless tenderfoot, though he wins the sympathy of Nell, the hotel’s cook, by defending her when Charley insults her.

That night, the outlaw Black Hank and his gang arrive searching for the money. Nell helps Alfred escape through an upstairs window, and he hides in a nearby tree without leaving a trail. When Charley reveals Alfred’s presence and guarded satchel, Hank suspects Billy of concealing the messenger. Unable to find Alfred, the gang decides to execute Billy to preserve Hank’s authority.

Nell tells the hidden Alfred that Billy is about to be killed. Grateful because she helped and kissed him—the first freely given affection he has ever received—Alfred abandons his safe escape and returns to intervene. Armed with two revolvers, he kills one outlaw who tries to draw, disarms the rest, frees Billy, and demonstrates extraordinary marksmanship to ensure their submission.

Alfred then reveals that the money is safely hidden outside, takes the gang’s horses and weapons to prevent pursuit, and continues his mission. Hank recognizes him as a renowned scout rather than a helpless tenderfoot. The following year, Alfred joins Billy and Jim Buckley in leading the only wagon train daring enough to cross into the Hills that summer.
