A little girl asks her busy father for a Christmas story, so he tells of another girl who loves Christmas enough to persuade the Christmas Fairy to make it occur every day for a year.

At first, the endless presents, sweets, feasts, and celebrations delight her. Soon, however, the repetition makes everyone exhausted, irritable, and poor. Food and fuel become scarce, forests are destroyed for Christmas trees, houses overflow with unwanted gifts, and other holidays are ruined or transformed into Christmas festivities. When people discover that the girl caused the disaster, they blame and shun her. She repeatedly begs the Fairy to stop the daily Christmases, but receives no answer.

After a full year, the enchantment finally ends. Everyone rejoices and destroys the accumulated presents and candy. The girl initially asks that Christmas never return, but the Fairy warns that this wish is also greedy. After reconsidering, the girl agrees that Christmas should come once a year, as it traditionally has. Her father finishes the tale, calling it a moral story, while his daughter happily insists that its details remain their secret.
