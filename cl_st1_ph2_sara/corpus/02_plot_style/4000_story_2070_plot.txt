The narrator recalls growing up in Berwick, Maine, a historic New England village shaped by rivers, forests, shipping, farming, and memories of earlier wars. Through her grandfather’s timber and West India trade, she encounters country people, sailors, and sea captains whose stories awaken her interest in human character and local tradition. After her grandfather dies and the Civil War hastens the decline of the village’s old maritime life, her physician father becomes her chief influence. He takes her on visits to rural patients, teaches her to observe people and places accurately, and urges her to portray life without affectation. Encouraged by her family’s love of books, she moves from writing childhood rhymes to prose and discovers her calling in depicting the country characters and landscapes she knows intimately. At nineteen or twenty, still shy and submitting under an assumed name, she achieves a major early success when one of her sketches is accepted by the *Atlantic*.
