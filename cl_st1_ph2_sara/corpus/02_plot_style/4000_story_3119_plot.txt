Twenty years after the impeccably genteel Lydia Carew dies, her distant Iowa relatives inherit and restore her Philadelphia home. The two Boggs sisters settle into society but soon encounter Lydia’s ghost, a lavender-scented, old-fashioned figure who silently rearranges their décor, adds prim embroidery and paintings, reads Jane Austen, and displays disapproval of their modern tastes.

The sisters learn that Lydia has briefly visited every family occupying the house, leaving whenever she disapproves of them. To their dismay, she seems to approve of the Boggs family and remains, making them increasingly uncomfortable despite their reluctance to admit that ghosts exist.

Their escape comes when a visiting Westerner joins them in the dimly lit drawing room. Lydia listens politely to his conversation but becomes progressively horrified by his poor grammar. When he uses a double negative, she vanishes abruptly and permanently, driven away by his offensive speech.
