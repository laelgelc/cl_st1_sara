A traveler befriends a solitary railway signalman who is tormented by a specter appearing beside the tunnel’s danger light. The figure calls, “Halloa! Below there! Look out!” and waves urgently. Its first appearance precedes a terrible railway accident; its second, a young woman’s sudden death aboard a train. When it returns a third time without revealing the nature or location of the coming danger, the conscientious signalman becomes desperate and fears another disaster.

The traveler initially attributes the visions to strained nerves and isolation, but grows concerned for both the signalman and railway safety. He plans to persuade him to consult a doctor. On returning the next evening, however, he learns that the signalman has been struck and killed by an engine. The driver explains that he shouted the same warning words and made the same gestures as the specter—covering his eyes with one arm while waving the other—revealing that the haunting had foretold the signalman’s own death.
