Widow Stimson hopes her prosperous farm might be joined with Deacon Hawkins’s through marriage, while the local Sewing Society eagerly speculates about their relationship. The deacon invites her sleigh-riding, chiefly to get her opinion of an expensive new trotting horse he hopes can finally defeat his longtime rival, Squire Hopkins.

When Hopkins overtakes them and challenges the deacon to an impromptu race, the deacon’s horse begins losing because of the widow’s added weight. Determined to help, she secretly leaps into a soft snowdrift, allowing the relieved horse to surge ahead and defeat the squire. After briefly forgetting her in the excitement of victory, the deacon realizes what she has sacrificed, races back in alarm, and finds her unharmed and celebrating his triumph. Moved by her devotion, he embraces her, strongly implying that their long-anticipated marriage will follow.
