An inquisitive English travel writer, Mr Wraxall, visits a Swedish manor in 1863 to research the history of the De la Gardie family. He becomes fascinated by the cruel seventeenth-century Count Magnus, who was rumored to have undertaken a “Black Pilgrimage” to the accursed city of Chorazin and returned with a supernatural servant. Local stories describe a hooded creature that hunts the count’s enemies and horribly mutilates its victims.

While examining the family mausoleum, Wraxall notices that the three padlocks securing Count Magnus’s sarcophagus are coming undone one by one. Increasingly obsessed, he repeatedly voices his wish to meet the count. During a final visit, the last lock falls away and the coffin lid begins to open. Wraxall flees Sweden, but two ominous figures—a tall cloaked man and a short, hooded creature—secretly follow him throughout his journey.

Back in England, Wraxall attempts to escape his pursuers by hiding in a remote village. They find him nonetheless, and he is discovered dead under such horrific circumstances that members of the inquest jury faint and refuse to describe his body. Years later, the narrator inherits and demolishes the house where Wraxall died, discovering the papers from which the story has been reconstructed.
