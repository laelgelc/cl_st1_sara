Whittaker “the Rube” Hurtle, a dominant minor-league pitcher newly purchased by Chicago, makes his major-league debut against Philadelphia, the reigning champions, who need one more victory to clinch the pennant. Watched by his former manager, his proud wife Nan, and their friends, the unknown pitcher is initially mocked by Philadelphia’s confident players.

The Rube quickly proves that his speed, control, varied pitches, and composure belong in the major leagues. As he repeatedly retires Philadelphia’s best hitters—including the celebrated Lane—the champions’ ridicule turns into aggressive attempts to rattle him, then grudging respect. Chicago scores a single run while the Rube holds Philadelphia scoreless through eight innings.

In the ninth, an error and two hits load the bases with two outs, bringing Lane to bat. With the game at stake, the Rube uses his rarely thrown rising fastball and strikes out the champion hitter, securing a 1–0 victory. Philadelphia’s leaders congratulate him, the crowd swarms the field, and Nan celebrates his triumphant arrival in the major leagues.
