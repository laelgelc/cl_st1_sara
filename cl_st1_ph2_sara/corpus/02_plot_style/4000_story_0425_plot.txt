Tryballot squanders the fortune left by his thrifty father and becomes a carefree, popular beggar known as Vieux par-Chemins. He spends decades wandering around Rouen, living on alms, gambling away his money, avoiding work, and boasting that poverty has preserved his health and freedom.

At eighty-two, after months without a lover, he sexually assaults a sleeping cowherd. He is arrested and sentenced to hang, but defends himself before Duke Richard by claiming that age has not diminished his desires and that the sight of the exposed young woman overwhelmed his reason. Amused by his argument, the duke decrees that he will be pardoned if he can demonstrate the same desire while standing beneath the gallows with the noose around his neck.

At the execution, a richly dressed noblewoman deliberately exposes her charms to give him a fair chance. His unmistakable physical response fulfills the duke’s condition, saving him from death and making him a local sensation. The town celebrates him, and the duke compensates the injured cowherd and compels her to marry Tryballot. Their union produces children and establishes a noble family whose descendants later change its indecent surname to Bonne-Chose. Tryballot lives another twenty-seven years but regrets that his new wealth and status have deprived him of his former wandering life.
