An escaped Georgia prisoner, known only as Baker or “Hundred and One,” arrives at a resort hotel seeking work. Mentally scarred by fifteen years of wrongful imprisonment and weekly whippings, he still imagines himself chained and instinctively prepares for punishment. The hotel’s proprietor, Mr. Clayton, recognizes his suffering, shelters him, and gives him work. Through kindness and friendship, Baker gradually regains his health, confidence, and peace of mind.

When a guest brings cholera to the crowded hotel, Baker’s past experience with the disease becomes invaluable. He identifies the illness, helps evacuate the guests quietly without causing panic, carries luggage and frightened people to safety, and then treats those who fall sick, saving many lives and even aiding the stricken physician. Exhausted and infected, Baker refuses care so that others may be helped first. As he dies, he reveals that he was innocent: his brother committed the crime for which Baker was imprisoned, and the governor was going to pardon him. He dies believing his long-awaited freedom is finally coming.
