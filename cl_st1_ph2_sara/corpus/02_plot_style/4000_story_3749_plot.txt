In autumn 1914, Holland responds to the flood of Belgian refugees by building an organized camp capable of housing thousands. The compassionate but stern commandant oversees its kitchens, schools, hospitals, workshops, and barracks, where displaced Belgians try to preserve their dignity and national identity.

Among the refugees is Mrs. Van Orley, a troubled woman who insists that her children were stolen and wanders the barracks calling for them. Others think she is insane, but the camp’s chief nurse believes her grief is genuine and arranges to employ her at the hospital.

Soon afterward, two unidentified Belgian children, a boy named Hendrik and a younger girl called Sooss, are sent to the camp. Though initially outraged at being given responsibility for them, the commandant quickly grows fond of the children. He brings them to the sewing room and asks the women there to adopt or care for them. Mrs. Van Orley suddenly recognizes Hendrik, rushes forward, and claims both children as her own. Hendrik recognizes her as his mother, and she collapses from overwhelming emotion, leaving the nurse to manage the family’s joyful reunion.
