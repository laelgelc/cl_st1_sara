A young British official named Imray mysteriously disappears from an Indian station, and extensive searches fail to find him. Months later, police officer Strickland rents Imray’s bungalow, where he and his dog, Tietjens, sense an unseen presence. While visiting Strickland, the narrator also experiences whispering, footsteps, moving curtains, and other signs of a haunting, while Tietjens refuses to sleep inside.

When snakes appear above the bungalow’s ceiling cloth, Strickland investigates the roof space. He dislodges a concealed corpse, which proves to be Imray with his throat cut. Suspecting one of the inherited servants, Strickland confronts Bahadur Khan, Imray’s former attendant. Khan confesses that he murdered Imray because he believed Imray’s admiring glance and touch had bewitched his young child, who subsequently died of fever. He hid the body among the roof beams.

As Khan is arrested, he reveals that a wounded venomous snake has bitten his toe. Viewing the bite as an honorable escape from public execution, he accepts his death and dies within an hour. Once Imray’s body and murderer are removed, the haunting ceases, and Tietjens peacefully returns to sleep inside the bungalow.
