Before attending a party, Alice proudly shows her aunt Helen an elaborate white dress laden with ribbons, jewelry, and flowers. Helen gently but frankly warns that the outfit is gaudy and suggests it reflects vanity rather than taste. Though hurt, Alice changes into a modest, elegant gown with minimal ornamentation.

At the party, Alice initially regrets her decision when she sees Mr. Benton, the young man she hopes to impress, talking with a richly dressed woman and apparently failing to recognize her. She soon resolves that she wants to be valued for more than showy clothing. Her tasteful appearance and intelligence ultimately draw widespread admiration, especially from Mr. Benton, who devotes much of the evening to her. After Alice tells Helen how successful the evening was, a visit from Mr. Benton the following night confirms that Helen’s advice has improved rather than harmed Alice’s romantic prospects.
