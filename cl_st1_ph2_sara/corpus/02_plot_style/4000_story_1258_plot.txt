May Rockett, the ambitious and proudly independent daughter of an invalid gardener who lives with his family in the lodge of Brent Hall, returns home for a brief rest. Regarding herself as socially and intellectually superior to her parents and their aristocratic employers, she refuses to show deference to the Shale family.

After Hilda Shale humiliates May at a social call by alluding to her inability to afford a bicycle, the two meet again at the Hall gates. May deliberately refuses to open the gate for Hilda and answers her rudely. In retaliation, Lady Shale orders the Rocketts to leave the lodge, threatening the elderly gardener’s security and the family’s livelihood.

At first May dismisses the eviction as an empty threat, but when Sir Edwin confirms that he will not overrule his wife, she realizes the consequences of her pride. For her family’s sake, she submits to a humiliating ordeal at the Hall, waiting among servants and apologizing directly to Hilda and Lady Shale. Although they dismiss her contemptuously without revealing their decision, May’s apology succeeds: the eviction is withdrawn. She returns to London without telling her family of her sacrifice and, unusually, takes quiet satisfaction in having helped them rather than boasting of what she has done.
