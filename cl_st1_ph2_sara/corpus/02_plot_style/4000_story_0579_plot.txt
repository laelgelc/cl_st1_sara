Five young Texans travel into Crow territory intending to steal the stakes from a horse race, but the Crows seize the money first and drive them off, wounding two of the men. Left penniless far from home, the group poses as a sheriff and deputies transporting a notorious outlaw. At each ranch, the narrator plays the prisoner, then stages an escape so the group can depart before being asked for payment.

The deception works until they stay with Colonel Purdy, a zealous Mexican War veteran. The supposed sheriff embellishes the narrator’s crimes, portraying him as a vicious murderer and horse thief. Purdy binds and mistreats the “outlaw,” then, during the staged escape, shoots him in the arm and pursues him for miles. The narrator survives only because his horse, Black Hawk, outruns the colonel’s mount. When his companions finally catch up, he refuses to continue playing the desperado and declares that from then on, he will be the sheriff.
