A Russian volunteer recalls charging through a forest under heavy fire, seeing comrades fall, and bayoneting a terrified Turkish soldier. Soon afterward, he is shot in both legs, loses consciousness, and awakens alone in dense bushes after his unit has moved on.

Unable to walk and tormented by pain, thirst, heat, and isolation, he discovers that the corpse beside him is the Turk he killed. As the body decomposes, he reflects bitterly on the senselessness of war: neither man bore the other any hatred, and both were driven into battle by forces beyond their control. Desperate for water, he painfully crawls to the corpse and retrieves its half-full canteen, which keeps him alive.

Over several days, his condition worsens. He considers using the Turk’s rifle to kill himself but resolves to endure in hope of rescue. A passing Cossack company fails to hear his cries, and in his despair he spills nearly all his remaining water. Haunted by thoughts of his mother, his fiancée, and a dying dog he once saw abandoned, he expects a slow death beside the rotting corpse.

After three and a half days, soldiers searching the battlefield find him just as they are preparing to bury the dead. He is given water and carried to a hospital. Doctors discover that his bones and major arteries were spared, though one leg must be amputated. He survives and later records his ordeal.
