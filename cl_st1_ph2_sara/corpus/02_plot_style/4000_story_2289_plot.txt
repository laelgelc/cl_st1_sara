Captain Markof leads a brutal punitive expedition in which suspected rebels and people without passports are summarily executed. While his company rests at a ruined estate on New Year’s Eve, he orders three prisoners—including a strangely calm old man—to be shot at dawn.

Suffering from malaria, Markof falls into a feverish sleep and sees the condemned old man beside his bed. The visitor challenges him to justify the slaughter, while Markof defends himself through military duty, patriotism, and obedience to orders. The old man condemns these excuses, recounts humanity’s long history of violence, and reveals himself as Cain, cursed to wander eternally and witness every act of bloodshed after murdering Abel.

At dawn, Markof awakens to learn that all three prisoners, including the old man, have already been executed. Shaken and physically broken, he relinquishes command and resolves to submit his resignation, suggesting that the vision has destroyed his ability to continue as an executioner.
