A timid Dublin clerk, known as Little Chandler, eagerly reunites with Ignatius Gallaher, an old friend who has become a successful London journalist. On his way to meet Gallaher, Chandler dreams of escaping Dublin, publishing poetry, and gaining recognition for his melancholy temperament.

Over drinks, Gallaher boasts about his travels, worldly experiences, and freedom from domestic obligations. Although Chandler finds him vulgar and patronizing, he envies his adventurous life and resents the limitations of his own marriage, career, and timid nature.

Returning home, Chandler broods over his wife, their modest household, and his unrealized literary ambitions while caring for their infant son. When the child’s crying prevents him from reading poetry, he angrily shouts at it, terrifying the baby. His wife rushes in, takes the child, and glares at Chandler with hostility. Ashamed of his outburst and painfully aware of his entrapment and failure, he withdraws into the shadows and weeps with remorse.
