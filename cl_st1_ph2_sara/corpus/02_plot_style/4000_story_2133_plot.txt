Tarrion, an ambitious young officer on leave in Simla, wants a comfortable permanent government appointment but lacks influential connections. After he helps the clever and socially powerful Mrs. Hauksbee outwit an aide-de-camp, she promises to secure him a post.

By chance, a messenger mistakenly delivers to Mrs. Hauksbee a confidential packet containing sensitive Government of India papers. She and Tarrion study the documents and realize that their secret information can be used as leverage. Tarrion boldly approaches one of the government’s most powerful officials, requests a well-paid Simla appointment, and demonstrates his apparent access to highly confidential matters by quoting from the papers. Alarmed by the breach and by Tarrion’s pointed criticism of favoritism, senior officials decide that it is safer to employ and transfer him than to oppose him.

After days of official turmoil, Tarrion receives an appointment paying five to seven hundred rupees a month. The Viceroy warns him that one may intimidate the government only once. Tarrion credits Mrs. Hauksbee’s abilities, while she privately reflects on how easily men can be manipulated.
