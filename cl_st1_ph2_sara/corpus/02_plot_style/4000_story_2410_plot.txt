Captain James Walshawe, a cruel and debt-ridden rake, elopes with the Irish heiress Peg O’Neill and lives on her income while abusing and terrifying her. After years of misery, Peg dies. During her wake, the drunken Captain angrily removes a holy candle from her dead hand. Her servant Molly Doyle curses him, declaring that his soul will be imprisoned in the candle’s wick until it burns out. The Captain locks the candle away and survives for more than forty years, growing increasingly old, diseased, and malicious.

When Walshawe dies without a will, his cousin and heir, Mr. Watson, travels to Wauling for the funeral. Important estate leases are missing, and Watson suspects the attorney has stolen them. That stormy night, unaware of its history, he finds the old holy candle and burns it beside his bed.

As the candle expires, Watson experiences strange touches, noises, and knocks. He then sees a tiny figure emerge from beneath the candle extinguisher, enlarge into a handsome young officer, and reveal a concealed compartment behind a dressing-table drawer containing the missing papers. The apparition rapidly ages into the bloated, decaying form of Captain Walshawe, then crumbles like ash and is drawn up the chimney amid a violent burst of wind.

The next day, Watson confirms that the candle was the one taken from Peg’s corpse and finds the leases exactly where the ghost indicated. The apparition has displayed Walshawe at successive stages of his life, suggesting that Peg’s servant’s curse trapped his soul in the candle until its final burning compelled him to reveal the papers he had hidden and intended to destroy.
