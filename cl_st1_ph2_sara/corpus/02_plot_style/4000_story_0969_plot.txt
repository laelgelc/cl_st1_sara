After the woman he loves marries someone else, wealthy, humorless Richard Herrick retreats to a model farm and devotes himself to nature. At a New York music hall, he is outraged by three trained bears—Bruno, Clara, and their comic cub Ikey—being forced to dance, perform tricks, and drink from bottles. Convinced that they long for freedom, he buys them from their trainer and releases them into his fenced game preserve, despite his friends’ warnings that the domesticated animals may prefer show business.

At first, the bears seem delighted by the forest, and Herrick proudly declares that he has restored them to their natural lives. That evening, however, music from the house draws them back. Bruno and Clara appear on the terrace and happily perform their waltz, while Ikey steals a decanter, drinks from it, and gives his practiced imitation of drunkenness. Herrick’s idealistic belief that the bears would abandon their training for the wild is thus humiliatingly disproved.
