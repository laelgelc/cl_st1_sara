John Longbowe, a boastfully “plain, blunt” English yeoman, accompanies the Earl of Southampton through a fantastically muddled Elizabethan London. After visiting the Globe, where Southampton’s rejected manuscripts are returned and a young Will Shakespeare mocks him, they encounter Queen Elizabeth’s masked revels on the frozen Thames. Southampton saves the falling queen with his cloak, then exchanges the wet garment for Longbowe’s dry one.

Kidnappers seeking Southampton mistake Longbowe for the earl because of the cloak. Discovering their error only after carrying him aboard a departing ship, they nevertheless send him to sea to keep him from revealing their conspiracy. Longbowe becomes violently seasick, joins the vessel’s pirate crew, and participates in attacks on Spanish ships. When the crew turns against its cruel captain, Longbowe claims to prevent bloodshed by secretly throwing the man overboard, after which he becomes captain himself.

Using a treasure map found among the dead captain’s possessions, Longbowe sails in search of an island. He reaches the wrong one, disguises his ship as a wreck to lure unarmed islanders aboard, kills them, seizes a vast treasure, and excuses himself from sharing it with the clergyman who supplied the original map because the island was not the intended destination.

After years away, Longbowe returns to England to find Southampton dead, many of his associates executed, and the country under a new monarch. He eventually marries Marian Straitways, barely mentioning that she had followed him disguised as a shipboy and that he rescued her from a villain, because he considers romance unworthy beside his supposedly heroic deeds.
