A wealthy Philadelphia merchant and his wife treasure a portrait of Willie, their youngest child. When Willie dies, the painting becomes the family’s cherished memorial of him.

Soon afterward, business failures leave the merchant insolvent. He honestly surrenders all his property to his creditors, whose trustees inventory even the family’s furniture and paintings. Despite his pleas, they include Willie’s portrait among the assets to be sold. Refusing to conceal it dishonestly, the family leaves the portrait behind and moves into a small, sparsely furnished house.

That evening, a prosperous young merchant visits the eldest daughter, Constance. His affection has not diminished with her family’s loss of fortune, and he proposes marriage. They wed three days later and depart briefly for New York. Upon returning, Constance discovers that her husband has bought her family’s former home and all its contents at auction, including Willie’s portrait, and restored everything to its former place. He also makes her father his business partner, valuing his experience and connections. The family’s fortunes and happiness are restored, while the rescued portrait remains doubly precious as both a memorial and a reminder of how nearly it was lost.
