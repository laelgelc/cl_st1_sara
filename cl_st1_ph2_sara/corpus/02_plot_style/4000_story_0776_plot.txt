Caroline Noble, a disciplined and practical woman, has built a secure, orderly life in reaction to her impoverished childhood among self-destructive artistic idealists. After the celebrated tenor Raymond d’Esquerre spends a month rehearsing with her at her seaside estate, she discovers that she is deeply affected by his charisma and by the romantic, imaginative life she has always suppressed.

Following his departure, Caroline secretly visits the garden lodge where they practiced, indulging in memories and fantasies she finds both irresistible and shameful. During a stormy night there, music and recollection overwhelm her self-control, forcing her to recognize her longing for d’Esquerre and her dissatisfaction with a life governed entirely by discipline and practicality. By dawn, horrified that these desires arose from within herself, she retreats from the lodge.

At breakfast, when her husband again suggests demolishing the lodge to build a summer house, Caroline agrees. Outwardly she resumes her composed, unsentimental manner, choosing to destroy the place associated with her emotional awakening and conceal the inner crisis that has shaken her.
