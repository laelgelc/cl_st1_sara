Little Joyce, a shy, neglected orphan living with her disapproving grandmother, is loved only by the terminally ill servant Denise. When Denise longs to hear the famous French Canadian singer Madame Laurin before dying, Joyce goes to the hotel intending to trade her treasured ancient Egyptian doll for a private performance. She encounters Madame Laurin herself, who is moved by Joyce’s devotion and agrees to sing for Denise without taking the doll.

After delighting Denise with old French songs, Madame Laurin asks Joyce to sing and discovers that the child possesses an extraordinary voice. She insists that Joyce receive musical training, offering to fund it if necessary. Grandmother Marshall, newly aware of her granddaughter’s talent and worth, promises to provide the training and begins treating Joyce with affection. Joyce keeps her beloved doll and gains both the prospect of becoming a great singer and the love she has long desired from her grandmother.
