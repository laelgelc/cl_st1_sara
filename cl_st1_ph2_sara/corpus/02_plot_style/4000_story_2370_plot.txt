A humorous narrator portrays barbers as sports-obsessed authorities whose true profession is dispensing athletic information, with shaving merely an excuse to trap and lecture customers. Barbers immobilize patrons with soap, towels, and intimidating techniques, subjecting them to exaggerated physical discomfort while discussing games and betting.

The narrator describes absurd barber colleges that teach hair destruction, dubious chemistry, and dangerous anatomy. In city shops, customers are steamed, “cooked,” and frightened with predictions of baldness, gray hair, and wrinkles until they purchase unnecessary treatments. Those who surrender are drenched and polished until they look varnished, while teams of attendants also shine their shoes, mend their socks, enamel their teeth, and reshape their joints.

Country barbering is depicted as even rougher: the customer is stripped to the waist, and the lone barber attacks the haircut like a physical contest, charging across the room and driving the clippers up the customer’s back and head like a lawn mower through tall grass.
