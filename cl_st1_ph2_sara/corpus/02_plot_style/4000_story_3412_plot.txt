A year after a young man faced the king’s infamous arena trial, five foreign dignitaries arrive seeking to learn whether he encountered a lady or a tiger. A court officer responds with another unresolved tale.

A visiting prince once asked to choose a wife from among the court’s beautiful women. The king abruptly ordered him married the next day, enforcing obedience through an executioner called the Discourager of Hesitancy. At the ceremony, the prince was blindfolded and wed to an unseen woman whose hand and voice enchanted him. Afterward, he was shown forty nearly identical ladies and commanded to identify his bride, with death as the penalty for choosing an unmarried woman.

Only two women offered clues: one smiled at him, while another frowned. Unable to decide what either expression meant, the prince was forced to choose within seconds. He selected one, and the king confirmed that she was indeed his lawful bride, but the officer does not reveal whether she was the smiling or frowning woman. Instead, he tells the visitors that once they solve that mystery, he will answer their original question. The five remain unable to decide.
