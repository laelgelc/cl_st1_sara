While living high on Mount Saint Helena to escape the coastal fogs that threaten his weak lungs, the narrator wakes at dawn to find Napa Valley submerged beneath a vast, silent sea of mist. He watches in fascination as the beautiful but dangerous fog rises, swallowing hills, trees, roads, and landmarks while spilling over mountain ridges like waves and cataracts.

At first he believes the fog is receding, but it soon surges higher, bringing cold, salt-laden air that makes him cough. Partly for safety and partly in imaginative play, he climbs farther up the mountain and observes the transformed landscape from above. Silverado remains temporarily protected by a spur of land even as the fog threatens to engulf it.

Near midday, mist begins to cross the natural barrier, but the wind changes before the settlement is overwhelmed. The fog breaks apart and retreats southward, restoring the familiar view of the valley. Although smaller patches later appear below, the heights are not invaded again that season.
