Born in Salzburg in 1756, Wolfgang Amadeus Mozart displays extraordinary musical ability almost from infancy. After discovering the three-year-old playing a perfect chord on the harpsichord, his musician father, Leopold, begins teaching him. Wolfgang quickly masters difficult pieces, imitates his sister Nannerl’s playing, learns the violin, and starts composing by the age of five.

Recognizing the talents of both children, Leopold takes them on concert tours throughout Europe. Wolfgang astonishes audiences at royal courts in Vienna, Paris, London, and elsewhere with his virtuosity, musical memory, improvisation, and charming personality. Despite illness, exhausting travel, professional jealousy, and changing public tastes, he continues composing sonatas, symphonies, concertos, and operas. In Rome, he famously hears the closely guarded “Miserere” and writes it down from memory.

After years of study and performance, Mozart is commissioned to compose the comic opera “La Finta Giardiniera.” At nineteen, he attends its Munich premiere anxiously with his father and sister. The opera proves a triumphant success, establishing his popularity and marking his emergence from celebrated child prodigy into an accomplished composer and musical genius.
