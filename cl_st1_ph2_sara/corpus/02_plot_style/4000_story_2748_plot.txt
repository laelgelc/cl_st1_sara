On a warm spring morning, a cheerful Parisian feels an intoxicating longing for freedom and romance. He boards a steamer on the Seine and becomes captivated by a charming young working woman. As they exchange glances and smiles, he prepares to approach her.

A melancholy stranger interrupts and warns him that springtime love is more dangerous than illness. To explain, the stranger recounts how, the previous spring, he met a pretty working girl on the same boat. Enchanted by the weather and her apparent tenderness, he followed her ashore, walked with her in the woods, and began seeing her every Sunday. Mistaking attraction and sentimentality for lasting love, he married her after three months. Marriage soon revealed their incompatibility: she constantly insults him, gossips, quarrels, sings irritatingly, and embarrasses him by sharing their private affairs.

When the boat reaches Saint-Cloud, the narrator’s young woman disembarks and gives him an inviting smile. He tries to follow, but the stranger physically restrains him and loudly prevents him from leaving. Unwilling to create a public scene, the narrator remains aboard as the disappointed woman watches the boat depart. The stranger then congratulates himself for having saved the narrator from repeating his own disastrous mistake.
