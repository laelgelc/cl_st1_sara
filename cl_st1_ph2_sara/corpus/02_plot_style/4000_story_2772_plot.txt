A hotel guest discovers a forgotten diary written by the previous occupant, who spent twenty-five days at the Chatel-Guyon health resort undergoing a tedious regimen of mineral water, baths, walks, and weigh-ins. His stay becomes exciting when he befriends two attractive women claiming to be widows. He accompanies them on excursions, dines with them, and even goes swimming with them in a secluded crater lake, while enjoying the envy and disapproval of other guests.

The women abruptly leave when two gentlemen arrive for them, and the diarist returns to his monotonous treatment and solitary sightseeing. He records local scenery, customs, and comic anecdotes, including a village priest who made women plant walnut trees as penance for sexual misconduct. After discovering that he has regained some of the weight he lost, he packs to leave, ending the diary. The new occupant remarks that his own experience of the resort was different because he never encountered the two widows.
