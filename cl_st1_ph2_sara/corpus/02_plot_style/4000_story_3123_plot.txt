Abandoned as a baby, Tig Braddock is taken in by Nora Finnegan, a warmhearted washerwoman who longs for a child. She raises him with love, laughter, and plentiful food, encouraging his ambition to become a writer. After Nora suddenly dies of pneumonia, Tig spends their savings on her funeral, loses his newspaper job, and resolves to support himself by writing fiction.

Tig submits a short story to a magazine contest but falls gravely ill with rheumatic fever. A poor, devoted boy nicknamed the Sparrow brings him coal and scraps of food until the boy also becomes sick and disappears. Starving and near death, Tig smells onions and sees a dish like one Nora used to prepare. Seeming to hear Nora’s skirts and laughter, he eats the miraculous meal and regains his strength. Soon afterward, a letter arrives announcing that he has won the contest’s thousand-dollar prize. When the Sparrow returns, still weak but carrying coal, Tig promises that, in Nora’s memory, the boy will never be hungry again.
