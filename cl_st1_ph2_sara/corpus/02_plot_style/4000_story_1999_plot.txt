After Mrs. Gribble inherits an income of two hundred pounds a year for life, her domineering husband seizes the payments, refuses her requests for better clothes and a new home, and quits his job to live on her money. Alarmed to learn that the income will end when she dies, he becomes anxious about her health.

Mrs. Gribble realizes she can exploit this fear. She develops alarming symptoms, and a sympathetic doctor warns Mr. Gribble that her weak heart requires rest, indulgence, and freedom from criticism. Terrified of losing both his wife and her income, Mr. Gribble hires a servant, pays for new clothes, and moves them into the better house Mrs. Gribble wanted.

Still resentful that her husband controls every penny, Mrs. Gribble finally opens the monthly payment herself and pockets it. When she appears breathless and unwell, Mr. Gribble is too frightened to challenge her. She then goes upstairs, where the sound of dancing reveals her triumphant recovery and successful seizure of control.
