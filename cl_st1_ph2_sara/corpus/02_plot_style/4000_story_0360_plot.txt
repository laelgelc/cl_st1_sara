At the end of time, Ahasverus, the immortal wanderer, believes humanity has vanished and rejoices that he can finally die. He encounters Prometheus, who predicts that a perfected race will replace humankind. Ahasverus recounts his punishment for refusing compassion to Christ: he has wandered through every age, condemned to endless idleness and forced to witness the repetitive rise, suffering, and collapse of civilizations.

When Prometheus reveals that he created humanity and gave it fire, Ahasverus blames him for all human misery and attempts to chain him to the rock for renewed punishment. Prometheus then tells Ahasverus that he is destined to become a new Hercules and the immortal king of the coming race, serving as the link between the old and new worlds. Enchanted by this promise, Ahasverus abandons his desire for death and dreams eagerly of eternal, perfected life while loosening Prometheus’s chains.

In reality, the encounter is Ahasverus’s final dream as he dies. Two eagles approach and observe that, despite claiming to hate life, the last man loved it so deeply that he dreams of continuing it at the moment of death.
