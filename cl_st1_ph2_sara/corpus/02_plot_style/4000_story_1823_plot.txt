Ohio becomes a state in 1803 after a narrowly decided constitutional struggle in which Judge Ephraim Cutler helps defeat a proposal for temporary slavery. The new state initially enjoys relative peace and agricultural growth, though poor roads and limited access to markets hinder its prosperity.

Tensions rise as British agents in Canada support Tecumseh and the Prophet in resisting American settlement. When the War of 1812 begins, General Hull’s surrender of Detroit exposes Ohio to British and Indigenous attack. General William Henry Harrison advances to recover the region, but part of his army is defeated and its prisoners massacred, despite Tecumseh’s efforts to stop the killing.

Harrison builds Fort Meigs and withstands two attacks by British General Proctor and Tecumseh’s warriors. The enemy then targets Fort Stephenson. Although ordered to abandon it, twenty-one-year-old Major George Croghan remains because retreat is no longer possible. After explaining his defiant message to Harrison, he is restored to command. Croghan’s small force crushes a British assault with a concealed cannon, then mercifully aids the wounded attackers. Proctor retreats in humiliation.

Soon afterward, Commodore Oliver Hazard Perry, having built an American fleet from resources transported through the wilderness, confronts the British on Lake Erie. Though his flagship is wrecked, Perry transfers to another vessel and turns near-defeat into victory, reporting, “We have met the enemy and they are ours.” Harrison then pursues the retreating British and Indigenous forces into Canada and defeats them at the Battle of the Thames, where Tecumseh is killed and organized Indigenous resistance in the region collapses.
