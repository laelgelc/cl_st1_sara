Thursdale, a sophisticated bachelor skilled at avoiding emotional commitment, becomes engaged to the candid Ruth Gaynor after years of maintaining an intimate but carefully undefined relationship with Margaret Vervain. Proud of having trained Margaret to conceal her feelings, he visits her after introducing the two women, expecting praise for how gracefully she handled the encounter.

Margaret reveals that Ruth returned privately to ask whether Thursdale was truly free. Learning that he had emotionally exploited Margaret—accepting her devotion while withholding love and commitment—Ruth becomes disgusted by his calculated detachment and may reconsider their engagement. Margaret offers to lie and claim that she and Thursdale had been lovers, hoping to give him the passionate past Ruth wishes he had. Thursdale refuses, realizing that disguising his nature would only transfer his punishment to Ruth. He leaves to face whatever message Ruth has sent, while Margaret remains alone, devastated by the emptiness of the life and relationship he shaped.
