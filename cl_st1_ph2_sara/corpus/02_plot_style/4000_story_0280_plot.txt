A fifteen-year-old Kentucky boy, obsessed with thoroughbred horses, persuades three friends to hop freight trains to Saratoga for the races. They hide near the stables with the help of Bildad Johnson, a cook from their hometown, and secretly enjoy several days at the track.

The boy eagerly anticipates a major race between two exceptional Kentucky horses, Sunstreak and Middlestride. Before the race, he shares a profound, wordless moment with Sunstreak’s trainer, Jerry Tillford, believing they both understand the horse’s restrained power. Sunstreak then wins and breaks the world record for a mile, while Middlestride finishes second. The boy idolizes Jerry, imagining his pride as comparable to a parent’s love for a child.

That night, hoping to remain near Jerry, the boy follows him and several drunken men to a brothel. Through a window, he watches Jerry boast that he alone made Sunstreak great, then gaze at and kiss a woman with the same shining expression he had shown beside the horse. Feeling that the sacred connection he witnessed at the track has been corrupted, the boy becomes furious and disillusioned.

He convinces his friends to return home without revealing what he saw. Months later, he still cannot reconcile Jerry’s deep understanding of a magnificent horse with his sordid behavior, and the memory diminishes the purity and pleasure he once found in racing.
