Veteran lumberjack Luther Dallas has long believed he will die beneath a falling tree, as his father did. Ordered to cut down a pine he instinctively fears, he hesitates but finally fells it. He misjudges its fall, and the rebounding trunk crushes his chest, leaving him permanently weakened and gravely ill.

Hoping a change of environment will prolong his life, his fellow lumbermen send him to Chicago, where he intends to find a younger sister he has not seen for years. Overwhelmed by the immense, chaotic city, he fails to locate her and spends his dwindling money on cheap lodging and food. As his health deteriorates, he becomes homeless, unable to work, and reduced to begging, while longing desperately for the familiar northern forest and logging camp.

Starving, feverish, and delirious, Luther begins imagining the city as the woods. Mistaking a utility pole for a tree and his walking stick for an axe, he strikes it and is arrested as a drunk. The police lock him in a cell without recognizing his severe illness. During the night, he retreats into visions of the forest and his sister calling him. By morning he has died from a hemorrhage, prompting only a belated, indifferent observation that such prisoners ought to be examined by a doctor.
