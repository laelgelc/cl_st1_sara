A lone traveler boards a night stagecoach from Wingdam to Slumgullion, already dispirited by the hotel’s dreary guests, awkward atmosphere, and strange bystanders who ceremonially spit on the coach. Expecting to rest in the empty carriage, he instead endures its cramped interior, jolting springs, and the maddening repetition in his mind of a woman’s maxim overheard at the hotel.

The driver’s silence leads him to imagine that highwaymen have seized the coach, prompting an elaborate fantasy in which he is a French marquis captured by brigands and rescued by a mysterious woman. Moonlight and the driver’s audible spitting eventually reassure him that nothing is wrong. He drifts in and out of sleep, amusing himself with theatrical associations and a discarded hairpin.

The coach finally accelerates and arrives at Slumgullion. When the traveler asks why they never changed horses, the driver explains that they did so two hours earlier, revealing that despite believing himself restless and alert, the traveler had slept soundly through much of the journey.
