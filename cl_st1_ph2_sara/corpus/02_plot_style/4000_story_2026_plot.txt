James Denton buys the diary of a local eighteenth-century antiquary and discovers inside it an old fabric sample whose pattern resembles flowing hair tied with ribbons. His aunt admires it and persuades him to have the design reproduced as curtains for their newly built manor. Although the craftsman who cuts the printing block senses something evil in the pattern, the curtains are manufactured and hung throughout several bedrooms.

Denton soon notices unsettling effects: the pattern seems to form figures peeping between the curtains, which sway and rustle despite the absence of wind. One night, while alone in his bedroom, he reaches down expecting to touch his dog but instead feels a motionless, hair-covered human shape crawling beside him. Its featureless, hairy face rises toward his own, and it pursues him down the passage until he escapes into a guest’s room.

Denton immediately leaves the manor and reexamines the diary. Separating several pages that had been pasted together, he finds an account of Sir Everard Charlett, a debauched young nobleman famed for his abundant hair. Charlett died mysteriously in a ditch with all his hair torn from his head; when his coffin later broke open, it was reportedly filled with hair. The fabric had come from hangings Charlett commissioned as a memorial to his hair, using one of his own locks as the model. Realizing the reproduced design has summoned or embodied Charlett’s malignant spirit, Denton has all the new curtains burned.
