After five years abroad, sculptor George Herkimer returns to visit his former friend Roderick Elliston and deliver a message from Roderick’s estranged wife, Rosina. He finds Roderick physically and mentally transformed, convinced that a snake lives in his bosom and continually gnaws him. Since separating from Rosina, Roderick has become obsessed with this supposed creature, withdrawn from society, sought ineffective cures, and then publicly tormented townspeople by identifying the metaphorical “serpents”—hatred, greed, ambition, jealousy, guilt, and vice—within them. His behavior leads to confinement in an asylum, but he is released when doctors decide he is eccentric rather than legally insane.

Herkimer visits Roderick with Rosina concealed nearby. Roderick admits that the serpent is sustained by his obsessive self-contemplation and that it could disappear only if he forgot himself. Rosina then appears and urges him to forget himself through love for another. Her touch seems to drive the serpent away, and Roderick regains his sanity and begs her forgiveness. Herkimer explains that the snake represented Roderick’s consuming egotism and jealousy, while Rosina’s selfless love offers him reconciliation and hope for a purified future.
