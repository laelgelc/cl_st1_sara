Bibi Haldar, a twenty-nine-year-old woman plagued by mysterious seizures, lives under the begrudging care of her cousin Haldar and his wife while working unpaid in their cosmetics business. Longing for marriage and motherhood, she becomes hopeful when a doctor claims that marriage may cure her. Her neighbors support her efforts to find a husband, but Haldar refuses to spend money on a wedding, and a blunt matrimonial advertisement attracts no suitors.

When Haldar’s wife becomes pregnant, she treats Bibi as contagious and increasingly isolates her, eventually forcing her to live alone in a rooftop storage room. The neighbors boycott Haldar’s shop in protest, driving the family out of business and away from town. Abandoned with only a small sum of money, Bibi withdraws from society.

Months later, the neighbors discover that Bibi is four months pregnant. She cannot or will not identify the father, leaving the circumstances—apparently an assault—unresolved. She gives birth to a son and, aided by the neighborhood women, becomes a capable mother. Using Haldar’s abandoned merchandise and the money he left, she opens her own cosmetics shop in the storage room, supports herself and her child, and ceases having seizures. Though the identity of the man who impregnated her remains unknown, motherhood and independence appear to cure her.
