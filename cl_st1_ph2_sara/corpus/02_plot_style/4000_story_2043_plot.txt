A writer overwhelmed by his work retreats from London to an isolated Yorkshire village, hoping solitude will restore his concentration. He takes lodgings with an elderly widow and her shy, worn-looking middle-aged daughter. While attempting to work, he discovers a beautiful miniature of a young woman hidden in a dusty bookcase. The face seems strangely familiar and begins haunting his imagination; he even sees what appears to be the young woman, dressed in an old-fashioned lilac gown, gazing sadly from his window.

His landlady reveals that a woman once arrived at the cottage dying and left behind books and personal belongings. Searching the bookcase, the writer finds an old diary and letters that tell of a young woman’s doomed romance with her childhood sweetheart, Christopher, an aspiring artist. After pledging himself to her and painting her portrait, Christopher leaves to pursue his career. His letters gradually become infrequent and cold as he falls in love with someone else. Persuaded to release him, the woman writes that she no longer cares, then records her anguish, jealousy, and regret at surrendering the man she still desperately loves.

One letter identifies Christopher as a famous man the writer knows personally. Though successful, Christopher now lives unhappily with a cold wife in a grand, joyless household. Believing the diary belonged to the woman who died at the cottage, the writer finally asks his landlady’s daughter about the miniature. She blushes and reveals that it is her own portrait, painted long ago by a friend. The writer then recognizes that the faded, seemingly ordinary woman before him is the beautiful, grief-stricken young woman whose past he has uncovered.
