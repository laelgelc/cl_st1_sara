Belle Carpenter, embittered by her petty and abusive father, secretly loves the powerful bartender Ed Handby but considers him beneath her social station. She therefore walks with and kisses the younger George Willard to relieve her desires and provoke Ed, who wants to marry her but can express himself only through possessiveness and threats.

One winter night, George leaves a pool room and wanders through Winesburg, experiencing an intense sense of spiritual awakening and newfound manhood. He visits Belle, believing she will recognize his transformation. Belle has just seen Ed lingering nearby after clumsily warning her to stay away from George, so she eagerly takes George out, hoping Ed will follow and become jealous.

On a secluded hillside, George mistakes Belle’s distracted willingness for surrender to his new masculine power. Ed suddenly appears, effortlessly throws George into the bushes, and takes control of Belle. George repeatedly attacks him but is tossed aside each time, eventually striking his head. Ed then marches Belle away. Humiliated, George returns home sick with self-hatred, unable to recover the exalted vision he had experienced earlier; the neighborhood that had inspired him now appears merely sordid and commonplace.
