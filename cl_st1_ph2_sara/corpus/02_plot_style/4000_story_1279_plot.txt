After her kind and sociable husband, Howie, is killed in a munitions-plant explosion, the shy, withdrawn Laura becomes consumed by grief. She learns that he was accidentally captured in the background of a motion picture, stopping to loosen a suffering dog’s muzzle and smiling warmly as he leaves. Desperate to see this brief living image of him, she follows the aging film from town to town, terrified that damaged reels, theater cuts, or the film’s impending destruction will erase the last trace she has of him.

Her brother tries to stop her obsessive journeys and draw her back into ordinary life, but Laura feels that Howie had been her only connection to other people. She eventually tracks the deteriorating film to a distant theater for what may be its final showing. As the worn reel begins to fail during Howie’s scene, the image unexpectedly steadies on his smile. At that moment, Laura notices a sleeping child beside her and gently supports the child’s head.

Through this small act of care, Laura realizes that she cannot preserve or reach Howie by chasing his image. She can remain close to him by living as he did—showing kindness and concern for others. By carrying his generosity into the world, she finds a lasting way to keep and fulfill what he gave her.
