George Bendemann, a successful young merchant, writes to an isolated childhood friend in Russia to announce his engagement to Frieda Brandenfeld. He has long concealed his growing prosperity and happiness, fearing that the news would wound his struggling friend.

Before mailing the letter, George visits his aging father. Their conversation becomes a confrontation: his father questions whether the Russian friend even exists, then suddenly claims to know him intimately and to have secretly corresponded with him. Accusing George of betraying his friend, disgracing his dead mother, neglecting his father, and selfishly pursuing marriage and success, the father reasserts his authority and condemns George to death by drowning.

Overwhelmed by the judgment, George runs from the house to a bridge. Declaring that he has always loved his parents, he throws himself into the river as traffic streams overhead.
