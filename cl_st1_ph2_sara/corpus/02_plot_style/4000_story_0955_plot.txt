When war breaks out with Spain, newspaper correspondent Chesterton insists on going to cover it despite the fears of Miss Armitage, the woman he loves. They console themselves with the belief that her love will act as a charm protecting him. Throughout the campaign, he repeatedly escapes disease and death while writing fervent love letters home.

After peace is declared, Chesterton rides through the night to catch a ship home. Unaware that Spanish forces have not heard the news, he passes an ambush unharmed because he drops a treasured matchbox Miss Armitage gave him; while he searches for it, the soldiers mistake him for a scout and postpone firing. Later, his pony refuses to cross a failing bridge. Chesterton instead descends to a ford to gather Miss Armitage’s favorite orchid, and the bridge collapses behind him.

When his pony goes lame in a village, hostile Spaniards plan to sell him another horse and then murder him. Before they can act, Chesterton uses medicine from a case Miss Armitage made him carry to save the innkeeper’s dying son. Grateful, the innkeeper abandons the plot and sends him safely onward.

Reunited with Miss Armitage, Chesterton declares that her charm protected him. She recalls spending one particular night praying desperately because she sensed he was in mortal danger. Chesterton laughs, believing he faced no danger that night because peace had already been declared, never realizing how narrowly her gifts and his devotion to her saved his life.
