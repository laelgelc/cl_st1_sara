A writer searching for a story lingers at Tony’s cosmopolitan Chicago newsstand, where she observes two strangers buying papers from their former homes. Mercedes Meron, a jaded burlesque performer originally named Sadie Hayes, reads a small Wisconsin paper, while Guy Peel, an Englishman turned successful Texas rancher, reads the London Times. Discovering their shared homesickness and sense of exile, they encourage each other to return home.

A year later, they meet again at the same newsstand. Both kept their promises but found that the homes they remembered no longer suited them. Mercedes felt alienated from her small-town family and missed Chicago’s noise and excitement, while Guy found English country life stifling and longed for his ranch, workers, animals, and Western landscape. They realize that home is not necessarily one’s birthplace, but the place where one belongs and is needed. Mercedes returns to theatrical life, now married to her manager, and Guy heads back to Texas. At Tony’s stand, each rejects the old hometown paper and instead buys one representing the life they have chosen.
