In 1803, twelve-year-old Yann Riel persuades his Breton smuggler father to take him on a voyage delivering brandy and fifteen jointed wooden dolls to Cornwall. During the nighttime landing, customs officers raid the beach. Yann, who has impulsively climbed onto an ungreased black mare named Lilith, is carried away with the smugglers’ packhorses while his father and crew escape by sea.

After a wild ride through the unfamiliar countryside, Lilith brings Yann to a hidden farmhouse. There he meets Heloise Keranguin, a Breton émigrée who taught the mare to respond to Breton. Heloise arranges Yann’s return home and reveals why she ordered the dolls: she has recreated the convent school of her youth as a miniature scene and needs additional figures for a religious festival. Her former life was destroyed during the French Revolution, and her intended husband died in exile in the farmhouse’s great bed.

Yann departs for a ship bound for Roscoff. Heloise gives him a silver crucifix in payment for the dolls, which he keeps for the rest of his life.
