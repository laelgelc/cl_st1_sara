A wartime nurse meets John, a gravely wounded Virginia blacksmith admired for his courage, selflessness, and quiet dignity. Initially mistaking his silence for independence, she realizes that he longs for comfort and begins caring for him tenderly. A doctor reveals that John’s lung wound is fatal, and the nurse reluctantly tells him. John accepts the news calmly, explaining that he enlisted from duty rather than ambition and does not regret risking his life, despite his responsibilities to his widowed mother and younger siblings.

John dictates a farewell letter to his brother, entrusting the family to him and hoping for a reply before he dies. During his final hours, he endures intense suffering without complaint, says goodbye to a beloved comrade, and dies at dawn while holding the nurse’s hand. A letter from home arrives an hour too late. The nurse places it in his hand, sends his ring and a lock of hair to his mother, and leaves with an enduring reverence for his character and sacrifice.
