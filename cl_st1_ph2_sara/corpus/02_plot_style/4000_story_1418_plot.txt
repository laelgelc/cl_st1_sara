As British rule collapses in Massachusetts, Sir William Howe prepares to abandon the Province House. He finds the elderly Esther Dudley, an impoverished aristocrat and fiercely devoted loyalist who has lived there for decades. She refuses to leave for Halifax, insisting that King George will regain the colony. Howe entrusts her with the mansion’s key, telling her to preserve it until a royal governor returns.

Esther remains in the deserted house throughout and after the Revolution, becoming a living emblem of the colonial past. Surrounded by memories and rumored ghosts, she entertains a few defeated Tories and captivates local children with stories of former governors and vanished grandeur. Increasingly detached from reality, she interprets American victories as British triumphs, celebrates the king’s birthday, and continually watches for the return of royal authority.

Years later, hearing that a governor is approaching, Esther assumes her long wait is over. Dressed in her faded finery, she opens the Province House and offers its key to the arriving dignitary. She then realizes that he is John Hancock, once condemned by the Crown and now the elected republican governor of Massachusetts. Devastated that she has welcomed the monarchy’s enemy, Esther collapses and dies, declaring that she has remained faithful and praying for the king. Hancock honors her loyalty while recognizing her as the final symbol of a dead political order, then urges the new generation to leave the past behind and move toward the future.
