Exiled in England by his domineering wife after their daughter marries an earl, devoted baseball fan J. Wilmot Birdsey secretly skips a family dinner to watch an exhibition game between the Giants and White Sox in London. Delighted to meet two fellow American fans, he invites them to dine at his Savoy suite.

During dinner, journalist Waterall recognizes the bearded “Mr Johnson” as John Benyon, a fugitive who robbed a New York bank and fled five years earlier. Benyon admits that he risked capture because exile had become unbearable and the game offered him a few hours of feeling at home again. Birdsey’s admiration for such extreme devotion to baseball outweighs his concern about the crime, but Waterall calls Scotland Yard.

Benyon draws a pistol but cannot bring himself to shoot Waterall and collapses. Outraged, Birdsey tackles the journalist and urges Benyon to escape. Afterward, he defends helping the fugitive on the grounds that exiled baseball fans must stick together, then realizes that his own greatest challenge remains explaining his disappearance to his wife.
