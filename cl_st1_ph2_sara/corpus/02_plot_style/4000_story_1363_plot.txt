During a violent spring storm, Mary is left alone with her sickly baby in an isolated cabin while her logger husband transports a raft across the bay. The river floods unexpectedly, filling the cabin and threatening to tear it apart. Mary climbs with her child onto an uprooted redwood lodged against the house, escaping just before the cabin collapses.

The tree drifts through the darkness toward the bay. Mary endures cold, collisions, uncertainty, and the loss of the family dog while fearing that she and her baby may be swept out to sea. The tide eventually carries the tree onto Dedlow Marsh, where it runs aground. Exhausted and terrified by her baby’s chilled, unresponsive appearance, Mary faints.

At daylight, Indigenous women gathering berries discover Mary and revive both her and the still-living child. Her husband, learning of the flood and recognizing signs that the rescued woman is his wife, returns and reunites with them. He later retrieves the redwood, uses it as the foundation of a new home built above the high-water line, and names the structure in honor of Mary’s ordeal.
