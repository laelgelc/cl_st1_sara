A famously long-winded and evasive politician is reelected as the member for Verbosity. Though his votes, principles, and past declarations are contradictory, he disguises every inconsistency with patriotic slogans, vague abstractions, and grandiose rhetoric.

At a constituency meeting, his persistent opponent Tipkisson asks what the politician and his divided parliamentary allies actually intend to do. The member replies that they are pursuing “the illimitable perspective,” then avoids explaining it by declaring that he supports English hearths, homes, and altars rather than mosques and Mohammedanism. The crowd turns against Tipkisson, and the same religious slander is used against the rival candidate. The electorate consequently returns the member to Parliament.

The narrator ironically celebrates this victory, presenting it as an example of how empty rhetoric, manufactured prejudice, electoral corruption, and political ambiguity triumph over reason and meaningful public debate.
