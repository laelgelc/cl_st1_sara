William leaves his brother Robert, his childhood home, and his beloved Ellen to seek wealth and status, despite Robert’s warning that contentment is more valuable than riches. Robert remains at the homestead, marries, and builds a peaceful, happy family life.

For ten years William sends no word. His pursuit of fortune repeatedly fails until he marries a wealthy woman for her money, betraying Ellen. The marriage is miserable, and his father-in-law distrusts him as a fortune hunter. When William’s wife dies, her father disowns him and denies him any inheritance, leaving him poor and humiliated. After one last desperate struggle for success nearly drives him into crime, William finally abandons his ambition and recognizes the truth of Robert’s advice.

William returns to the old homestead, uncertain whether he will still be welcome. Robert recognizes and warmly embraces him. William then discovers that Ellen has remained there, still unmarried and faithful to him. Overcome by love and repentance, he embraces her, while his return confirms through bitter experience that contentment and loving relationships are worth more than wealth.
