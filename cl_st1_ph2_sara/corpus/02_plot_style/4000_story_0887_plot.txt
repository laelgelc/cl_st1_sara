High school senior Chester Jessup idealizes Mildred Wrigley as a delicate, ethereal girl who survives on tiny portions. After learning that her father plans to send her to boarding school, Chester declares his love and persuades her to elope with him to New York, using his savings to finance the trip.

Their attempt to marry is repeatedly delayed because the constantly hungry Mildred insists on stopping for candy, sundaes, pralines, pancakes, sausage, and finally an enormous lobster lunch. They reach the marriage-license office after it has closed and are also told they are too young, so they head for Hoboken instead. On the ferry, Mildred becomes seasick from overeating, blames Chester, and demands to go home.

The failed elopement destroys Chester’s romantic illusions. He returns home disgusted by both Mildred and the thought of food, and when told she is still being sent to boarding school, he remarks only that he hopes she gets enough to eat.
