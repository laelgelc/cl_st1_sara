In colonial Virginia, Justice of the Peace Pendleton is summoned to the home of Zindorf, a former cleric turned avaricious slave trader. Zindorf plans to sell a beautiful young woman to the wealthy and dissolute Lucian Morrow for ten thousand dollars. They claim she is legally enslaved because her mother was an octoroon slave and because her father, Zindorf’s former partner Ordez, assigned his slaves to Zindorf before disappearing.

Asked to draft an unassailable deed of sale, Pendleton insists on seeing the young woman. She appears dressed like a bride and innocently believes her absent father has arranged her marriage to Morrow. Pendleton recognizes the deception and announces that she will instead travel to her grandfather’s home in Havana, where Morrow may properly seek her hand.

Pendleton then reveals documents proving that the woman is free: she is the legitimate daughter of Ordez and the daughter of a prominent Spanish family, not the child of the enslaved woman Suzanne. The papers were found sewn inside Ordez’s coat after Pendleton and others discovered his murdered body near an abandoned church.

As local citizens converge on the house, Pendleton explains how he identified Ordez’s killer. The murderer had knelt in damp clay to rob the corpse, leaving impressions made by the distinctive patches on Zindorf’s knees. Thus the coarse clothing that made Zindorf resemble a humble monk—a sacred sign corrupted by greed—becomes the evidence that exposes him as Ordez’s murderer.
