During a regiment’s lavish ball, Private Mulvaney shares stolen supper and champagne with his companions and recounts an exploit from his youth. As a corporal, he discovered that a dishonest, debt-ridden captain planned to elope with the colonel’s naïve daughter after an amateur theatrical performance.

Determined to prevent the scandal without openly defying a superior officer, Mulvaney secretly removed the woman’s luggage from her home, intercepted and knocked out the hired carriage driver, and disguised himself as the driver. He collected the daughter after the play but drove her back home instead of to the railway station, where the captain was waiting. Mulvaney then restored her luggage and arranged matters so the furious captain blamed and beat the original driver. When the captain encountered Mulvaney, Mulvaney hinted that the failed elopement deserved punishment, but the captain remained silent and left.

The elopement never occurred, and the affair was buried. Mulvaney concludes by complaining that, after once saving an officer’s family from scandal, he hardly deserved a later punishment of ten days’ confinement for drunkenly trying to bathe in the fort ditch.
