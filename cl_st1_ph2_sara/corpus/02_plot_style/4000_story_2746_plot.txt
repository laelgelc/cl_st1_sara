During the Cannes Flower Festival, two wealthy young women, Marguerite and Simone, leave the lively floral parade for a quiet carriage ride along the coast. As they discuss love, Marguerite argues that any man’s affection can be flattering, even a servant’s, and recounts a strange experience.

Years earlier, she hired an exceptionally capable and attentive maid named Rose, whom she soon trusted completely. After a month, police arrived to search her household for an escaped convict. They identified Rose as John Nicholas Lecapet, a man sentenced to life imprisonment for murder and rape who had disguised himself as a woman. Marguerite was less disturbed by his intimate service—dressing, bathing, and massaging her—than humiliated by the fact that a convicted rapist had shown no apparent desire for her.

After hearing the story, Simone remains silent and stares at the shining buttons on her coachman’s back with an enigmatic smile.
