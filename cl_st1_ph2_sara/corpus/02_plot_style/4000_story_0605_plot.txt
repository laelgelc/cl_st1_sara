Thomas Chadwick, a dignified but weak and chronically unsuccessful former policeman, passes through countless occupations before becoming a tram conductor. A staunch anti-Federationist and admirer of wealth and social rank, he treats the aristocratic Mrs Clayton Vernon with reverence while insulting three Federationist passengers, including the amused young clerk Paul Ford.

After Mrs Vernon briefly boards his tram, she leaves behind a valuable gold purse containing more than fifty pounds. Chadwick finds it and personally returns it to her, expecting his honesty and deference to be properly appreciated. Instead, she calmly assumes the purse was always safe, gives him only a glass of beer, and presents two threepenny pieces for his twin grandsons.

When Ford later asks what reward he received, Chadwick protects his pride and his idealized image of Mrs Vernon by claiming she gave each twin a sovereign. Soon afterward, Mrs Vernon boards the tram and publicly gives Chadwick another threepenny piece for a third grandchild, inadvertently exposing his lie. Though humiliated and furious, Chadwick courteously thanks her and continues treating her as a true lady, remaining stubbornly loyal to his proclaimed ideal. His temperament and behavior eventually cost him the conductor’s job, leaving him unemployed once again.
