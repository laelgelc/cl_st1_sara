American consul Johnny Atwood helps Mr. Hemstetter open a shoe store in Coralio, hoping to impress and marry Hemstetter’s daughter, Rosine. Because the largely barefoot townspeople have no interest in shoes, Johnny’s expatriate friends temporarily sustain the business by buying absurd quantities of footwear.

To create a genuine need, Johnny imports hundreds of pounds of cockleburrs from his hometown and, with Billy Keogh, secretly scatters them throughout Coralio’s streets. The sharp burrs torment the barefoot residents, who rush to buy shoes after realizing they provide protection. Johnny and Keogh replenish the burrs nightly, and Hemstetter sells most of his stock.

Johnny eventually confesses the scheme to Rosine and her father. Rosine forgives him and plans their wedding, while Hemstetter demands that the remaining shoes be sold. Johnny repeats the cocklebur trick in another town, sells the rest, resigns as consul, and returns home with the Hemstetters, leaving Keogh as acting consul.

Soon afterward, Pinkney Dawson arrives with two schooners full of cockleburrs, having misunderstood Johnny’s orders as evidence of a profitable industrial market. Keogh collapses in delighted laughter at the enormous, now-useless shipment while Coralio’s newly shod citizens tramp through the streets.
