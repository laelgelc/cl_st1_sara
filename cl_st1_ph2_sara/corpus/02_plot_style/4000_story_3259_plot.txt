Charsky, a wealthy St. Petersburg poet embarrassed by the public attention attached to his vocation, conceals his literary life beneath the manners of a fashionable socialite. When an impoverished Italian improvisational poet approaches him for help, Charsky initially rebuffs him but, realizing his talent and need, arranges a fashionable performance in Princess N——’s salon.

In private, the Italian astonishes Charsky by instantly composing brilliant verse on a proposed theme, though his preoccupation with ticket prices and profit soon dampens Charsky’s admiration. At the crowded public performance, audience members submit subjects for improvisation, and “Cleopatra and her lovers” is selected by lot. Charsky explains the historical premise: Cleopatra supposedly offered her love at the price of death, yet still found willing admirers. Seized by inspiration, the Italian begins an impassioned poem depicting Cleopatra at a magnificent feast. The story ends before the improvisation or its consequences are completed.
