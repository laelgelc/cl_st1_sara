Ming-Y, a gifted young scholar, becomes tutor to a nobleman’s children near Tching-tou. While traveling home for a spring festival, he glimpses a beautiful woman among flowering peach trees. On his return, he discovers her elegant woodland mansion and is invited inside. The woman, Sië, claims to be a young widow related to his employer. Enchanted by her beauty, learning, music, and ancient manuscripts, Ming-Y falls in love with her, and they begin meeting secretly every night.

For months they drink wine, sing songs from the Thang dynasty, play chess, compose poetry, and become lovers. Ming-Y lies to his employer, claiming he spends his nights with his parents. When the deception is discovered, a servant follows him but sees him mysteriously vanish in the woods. That same night, Sië tells Ming-Y that they must part forever. After one final evening together, she gives him a carved agate brush-case as a farewell gift.

Confronted by his father and employer, Ming-Y confesses everything and produces Sië’s gifts and poems. The objects appear centuries old, while her writing perfectly matches the style of ancient Thang poets. The three men return to her supposed mansion, but find only a ruined, moss-covered tomb.

Ming-Y’s employer deciphers Sië’s cryptic story and identifies her as Sië-Thao, a celebrated courtesan and poet who lived centuries earlier and was beloved by the poet-governor Kao-pien. Her mansion was an illusion, and the woman Ming-Y loved was her lingering spirit. Fearing for him, his father sends him away. Ming-Y later gains honor, marries, and has accomplished children, but he never forgets Sië or speaks of her, keeping her jade lion and agate brush-case on his writing table for the rest of his life.
