Herbert Loring invites a carefully selected audience to a private exhibition of curiosities from the East. After dazzling them with artificial fireworks, he presents a “magic egg,” vividly describing a chick that hatches, grows into a full-sized rooster, and then shrinks back into the egg. The spectators react with amazement and applause.

Loring’s fiancée, Edith Starr, arrives late and secretly watches from the back. Unlike the others, she sees that none of the objects Loring describes actually exist. He later explains that the fireworks hypnotized the audience, allowing him to make them imagine the entire performance. Edith realizes he had intended to hypnotize her as well, without her knowledge or consent. Viewing this as a profound betrayal and proof that he would manipulate her whenever he could, she ends their engagement. Loring leaves recognizing that the unforeseen result of his triumphant experiment is the loss of the woman he loves.
