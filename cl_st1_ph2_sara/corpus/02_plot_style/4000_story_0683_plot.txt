A traveler is fascinated by a mysterious ravine on the property of Whisky Jo Dunfer, a drunken, violently anti-Chinese saloonkeeper. Jo recounts how, years earlier, he employed a Chinese servant named Ah Wee while building a cabin in the ravine. His evasive, contradictory story implies that he killed Ah Wee, supposedly for refusing to cut trees properly. During the conversation, Jo sees a large black eye staring through a knothole and collapses in terror.

Later, the traveler enters the ravine and finds the abandoned building site and Ah Wee’s grave. Its bizarre inscription, carved by Jo, combines racist mockery, confession, and unexpected affection, even referring to Ah Wee as female.

Four years afterward, the traveler returns and meets Gopher, Jo’s eccentric former worker, who leads him to the clearing. Jo is now buried beside Ah Wee. Gopher explains that Jo deeply loved Ah Wee, who was actually a woman, but concealed his attachment out of shame and prejudice. One day Jo found Gopher removing a tarantula from the sleeping Ah Wee’s sleeve. Mistaking the scene for intimacy and overcome by jealousy, Jo swung an axe at Gopher. Gopher dodged, and the blow fatally struck Ah Wee. As she died, she lovingly pulled Jo’s head toward her.

Gopher was bitten by the tarantula and subsequently went insane. Jo publicly justified the killing through racist rhetoric, while privately grieving, drinking heavily, and tending Ah Wee’s grave. Gopher later frightened Jo by staring through the knothole, and Jo died soon afterward. At Jo’s request, Gopher buried him beside Ah Wee, though he remains afraid that Jo’s ghost may still haunt the ravine.
