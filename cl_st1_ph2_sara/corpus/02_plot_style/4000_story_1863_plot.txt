A feverish traveler is stranded by relentless Sunday rain at an inn in Derby. Bored and isolated, he becomes fascinated by an unseen guest known only as the “Stout Gentleman” in room No. 13. From the man’s late breakfast, repeated complaints, heavy footsteps, newspaper request, treatment of the chambermaid and landlady, private dinner, drinking, and patriotic singing, the narrator constructs and repeatedly revises elaborate theories about his age, appearance, temperament, politics, and social status.

By midnight, curiosity and nervous excitement drive the narrator to investigate, but he finds the guest’s room newly deserted and later notices only a large pair of wax-topped boots. The next morning, hearing that the stranger is leaving by mail coach, he rushes to the window for a final chance to see him. He catches only a fleeting rear view of a brown coat and broad drab breeches as the man boards the coach, leaving the mystery unresolved.
