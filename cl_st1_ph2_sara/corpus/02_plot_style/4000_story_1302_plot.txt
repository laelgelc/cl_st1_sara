A Moscow student lives opposite Teresa, a rough, intimidating Polish woman of ill repute whom he judges harshly and avoids. One day, she asks him to write a loving letter to her supposed sweetheart, Boles. Later, she asks him to compose a reply from Boles to “Teresa,” leading the student to accuse her of deception.

Teresa then admits that Boles does not exist. Lonely and without anyone to care for her, she invented him so that letters written to and from him could make his affection feel real and ease her suffering. Ashamed of his cruelty, the student begins writing both sides of the imaginary correspondence twice a week. Teresa listens to the letters, weeps, and repays his kindness by mending his clothes. Three months later, she is imprisoned and disappears from his life, leaving him with a lasting realization about loneliness, prejudice, and society’s self-righteous treatment of those it considers fallen.
