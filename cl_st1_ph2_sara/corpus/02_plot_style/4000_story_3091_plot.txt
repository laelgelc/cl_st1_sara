Detroit detectives Lawrence Peyton and his son Jimmy investigate the murder of Lucy Welch, whose throat was cut in her bedroom. The method resembles a series of unsolved killings committed seven years earlier by someone calling himself “Mephistopheles,” and a letter soon confirms that Lucy is his latest victim.

Suspicion initially falls on Lucy’s estranged husband, George Welch, and her violent acquaintance, Eric Dimke. While Lawrence becomes consumed by memories of the earlier investigation, Jimmy discusses the case and his own disturbing impulses with his psychiatrist, Dr. Whitney Larsen. Larsen points out that Welch lied about his relationship with Lucy, could easily have approached her in the bedroom, and first appeared in Lucy’s life just after the original murders abruptly stopped.

Jimmy finds an old yearbook photograph of Welch and shows it to people connected with the earlier victims. A former employer recognizes Welch as a man who had harassed the first victim. Concluding that Welch kills women who reject him, Jimmy follows him and catches him preparing to murder Patti Bukowski, a married woman who has decided to return to her husband. Welch carries a weighted chain, a bloody knife, and a note announcing Patti’s death under the Mephistopheles name.

Welch is exposed as the serial killer: he stopped murdering after becoming involved with Lucy, then resumed when she rejected him. After the arrest, Larsen turns the focus back on Jimmy, asking why he was so obsessed with the killer and how much of himself he sees in Welch.
