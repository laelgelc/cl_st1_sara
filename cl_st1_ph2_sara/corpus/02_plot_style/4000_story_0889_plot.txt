A timid, mild-mannered rug salesman discovers an old bottle of liquor in his cellar. His confident neighbor, a successful shoe buyer, praises the rare drink and urges him to overcome his lifelong fearfulness by making a bold decision. Inspired, the salesman resolves to get drunk, and the two men consume the bottle before wandering through Manhattan.

When his companion leaves to avoid his wife’s anger, the intoxicated salesman finally fulfills another long-delayed ambition: taking a Turkish bath. He becomes lost among the dressing rooms and repeatedly fails to cross the steam room, but persistence eventually carries him through the ordeal. Emerging exhilarated, he believes he has conquered his timidity and resolves to demand a substantial raise.

The next morning, he boldly tells his manager that another company has offered to double his salary and gives his employer until evening to match it. Though privately terrified, he works with unusual energy while awaiting the decision. After reviewing his value to the store, the manager agrees to double his pay.
