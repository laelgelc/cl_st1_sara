In Elizabethan London, an agile young man flees two armed gentlemen through the dark streets, evading the watch and hiding in the home of his bookish friend, Wessel Caxter. The pursuers reveal that the fugitive has committed violence against their sister, but Wessel misdirects them before realizing the seriousness of his guest’s apparent sexual crime.

When confronted, the fugitive refuses to explain directly. Instead, he asks for writing materials and spends the rest of the night transforming the incident into a literary work. At dawn, exhausted, he gives Wessel a large manuscript whose opening lines identify it as Shakespeare’s poem about Tarquin and Lucrece.
