Sam Lawson recounts how Mary “Miry” Brown, a strong-willed young woman, was courted by several men but fell in love with the poor, ambitious Bill Elderkin. Her miserly father opposed the match, intercepted their letters, and demanded that Miry renounce Bill. Despite nursing her difficult father through a long illness, she refused, and he apparently disinherited her.

After his death, the will left the valuable property to Miry’s siblings and gave her only an old, cracked pitcher she had always despised. Enraged by the insulting bequest, she hurled it against a wall. The pitcher shattered, revealing hundreds of hidden gold coins. Miry thus received the largest fortune of all, invested it wisely, and married Bill after he completed his law studies. Their later prosperity illustrates Sam’s lesson that seeming misfortunes may prove to be unexpected blessings.
