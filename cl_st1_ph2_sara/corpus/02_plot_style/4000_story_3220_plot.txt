Touch-and-go Bullet-head, an irascible and stubborn editor from the East, moves to the western town of Nopolis expecting to establish its only newspaper. Discovering that John Smith already publishes the local Gazette, he launches a rival paper, the Tea-Pot, directly across the street and begins attacking Smith in print.

Smith mocks Bullet-head’s florid, O-filled writing style, claiming he cannot write a word without that vowel. Enraged, Bullet-head vows not to change his style and composes an insult-filled editorial deliberately packed with O’s. Unknown to him, however, a printer’s apprentice from the Gazette has stolen every O from the Tea-Pot’s type cases. Ordered to print the article anyway, the young typesetter replaces each O with an X.

The resulting editorial appears as a nearly unreadable mass of X’s. Believing the strange text may conceal treason, the townspeople rush to punish Bullet-head, but he has mysteriously fled and is never seen again. The public is left to debate the article’s meaning through a series of X-related puns, while the typesetter conceals his role and blames Bullet-head’s behavior on excessive consumption of XXX ale.
