Dr. James Ripley, a successful and studious country physician, is dismayed when a highly qualified rival, Dr. Verrinder Smith, opens a nearby practice—and even more outraged when he discovers that she is a woman. His prejudice turns to bitter resentment as her skill, modern methods, and willingness to perform difficult operations rapidly win over his patients.

While racing to beat her to an urgent case, Ripley crashes his carriage and suffers a compound leg fracture. Smith finds him, expertly treats the injury, and becomes his physician during his long recovery. Through their daily contact, Ripley recognizes both her professional excellence and her warmth, revises his views about women in medicine, and falls in love with her.

When he recovers, Ripley proposes marriage. Smith declines, explaining that she has chosen to devote her life to science and has accepted a position at a physiological laboratory in Paris. She leaves Hoyland, restoring Ripley’s medical monopoly but leaving him permanently saddened and uninterested in other women.
