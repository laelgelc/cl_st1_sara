The story presents three examples of wives remaining devoted to their husbands through death. Arria kills herself to encourage her condemned husband, Paetus, to face his own execution. Natalia, a Christian, supports her newly converted husband Adrian through torture and martyrdom, then preserves one of his severed hands and lives out her life as a widow.

The main account concerns Gertrude, wife of Rudolf von der Wart. Rudolf becomes involved in a conspiracy by Johann of Hapsburg against Emperor Albrecht I, although he claims he never knew the conspirators intended murder. Johann and his allies assassinate Albrecht, after which the emperor’s children—especially Agnes and Leopold—pursue brutal revenge against the conspirators and their families.

Rudolf is sentenced to be broken on the wheel and left suspended until he dies. Gertrude escapes confinement and returns to the place of execution. Despite danger, hostile commands, bad weather, and Rudolf’s pleas that she save herself, she refuses to leave him. For two nights and days she prays beneath the wheel, comforts him, covers him against the cold, and brings water to his lips. Rudolf finally dies after praising her faithfulness unto death. Gertrude then enters a convent, where she spends the rest of her life in prayer and charitable works.
