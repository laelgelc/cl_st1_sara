In rural West Issacshire, the elderly, eccentric Mr. Icky reflects with young Peter on his criminal past, his supposed rejuvenation through transplanted glands, and the departure of his daughter Ulsa for London. Ulsa returns, followed by her wealthy suitor, Rodney Divine. Their romantic quarrel—over whether boxer Jack Dempsey or Frank Gotch would win a fight—ends when Divine concedes, restoring their engagement.

Mr. Icky’s son Charles then announces that he is leaving for a life at sea. Soon, dozens of Mr. Icky’s other children also emerge, declaring their intention to abandon farm life for modern excitement, travel, and jazz. Mr. Icky tries to defend rural tradition and persuade them to stay by reading random passages from a damp Bible, but they ridicule him and depart.

Left alone and despondent, Mr. Icky lies down outside the cottage and is covered by moths. Peter returns and tenderly places a mothball on him as a final gift of affection, frightening the moths away.
