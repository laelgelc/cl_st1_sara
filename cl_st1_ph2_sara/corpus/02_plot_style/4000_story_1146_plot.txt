After serving a prison sentence for embezzlement, former bank cashier Ted Terrill returns to his hometown intending to flee his disgrace. Hotel owner Jo Haley persuades him to stay and offers him a bookkeeping job, arguing that hiding elsewhere will not erase his past. Although most townspeople shun Ted, head waitress Birdie Callahan treats him with loyalty and kindness.

When hotel funds go missing, departing bookkeeper Minnie Wenzel produces records apparently showing that Ted stole the money. Terrified that he may have repeated his crime without realizing it, Ted nearly accepts the accusation. Birdie intervenes, revealing that Minnie has been spending far beyond her salary on an extravagant wedding trousseau. Minnie confesses that she took the money and forged the evidence against Ted.

Vindicated, Ted tries to express his gratitude by asking to walk Birdie home. Though she cares deeply for him, Birdie refuses, fearing that association with a working-class waitress would give the town fresh cause to scorn him and hinder his recovery. She sends him back to work, urging him to hold his head high and make the most of his second chance.
