After an injury-plagued football season, Harvard students try to rally enthusiasm before a heavily unfavorable game against Yale. When a promised pep meeting is canceled, thousands of students spontaneously march through campus and decide they need a band. A few friends recruit McTurkle, an isolated, studious French-horn player, by persuading him that the university urgently needs his leadership.

Delighted to be wanted, McTurkle heads the procession, playing ill-suited tunes while the students sing, cheer, march to the stadium, and celebrate him as their band. He later gives an awkward speech revealing that he barely understands the upcoming contest, even mistaking football for baseball. The next day, despite his newfound prominence, he skips the game to study and practice his horn. Harvard loses, but the narrator jokingly insists that the defeat cannot be blamed on McTurkle.
