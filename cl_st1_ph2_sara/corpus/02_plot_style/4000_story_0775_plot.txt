On the last night before the narrator leaves Sandtown to teach in the countryside, he camps with five boyhood friends on a newly formed river island. Beneath the stars, they discuss distant places and ancient peoples until Tip Smith recounts a legend about an inaccessible red bluff in New Mexico. Long ago, a peaceful Indigenous community lived atop it, but a storm destroyed their only staircase while the men were away hunting. The hunters were massacred by enemies, and the villagers trapped above eventually starved. No one has climbed the bluff since.

Tip declares that he has devised a way to scale it, and the boys promise that whoever reaches it first will tell the others what he finds. They renew this pledge at Christmas, but twenty years pass and none fulfills it. Adulthood confines them through work, injury, habit, family responsibilities, and death. Yet Tip continues to dream of the journey and passes the story—and the longing for adventure—on to his young son.
