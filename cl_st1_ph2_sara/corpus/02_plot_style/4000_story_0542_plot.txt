Gilligren, a mistreated orphan, leaves his cruel aunt and uncle with only sixpence, hoping to make his fortune in London during the new king’s coronation. A dishonest farmer tricks him into exchanging the coin for a sack containing only a handful of rye. Gilligren scatters the rye before sleeping, then awakens to find blackbirds eating it. Using the sack, he captures twenty-four birds and carries them to London.

At the royal kitchen, Gilligren learns that the king has ordered a blackbird pie, but he refuses to let the cook kill the birds. Instead, he proposes hiding them alive inside a large pie, on condition that he receive a position in the royal household. When the pie is opened at dinner, the birds emerge singing and flying, delighting the king and his court. Impressed by Gilligren’s cleverness, the king appoints him as a personal page. Gilligren serves faithfully, eventually gaining honor and wealth, thereby fulfilling his claim that sixpence could make his fortune.
