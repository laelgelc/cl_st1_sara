In a future industrial oligarchy, generations of the Vanderwater family rule factories and an enslaved, deliberately illiterate working class. A clandestine storyteller explains that the dynasty began with Bill Vange, a slave who betrayed his own people, crushed rebellions for the ruling class, and amassed great wealth. His children were eventually admitted into the master class and adopted the name Vanderwater.

Centuries later, workers at Roger Vanderwater’s textile factory, “Hell’s Bottom,” are exploited by overseers Joseph Clancy and Adolph Munster. The overseers steal a relief fund intended for sick and injured laborers and violently suppress complaints. After the workers petition Vanderwater, he sends their complaint back to the overseers, who have the workers beaten; the literate slave who wrote the petition is injured so badly that he begins dying.

When worker Tom Dixon loses an arm in a factory accident and is denied relief, the dying literate slave writes another petition. Disguised as a farm worker delivering Vanderwater’s prized strawberries, he enters the master’s palace and presents a tray containing the fruit, Dixon’s severed arm, and the petition clutched in its fingers. His courage forces Vanderwater to investigate. Finding the accusations true, Vanderwater brutally punishes the overseers and temporarily restores proper management of the workers’ fund.

The storyteller reveals that the brave, literate slave was his father, who secretly taught him to read before dying from his injuries. Although Vanderwater later offered him opportunities to rise within the system, he chose to become a wandering storyteller, secretly teaching slaves their history and urging them to learn to read so they can gain the knowledge and strength needed to abolish masters and slavery.
