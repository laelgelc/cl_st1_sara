A young, inexperienced writer meets the charming Mrs. Clifton Courtenay, who flatters him by professing admiration for his work and claiming that, beneath her artificial social life, she longs for a sincere friend who understands her. He is captivated and believes they share a special connection.

Soon afterward, another young author describes an almost identical encounter with her. At one of her crowded social gatherings, she asks the narrator about his friend’s writing, memorizes a striking line he quotes, and later repeats it to the friend as though she had long treasured his words.

It becomes clear that she treats many people in the same way. Clergymen of opposing denominations, a Theosophist, a conservative colonel, and a radical reformer all believe they have gained her confidence and converted her to their own beliefs. By mirroring each person’s ideas and making each feel uniquely influential, she wins universal admiration while revealing no definite convictions of her own. Despite their contradictory claims about her true loyalties, everyone agrees that she is charming.
