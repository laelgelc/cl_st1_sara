Kansas City ballplayer Billie Sheldon, mired in a slump and facing release, accuses star catcher Burns Carroll of undermining teammates. Madge Ellston, the club owner’s niece, is romantically drawn to Carroll but also cares for Billie. After learning from manager Pat Donahue how a “knocker” advances himself by belittling others and damaging team morale, she urges Billie not to quit and to regain his confidence despite his love for her.

Inspired, Billie performs brilliantly in a crucial game against Denver, hitting a spectacular home run and helping Kansas City take the lead. Carroll also proves his exceptional ability, directing the team and engineering a rare game-ending triple play. Yet afterward, when Madge tests him by praising other players, Carroll dismisses every achievement as luck or the product of his own superiority, confirming that he habitually disparages his teammates.

Madge realizes Carroll’s attraction is merely physical and chooses Billie, whose restored confidence also saves his career. Donahue withdraws Billie’s release notice and trades Carroll away despite his greatness, deciding that team harmony matters more than keeping a talented but destructive knocker.
