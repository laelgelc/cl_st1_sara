After separating from her violent, alcoholic husband, the shrewd Mrs. Mooney opens a Dublin boarding house and raises her daughter, Polly, among its young male lodgers. When Polly begins an affair with Mr. Doran, a respectable older clerk, Mrs. Mooney quietly allows the relationship to develop before confronting them.

Once Polly admits the affair, Mrs. Mooney decides that marriage is the only acceptable reparation for her daughter’s compromised reputation. She knows Doran is vulnerable to social scandal, religious guilt, the possible loss of his job, and intimidation from Polly’s protective brother. Doran, though attracted to Polly, fears marriage and suspects he has been trapped. Nevertheless, unable to face public disgrace or abandon his sense of honor, he goes downstairs to meet Mrs. Mooney. Polly waits calmly, imagining her future, until her mother calls her down because Doran wishes to speak with her, implying that he has agreed to marry her.
