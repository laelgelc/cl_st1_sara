An invalid narrator moves with his wife, three children, and servants into a seemingly modern seaside house. Soon, the occupants experience unexplained phenomena: his wife sees a towering figure beside her bed; servants repeatedly encounter a ragged, one-eyed woman who vanishes into sealed coal vaults; mysterious shadows cross walls against the laws of light; doors knock and handles turn by themselves; rhythmic hammering sounds from the roof; and the family’s horse displays terror in its stable.

The narrator and butler initially suspect thieves or smugglers using a hidden entrance, especially after both apparently see the one-eyed woman in public, but searches reveal no access point. The children unknowingly add to the mystery. One daughter sees a red-faced man prowling through the nursery as though searching for something, while another sees the apparition of a pale, dark-haired young woman with a bloody scar across her throat. The children also regularly observe the ragged woman digging near the stable wall. When they excavate that spot, they uncover part of a decayed human jaw containing sound teeth.

Despite mounting evidence, the narrator continues to assume natural causes and leaves once his health improves. Shortly before departing, an elderly visitor reveals that the supposedly new house is actually a converted government store that had stood fifty years earlier in an isolated location well suited to a secret crime. The narrator conjectures that the red-faced man may have murdered the young woman, that the one-eyed woman helped conceal her body, and that their apparitions reenact parts of the crime. He never investigates further, leaving the haunting unexplained.
