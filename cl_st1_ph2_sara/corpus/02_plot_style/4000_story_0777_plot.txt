Seven American art students gather in sculptor Lyon Hartwell’s Paris studio to bid farewell to their friend Bentley, who has been unexpectedly summoned home. Their attention falls on Hartwell’s new sculpture of a young Civil War color sergeant. Hartwell reveals that the figure was inspired by his uncle and namesake, who enlisted at fifteen and died heroically in battle.

Hartwell recounts how, though born and raised in Europe, he traveled to his family’s Pennsylvania home to care for an invalid aunt. Initially alienated by America’s harsh industrial landscape and unable to feel any connection to his ancestral house, he became fascinated by his uncle’s portrait and story. He learned that the boy had continued carrying the regiment’s flag after losing one arm to a shell, only to be killed when another shell tore away the other.

On Memorial Day, Hartwell discovers a trunk containing his uncle’s childhood possessions. In a schoolbook he finds a crude drawing of the American flag accompanied by lines from the national anthem. The intimate trace of the boy suddenly makes Hartwell feel that he truly knows him. Sitting beneath a tree his uncle planted and hearing the flag move overhead, Hartwell experiences a profound sense of kinship, national identity, and rootedness that transforms his understanding of himself and later inspires his art.

After Hartwell finishes, the concierge announces that Bentley’s cab has arrived. The friends silently accompany Bentley outside and watch him depart across Paris, Hartwell’s story having given deeper significance to his journey home.
