Henry Floyd, an eccentric but principled writer and inventor, endures poverty, an early romantic betrayal, and the sudden death of the woman who restored his capacity for love. Grief turns him into a lonely recluse, though he remains generous, supports others quietly, befriends children, and gives his inventions freely for the public good.

Years later, Floyd meets the beautiful Sarah Dangerlie and is revived by what seems to be her sympathy and shared values. Trusting her completely, he falls in love and dismisses rumors that she is engaged to the wealthy young Router after she herself appears to deny them. Preparing to visit her with roses from his mother’s old garden, Floyd receives a letter and then discovers a newspaper announcement revealing that Sarah and Router have long been secretly engaged and are about to marry.

Devastated by another betrayal, Floyd writes Sarah that he does not hold her accountable. He then gives the roses and money intended for her to a destitute woman on the street, urging her to go home for the night, and walks away alone.
