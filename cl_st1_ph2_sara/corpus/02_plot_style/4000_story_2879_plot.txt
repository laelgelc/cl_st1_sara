After escaping the Atlantic Doldrums, Captain Charles Pitkin decides to revive the traditional equator-crossing ceremony aboard the barkentine Mohawk, chiefly to initiate three inexperienced sailors—Christian, Oscar, and Josef. He appoints the large, bearded carpenter as Neptune and has the clever sailor Mike play Neptune’s mermaid daughter. Without permission, the captain takes an old skirt, a cherished red dressing jacket, and a green veil from his sister Rose’s cabin for Mike’s costume.

During the ceremony, the disguised pair question and humiliate the three novices. Christian is smeared with tar, dumped into a tub, hoisted from a yardarm, and repeatedly ducked into the sea. Terrified by what he sees, Oscar escapes up the rigging, while Josef is captured and begins undergoing the same treatment.

Rose, already upset because the ship’s cat killed her canary, comes on deck and recognizes her clothes on the “mermaid.” Believing a sailor has broken into her cabin, she angrily disperses the crew, strikes and dethrones Neptune, and chases Mike into the rigging. Mike reveals that the captain supplied the clothes, so Rose confronts her brother. He confesses, apologizes, and promises to replace the garments in Buenos Aires. After reminding him how dependent he has become on her management of the ship’s domestic affairs, Rose relents and forgives him.

The ceremony is dismantled. Christian is relieved to have survived, Josef is grateful that his initiation was interrupted, and Oscar regards Rose as his deliverer. The Mohawk crosses the Equator that afternoon, leaving Oscar and Josef technically uninitiated.
