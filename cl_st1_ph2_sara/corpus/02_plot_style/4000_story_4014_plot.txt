An aged knight, the last member of a mystical fellowship devoted to dying in worthy causes, encounters peasants pursuing thieves who have stolen a pious old man’s pigs. He persuades the reluctant peasants to fight by promising payment for every thief killed and leads them against a much larger band. The pigs are recovered, but the knight suffers mortal wounds while shielding the peasants.

Later, a simple-minded boy returns, collects the promised bounty for the dead thieves, and tends the knight. Asked why he risked his life over so small a matter, the knight explains that his fellowship served the divine Rose by defending good causes without deliberately seeking death, leaving the manner and time of their deaths to God. Having long feared that he would never find a worthy end, he rejoices that his quest is finally complete and dies singing.

The boy begins digging the knight’s grave, but at dawn he hears a cock crow in the valley. Obsessed with collecting cocks for fights, he abandons the nearly finished grave and runs off after the bird.
