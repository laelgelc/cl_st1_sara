Mail contractor Harry Chatswood gives impoverished Old Jack a lift and lends him fourteen shillings to repay a drinking debt to Mother Mac, who secretly sells liquor from her isolated bush hut. When they stop there with a reserved commercial traveller, Harry mischievously tells Mother Mac that the traveller is an excise officer. Alarmed, she vehemently denies keeping alcohol and rebuffs the traveller’s attempts to buy a drink.

Harry then prompts Jack to repay his debt, but Mother Mac, determined not to incriminate herself, denies ever selling him liquor and angrily drives him out as though he has slandered her. After the coach departs, she regrets her harshness toward Jack. The next morning, a rider reveals that the passenger was merely a commercial traveller, not an exciseman. Realizing Harry tricked her, cost her several sales, and caused her to mistreat Jack, Mother Mac vows revenge. Jack never returns with the money, but she eventually succeeds in getting even with Harry.
