With most able-bodied men away at war, Major Tom Boldin becomes the village’s self-appointed protector and adviser. When an excited boy reports seeing a fearsome rebel hiding in nearby woods, the major seizes an old rifle and sets out to capture him, imagining the gratitude and glory he will earn.

Old Peter Witheby follows, while the village women and boys trail behind at a distance, increasingly frightened by the cornfield, woods, and ordinary sounds around them. The major and Peter advance nervously, each reluctant to face the supposed enemy. Eventually, the major enters the woods alone while Peter retreats to summon help and obtain a gun.

The villagers wait in suspense until the major comes rushing back. He announces that the “rebel” is merely Milton Jacoby, a local man lying drunk in the woods. Their excitement turning to disappointment, the villagers mock the major for not recognizing the truth sooner.
