An awkward, ill-tempered chemist uses an inheritance to buy an overpriced suburban pharmacy that also serves as a post office, expecting postal traffic to attract customers. Instead, most visitors want only postal services, while his poor manners, frequent outbursts, and mismanagement alienate the neighborhood and ruin his reputation.

His frustration peaks when a demanding postal customer interrupts him as he prepares medicine for a wealthy household. After angrily driving her away, he fears he has used the wrong ingredient and rushes to recover the delivered prescription. Although the medicine proves correct, his panicked behavior and false claim that an assistant made the mistake inspire gossip that he is dangerously incompetent. Forced out of business, he retreats to a large town, exhausts the remains of his fortune while seeking work, and dies after illness brought on by poverty and exposure.
