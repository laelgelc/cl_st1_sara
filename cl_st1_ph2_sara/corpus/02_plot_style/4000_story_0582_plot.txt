While mining with a companion, Bill recalls how prospecting fever once led him into the Arizona wilderness with his eccentric partner, Kink Martin. Traumatized by witnessing his parents’ murder by Indigenous attackers as a child, Kink harbors a violent hatred of Native Americans and shoots two peaceful reservation men. When one apparently escapes to alert the nearby fort, Kink flees toward Mexico, while Bill stays behind and pretends to be mute so he cannot betray his partner.

Soldiers arrest Bill and take him to the fort, where he stubbornly maintains his silence despite interrogation. His deception collapses when an incompetent teamster mishandles six mules. Unable to restrain himself, Bill erupts in elaborate profanity, takes command of the team, and skillfully drives it to safety. The colonel recognizes Bill’s distinctive talent: years earlier, Bill had rescued him during an Apache ambush by performing a similarly daring feat with an army wagon.

Bill confesses the truth but explains that loyalty compelled him to give Kink time to escape, recalling how Kink had repeatedly risked his life for him. Sympathetic but unable to clear Bill now that everyone knows he can speak, the colonel quietly points out his horse and advises him to flee. Bill escapes across the border, later selling the exhausted horse.
