A woman with a notorious past leaves the town brothel she owns and buys a cottage in a respectable neighborhood, hoping to live openly and decently among ordinary families. Although she improves her home, keeps house, gardens, dresses more modestly, and even joins a church, the townspeople refuse to accept her. Neighbors avoid her, a woman publicly abandons the pew beside her, and a young mother prevents her child, Snooky, from accepting cookies from her.

During a violent winter storm, Snooky becomes dangerously ill with croup while her father is away and no doctor can be reached. The terrified mother seeks the woman’s help. Drawing on the painful experience of having lost her own baby, the outcast woman takes charge, treats the child, and stays through the night until Snooky is safe.

Despite this act, Snooky’s father orders his wife never to speak to their neighbor again. When the couple later passes her without acknowledgment, the woman realizes that even saving their child has not earned her basic human acceptance. Bitterly defeated, she abandons the cottage and returns to her former establishment, while the self-righteous townspeople cite her failure as proof that women like her can never change.
