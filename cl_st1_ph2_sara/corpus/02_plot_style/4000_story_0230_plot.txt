A vain beetle living in the Emperor’s stable becomes indignant when the Emperor’s heroic horse receives golden shoes while he does not. Convinced that this is a personal insult, he leaves to seek his fortune. During his travels, he scorns the beauty and contentment others find in gardens, sunshine, rain, family life, and simple comforts, continually boasting of his royal origins and supposed importance.

After enduring a storm, sheltering among earwigs, and finding fellowship with other beetles in a muddy ditch, he hastily marries a young beetle but abandons her when responsibility arises. He then escapes human collectors, enjoys temporary refuge in a hothouse, and is captured by boys who tie him to the mast of a toy boat and leave him drifting on a lake. Believing himself doomed and unfairly treated by the world, he is eventually rescued and freed by a group of girls.

The beetle flies through an open window and lands back in the royal stable on the mane of the Emperor’s horse. Rather than recognizing his vanity or the horse’s merit, he concludes that the horse received golden shoes on his account. Pleased by this self-serving interpretation, he decides that travel has broadened his mind and that the world is not so bad after all.
