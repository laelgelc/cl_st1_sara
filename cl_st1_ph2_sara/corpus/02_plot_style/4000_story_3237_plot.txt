Miss Carstair, an impoverished young woman being steered by her wealthy aunt into marrying Lord Eckhart, struggles with the decision. She does not know whether she loves him, but believes he sold all his possessions during five dissolute-seeming years in India to buy her a priceless ruby necklace. His apparent sacrifice appeals to the romantic idealism she inherited from her father, Major Carstair, who vanished while searching the Gobi Desert for a powerful anesthetic that could relieve humanity’s pain.

Late one night, a Tibetan emissary, Dr. Tsan-Sgam, visits her with an ivory image of the monument erected over her father’s recovered body. He explains that Major Carstair had earlier saved the Dalai Lama’s life through a blood transfusion, after which he entered the Gobi despite warnings and died pursuing his hoped-for medical discovery.

Miss Carstair asks the emissary about Lord Eckhart and reveals that she feels obligated to honor his supposed sacrifice by marrying him. Dr. Tsan-Sgam then discloses that Eckhart never bought the rubies: the necklace was a gift from the grateful Dalai Lama to Miss Carstair, merely entrusted to Eckhart for delivery. The revelation destroys the idealized basis on which she had been considering the marriage.
