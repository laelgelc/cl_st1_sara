During World War I, elderly Norfolk farmworker Sam Gates is irritated by his niece’s daily request for news, believing nothing noteworthy ever happens to him. While thinning turnips, he sees a German aeroplane crash-land in a field. The pilot, struck by Sam’s uncanny resemblance to the notorious German spy Paul Jouperts, abducts him at gunpoint and flies him to German-held territory.

German officers plan to kill Sam and leave his body in an abandoned trench so the British will believe Jouperts is dead, allowing the real spy to continue working undetected as a gardener near British headquarters. Before Sam can be shot, an explosion kills his guard and knocks him unconscious. British soldiers find him and, also mistaking him for Jouperts, take him into custody.

Sam proves his identity with local details and recalls the Germans revealing the real spy’s location. The British arrest Jouperts, recognize Sam’s valuable service, and arrange to fly him home. He returns to the turnip field before tea and resumes work. When his niece again asks whether there is any news, Sam angrily insists that nothing ever happens to an ordinary man like him.
