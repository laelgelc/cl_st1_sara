Shenah Pessah, a young Jewish immigrant trapped in unpaid janitorial drudgery for her abusive uncle, sustains herself with memories of a compassionate professor who once treated her as an equal and briefly kissed her. After rebelling against her uncle, she leaves home, finds factory work, and is sheltered by a coworker. Her skill quickly earns her advancement and strengthens her determination to educate and transform herself in America.

Sam Arkin, a kind, self-made fellow worker, falls deeply in love with her and offers her his savings, marriage, and material security. Although moved by his devotion, Shenah refuses. She remains devoted both to her idealized love for the professor—who represents learning and a higher life—and to her need to rise through her own efforts rather than through a husband’s money. Her rejection devastates Sam, but she urges him to draw on his own strength and turn his suffering into the ambition to become someone significant in America.
