A civil engineer staying at a remote tavern becomes intrigued by Silas Jaffrey, an eccentric elderly boarder who never proposed to Mehetabel, the woman he loved, before she died. Jaffrey imagines the life they might have shared, including a son named Andy. Night after night, he describes the nonexistent boy’s rapid growth, illnesses, education, mischief, and future so convincingly that the narrator begins to think of Andy as real.

During a violent storm, Jaffrey announces that eleven-year-old Andy has fallen from a ladder and broken his neck. By the next morning, however, Jaffrey is cheerful and says nothing of him. As the narrator departs, the landlord reveals that Jaffrey repeatedly invents the same imaginary son, always kills him in an accident at age eleven, and begins the entire fantasy again whenever he finds a new listener.
