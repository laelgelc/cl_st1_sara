Captain Gilbert, a commanding naval hero, dominates his devoted wife Fanny through force of will and passionate possessiveness. Before dying, he warns that she must never remarry and vows to return even from the farthest star to claim her.

Widowed and initially poor, Fanny considers several proposals but rejects each after vivid dreams or visitations revive Gilbert’s hold over her. Years later she falls genuinely in love with John Mowbray, whose strong will and protective nature rival Gilbert’s. After accepting him, she again dreams that Gilbert has returned. She tries to withdraw, but Mowbray persuades her that her loyalty to her dead husband need not prevent a new earthly marriage, and they wed.

Travel and married happiness briefly transform Fanny, but she gradually becomes restless, fearful, and physically frail. She finally confesses that Gilbert visits her every night and that she feels divided: Mowbray’s by day, Gilbert’s by night. Convinced that the dead captain will eventually take her, she wastes away.

While Mowbray holds her, Fanny senses Gilbert’s arrival and dies. Mowbray perceives the captain claiming her spirit, declaring that his unconquerable will has prevailed. Mowbray defies him and insists that Fanny loved him best, but sees Gilbert disappear into an intense light with Fanny beside him, leaving Mowbray alone in darkness.
