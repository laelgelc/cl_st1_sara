After King Albert of Hungary dies in 1439, the nobles plan to offer the throne—and the widowed Queen Elizabeth’s hand—to the King of Poland. But Elizabeth, expecting a child, hopes to preserve the crown for a possible son. Because only a ruler crowned with the sacred Crown of St. Stephen is considered legitimate, she secretly orders her loyal attendant, Helen Kottenner, to steal it from the heavily guarded vaults of Vissegrad Castle.

With a courageous accomplice, Helen enters the vault at night, breaks through its locks and chains, and conceals the crown inside a velvet cushion. She transports it toward the queen, narrowly escaping discovery and surviving a dangerous crossing of the thawing Danube. Upon reaching Komorn, she learns that Elizabeth has just given birth to a son, Ladislas.

As the Polish king advances to claim Hungary, Elizabeth decides to have her infant crowned before her opponents can act. Helen makes coronation robes for the child and hides the crown beneath his cradle mattress during the perilous journey to Weissenburg. Despite fears of attack, severe weather, and the baby’s constant crying, the party reaches the loyal city safely.

At twelve weeks old, Ladislas is anointed, knighted, and crowned with St. Stephen’s Crown while Helen holds him through the ceremony. His mother then takes him to Austria for safety. The tale presents Helen’s dangerous theft and steadfast service as an extraordinary act of loyalty, courage, and faith, while leaving the justice of the queen’s political scheme open to question.
