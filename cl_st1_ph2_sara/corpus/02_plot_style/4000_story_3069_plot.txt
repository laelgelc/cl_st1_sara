Three sisters cultivate a newly wealthy aunt, hoping to inherit much of her fortune, but resent her intention to leave a substantial share to Roger, a reckless, gambling nephew who had treated her kindly when she was poor. To expose Roger’s supposed depravity, the youngest sister, Veronique, proposes taking their aunt to a French resort where she can witness him gambling.

The plan backfires when the aunt becomes fascinated by casino games. After an initial win, she gambles increasingly large sums, sells investments to cover her losses, and discovers a taste for extravagant living and lively company. Rather than being disillusioned with Roger, she bonds with him and embraces the pleasures her nieces had tried to keep from her.

Unable to restrain her, the sisters gradually return home, bitterly watching their anticipated inheritance disappear. Their aunt becomes a confirmed gambler, living on what remains of her income, while the sisters are left visibly depressed—especially Veronique, whose scheme caused the disaster.
