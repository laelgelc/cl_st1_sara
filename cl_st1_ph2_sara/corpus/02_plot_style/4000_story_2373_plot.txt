On the anniversary of a supposed friend’s death, the laundromat owner Ah-Yen tells the narrator how he constructed the life story of customer Fifty-Six entirely from his weekly laundry. From changing clothes and stains, Ah-Yen infers that the unseen young man attended university, studied law, fell in love, became engaged, quarreled with his sweetheart, was abandoned, descended into despair, and finally shot himself. Believing a bloodstained, bullet-pierced shirt proves the suicide, Ah-Yen draws an imaginary portrait to memorialize the customer he never met.

After leaving, the narrator realizes that he himself was Fifty-Six. The long absence merely reflected a trip, the unusually large final bundle was accumulated laundry, and the supposedly fatal shirt had been stained by red ink and burned by cigar ash. Unwilling to destroy Ah-Yen’s cherished tragedy, the narrator keeps the truth to himself.
