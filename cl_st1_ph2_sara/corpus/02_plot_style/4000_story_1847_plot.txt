In a letter to an editor, Geoffrey Crayon argues that names strongly shape how people and places are perceived. He criticizes America’s borrowed European, classical, political, and vulgar place-names, advocating the restoration of original Indigenous names such as Neperan and Pocantico. He proposes that historical and literary societies coordinate a nationwide reform of geographical nomenclature, including removing the prefix “New” from cities and states. He suggests renaming New York State “Ontario” or “Mohegan” and New York City “Manhattan.” Finally, he calls for a distinctive national name, proposing “Alleghania” for the United States and “Alleghanian” for its citizens, believing such a name would strengthen national identity and unity.
