A young Tsar, exhausted after weeks of official duties and decrees, falls asleep on Christmas Eve. In a dream, a mysterious guide shows him the suffering caused in his name: brutal prisons, lethal customs enforcement, state-sponsored drunkenness, unjust trials, crushing taxes, religious persecution, corruption, military abuse, poverty, exile, torture, and execution. The Tsar protests that he desires only his people’s welfare, but realizes that his authority makes him responsible for the state’s actions.

After awakening within the dream, he recounts the vision to his wife and an old courtier. The courtier dismisses his fears, insisting that the existing order is sound and that the Tsar’s duty is to preserve his power. His wife advises delegating much of that power to representatives of the people. Meanwhile, the guide’s voice urges him to think beyond his role as ruler and fulfill his deeper moral duty as a human being before God.

The Tsar then truly awakens beside his wife. Whether he preserves autocracy, shares power, or follows the demands of his conscience is left unresolved, to be revealed only by the future.
