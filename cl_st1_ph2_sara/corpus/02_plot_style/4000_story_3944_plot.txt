On the eve of his coronation, a sixteen-year-old heir raised by a poor goatherd delights in the magnificent robe, jeweled crown, and pearl-studded sceptre prepared for him. During the night, however, he has three dreams revealing the suffering behind these treasures: starving laborers weave his golden robe, an enslaved diver dies retrieving the finest pearl for his sceptre, and countless miners perish while seeking rubies for his crown.

Horrified, the young King refuses the regalia. He dresses instead in his old shepherd’s tunic and cloak, carries his staff, and makes a crown from wild briar. Courtiers, commoners, soldiers, and even the Bishop mock or oppose him, arguing that exploitation and misery are unavoidable and that royal splendor sustains society. Nevertheless, he enters the cathedral and prays before the image of Christ.

When armed nobles arrive intending to kill him for disgracing the kingdom, a miracle occurs. Sunlight clothes him in a robe more beautiful than gold, his staff blossoms with lilies whiter than pearls, and his thorn crown blooms with roses redder than rubies. Recognizing that a power greater than the Bishop has crowned him, everyone kneels in homage. The young King returns to the palace transformed, his face shining like that of an angel.
