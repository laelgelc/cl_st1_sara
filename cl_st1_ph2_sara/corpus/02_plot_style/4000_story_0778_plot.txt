Aaron Dunlap, a compassionate American portrait painter in Paris, is commissioned to paint Virginia Gilbert, a beautiful young woman whose face is partly disfigured by a severe burn. Admiring what he believes to be her courage and hidden sensitivity, he falls in love with and marries her, expecting that she will eventually confide in him about her scar.

Instead, Virginia proves vain, pleasure-seeking, and obsessed with physical beauty. She rejects their frail, unattractive daughter, while Dunlap grows increasingly disillusioned and comes to see her scar as reflecting a deeper emotional deformity. Their daughter finds affection in Virginia’s kind cousin, Eleanor Vane, and Dunlap also develops feelings for Eleanor.

During an argument, Dunlap finally mentions Virginia’s scar, shattering the family’s long-maintained pretense that it does not exist. Virginia abruptly leaves for Nice. That evening, Eleanor’s alcohol lamp mysteriously explodes, badly burning her face as she saves the child from the resulting fire; the circumstances suggest Virginia may have tampered with it. Virginia later goes abroad, divorces Dunlap, and remains famous for her extravagant clothes. After the divorce, Dunlap marries Eleanor, leaving society to note only the cruel irony that both of his wives bore facial scars.
