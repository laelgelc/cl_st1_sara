The narrator recalls childhood visits to the elderly Anne and Susan Baily at Lough Guir, where Anne shared supernatural traditions surrounding the family’s ancient castle and lake.

According to legend, the magician Earl of Desmond once demonstrated his powers to his curious young wife after warning her that she must remain silent through a series of terrifying transformations. When she screamed, the castle, its inhabitants, and the earl’s household sank beneath the lake under an enchantment. Every seven years, the earl and his spectral retinue emerge at night. He can be freed only when the silver shoes of his white horse wear through, and he attempts to trick witnesses into speaking to him. A smith named Teigue O’Neill encounters the cavalcade and narrowly avoids the trap, while a servant, Moll Rial, nearly accepts a ring from the disguised earl before seeing the lake turn blood-red and invoking a sacred name, causing him to vanish.

The Baily family also experiences other omens and apparitions. Anne and Susan hear mysterious, mournful music near the castle while watching over their dying sister, interpreting it as the family banshee. Their governess repeatedly dreams of a man who gives precise directions to buried treasure. An excavation eventually uncovers an empty stone chamber bearing evidence that a vessel had once stood there, leading the family to believe someone else had already removed the treasure.

Later, while sitting alone in the castle’s gloomy Earl’s Hall, the governess encounters a sinister little man dressed in red who silently commands her to follow him. Terrified, she obeys as far as the doorway, then collapses. Convinced the apparition foretells her death, she falls ill with fever and dies soon afterward.
