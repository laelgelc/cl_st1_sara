A proud Shanghai hen worries because one of her twelve chicks remains thin while the others grow plump. She eventually discovers that he refuses to eat gravel, believing it tastes bad and that he is old enough to ignore her advice. She explains that, because chickens have no teeth, gravel is needed in the gizzard to grind grain and help the body gain strength, but he stubbornly refuses.

As the chick grows weaker and is mocked by the other fowls, he secretly begins eating gravel. He soon feels better and gains weight, yet remains unhappy because he has not admitted his mistake to his mother. When he hears his brother resist eating gravel, he finally confesses his foolishness and advises him that avoiding unpleasant but necessary duties only causes greater hardship. Now healthy, wiser, and admired by a Dorking chick who once laughed at him, he makes his mother proud and happy.
