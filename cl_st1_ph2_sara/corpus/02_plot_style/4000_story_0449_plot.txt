A wealthy goldsmith forces his beautiful daughter to marry Feron, an old, repulsive advocate, in exchange for an estate. She openly warns Feron that she despises him and will never be faithful, but he assumes she is merely being coy. Before the wedding, she attracts the king’s attention and receives his dagger, which she uses on her wedding night to prevent her husband from consummating the marriage.

The next morning she leaves Feron and becomes the king’s mistress. She gains enormous influence and wealth by accepting estates and money from other noblemen in exchange for secret favors, while her abandoned husband remains obsessed with possessing her. When Feron learns that a courtier has arranged to spend a night with her, he bribes her maid with twelve thousand crowns to dismiss the courtier and secretly substitute him.

The maid reveals the scheme to her mistress, who turns it against Feron. She has the maid take her place in the darkened bedchamber, while Feron believes he is finally sleeping with his wife. Near dawn, his wife slips into the bed beside him, preserving the deception. Feron leaves triumphantly, though a dark hair he plucked during the night briefly makes him suspicious; the maid persuades him that it merely changed color after being removed.

When the humiliating trick becomes public, Feron dies of shame, having been the only man unable to enjoy his own wife. She later leaves the king, marries a young count, and recounts the episode with amusement, insisting she never had to endure her first husband.
