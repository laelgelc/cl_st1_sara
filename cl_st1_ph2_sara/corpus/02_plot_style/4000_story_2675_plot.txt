A married couple miss their train while traveling abroad, and the wife relentlessly blames her passive, long-suffering husband for every inconvenience. During a carriage ride to catch another train, she complains about the dust, the driver, begging children, her husband’s smoking, and the rough road. When her treasured parasol falls from the carriage, she walks back alone to retrieve it, saying she needs to escape him before she goes mad.

Left behind, the husband sinks into an intense moment of stillness. The sight of a magnificent tree and the sound of an unseen woman singing awaken in him a profound sense of peace and release from his wife’s oppressive presence. Later, aboard the night train, he stands alone in the corridor while his wife talks incessantly to another passenger. Rather than return to her, he remains outside the compartment, sustained by his newfound inner happiness and wishing the moment could last forever.
