Robinson Asbury rises from bootblack to successful barber and influential political leader in Cadgers’s Black district. After becoming a lawyer, he remains among his community, strengthening his popularity while provoking the jealousy of rival Black attorneys Bingo and Latchett. Their attempt to displace him through a rival faction fails when Asbury overwhelms them with a much larger Emancipation Day parade.

Asbury later trusts Bingo enough to involve him in corrupt election dealings. When reform fever follows the party’s narrow victory, party leaders make Asbury their scapegoat, and Bingo supplies damaging testimony. Convicted, Asbury publicly exposes the corruption of nearly everyone involved, though he spares Judge Davis out of friendship. He serves ten months and returns as a widely pitied martyr, quietly opening a newsstand and claiming to be finished with politics.

Secretly, Asbury retains control of his supporters. He pretends to help Bingo defeat school principal Isaac Morton in another Emancipation Day contest, then redirects Bingo’s entire organization to Morton, destroying Bingo’s standing and career. When the political establishment tries to appease him, Asbury repeatedly insists that, as an ex-convict, he is not in politics. Yet on election day he discreetly mobilizes the Black electorate to vote the ruling party out of office, sparing only Judge Davis. Though he continues denying any political role, Cadgers finally recognizes the extent of his power.
