During the Civil War, a Union cavalry company drives Confederate sharpshooters from a grove and discovers a secluded house. Suspecting enemy soldiers are hiding inside, the captain enters with several men and confronts a terrified young woman who denies anyone else is present. She has secretly taken her grandfather’s unloaded pistol to prevent him from attacking the soldiers and being killed.

The search reveals her wounded Confederate brother upstairs. Though the young man insults and challenges the captain, the girl’s pleas persuade the officer to spare him. Her furious grandfather then accuses Union troops of pillaging his property, but the captain orders his men to withdraw without further searching or harming anyone.

Before leaving, the captain and the girl develop a shy mutual attraction. He asks whether they might ever meet again after the fighting, and she cautiously suggests that she sometimes walks near an old oak tree when no troops are nearby. She immediately retracts the admission in alarm, then, as rain begins to fall and the disappointed captain prepares to leave, quietly concedes that she might occasionally go there after all.
