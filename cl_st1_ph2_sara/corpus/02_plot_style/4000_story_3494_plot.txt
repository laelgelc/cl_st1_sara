On a remote island, the Kingdom of Cards lives under rigid rules that determine every citizen’s rank, place, and behavior. Its inhabitants never think or choose for themselves, and their society remains orderly but lifeless.

Across the sea, a young prince leaves his sorrowful, exiled mother to seek his fortune with the sons of a merchant and a kotwal. After years of gathering riches, a tempest destroys their ships and casts the three companions onto the Island of Cards. Their arrival creates confusion because the island’s laws cannot classify them. Ignoring the endless debate, the hungry strangers eat freely, violate regulations, and laugh at the Cards’ mechanical behavior.

The prince decides to awaken the islanders to life. When challenged, the companions explain that they act according to their own wish. Gradually, the Cards discover personal choice, begin moving and behaving differently, and abandon their fixed roles. Individuality brings confusion but also emotion, ambition, jealousy, beauty, music, and love.

The Queen of Hearts falls in love with the prince and repeatedly leaves her assigned place to stand beside him. As desire spreads throughout the kingdom, the prince arranges a ceremony in which she may freely choose her mate. She places a jasmine garland around his neck, choosing him before the entire assembly. The islanders celebrate, crown the prince and the Queen of Hearts, and welcome the prince’s mother to their new kingdom. Thereafter, the citizens live not by inflexible rules but according to their own wishes, capable of being good, bad, or both.
