Three historical episodes celebrate sons who risk everything for their fathers. At the Battle of Ticinus, seventeen-year-old Scipio rescues his wounded father from the collapsing Roman army and carries him safely back to camp.

During the English Civil War, the Earl of Lindsay resigns as commander after his cautious strategy is rejected before the Battle of Edgehill. Fighting with his own regiment, he is mortally wounded. His son, Lord Willoughby, forces his way through enemy soldiers, surrenders himself, and remains beside his father through the freezing night, though Lindsay ultimately dies from blood loss.

At the Battle of the Nile, the ten-year-old son of the French captain Casabianca remains at his assigned post aboard the burning flagship L’Orient because his wounded father has not ordered him to leave. Refusing rescue, the boy attempts to save his father by tying him to a spar and swimming with him after the ship explodes. Both disappear amid the wreckage, and the child’s loyalty is honored as the battle’s noblest act.
