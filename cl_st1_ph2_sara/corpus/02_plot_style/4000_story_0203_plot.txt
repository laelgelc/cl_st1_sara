On her twelfth birthday, poor but kindhearted Marjorie dreams of a fairy tale in which a girl receives a lucky penny, a charm for happiness, and a prince who takes her to a loving kingdom. Her own day soon mirrors the tale. After she helps a lame old man water his pony, he rewards her with a gold dollar for her new savings bank, encouraging her industrious plans to earn money and help her grandmother.

Later, Marjorie retrieves a lady artist’s windblown hat. The woman gives her a beautiful painting of her humble home and teaches her to seek the sunny side of ordinary life, inspiring greater cheerfulness and gratitude. Finally, Marjorie’s friend Billy arrives with local children for a surprise picnic. They carry her off in a decorated wagon, crown her queen, and celebrate her birthday with games, food, and affection.

That night, Marjorie recognizes that her experiences resembled the fairy tale, but she is glad to return to her grandmother and beloved home. Her three true gifts are the guiding virtues of industry, cheerfulness, and love.
