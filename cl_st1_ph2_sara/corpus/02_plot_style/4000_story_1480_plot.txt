In New York, sixty-five-year-old Mr. Ellenwood prepares to marry Mrs. Dabney, his first love, after forty years of separation and two intervening marriages on her part. She has become wealthy, worldly, and determined to disguise her age, while he has led a lonely, aimless life haunted by their broken engagement.

As the lavishly dressed bride enters the church, a bell begins tolling like a funeral knell. A hearse arrives outside, and a procession of elderly mourners enters, followed by Ellenwood dressed in a burial shroud. He declares that their wedding should resemble a funeral because other men received her youth and affection, leaving him only her old age and approaching death. He accuses her of having destroyed his hopes when they were young and summons her to marry him at the threshold of the grave.

Shocked into abandoning her vanity, the widow admits that her life has been empty and insists that her renewed love is sincere. Ellenwood’s bitterness gives way to tenderness, and they forgive each other. They marry as lovers reunited at the end of life, viewing their union as one meant for eternity rather than earthly happiness. As the ceremony concludes, the church organ rises above the funeral bell, transforming the mournful spectacle into a solemn spiritual triumph.
