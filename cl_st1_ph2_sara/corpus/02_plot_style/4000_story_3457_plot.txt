On a stormy winter evening in Oldtown, two boys persuade the local storyteller, Sam Lawson, to recount a favorite supernatural tale. Sam tells of Jehiel Lommedieu, a traveling peddler who mysteriously vanished after promising to return before Thanksgiving.

Years later, during a fierce snowstorm, Captain Eb Sawin loses his way and takes shelter at the isolated mill of Cack Sparrock, a drunken recluse. Around midnight, Ketury, an Indigenous woman reputed to be a witch, arrives despite the impassable snow. Staring up the chimney, she repeatedly commands, “Come down,” causing a human figure to descend piece by piece—feet, legs, torso, arms, and finally a head. The completed apparition is recognized as the missing Lommedieu.

The ghost points toward the chimney before vanishing with Ketury in a violent blast of wind. Terrified, Cack confesses that his father murdered Lommedieu for his money and that Cack helped conceal the body inside the chimney. Cack soon dies after giving a formal deposition and asking that the mill be demolished. When the chimney is pulled down, Lommedieu’s skeleton is found, proving the crime.

After Sam finishes, Aunt Lois dismisses the apparition as a drunken dream, while the boys’ grandmother defends the story. Sam humorously recommends keeping a “happy medium” between truth and falsehood, then hints that skeptical Aunt Lois has a mysterious story of her own.
