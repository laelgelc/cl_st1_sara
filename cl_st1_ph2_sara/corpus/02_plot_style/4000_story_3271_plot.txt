On a ship returning from South Africa to England, wealthy first-class passengers remain largely indifferent to the impoverished emigrants traveling below deck. Among the latter is a consumptive London baker who emigrated with his pregnant wife in hopes that the climate would restore his health. Unable to find suitable work and knowing he is likely returning home to die, he nevertheless takes comfort in having employment waiting for him and in the future of his healthy infant son.

As the ship approaches England, a millionaire excitedly wins a wager over the moment the Eddystone light appears. For the baker, however, the sight carries far deeper meaning. He rushes below, returns with his baby, and tearfully holds the child toward the distant light, repeatedly urging him to look at England—the home and inheritance to which the father is bringing him, even as his own life nears its end.
