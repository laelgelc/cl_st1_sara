During a violent ethnic and religious riot in Kano, Chika, an Igbo Christian medical student, is separated from her sister, Nnedi, at a market. A Hausa Muslim onion seller guides her into an abandoned shop, where the two women hide together. Despite their differences in religion, ethnicity, class, and education, they gradually connect through their shared fear and concern for missing loved ones: Chika searches for Nnedi, while the woman worries about her daughter Halima.

Their intimacy deepens when Chika examines the woman’s cracked nipples and offers medical advice, and the woman later cleans and bandages Chika’s injured leg with her scarf. Chika briefly ventures outside but returns after encountering a freshly burned corpse. The women spend the night together as violence continues nearby.

At dawn, they learn that the immediate danger has passed and part ways, exchanging greetings for their families. Chika asks to keep the bloodstained scarf. She later searches hospitals, mortuaries, and public places for Nnedi but never finds her. The encounter leaves Chika unable to accept simplified portrayals of the riot: amid the sectarian violence, she remembers the humanity and gentleness of the Muslim woman who sheltered her.
