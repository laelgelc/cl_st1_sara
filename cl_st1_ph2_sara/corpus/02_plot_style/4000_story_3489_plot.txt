After recovering from a grave illness, Kiran stays with her husband, Sharat, at a secluded riverside house. During a storm, Nilkanta, a young performer from a theatrical troupe, survives a boat wreck and takes refuge with them. Kiran grows fond of the homeless boy, indulging and entertaining him, while Sharat and the rest of the household become irritated by his mischief.

As Nilkanta matures, his gratitude and affection for Kiran deepen into an intense, possessive attachment. When Sharat’s younger brother Satish arrives, Kiran devotes herself to joking and playing with him. Feeling displaced and neglected, Nilkanta becomes bitter, torments those weaker than himself, and secretly causes Satish petty annoyances.

When the family prepares to return home without Nilkanta, Kiran gently advises him to leave, while Satish mocks his tears. Seeking revenge, Nilkanta takes Satish’s prized inkstand, intending to throw it into the river, but hides it in his box instead. After Satish accuses him of theft, Kiran fiercely defends Nilkanta and prevents his belongings from being searched.

Later, while secretly placing farewell gifts in Nilkanta’s box, Kiran discovers the inkstand. Nilkanta witnesses this and believes that she now regards him as a common thief, though he sees his act as revenge rather than theft. Kiran conceals the discovery and leaves the gifts, but the ashamed and wounded boy disappears the next day without a trace. She later throws the inkstand into the river, preserving his secret. The family departs, leaving behind Nilkanta’s starving dog, which wanders the riverbank mourning its vanished master.
