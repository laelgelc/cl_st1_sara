A Dartmoor farmer’s wife secretly helps a young American prisoner escape from Princetown War Prison by hiding him in an empty rabbit hutch and carrying him home. She and her husband shelter the fugitive in their garret while deciding how to send him safely onward.

The next day, the farmer watches the nearby road for search parties. As a joke, he imitates an American accent in front of Nathan Nathaniel, a traveling Jewish clothes dealer, who mistakes him for the escaped prisoner. Soldiers arrest the farmer and take him to the prison, where the governor immediately recognizes him. Humiliated, Nathan is forced to pay the farmer five guineas in compensation and is later chased and beaten by the angry soldiers.

That night, the restless prisoner is allowed to walk outside. Overcome by the sensation of liberty after fifteen months of captivity, he wanders too far and is caught by dawn near a scarecrow. He hides behind it just as Nathan, lost on the moor overnight, approaches. Coveting the scarecrow’s old beaver hat, Nathan reaches for it, whereupon the hidden fugitive speaks and punches him unconscious. The young man steals Nathan’s coat, hats, and bag as a disguise and escapes toward Plymouth.

Nine months later, a letter from America explains what happened. The fugitive apologizes for leaving without saying goodbye, thanks the farmer’s wife for saving his life, and reports that he is married and happy. He sends her a silhouette made in prison as a keepsake.
