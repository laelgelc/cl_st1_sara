After years of wandering, the aging poet Owen Hanrahan settles in an abandoned cabin and supports himself by teaching local children. Returning from a night away, he meets Nora, a young woman being forced to marry the wealthy, elderly Paddy Doe. She asks Hanrahan to shame Paddy with a satirical song. When Nora casually says Paddy is as old as Hanrahan, the poet is devastated by the realization that others now see him as an old man.

Angry at age, decay, and his fading appeal, Hanrahan composes a curse against ancient creatures, local old men, and especially Paddy Doe, while blessing May blossoms because they die in their beauty. He teaches the song to his pupils and sends them across the countryside to spread it. The insult soon provokes a mob of old men, led by those named in the song, who march on his cabin with sticks. Hanrahan flees, then watches them set fire to his home. Having turned old age itself against him, he is forced to become a wanderer once more.
