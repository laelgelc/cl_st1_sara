On the final day of ouananiche season in Quebec, an angler and his companions find their favored river pool swollen by a flood and seemingly impossible to fish. After spotting a large salmon in the torrent, the narrator makes a perilous journey along a cliff and across an unstable log to reach casting distance. The fish rejects every artificial fly he tries, but the angler catches a grasshopper and uses it as bait, prompting the salmon to strike.

After a long struggle with the powerful fish, the guide arrives with a landing net. The net’s handle breaks just as the salmon is caught, sending both net and fish drifting downstream, but the guide wades into the river and retrieves them with a crooked stick. The narrator concludes that the true turning point was his split-second capture of the grasshopper, reflecting that life is full of unnoticed crises and opportunities.
