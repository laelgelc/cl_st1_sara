The narrator adores her imaginative young godchild, Elsbeth, who leads her and her two little brothers through the woods to a secret clearing where they joyfully pretend to see fairies. Soon afterward, the narrator travels away and learns that Elsbeth has died after bravely wishing she could remain through Christmas to finish her gifts and receive her own.

On Christmas Eve, Elsbeth’s grieving parents prepare stockings and toys only for the two boys. The brothers later claim they saw Elsbeth’s ghost counting the two stockings and weeping because none had been left for her before she vanished.

The following Christmas, the narrator prepares a third stocking and a pile of presents for Elsbeth, including the autoharp she had once wanted. Although the room is watched and the boys are locked in their chamber, Elsbeth’s stocking and gifts disappear without a trace. Later that night, the narrator hears the distant sound of a stringed instrument and a child’s joyful laugh. Realizing that Elsbeth has received her presents, she bids the little ghost farewell and tells her to rest. Elsbeth never returns.
