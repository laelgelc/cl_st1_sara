John Conyers, an isolated and impoverished inventor in colonial India, waits with a loaded pistol nearby for news about a gun he has designed. His wealthy young friend Hugh Palliser arrives to announce that the invention has been officially approved and will make Conyers’s fortune.

During their celebration, Hugh reveals that he is engaged to Damaris, an older woman who spent fifteen years waiting for a disgraced former lover whose father was imprisoned for forgery. Conyers realizes that he himself is the vanished man she once loved. Though Hugh passionately declares he would kill the former lover if he returned, Conyers conceals his identity and drinks a toast to “the one woman.”

After Hugh leaves, Conyers considers using the pistol on himself. He ultimately closes the drawer, deciding for Damaris’s sake not to disrupt her new happiness and to endure his loneliness until death.
