A narrator travels to the remote jungle settlement of Bungloore to discover why the civil servant Sparkley abruptly left Simla after apparently breaking off his engagement to Mary Millikens. Sparkley now claims he can hear inanimate objects speak. He explains that during a darkened theatrical performance, repeated shoves caused him unknowingly to deliver different parts of his marriage proposal to Mary, her sister, and their widowed mother, leaving him in an impossibly embarrassing situation. Before the narrator departs, Sparkley reveals that he owns Mrs. Awksby’s former armchair and enjoys hearing its recollections of her private affairs. When confronted with this news, Mrs. Awksby dismisses Sparkley as a liar.

In a second episode, a colonel confides that the habitually drunken and destructive soldier Mulledwiney has taken offense because the colonel playfully touched him with a stick. To restore the private’s honor, the colonel agrees to fight him bare-knuckled in the jungle and asks the narrator to tell his family that he died of measles if he is killed. A funeral follows, though the narrator never learns exactly what happened.

In the final episode, a cow, a cat, and several horses gather at a watering place and boast about their relationships with humans while parodying heroic animal tales. They observe a mob of unruly British schoolboys, praising the boys’ cruelty and emotional restraint as qualities that will make them imperial leaders. Then Mulledwiney and his companions Bleareyed and Otherwise arrive with stolen property and begin telling grotesquely exaggerated war stories. Mulledwiney’s tale of eating enemy soldiers becomes so revolting that all the animals flee, leaving the soldiers alone as the surreal jungle scene dissolves into dawn.
