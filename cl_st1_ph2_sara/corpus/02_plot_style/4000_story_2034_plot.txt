In 1718 Islington, Dr. Abell, a physician fascinated by supernatural beings and the possibility of animating inanimate objects, loses his longtime servant Luke Jennett after an unexplained but terrifying incident involving a bedstaff. Jennett later enters the service of Dr. Quinn, an honest rival physician who begins attracting some of Abell’s former patients.

Abell secretly visits Quinn’s house, searching his study, dispensing room, and bedroom. Soon afterward, Quinn suffers recurring nightmares in which he is compelled to dig up a shrouded, chrysalis-like body bearing his own dead face. He then acquires unusually fine, secondhand bedding marked with aristocratic emblems and begins sleeping unnaturally deeply, his pillows closing around his head.

Quinn is eventually found suffocated in his locked bedroom. His sheets hold him motionless while the ends of his pillow have folded over his face. There is no evidence that anyone entered, and his death is officially attributed to divine visitation. Documents concerning the case also describe the robbery of a noble family’s mausoleum and the punishment of a dealer who received the stolen goods. The implication is that Quinn’s bedding was taken from a corpse in the violated tomb and that Abell, using forbidden supernatural knowledge, caused the grave-clothes to animate and murder his rival.
