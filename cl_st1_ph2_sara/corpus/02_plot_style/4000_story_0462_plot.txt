Practical, self-serving Jane scorns romantic love, unlike her cousin Mattie, who remains devoted to the poor blacksmith’s assistant Jack Halibut. Mattie’s father forbids their courtship until Jack can support a wife, so Jack emigrates to America to make his fortune.

Three years later, Jack returns wealthy and secretly asks Mattie to meet him near the church. Jealous of Mattie’s opportunity, Jane follows her, locks her inside the church, and meets Jack herself. She falsely claims Mattie no longer loves him and implies that she, Jane, has remained faithful to him. Hurt and angry, Jack impulsively proposes to Jane, who accepts because of his wealth.

Jane forgets to release Mattie, who rings the church bell for help. Jack and several villagers arrive and find her trapped. Mattie joyfully embraces Jack, exposing Jane’s deception. Reunited, Jack and Mattie receive her father’s blessing and prepare to marry, while Jane is disgraced and loses her uncle’s trust, though she continues to insist that she acted for the best.
