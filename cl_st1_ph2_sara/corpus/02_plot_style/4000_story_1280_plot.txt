When carpet tackers hired to prepare the State Capitol for Prince Ludwig’s visit form a union overnight and strike for double pay, the Governor refuses to be pressured. Unable to hire replacements, state officials, legislators, judges, and other prominent citizens unite to lay the carpets themselves, setting aside political rivalries and working enthusiastically to finish before the Prince’s scheduled arrival.

Meanwhile, a U.S. senator secretly arranges for the Prince to arrive early, believing he should witness Americans as they truly are rather than through formal ceremonies. Hidden above the rotunda, the Prince watches the disheveled dignitaries cooperate, mend disputes, and perform manual labor. When discovered, he joins them and enjoys the spontaneous scene more than any staged reception, concluding that the best way to see America is when America does not know it is being observed.
