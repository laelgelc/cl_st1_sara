A Nigerian journalist’s wife waits outside the American embassy in Lagos to seek asylum after government agents target her family. Her husband, a prominent critic of General Abacha’s regime, has already escaped the country after learning that soldiers plan to arrest and kill him. When armed men raid their home looking for him, one of them accidentally shoots and kills their four-year-old son, Ugonna. The mother escapes by jumping from the balcony, later buries her child, and is urged to use the murder as evidence of political persecution so she can join her husband in America.

While waiting in the visa line, she relives the raid, struggles with grief and guilt, and reflects bitterly on the personal cost of her husband’s celebrated political courage. During the interview, the official asks her to provide details and proof that government agents killed her son and that she remains in danger. She realizes she cannot reduce Ugonna’s life and death to evidence in exchange for safety. Deciding that the “new life” she wants is not in America but beside her son’s grave, she abandons the interview and walks out of the embassy.
