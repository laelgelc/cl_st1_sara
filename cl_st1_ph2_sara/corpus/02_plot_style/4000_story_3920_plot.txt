A wealthy man takes his daughter and her fiancé across the plains to Deadwood under the protection of Alfred, a diminutive, painfully bashful but highly skilled scout. The young couple mocks Alfred’s timid manner and ignores his warnings about leaving the wagon train. Seeking solitude, the daughter secretly rides away alone.

After discovering signs of a nearby Sioux war party, Alfred learns she is missing and tracks her to a prairie-dog town. About thirty warriors surround them, leaving escape impossible. Alfred prepares to repel their charge and gives the terrified woman a pistol, instructing her to kill herself rather than be captured if he falls.

Alfred waits until the charging warriors are close, planning to break their nerve without firing. Just as he raises his rifle, however, the ground collapses beneath him and he falls. Believing he has been killed and capture is imminent, the woman shoots herself. Alfred immediately recovers, and the warriors retreat as he predicted, but it is too late to save her. Grieving over the needless death, he kills three incapacitated warriors before the wagon train arrives.
