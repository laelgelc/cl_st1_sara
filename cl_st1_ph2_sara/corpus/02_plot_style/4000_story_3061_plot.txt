During a hot, tedious train journey, an aunt struggles to control three restless, endlessly questioning children. Her attempt to entertain them with a conventional moral tale about a good girl fails because they find it dull and unconvincing.

A bachelor sharing the carriage challenges the aunt’s claim that children are difficult to entertain and tells his own story. His heroine, Bertha, is so exceptionally good that she earns three medals and is allowed into a prince’s private park. When a wolf enters, Bertha hides successfully, but the clinking of her medals reveals her location, and the wolf devours her while the park’s pigs escape.

The children are delighted by the shocking ending, while the aunt condemns the story for undermining their moral education. The bachelor points out that, unlike her, he kept them quiet, then leaves amused that they will now pester their aunt for more “improper” stories.
