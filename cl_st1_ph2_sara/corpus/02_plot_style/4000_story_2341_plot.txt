A hardworking, deaf narrator spends a hot day repairing and improving his rented home, then meticulously prepares an elaborate hot bath. He heats the water, rigs a canvas steam cover and headrest, lays out toiletries, reading material, clean clothes, coffee, and food, and carefully carries boiling water to the tub. Only when he steps in does he discover that all the water has drained away because he forgot to insert the plug.

Exhausted and disappointed, he abandons the bath and falls asleep in his dirty work clothes. He compares this simple but disastrous oversight to the lives of the impoverished, transient residents of a nearby terrace: many people begin life confidently and plan carefully, yet fail because of one neglected essential detail, sometimes through physical disadvantages beyond their control.
