A writer struggling to create a Christmas story during wartime declares that Christmas has been destroyed by commerce, greed, and violence. Father Time appears and challenges him, then invites in Father Christmas, who has become ragged, frightened, and grief-stricken after years of wandering through a war-torn world. Traumatized by mines, machine guns, and the deaths of children, Father Christmas briefly regains his old warmth when given shelter, a fire, a drink, and the chance to mend a child’s broken toy. Yet the damaged books and undelivered gifts he carries remind him of children suffering and dying across Europe, and he collapses in despair.

Father Time tells the writer that everyone can help restore Christmas by shielding children, for now, from the hatred and horror of war and preserving for them its joy, charity, and kindness. He promises that the sacrifices of the dead may eventually produce a better world and a renewed peace. When the supernatural visitors vanish at dawn, the writer resolves to begin this work by mending his child’s broken toy horse.
