Ivy Keller returns home from school and quickly becomes infatuated with baseball, especially handsome local pitcher Rudie Schlachweiler. Her admiration develops into a romance, despite her parents’ objections, though Ivy remains more interested in Rudie as a baseball hero than as a man.

When Ivy threatens to leave home for him, her father persuades her to wait until the season is over, avoid all contact with Rudie for a month, and then visit him at his winter job. In November, Ivy and her father find Rudie working as an ordinary shoe clerk. Seeing her glamorous athlete reduced to fitting shoes destroys Ivy’s idealized image of him. She pretends she is merely passing through and leaves immediately.

Six months later, when her father mentions that Rudie has been traded to another team and praises his pitching, Ivy dismisses Rudie as a hopelessly poor player, revealing that her love vanished with her hero worship.
