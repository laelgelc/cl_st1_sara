The arrival of a cavalry regiment transforms a dreary provincial town by bringing military bustle and social excitement. At a lavish dinner hosted by the brigade’s general, Pythagoras Pythagoravitch Tchertokoutski, a boastful former cavalry officer and local landowner, claims to own a magnificent Viennese calash worth four thousand rubles. Eager to impress the officers, he invites the general and the entire company to dine at his estate the next day and inspect it.

Instead of returning home to prepare, Tchertokoutski stays to gamble and drink until early morning. He comes home intoxicated, forgets the invitation, and sleeps late. When his wife sees the general and his officers approaching, she wakes him, but there is no dinner and no time to arrange one. Panicking, Tchertokoutski orders the servants to say he has gone away, then hides inside the supposedly splendid calash in the coach-house.

Annoyed by their host’s unexplained absence, the visitors decide at least to inspect the celebrated vehicle. They find it entirely ordinary and worth far less than Tchertokoutski claimed. When they open its leather apron to examine the interior, they discover him crouched inside in his dressing gown. The astonished general simply covers him again and leaves with the officers.
