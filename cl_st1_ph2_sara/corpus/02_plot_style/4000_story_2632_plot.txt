In November 1896, a genealogist cycling through the remote Miskatonic Valley is caught in a violent storm and seeks refuge in an ancient, sinister farmhouse. Inside, he finds furnishings and books untouched since colonial times, including a rare illustrated account of the Congo that repeatedly opens to an engraving of a cannibal butcher’s shop.

The house’s filthy but powerful elderly inhabitant appears and welcomes him in an archaic dialect. While discussing the book, the old man becomes increasingly excited by the cannibalism engraving. He confesses that it inspired him to enjoy slaughtering sheep and then awakened a craving for human flesh, which he believes might prolong his life. His references to people who mysteriously disappeared suggest that he has acted on this desire.

As he speaks, blood drips from the ceiling onto the open illustration, revealing that a freshly killed victim lies in the room above. Before the horrified narrator can react, an immense lightning strike destroys the farmhouse. He later awakens among its blackened ruins, spared from both the cannibal and complete madness.
