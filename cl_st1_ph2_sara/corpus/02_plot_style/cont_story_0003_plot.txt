A struggling, insecure antiques-shop owner participates in a charity auction alongside more attractive and successful local businessmen. When the audience responds to his awkward runway appearance with pity cheers, he mistakes their kindness for genuine admiration. After a fellow participant tries to reassure him but calls him by the wrong name, he feels humiliated and spitefully kicks the man’s wallet and car keys beneath some risers.

He then overhears that the man is rushing to collect his daughter from a painful medical treatment intended to correct her deformed foot. Guilty, he leaves but considers returning and revealing where the missing items are. Instead, he fantasizes about turning their recovery into an act of brilliance and “moral courage” that would earn him friendship, social status, and admiration. Ultimately, he rationalizes his behavior and drives away.

As he returns to his failing shop, his thoughts alternate between grandiose dreams, resentment, shame, and cruelty toward others. Confronted by a destitute old man, he imagines attacking him, but when the man offers a weak smile, he quietly smiles back, ending on a small moment of shared vulnerability rather than violence.
