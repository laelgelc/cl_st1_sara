Lucy, a quiet young cook, marries Willy Horsepool, a proud, hardworking but simple young miner. Though initially unsettled by his rough habits and physical intensity, she builds a happy home with him, and he takes immense pride in their marriage. They survive a long miners’ strike and the debts it causes, but soon afterward Willy suffers a serious injury underground that leaves him in prolonged, mysterious pain.

Confined to the house while another national strike fills the street with idle, excited miners, Willy becomes mentally unstable from pain, fear, and isolation. Hearing men organizing a walk to a football match, he suddenly insists on joining them despite being unable to travel. When Lucy restrains him, he turns on her, blaming her for his pain and repeatedly threatening to kill her. Their neighbor Ethel helps calm him, and Willy, once lucid again, is horrified and breaks down in tears. Lucy forgives him, knowing he was not himself, but fears that if word of his breakdown spreads, the authorities may stop his compensation, leaving them without support.
