After the High-Queen dies in childbirth, her infant son is visited by ancient hawk-like women of the Sidhe, who mingle their supernatural blood with his. He grows into a child of extraordinary wisdom, but grey hawk feathers sprout from his hair. Because an ancient law forbids anyone with a bodily blemish from ruling, the kingdom’s poets and lawmakers order everyone to wear hawk feathers, concealing his difference from him.

The boy becomes a renowned king and philosopher whose profound teachings fascinate visitors but often leave them alienated from ordinary life. Despite his wisdom, he remains isolated and unable to understand practical human concerns. He falls in love with a foreign king’s daughter, mistaking her beauty for spiritual depth, but she fears his inhuman nature and secretly loves a young warrior.

Overhearing the lovers, the king discovers that the feathers worn by his people are a deception. Realizing that he has been made to violate both human law and the proper boundaries of divine wisdom, he renounces the throne, names a more ordinary man as his successor, removes the false feathers from his courtiers, and departs to seek his true supernatural kindred. He is never seen again, and legends claim he joined either demons or the dark goddesses of the forest.
