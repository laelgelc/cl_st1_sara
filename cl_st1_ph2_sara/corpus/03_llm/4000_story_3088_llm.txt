# THE LADY OF THE TORN CARD

Bunt McBride was mending a cinch strap on the veranda of the little hotel at Santa Ysabel when the talk turned upon revolutions. He had been a cowboy, packer, deck hand, prospector, cook, and, by his own account, assistant secretary to the president of a Central American republic for a period of eleven minutes. He was spare, leather-coloured, and deliberate in movement; but once launched upon a reminiscence his speech ran away with him like a stampeded remuda.

“Revolutions,” he observed, drawing the thong tight with his teeth, “is easy enough, Mr. Dixon, provided you don’t mix ’em up with females. Rifles, ammunition, patrol boats, custom-house spies, an’ governments which is anxious to put your head in a gunny-sack—all them is plain business. But once let a female party into the layout, an’ you’ve got weather. An’ if she ain’t a female party at all—why, then you’ve got climate.”

I asked him what he meant.

Bunt tested the repaired strap, nodded at it, and settled back against a post.

“Which I’m a-goin’ for to relate,” he said. “It was the time Hardenberg an’ Strokher fought two hours bare-handed on the fore-deck of Ally Bazan’s schooner, account of a lady with black eyes. They fought honourable, scientific, an’ complete; an’ when they got through they looked like a pair of overripe tomatoes that had fell down a mine shaft. An’ all for a lady, Mister Man—all for a lady which wasn’t there.”

He paused to light a cigarette.

“This Hardenberg,” he continued, “was an American by profession an’ a trouble-maker by natural gifts. He stood six feet two, weighed maybe one-ninety, an’ had freckles over him as plentiful as bullet holes in a Sonora cantina sign. His hair was red, his fists was square, an’ he could smile friendly-like while explainin’ exactly where he aimed to bury you.

“Strokher was English. That was his principal misfortune, though he had others. He had a yellow moustache, one buck tooth, an’ shoulders like a grain warehouse. He says ‘Really’ when he’s mad, ‘Extraordinary’ when he’s dangerous, an’ ‘My word’ when he’s fixin’ to commit murder. Him an’ Hardenberg had been partners off an’ on for three years, which is considerable longer than most married people stays peaceable.

“Ally Bazan was captain of the schooner *Two Brothers*, although there wasn’t no two brothers connected with her, nor never had been. Ally was a little, dark, dried-up party, with one eye that looked at you an’ another that looked into the middle distance for police. He could sail through fog by smell, cheat a customs officer with documents printed upside down, an’ locate whisky aboard a vessel that had been built by prohibitionists.

“Then there was me.

“I was younger in them days, Mr. Dixon, an’ liable to confidence. I had not yet learned that when three men whispers together after midnight an’ says there’s a thousand dollars apiece in it, the thousand is mostly imagination an’ the danger is cash on delivery.

“We was all four in San Francisco, livin’ at a lodging-house on Green Street where the wallpaper was loose enough to wave in the wind, though the windows was shut. Hardenberg comes into my room one night an’ shuts the door with great secrecy.

“‘Bunt,’ says he, ‘how do you stand on liberty?’

“‘I generally sets on it,’ says I, ‘if liberty means one of Ally’s schemes.’

“‘This here,’ says he, ‘is a patriotic undertaking.’

“‘Whose patriotism?’

“‘Guatemala’s.’

“‘I ain’t acquainted with Guatemala,’ says I.

“‘That don’t matter. Guatemala needs rifles.’

“‘Most countries does,’ I says, ‘after they get politicians.’

“Then in comes Strokher, twirlin’ his moustache, an’ after him Ally, carryin’ a chart rolled under his arm. They spread the chart on my bed, an’ Ally puts his thumb on a piece of coastline.

“‘Here,’ he says, ‘is Puerto San José.’

“‘Looks peaceful,’ says I.

“‘It ain’t,’ says Hardenberg. ‘Barreto Palachi is goin’ to start a revolution.’

“‘When?’

“‘As soon as we bring him the guns.’

“That, Mr. Dixon, was the size an’ architecture of the proposition. Palachi had eight hundred patriots hid in the mountains, but only sixty rifles among ’em, an’ half of them rifles was of such ancient extraction that they loaded at the wrong end. Ally had arranged for five hundred new repeaters, packed in cases marked AGRICULTURAL IMPLEMENTS, because a rifle, properly considered, is an implement for cultivatin’ a revolution.

“We was to take the *Two Brothers* south, stop at San Diego for Palachi’s representative, then run on down to a quiet piece of coast where the patriots would meet us with mules. For this we got a thousand apiece, half in advance an’ half when Palachi was sittin’ in the president’s chair, or somebody else’s chair, anyhow.

“‘Who gives the orders?’ I asks.

“Ally looked at Hardenberg, Hardenberg looked at Strokher, an’ Strokher smoothed his moustache.

“‘A lady,’ says Strokher.

“Now I’d noticed all three had brushed their coats, which should have warned me. Men don’t brush their coats for patriotism.

“‘Her name,’ says Hardenberg, slow an’ solemn, ‘is Señorita Esperanza Ulivarri.’

“He pronounced it like he was unwrappin’ a present.

“‘She is Palachi’s confidential agent,’ says Strokher.

“‘She’s a weather-breeder,’ says I.

“‘You ain’t seen her,’ says Hardenberg.

“‘I don’t need to see a cyclone to know the roof is loose.’

“Just then somebody knocked. Hardenberg stood up so quick he kicked the chart off the bed. Strokher rubbed his moustache into two yellow spears. Ally put on a necktie he had in his pocket. An’ I says to myself, ‘Bunt, here comes Guatemala’s foreign policy.’

“She come in.

“Now I don’t hold with females in dangerous business, Mr. Dixon, an’ I say so candid, but I ain’t blind. Esperanza was tall, not too tall; slim, not thin; dark-haired, pale in the face, an’ dressed all in black, with a red flower at her shoulder. Her eyes was the kind that appears to know what you was doin’ last Tuesday night an’ why you lied about it Wednesday mornin’. When she walked, the room seemed to rearrange its furniture for her convenience.

“Hardenberg bowed till his freckles near slid off his face. Strokher bowed English, which means he bent in the middle like a folding ruler. Ally tried to kiss her hand an’ missed it. I stayed seated, account of philosophy.

“‘Gentlemen,’ she says, ‘I bring the final instructions from General Palachi.’

“Her voice was low an’ soft, but there was somethin’ leathern underneath, though none of us detected it then. Hardenberg was too busy lookin’ at her eyes, an’ Strokher was studyin’ the red flower as if England’s honour depended on botany.

“She told us the rifles must be aboard by Friday night. We was to sail at midnight without clearance papers. At San Diego a man would come to us in a shore boat an’ present half of a torn card. Ally would have the other half. If the pieces matched, the man was Palachi’s representative an’ would guide us south.

“She took out a calling card with BARRETO PALACHI printed on it, tore it across the middle, an’ gave one piece to Ally.

“‘Guard it,’ she says. ‘Many lives depend upon it.’

“‘Señorita,’ says Strokher, ‘you may depend upon us absolutely.’

“‘More than absolutely,’ says Hardenberg.

“I could see the campaign start right there.

“Esperanza smiled at ’em both, but not enough to determine ownership, an’ this was unfortunate. If she had smiled one inch more toward either party, we might have saved two hours, seven teeth, an’ near a quart of assorted blood.

“She talked business for maybe twenty minutes. Every time she says ‘rifles,’ Hardenberg gazes at her mouth. Every time she says ‘danger,’ Strokher moves his chest forward. She warned us the police was huntin’ Palachi’s agents all through California, an’ there might be spies at the docks.

“‘An’ you, señorita?’ Hardenberg asks. ‘Are you in danger?’

“She gives him a look full of midnight music.

“‘Always.’

“Strokher steps up.

“‘Then permit me to offer my protection.’

“‘She don’t need protection from a foreign power,’ says Hardenberg.

“‘I beg your pardon?’

“‘You got it.’

“Esperanza looks from one to the other, an’ I’ll swear there was amusement in them black eyes, but she only says:

“‘The cause requires courage, gentlemen, not quarrels.’

“Then she put her veil down, wished us fortune, an’ went away.

“For a full minute nobody spoke. Hardenberg stared at the door. Strokher stared at the chair she had used. Ally stared at the card. I stared at the three of ’em an’ felt old.

“‘A remarkable woman,’ says Strokher at last.

“‘You keep your remarks respectable,’ says Hardenberg.

“‘My remarks are always respectable.’

“‘That’s what makes ’em suspicious.’

“From there on it developed scandalous.

“We loaded the rifles Friday after dark. They come down in wagons under sacks of onions, an’ we stowed ’em below the water casks. Two policemen walked past while we was workin’, an’ one of the cases split open just as they turned the corner. Ally set on the loose rifles an’ sang a hymn till they went away.

“At midnight we dropped down the bay without lights. There was a fog thick enough to carve names in, an’ the *Two Brothers* went feelin’ through it like a blind cow in a strange pasture. Once a ferry hooted close by, an’ Ally answered with a noise he claimed was regulation maritime language, though it sounded like a mule sneezin’ in a barrel.

“By daylight we was outside the heads an’ rollin’ south.

“I don’t favour the ocean. It is too loose. A prairie stays where the Almighty put it, but the sea is forever risin’ up under a man without invitation. For three days my stomach conducted a revolution of its own. I laid alongside the rail with my leathern valves pumpin’ salt air, while the *Two Brothers* climbed one blue hill after another an’ slid down the back of it sideways.

“Hardenberg an’ Strokher was sick, too, but neither would admit it because each feared the other might mention it to Esperanza. They stalked around green in the face, speakin’ polite an’ swallowin’ hard.

“‘Fine weather,’ says Hardenberg, hangin’ onto a stay.

“‘Perfectly delightful,’ says Strokher, grippin’ the cabin roof.

“Then both of ’em leaned over opposite rails at the same time, sufferin’ for their national reputations.

“When the sea calmed they got worse. They talked about Esperanza from breakfast till dog watch. Hardenberg said her eyes was Western eyes, free an’ fearless. Strokher said they possessed the mystery of old Spain. Hardenberg said she had smiled at him three times. Strokher said she had smiled at everybody because she was well-bred.

“‘She give me the red flower,’ says Hardenberg.

“‘She dropped it,’ says Strokher. ‘You picked it up.’

“‘Same result.’

“‘Not in civilized society.’

“Hardenberg had that flower pressed in the ship’s Bible. Strokher had the chair back from the lodging-house because Esperanza’s hand had rested on it. How he stole the chair back aboard I don’t know, but there it was in his bunk, wrapped in sailcloth like a deceased aunt.

“Each commenced preparin’ his courtship for San Diego. Hardenberg wrote poetry. This was a thing nature had never intended. He used words like ‘moon,’ ‘June,’ ‘passion,’ an’ ‘Arizona,’ an’ could not make none of ’em fit. Strokher practised Spanish out of a book. For three days he went around sayin’ ‘Donde está la biblioteca?’ which Ally told him meant ‘Your eyes are brighter than stars,’ but which I suspicioned was inquiry concernin’ a library.

“They cleaned their boots, traded shirts, combed themselves, an’ asked me private questions.

“‘Bunt,’ says Hardenberg, ‘does a small scar improve a man’s face?’

“‘Depends,’ says I. ‘On you, a landslide might help.’

“Strokher comes later.

“‘McBride, do you consider the American temperament reliable in matters of the heart?’

“‘I don’t consider no temperament reliable once the heart gets to interferin’ with the brain-pan.’

“He gives me a shillin’ not to tell Hardenberg he had asked. Hardenberg had already give me two dollars not to tell Strokher about the poetry. Which I accepted both, bein’ neutral.

“By the fourth day they was jiggered an’ jiggered with jealousy. If Hardenberg said Esperanza was beautiful, Strokher accused him of familiarity. If Strokher called her noble, Hardenberg wanted to know what right a British subject had to inspect an American hemisphere.

“‘She ain’t American,’ says Strokher.

“‘She’s on this side of the ocean.’

“‘So is the Sandwich Islands.’

“‘Don’t you compare that girl to no sandwich.’

“That near started it, but Ally separated ’em with a bucket.

“The finish come after supper. We was maybe eighty miles north of San Diego, with a flat sea, a pale moon, an’ hardly wind enough to disturb a sick grasshopper. Ally was at the wheel. I was splicin’ rope. Hardenberg was leanin’ against the mainmast, sighin’. Strokher was by the rail, sighin’ English.

“After a while Hardenberg says:

“‘There ain’t room for both of us.’

“Strokher turns.

“‘On the schooner?’

“‘In the señorita’s affections.’

“‘I was not aware you occupied any portion of them.’

“‘I occupy the American half.’

“‘There is no American half.’

“‘Then I’ll annex one.’

“Strokher’s moustache begun to climb.

“‘Hardenberg,’ he says, ‘you are presuming upon our friendship.’

“‘No, I ain’t. I’m suspendin’ it.’

“‘For what purpose?’

“‘For purposes of a fair decision.’

“‘Do you propose cards?’

“‘No. You’d claim poker was an American prejudice.’

“‘Pistols?’

“‘Too short. Also too liable to damage the merchandise.’

“‘Swords?’

“‘I ain’t dressed for sewing.’

“Then Hardenberg takes off his coat, folds it careful, an’ lays it on the hatch.

“‘Fists,’ he says. ‘Bare knuckles. Winner gets the first free field to court Esperanza. Loser quits.’

“Strokher looks at him, very cold an’ royal.

“‘That,’ he says, removin’ his coat, ‘is barbarous, ridiculous, and entirely satisfactory.’

“I stood up.

“‘Boys,’ says I, ‘this is foolishness of the prize grade. The lady ain’t a land claim. You can’t stake her off by poundin’ each other.’

“‘Bunt,’ says Hardenberg, ‘you keep out.’

“‘McBride,’ says Strokher, ‘kindly oblige.’

“Ally put the wheel over to me.

“‘Let ’em,’ he says. ‘If we stop ’em now, they’ll shoot each other later.’

“Ally arranged the articles of combat. No bittin’, no kickin’, no gougin’, no hittin’ below the belt, an’ no usin’ belayin’ pins, pistols, knives, chairs, poetry, or foreign alliances. They was to fight three-minute rounds, one minute rest, until one quit or could not come back.

“For a bell Ally hung the cook’s iron skillet from the boom an’ hit it with a spoon.

“Bong!

“They come together.

“I ain’t goin’ to relate every blow, Mr. Dixon, because there was too many, an’ after the first half hour they all resembled each other. Hardenberg fought wide an’ free, like he was throwin’ rocks at a bear. Strokher fought straight an’ formal, with his left hand out an’ his right hand preservin’ the Empire.

“In the first round Hardenberg hit Strokher on the buck tooth. Strokher hit Hardenberg on the freckled nose. Both drew blood, both smiled, an’ all ordinary reason departed from the vessel.

“Bong!

“They rested.

“‘Had enough?’ says Hardenberg.

“‘I had scarcely begun,’ says Strokher.

“Bong!

“They fought another.

“At the end of ten rounds Hardenberg’s left eye was closin’ an’ Strokher’s moustache pointed northeast on one side an’ due south on the other. At twenty rounds they had lost all science an’ was proceedin’ by memory. At thirty, neither could remember why he wanted to hit the other, but honour had took charge.

“The moon went down. The wind freshened. The *Two Brothers* rolled, which complicated the campaign. Sometimes Hardenberg swung at Strokher an’ hit California. Sometimes Strokher aimed at Hardenberg an’ the schooner moved him six feet east. Once they both missed, went around each other complete, an’ met back to back with such surprise that Ally had to stop laughin’ before he could ring the skillet.

“We tried to quit ’em at the end of an hour.

“‘Draw,’ says Ally.

“‘No draw,’ says Hardenberg through a lip the size of a door cushion.

“‘Carry on,’ says Strokher, whose tooth had vanished from public life.

“They come up again.

“It ceased to be a fight an’ became geology. They wore each other down by erosion. Their arms moved slower an’ slower. They leaned together like two tired horses at a hitch rail, separated because the rules required it, then stumbled forward an’ leaned again.

“An’ mind you, they was friends. That’s the foolish part. Hardenberg would knock Strokher down an’ help him find his corner. Strokher would hit Hardenberg in the ribs an’ ask polite if the blow was too low. Once Hardenberg slipped, an’ Strokher held him up so he could knock him down honourable on the next exchange.

“At one hour an’ forty minutes, I says:

“‘This has went beyond romance into public nuisance.’

“Ally says, ‘The trouble is they’re too tough to stop an’ too weak to finish.’

“So we let ’em proceed.

“At two hours both swung at once. Each connected. Hardenberg sat down on a coil of rope. Strokher sat down on the deck. The skillet sounded without Ally touchin’ it, account of the boom rollin’.

“‘Come on,’ says Hardenberg, but he don’t move.

“‘With pleasure,’ says Strokher, equally stationary.

“Ally counted ten. Then he counted twenty, to be safe. Neither got up.

“‘Draw,’ he announces.

“‘No objection,’ says Strokher.

“‘Temporary arrangement,’ says Hardenberg.

“Then they crawled together an’ shook hands.

“That’s men for you, Mr. Dixon. Two hours before, each wanted to convert the other into fragments account of a woman. Now, havin’ done everything except that, they loved each other like brothers. Strokher gave Hardenberg half his whisky. Hardenberg tucked a blanket around Strokher. Each assured the other he was the bravest man alive, barrin’ himself.

“We patched ’em up with sail needles, cold beef, arnica, an’ language. By mornin’ Hardenberg’s face had six corners. Strokher looked like somebody had folded him while wet. But they was cheerful. The draw meant neither had to withdraw, so both still had hope.

“Near noon we sighted Point Loma, an’ before sundown we slipped into San Diego harbour an’ anchored away from the busy docks. The rifles was covered, the papers was ready, an’ Ally had Palachi’s half card in his pocket.

“Now the boys remembered Esperanza might meet the schooner. That thought produced activity.

“Hardenberg borrowed my razor, though there wasn’t enough level country on his face to use it. Strokher tried to comb his moustache, but one side had resigned. They put on clean shirts an’ debated how to explain their injuries.

“‘We must tell her the truth,’ says Strokher.

“‘Not all of it,’ says Hardenberg. ‘Leave out them parts where I knocked you down.’

“‘Then we must also omit the occasions upon which I returned the compliment.’

“‘We say we fought for her honour.’

“‘Against whom?’

“‘Each other.’

“Strokher considered.

“‘That may sound peculiar.’

“‘Women like sacrifice.’

“‘True.’

“They planned to stand side by side an’ tell her the combat had been chivalrous, unavoidable, an’ inspired by respect. Then each privately planned to tell her he would have won if Ally had allowed one more round.

“About nine o’clock a shore boat come out of the dark. One person was sittin’ in the stern, wrapped in a black cloak. Ally shaded the lantern.

“‘Who goes?’ he calls.

“A low voice answers in Spanish.

“The figure climbs the ladder.

“It was Esperanza.

“Same black hair, same pale face, same eyes that knew last Tuesday’s business. She had the red flower at her shoulder again. Hardenberg made a sound like a bullfrog surprised at prayer. Strokher stood so straight his bruises must have hurt internal.

“‘Señorita!’ says both.

“She smiles.

“‘Gentlemen, I am pleased to see you.’

“Hardenberg stepped forward, but Strokher stepped at the same time. Their shoulders collided. Both winced, backed off, bowed, an’ motioned for the other to proceed. After you. No, after you. It was beautiful conduct for parties that had lately been rearrangin’ each other’s features.

“‘You are injured,’ Esperanza says.

“‘A mere incident,’ says Strokher.

“‘A matter of honour,’ says Hardenberg.

“‘Indeed?’

“‘Your honour,’ says Hardenberg.

“Strokher glared.

“‘Our devotion to your safety,’ he explains, ‘led to a slight difference as to which of us was better qualified to defend you.’

“‘It was a draw,’ says Hardenberg quick.

“‘Technically,’ says Strokher.

“Esperanza looked at one, then the other. Her mouth commenced to twitch.

“‘You fought?’

“‘Two hours,’ says Ally, proud as a promoter. ‘Bare knuckles.’

“Esperanza turned away. Her shoulders shook.

“Hardenberg whispered, ‘She’s overcome.’

“Strokher whispered, ‘Naturally.’

“Then Esperanza laughed.

“It wasn’t the soft, silver laugh they expected. It come out deep an’ hearty, like a man hearin’ the end of a particularly useful joke. Hardenberg’s good eye narrowed. Strokher’s surviving section of moustache stiffened.

“Esperanza reached up, took hold of the black hair, an’ lifted the whole arrangement off.

“Underneath was short, wiry hair.

“Then she rubbed a handkerchief across one cheek. The pale colour come away. Next went the eyebrows. Then a little padding come out of the dress at the shoulders, an’ finally the red flower dropped to the deck.

“There stood a slim, dark man of maybe forty, with a sharp nose, a small black moustache, an’ the pleased expression of a fox that has just sold insurance to the chickens.

“He bowed.

“‘Caballeros,’ he says, in that same voice, only no longer soft, ‘permit me. Barreto Palachi.’

“There was silence.

“You could hear water lickin’ the schooner’s side. You could hear Ally’s lantern hiss. You could near hear Hardenberg’s freckles turn pale.

“Palachi took half a torn calling card from inside the black dress. Ally, movin’ very careful because he was about to bust, produced the other half. They fitted.

“‘The police watched every train, every steamer, every road,’ says Palachi. ‘They searched for a revolutionary gentleman. So—I was not a gentleman.’

“He smiled at Hardenberg.

“‘Your flower, señor.’

“Hardenberg stared at it on the deck.

“Palachi turned to Strokher.

“‘And I trust the chair travelled comfortably?’

“Strokher’s mouth opened. No words come, only a little whistle through the gap where his tooth had been.

“‘You knew?’ I asks.

“‘I saw him carry it from the lodging-house,’ says Palachi. ‘It was touching.’

“Then Ally exploded. He dropped onto a rifle case an’ laughed till the lantern swung. I slid down the rail. Even Palachi had to hold his ribs.

“Hardenberg an’ Strokher did not laugh.

“They looked at Palachi. They looked at each other. They looked at their battered hands, their swollen eyes, their noble clean shirts, an’ the red flower between ’em.

“Then, without a word, both moved.

“For one instant I thought they aimed to murder Palachi, Ally, me, an’ the whole republic of Guatemala. But they passed us, reached the rail, an’ jumped into the shore boat. Hardenberg took the oars. Strokher cast off. They rowed for land at a speed no eight-oared crew could have matched, Strokher steerin’ an’ Hardenberg pullin’ like the devil was collectin’ fare.

“Palachi called after ’em:

“‘But gentlemen! The revolution!’

“Hardenberg never turned.

“‘Revolve without us!’ he yells.

“Strokher raised one hand toward the schooner.

“‘My word!’ he says, an’ that was all.

“They reached shore, abandoned the boat, an’ disappeared into San Diego.

“We took Palachi an’ the rifles south ourselves. The revolution lasted nine days. Palachi became minister of war, then president, then a private citizen in a hurry. Ally got paid half of the second half, an’ I received a medal with somebody else’s name on it. But that ain’t the important portion.

“Six months later I met Hardenberg an’ Strokher in Tucson. They was partners again, ownin’ a freight outfit. Hardenberg’s nose leaned slightly west, Strokher had a gold tooth, an’ they appeared prosperous.

“We drank together.

“They asked me about the rifles, the voyage, an’ Guatemala. I told ’em everything suitable.

“But I never mentioned Esperanza. No, sir.

“I ain’t what you’d call a coward, Mr. Dixon, but Hardenberg still carried a .45, Strokher had fists like hams, an’ both of ’em was lookin’ at me mighty steady whenever San Diego come into the conversation.

“So I displayed wisdom.

“Wisdom,” Bunt concluded, tightening the repaired cinch once more, “is knowin’ a thing, rememberin’ a thing, an’ possessin’ sense enough not to say a thing—especially when the thing wore black hair, carried a red flower, an’ caused two able-bodied fools to pound each other into sausage for the love of Barreto Palachi.”
