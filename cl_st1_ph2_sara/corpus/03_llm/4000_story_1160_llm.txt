## CLEANLINESS, WITH COMPLICATIONS  
*A One-Act Farce*

**THE PERSONS**

**JULIE MARVIS**, who has never mistaken embarrassment for a virtue.  
**LOIS MARVIS**, her sister, who has.  
**MR. ADRIAN CALKINS**, a literary gentleman, divorced, serious, and thirty-seven in a favorable light.  
**THE AUDIENCE**, unwilling accomplices.

---

The bathroom at the Marvis summer cottage is large enough to encourage hope and inconvenient enough to punish it.

Across the walls marches a blue frieze of fishermen drawing blue nets from a blue sea while, immediately behind them, a blue ship sails with calm determination toward what appears to be the bathroom door. The fishermen have been hauling the same three fish since 1906. They are not in the plot. We mention this firmly because experienced audiences, observing any repeated design in a first act, expect it to conceal either a secret passage or the deceased uncle’s will.

At the left is a door leading to the hall. At the right, rather high in the wall, is a broad window opening upon a veranda roof. A person outside may see the upper portion of a person standing within, but not the lower portion; and a person reclining in the bathtub may see somebody at the window while remaining almost entirely unseen. Nature did not arrange this window. A dramatist did.

The bathtub occupies the centre of the room with the aggressive dignity of an elderly touring car. It has a high porcelain tonneau, four short clawed legs, and taps suggesting the brass goggles of a motorist who has taken a wrong turn and intends never to admit it. Beside it stands a chair. On the chair there is nothing.

This nothing is important.

At the rise, **JULIE MARVIS** is in the tub. Only her head, throat, bare shoulders, and one speculative knee—withdrawn before anyone can become statistical—are visible. Her dark hair is piled recklessly on top of her head and secured with a yellow pencil. She is twenty-two, which is an age at which a pencil may be considered a hair ornament and consequences a form of entertainment.

She is sailing a cake of soap along the rim.

**JULIE**  
(in a deep nautical voice)  
Iceberg to starboard! Women and authors first!

The soap slips into the water.

**JULIE**  
There goes American literature.

A pounding comes at the door.

**LOIS**  
Julie!

**JULIE**  
No forwarding address.

**LOIS**  
You’ve been in there forty minutes.

**JULIE**  
Thirty-eight. The other two were ablutions.

**LOIS**  
Open this door.

**JULIE**  
I’m not dressed for callers.

**LOIS**  
You’re never dressed for anything on time.

**JULIE**  
That’s what makes getting dressed worth watching.

The knob turns uselessly.

**LOIS**  
Mother says I’m to have the bathroom immediately.

**JULIE**  
Tell Mother Cleanliness refuses to surrender to Godliness.

**LOIS**  
I don’t know what you mean.

**JULIE**  
You’re Godliness. I’m Cleanliness. We were separated in childhood by a cruel grammarian.

**LOIS**  
Mr. Calkins will be here at four.

**JULIE**  
Then he can be Next-to-Godliness.

**LOIS**  
Julie!

**JULIE**  
I don’t see why a literary man needs you scrubbed. Aren’t they supposed to admire the natural woman—barefoot in the wheat, with a soul like a sunset and no knowledge of punctuation?

**LOIS**  
Adrian does not admire vulgarity.

**JULIE**  
That must make shaving difficult.

There is a brief silence outside, the sort in which a sister counts ten and discovers she has counted the same murder ten times.

**LOIS**  
I laid out your things on the bed.

**JULIE**  
Sweet of you.

**LOIS**  
Did you bring a robe in there?

**JULIE**  
No.

**LOIS**  
Your slippers?

**JULIE**  
No.

**LOIS**  
Your clothes?

**JULIE**  
Lois, if I had brought my clothes into the bathtub they’d be damp. Even I know that.

The door flies open. **LOIS** enters because Julie, with the splendid inconsistency of the carefree, has locked nothing. Lois is twenty-eight, fair, composed, and dressed in a pale summer frock which looks as if it has never sat down impulsively. Over one arm she carries a fresh towel.

She stops.

**LOIS**  
You haven’t anything with you!

**JULIE**  
I have soap, water, and a philosophy.

**LOIS**  
You came through the hall without a stitch?

**JULIE**  
Not exactly. I wore an expression.

**LOIS**  
Suppose someone had seen you?

**JULIE**  
Then it would have been someone’s lucky Thursday.

**LOIS**  
Mother is downstairs!

**JULIE**  
Mother never looks up before breakfast. It’s one of her moral principles.

**LOIS**  
It’s nearly four.

**JULIE**  
Then she never looks up before tea.

Lois places the towel on the chair, then notices that this is precisely what Julie wants.

**LOIS**  
You did this deliberately.

**JULIE**  
I never do anything deliberately. It spoils the surprise.

**LOIS**  
You knew I had an appointment.

**JULIE**  
A rendezvous.

**LOIS**  
An appointment.

**JULIE**  
With a divorced man on a summer afternoon? Darling, if you call that an appointment, what do you call the dentist?

**LOIS**  
Mr. Calkins’s marriage was a tragedy.

**JULIE**  
Most marriages are, if you write reviews of them afterward.

**LOIS**  
You’ve met him twice.

**JULIE**  
That’s enough to dislike a person intelligently. Love takes three meetings because one has to forget the first two.

**LOIS**  
Mother has prejudiced you.

**JULIE**  
Mother says he’s conceited, gloomy, and writes books in which married people stare at rain. I made up my own mind to agree with her.

**LOIS**  
Adrian understands women.

**JULIE**  
Has he apologized?

**LOIS**  
He has suffered.

**JULIE**  
Everybody has suffered. I had mumps during the fox-trot craze, but I don’t publish novels about my neck.

Lois snatches up the towel.

**JULIE**  
Here! Put that back.

**LOIS**  
No.

**JULIE**  
That towel is my future.

**LOIS**  
You may remain where you are until I have bathed and dressed.

**JULIE**  
You can’t bathe while I’m here.

**LOIS**  
Then perhaps you’ll learn foresight.

**JULIE**  
I tried foresight once. Everything I foresaw was dull.

**LOIS**  
You can call for Mother.

**JULIE**  
I’d sooner send a wireless to Siam.

**LOIS**  
Good.

**JULIE**  
Lois, if you take that towel, history will hold you responsible.

**LOIS**  
History may telephone me.

She exits, taking the towel. The door closes.

Julie regards it.

**JULIE**  
Lady Macbeth with Turkish cotton.

She sinks lower in the tub. We now possess the essential elements of high comedy: one naked young woman, one absent towel, and a door. Sophocles required a royal house and several generations of sin, but Sophocles did not understand summer rentals.

A man’s face appears at the window.

This is **MR. ADRIAN CALKINS**. He wears white flannels, a soft collar, and the expression of a person arriving unexpectedly but not, he hopes, undesirably. His hair is brushed backward in a manner which must be renewed after every strong opinion. He carries a book.

Julie sees him. He cannot see the tub, concealed from his angle by the high porcelain side. He sees only Julie’s head and shoulders above it, which might belong to a young woman sitting in a low chair. You begin to suspect the old rusty pivot upon which our plot will revolve. Do not groan. It has supported better plays than this.

**CALKINS**  
Lois!

Julie opens her mouth, closes it, and considers virtue. Virtue loses.

**JULIE**  
Good afternoon.

**CALKINS**  
I startled you.

**JULIE**  
You startled several of me.

**CALKINS**  
I came over the veranda roof. Your mother was in the garden.

**JULIE**  
So you chose the alpine route.

**CALKINS**  
She still objects to me.

**JULIE**  
Mother objects to almost everything that can vote.

**CALKINS**  
I couldn’t endure the formalities today.

**JULIE**  
Were they chasing you?

**CALKINS**  
May I come in?

Julie’s eyes travel from the window to the door, from the door to the vacant chair, and from the vacant chair to heaven, which has plainly withdrawn its management.

**JULIE**  
No!

**CALKINS**  
No?

**JULIE**  
I mean—you’re charming where you are. Like a portrait with ivy.

**CALKINS**  
You’re alone?

**JULIE**  
Profoundly.

**CALKINS**  
Then why shouldn’t I enter?

**JULIE**  
Because I’m not receiving.

**CALKINS**  
You seem dressed.

**JULIE**  
Appearances have started wars.

**CALKINS**  
What are you wearing?

**JULIE**  
My hair.

**CALKINS**  
(charmed)  
Your humor is different today.

**JULIE**  
It shrank in the wash.

A tiny slosh is heard as she moves.

**CALKINS**  
What was that?

**JULIE**  
A goldfish.

**CALKINS**  
You keep goldfish in your room?

**JULIE**  
Only one. He’s sensitive and can’t stand bowls. They make him feel watched.

**CALKINS**  
This isn’t your room.

**JULIE**  
He travels.

Calkins smiles with literary tolerance—the smile of a man willing to let reality be fanciful if he can take credit for noticing.

**CALKINS**  
You’ve been avoiding me, Lois.

**JULIE**  
Have I been successful?

**CALKINS**  
Cruelly.

**JULIE**  
Then why criticize technique?

**CALKINS**  
Your letters became guarded.

**JULIE**  
There are burglars everywhere.

**CALKINS**  
Yesterday, when I spoke of my new book, you changed the subject.

**JULIE**  
What did I change it to?

**CALKINS**  
Tennis.

**JULIE**  
A better seller.

**CALKINS**  
You said my heroine needed exercise.

**JULIE**  
Did she?

**CALKINS**  
She was dying of disillusionment.

**JULIE**  
Then exercise would have tired her into believing something.

**CALKINS**  
You liked Helena when I first described her.

**JULIE**  
I hadn’t lived with her for three hundred pages.

**CALKINS**  
You haven’t read the manuscript.

**JULIE**  
That may be why I still like her.

He leans upon the window ledge, wounded but fascinated.

**CALKINS**  
I had not suspected this lightness in you.

**JULIE**  
It’s the hot water. It brings out hidden colors.

**CALKINS**  
Hot water?

A pipe gives a long metallic knock beneath the tub.

**JULIE**  
The goldfish has a hammer.

**CALKINS**  
Lois—

**JULIE**  
He’s building a little garage.

**CALKINS**  
There’s something strange here.

**JULIE**  
There generally is when one looks closely at plumbing.

**CALKINS**  
I can hear water.

**JULIE**  
The sea.

**CALKINS**  
We’re half a mile from the sea.

**JULIE**  
Sound travels. That’s why gossip beats automobiles.

Calkins shifts position. Julie promptly sinks until only her eyes and hair are visible.

**CALKINS**  
Where did you go?

**JULIE**  
South.

**CALKINS**  
Please sit up.

**JULIE**  
I’m looking for a motive.

**CALKINS**  
For what?

**JULIE**  
Your coming through a bathroom window.

**CALKINS**  
I came because I must speak to you before your mother poisons your feeling against me.

**JULIE**  
Mother never poisons. She serves prejudice cold with mayonnaise.

**CALKINS**  
She considers me dangerous because I have been married.

**JULIE**  
Experience always frightens people who haven’t enjoyed it.

**CALKINS**  
You don’t condemn me, then?

**JULIE**  
I don’t know enough about you. Condemnation without research is low-brow.

**CALKINS**  
You know my past.

**JULIE**  
Only the parts with good punctuation.

**CALKINS**  
I told you everything.

**JULIE**  
Nobody ever tells everything. Half the human race would stop speaking if that were the rule.

He becomes solemn. Julie recognizes the approach of a speech and searches discreetly for the soap, perhaps as a weapon.

**CALKINS**  
My marriage taught me that love without spiritual companionship is merely a room in which two strangers hear each other breathe.

**JULIE**  
Couldn’t they open a window?

**CALKINS**  
You make a joke of pain.

**JULIE**  
Pain makes such poor conversation by itself.

**CALKINS**  
Clara never understood my need to create.

**JULIE**  
Did you create at breakfast?

**CALKINS**  
Sometimes.

**JULIE**  
Then I understand Clara.

**CALKINS**  
She mocked my ideals.

**JULIE**  
Ideals look funny in pajamas.

**CALKINS**  
Lois, this is not like you.

**JULIE**  
That’s the most encouraging thing you’ve said.

A footstep sounds in the hall. Julie freezes. The audience freezes, too, though it has paid to do so in comfort.

The knob turns. The door is locked now—not by foresight but because Lois, departing in wrath, turned the key from outside and pocketed it. This is either a fortunate accident or shameless construction. We prefer “construction.”

**LOIS’S VOICE**  
Julie?

Julie raises one warning finger toward Calkins.

**CALKINS**  
(whispering)  
Who is that?

**JULIE**  
Julie.

**CALKINS**  
Your sister?

**JULIE**  
Frequently.

**LOIS’S VOICE**  
Are you still in there?

**JULIE**  
Where else would I be?

**LOIS’S VOICE**  
Mother wants the blue powder.

**JULIE**  
Tell her I’m using it.

**LOIS’S VOICE**  
For what?

**JULIE**  
The goldfish has a rash.

There is a silence.

**LOIS’S VOICE**  
You’re an idiot.

**JULIE**  
Family resemblance!

Lois’s footsteps retreat.

Calkins studies Julie with new tenderness.

**CALKINS**  
You didn’t tell her I was here.

**JULIE**  
She’s nervous about wildlife.

**CALKINS**  
You protected me.

**JULIE**  
Don’t sentimentalize it. I protected the window.

**CALKINS**  
I had thought your sister understood me.

**JULIE**  
Julie understands everybody. That’s why she doesn’t approve of them.

**CALKINS**  
She dislikes me.

**JULIE**  
She thinks you’re an old man.

**CALKINS**  
I’m thirty-seven!

**JULIE**  
To twenty-two, thirty-seven is an historic monument with visiting hours.

**CALKINS**  
She is impertinent.

**JULIE**  
She’s young.

**CALKINS**  
Youth is no excuse for shallowness.

**JULIE**  
What is youth an excuse for? It ought to excuse something. Otherwise it’s just bad skin and no money.

Calkins is silent, struck despite himself.

**CALKINS**  
That’s rather good.

**JULIE**  
Write it down and misquote me.

**CALKINS**  
You’ve changed since yesterday.

**JULIE**  
Perhaps yesterday was badly cast.

**CALKINS**  
You seem freer—more vivid.

**JULIE**  
I’m au naturel.

**CALKINS**  
In manner?

**JULIE**  
Among other departments.

He fails to understand. We do not. A murmur travels through the imaginary orchestra seats and is suppressed by ushers of unusual moral stamina.

**CALKINS**  
I used to feel a reserve in you.

**JULIE**  
I’ve mislaid nearly everything today.

**CALKINS**  
Your reserve, your formality—

**JULIE**  
My towel.

**CALKINS**  
What?

**JULIE**  
My—towel of ivory. A poem. By—Shelley Longfellow.

**CALKINS**  
There is no such poem.

**JULIE**  
It was suppressed in Boston.

She reaches for the soap, misses, and splashes water over the side.

**CALKINS**  
There! Water again.

**JULIE**  
The goldfish applauded.

**CALKINS**  
I’m coming in.

**JULIE**  
Adrian, if you put one literary knee over that sill, I’ll scream the roof loose.

**CALKINS**  
You called me Adrian.

**JULIE**  
I called you several things internally.

**CALKINS**  
You never called me Adrian before.

**JULIE**  
Perhaps intimacy is a disease. It advances with the temperature.

He gazes at her. His expression softens into the dangerous earnestness of a man who has mistaken bewilderment for revelation.

**CALKINS**  
Do you know what has happened?

**JULIE**  
Yes, and when Lois finds out, it will happen harder.

**CALKINS**  
For months I’ve admired your serenity, your delicacy, your grave understanding. But today—today I see the woman beneath them.

Julie glances down into the tub.

**JULIE**  
You have remarkable eyesight.

**CALKINS**  
The real woman: capricious, mocking, elemental.

**JULIE**  
Don’t forget damp.

**CALKINS**  
I love this new courage in you.

**JULIE**  
It’s easy to be courageous when there’s nowhere to run.

**CALKINS**  
You have ceased to fear life.

**JULIE**  
I’m beginning to fear the hallway.

**CALKINS**  
Lois, listen to me.

**JULIE**  
I’ve been trying not to.

**CALKINS**  
My first marriage failed because we built upon illusion. I will make no such mistake again. Between us there must be absolute candor.

**JULIE**  
Let’s begin tomorrow.

**CALKINS**  
Now.

**JULIE**  
Tomorrow has better facilities.

**CALKINS**  
Tell me what you feel.

**JULIE**  
Warm around the edges.

**CALKINS**  
You evade me because you are moved.

**JULIE**  
That is not why I’m avoiding movement.

**CALKINS**  
Can’t you come nearer the window?

**JULIE**  
Not without launching the tub.

**CALKINS**  
Then let me see your face.

She rises a cautious inch. A gleaming bare shoulder appears. Calkins sighs.

**CALKINS**  
Surpassing loveliness!

**JULIE**  
Jumping cats.

**CALKINS**  
You’re like Botticelli’s Venus.

**JULIE**  
She had attendants with towels.

**CALKINS**  
You’ve read Botticelli?

**JULIE**  
Not since I left school.

**CALKINS**  
He was a painter.

**JULIE**  
Then he should have used shorter sentences.

Calkins laughs, genuinely this time.

**CALKINS**  
You destroy all my poses.

**JULIE**  
I thought they were furniture.

**CALKINS**  
That is what I need! Someone who will laugh me out of solemnity, challenge me, keep me young—

**JULIE**  
At thirty-seven you need a nurse with roller skates.

**CALKINS**  
Someone precisely like you.

Julie’s mischief pauses. For the first time she looks faintly alarmed—not by him, perhaps, but by the speed with which a joke can acquire a hat, coat, and legal intentions.

**JULIE**  
Now don’t go making anything permanent. This is only Thursday.

**CALKINS**  
Why did I never see it before?

**JULIE**  
Bad lighting.

**CALKINS**  
I thought love would come with music, with some great spiritual recognition.

**JULIE**  
It generally trips over the plumbing.

The pipe bangs again with orchestral emphasis.

**CALKINS**  
Even that seems like a sign.

**JULIE**  
It’s a sign the cottage needs repairing.

He presses both hands to the sill.

**CALKINS**  
Lois, I love you.

At this precise moment the key turns in the door.

We could delay it. We could permit Julie an answer, or send a bird through the window, or cause the blue fishermen to drop their net. But farce, like an express train and a jealous sister, has a schedule.

The door opens. **LOIS** enters with the towel over her arm.

**LOIS**  
Julie, Mother says if you don’t—

She sees Calkins at the window.

He turns and sees Lois.

He looks at Lois. He looks at Julie. He looks again at Lois, because hope is a persistent little animal.

**CALKINS**  
Lois?

**LOIS**  
Adrian!

**JULIE**  
Introductions seem unnecessary.

**LOIS**  
What are you doing here?

**CALKINS**  
I was speaking to—you.

**LOIS**  
To me?

**JULIE**  
Spiritually.

**CALKINS**  
But she— You— There are two—

**JULIE**  
That’s what Mother says when expenses come in.

Lois advances and, following Calkins’s horrified stare, sees the bare shoulders above the bathtub, the wet arm, the high porcelain rim, and at last the whole magnificent geometry of the misunderstanding.

**LOIS**  
You have been talking to my sister!

**CALKINS**  
I thought she was you.

**JULIE**  
He has a gift for character analysis.

**LOIS**  
Julie! You—you haven’t any clothes on!

**JULIE**  
I hadn’t noticed. The conversation was so elevating.

**LOIS**  
And he’s been standing there!

**CALKINS**  
I couldn’t see!

**JULIE**  
That was his first attraction.

**LOIS**  
What did you say to her?

**CALKINS**  
Nothing—everything—I—

**JULIE**  
He compared me to Venus and asked for absolute candor. One can’t have both.

**LOIS**  
Adrian!

**CALKINS**  
I declared my love.

Lois gives a small, civilized gasp, the kind approved by mothers, and faints with complete uncivilized efficiency.

The towel falls from her arm.

Julie’s eyes fasten upon it.

Calkins struggles with the window.

**CALKINS**  
She’s fainted! I’m coming in!

**JULIE**  
No!

**CALKINS**  
I must help her!

**JULIE**  
Help her from there. Send thoughts.

He gets one leg over the sill.

Julie rises sufficiently to snatch the fallen towel. Exactly how much the audience sees depends upon its seats, its conscience, and the management’s negotiations with the censor. She wraps the towel around herself with the speed of a magician producing a flag.

Calkins gets his second leg over.

**JULIE**  
Wait till I get out!

**CALKINS**  
Out of what?

Julie plants one foot on the floor, grips the towel, and looks toward the hall. Lois lies across her path. Calkins is halfway through the window. The goldfish, if there ever was one, has wisely left the profession.

**JULIE**  
(to the audience)  
If anybody asks, you saw nothing.

The lights vanish—swiftly, magnificently, and with more discretion than any character has shown all afternoon.

A Belasco midnight.
